# Branch cleanup audit

## Audit position

This audit records the cleanup boundary before any historical material is
deleted or any current claim is rewritten. It compares
`work/v3.2.2-review-fixes` at `7b634926` with `origin/main` at
`69b968906eeb88622f6480cd65a011a7d021b890`.

The branch is six commits ahead of `main` and changes 4,547 paths:

- 2,573 `build/` paths are deleted or changed; these are overwhelmingly
  generated compilation, probe, FPGA, and vacuity outputs.
- 948 vendored Coq-Undecidability source files are added. They are active
  inputs because `coq/_CoqProject` maps them into the `Undecidability`
  namespace; they are not review debris.
- 721 Coq paths are added or changed, including the active `kami_hw` proof
  layer and the kernel foundation additions.
- 219 `artifacts/` paths are added or changed.
- 37 scripts and 23 tests are added or changed.
- 10 monograph paths are changed, including both TeX sources and derived
  outputs.

The ordinary CI workflow is green on this revision, including proof
verification and the reproducibility gate. The separate `CI (Full)` workflow
is still running its hardware, FPGA, and fast-verify jobs; its completed jobs
are green at the time of this audit.

## What is canonical now

The following material is part of the current implementation and should be
kept, then reviewed for stable wording and source-of-truth consistency:

1. The active Coq corpus listed by `coq/_CoqProject`, including
   `coq/kami_hw/` and `coq/kernel/foundation/`.
2. The pinned `vendor/coq-undecidability` source required by the active Coq
   project, with its upstream/license provenance kept explicit.
3. The CI and reproducibility changes in `.github/workflows/`,
   `scripts/proof_gate_reproducible.sh`, and the Coq cache configuration.
4. The Inquisitor implementation and its generated zero-finding report.
5. Machine-readable receipts and manifests that are regenerated by a named
   command and consumed by CI, including assumption receipts, proof
   connectivity, vacuity, RTL pipeline, transform, and final-claim records.
6. The actual monograph and mathematical specification sources in
   `monograph/*.tex`, subject to the source-of-truth cleanup below.

These files are not candidates for blind deletion. Their comments, names,
cross-references, and scope statements do need a deliberate consistency pass.

## Findings requiring cleanup

### 1. The branch history is not release-shaped

The first and largest commit is named `wip: verify pre-commit gate` and
contains most of the active proof/hardware revision plus a review-session
snapshot. The later commits repair CI, but the branch still presents the
initial working-session shape. Before handoff, the commit history should be
made intentional: either retain a clear sequence of implementation commits
with release-quality messages or squash the working-session commit after the
content cleanup. Do not rewrite the public branch until the content and CI
state are final.

### 2. `artifacts/review_revision/` mixes evidence, fixtures, and history

This directory contains 411 files and is approximately 43 MB. It includes:

- 199 newly added paths in this branch;
- dated reviews, status ledgers, completion contracts, research requests,
  backlogs, and handoff documents;
- repeated Coq probes and their reports;
- compiled `.vo`, `.glob`, `.vok`, and `.vos` outputs;
- generated reports, JSON validation records, source manifests, and a
  36.7-MB `source.patch`.

The directory is not unused: scripts and current README sections reference
selected probes and reports. It is nevertheless not a coherent current
documentation surface. Its contents explicitly describe themselves as
historical, superseded, in progress, optional, or next work. The required
decision is to split it into three classes:

- current assurance records that belong under a small, stable evidence
  directory;
- executable fixtures that belong under `tests/` or a clearly named probe
  directory and are invoked by tests;
- historical session material that belongs in an archive or is removed after
  confirming no provenance or CI requirement depends on it.

Compiled proof objects and generated reports must not remain in the current
record merely because they were present in a review snapshot. Every retained
artifact needs an owner, generator, input manifest, and consumer.

The first files to refactor are `STATUS.md`, `COMPLETION_CONTRACT.md`,
`LOCAL_CONTRACT_REVIEW.md`, `NATIVE_REPRODUCTION.md`,
`REALIZATION_ASSURANCE.md`, `RESEARCH_BACKLOG.md`, and the many
`*_REVIEW.md`/`*_REPORT.txt` companions. They should become either stable
scope/assurance documents or explicitly archived history, not a mixture of
both.

### 3. Current documents depend on historical snapshot paths

The root README, contribution guide, monograph, active Coq comments, and
reproduction scripts point into `artifacts/review_revision/`. In particular,
the README uses that directory as the authority for realization assurance,
review status, B3, B4, and native reproduction. This makes the current public
description depend on a working-session snapshot.

The cleanup must move current claims to stable documents and leave historical
links only in an archive index. The following scripts also require a decision
rather than silent deletion:

- `scripts/reproduce_coq.py`
- `scripts/package_review_revision.py`
- `scripts/check_review_contracts.py`
- `scripts/verify_revision_snapshot.py`

If a probe remains part of verification, give it a stable home and test entry
point. If it is only evidence from the design session, preserve its provenance
in an archive manifest or remove it.

### 4. The monograph has multiple unsynchronized representations

`monograph/monograph.tex` is the narrative source and
`monograph/thiele_machine_math_spec.tex` is the mathematical-specification
source. The repository also tracks PDFs, layout plaintext, TeX auxiliary
outputs, and a second plaintext specification. `build_monograph.sh` rebuilds
only the narrative PDF and `monograph.txt`; it does not build or validate the
mathematical-specification PDF or `math_spec_plaintext.txt`.

This is a source-of-truth defect. The cleanup must establish, in the build
script and documentation:

- which TeX files are authoritative;
- which PDFs are publication outputs;
- whether `monograph.txt` and `math_spec_plaintext.txt` are generated or
  maintained sources;
- which auxiliary files are disposable and should not be tracked;
- how generated text is checked against the corresponding TeX/PDF.

The wording pass must also remove design-session narration from the active
monograph. Real mathematical limitations remain, but they must be stated as
stable scope, assumptions, or non-results rather than as a diary of what was
previously believed, repaired, deferred, or discovered.

### 5. Active claims and open limitations need one stable vocabulary

The current README, monograph, Coq comments, and review artifacts use several
overlapping status vocabularies: “open,” “remaining,” “deferred,” “in
progress,” “historical,” “superseded,” “alternative construction,” “repair,”
and “next action.” Some of these identify genuine mathematical boundaries;
others describe the sequence of the implementation session.

The rewrite must preserve every real limitation while removing chronology
from active statements. The stable categories should be:

- proved by the current checked source;
- tested or reproduced but not semantically proved;
- assumed by the stated theorem;
- outside the current scope;
- historical evidence, not a current claim.

No document should imply that an artifact is current merely because it is
committed, and no document should imply that a theorem is unproved merely
because an old review called it a next step.

### 6. Generated artifacts need explicit retention rules

The branch already removes a large amount of stale `build/` material, but the
repository still tracks generated outputs in both `build/` and `artifacts/`.
The audit found tracked build files in OCaml/ML, JSON, text, Verilog/VVP,
graphs, and other generated formats, while `.gitignore` says most build
outputs are regenerated. The retained set needs to be reduced to outputs that
are required for CI, release inspection, or reproducibility.

For every retained generated file, record:

1. its generator command;
2. its source/input manifest;
3. its consumer or reason for publication;
4. whether byte identity is required;
5. the clean command that removes it.

The same rule applies to assumption probes, vacuity probes, proof DAGs,
Inquisitor reports, RTL manifests, synthesis summaries, and FPGA outputs.

### 7. Active comments need a semantic, not historical, pass

The newly added `coq/kami_hw` and `coq/kernel/foundation` sources contain
useful contract explanations, but the branch-wide comment scan also finds
legacy/snapshot wording, deferred obligations, repair notes, and references to
old proof paths. Those comments must be checked against the actual theorem
types and active `_CoqProject` closure.

The cleanup should remove comments that describe how a proof was discovered or
fixed. It should retain comments that explain a non-obvious invariant, a
deliberate abstraction boundary, a trusted translation step, or a real
assumption. Comments must not claim full refinement, physical synthesis, or
unbounded behavior when the checked theorem only covers a bounded or selected
case.

## Proposed cleanup sequence

1. Freeze this audit and the green CI evidence for the current revision.
2. Build an ownership/index manifest for all tracked `artifacts/`, `build/`,
   review probes, receipts, and monograph outputs.
3. Move current assurance documents and executable probes to stable locations;
   archive or remove historical session material only after reference and CI
   checks pass.
4. Establish the monograph/specification source-of-truth and regenerate every
   retained derived output from it.
5. Rewrite README, contribution guidance, Coq READMEs, monograph prose, and
   active comments into stable present-tense scope/assurance language in the
   author's voice.
6. Regenerate receipts, manifests, reports, and dependency graphs from the
   cleaned tree; remove stale generated outputs.
7. Run local hooks, the full closeout gate, ordinary CI, and full CI again.
8. Review the final diff against `main` for unused paths, historical leakage,
   stale links, generated-file drift, and claims unsupported by checked
   evidence.

## Completion criteria for this cleanup

The cleanup is complete only when the repository has one discoverable current
description of its proof and implementation surface; historical evidence is
clearly separated; every retained generated artifact has a reproducible owner;
the monograph and mathematical specification agree with the checked source;
active comments describe current semantics; and the full verification gates
remain green after regeneration.

No content deletion or historical rewrite is authorized by this audit alone.
Those changes will be made in the classified groups above and verified before
the corresponding evidence is removed.
