4. The front-matter account of the tools

CURRENT:

I used language models to write the code and the proofs in this project, and I trust language models about as far as I can throw them, which is exactly why every formal declaration is checked by a proof assistant that doesn't care who or what wrote it. Coq rejects a wrong proof the same whether a tenured professor or a model hands it over, and it accepted these, with none of my own axioms anywhere in them, re-checkable by the procedure in Part V. Being self-taught and using modern tools didn't make this looser. It's the reason I made a machine check the formal corpus, and then built an auditor to read over the checker's shoulder for every way I might have cheated, on purpose or by accident (Part V says what it looks for). It doesn't trust me either. Good.

PROPOSED:

I used language models to write the code and the proofs in this project, and I trust language models about as far as I can throw them. Every argument still has to hold up when you read it. I used a proof checker to look for gaps in the formal derivations, and built an auditor to look for the ways I'd let an assumption do the work. Neither can tell me that I've asked the right question or that a definition means what I say it means. Part V gives the procedure and its limits. The work stands or falls on the arguments.

7. The shared foundation

CURRENT:

It doesn't mean Coq's own foundation is proved consistent; it's believed consistent, and that can't be proved from inside, by Gödel (Section 13), so the foundation is trusted, and “trusted” has substance: it's the same kernel CompCert and the four-colour proof stand on, and a bug in it would take all of them down together, which is a kind of safety in numbers.

PROPOSED:

It doesn't mean Coq's own foundation is proved consistent. Gödel's second incompleteness theorem limits what a sufficiently strong, effectively axiomatized theory can establish about its own consistency, if it is consistent (Section 13). The foundation and the implementation of the checker remain dependencies. CompCert and the four-colour formalization share them; that does not make them independent checks of those dependencies.
