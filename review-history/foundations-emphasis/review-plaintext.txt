F20. Replace the “zero Admitted” subsection with a short dependency note — source line 16148

Remove the boast, the repeated denials, the theorem census, and the claim that zero project axioms defines every edition. Keep one accurate explanation of formal dependencies and where their detailed record lives.

CURRENT:

What “zero Admitted” means

“Zero Admitted” is literal. Nowhere in the proof tree did I write “trust me” and walk on. Every step the checker could see, it saw, end to end, with no “and the rest is obvious.” It's a hygiene fact about the gap-catcher. It passes no verdict on the argument: it says I didn't let myself skip a step where the machine was watching. It does not say the statements are the right statements.

What it doesn't mean, listed. It doesn't mean the theorems prove physical reality; they prove things about the machine as formalised, and whether that describes the world is answered by experiments (Section 4, Section 21). It doesn't mean Coq's own foundation is proved consistent. Gödel's second incompleteness theorem limits what a sufficiently strong, effectively axiomatized theory can establish about its own consistency, if it is consistent (Section 13). The foundation and the implementation of the checker remain dependencies. CompCert and the four-colour formalization share them; that does not make them independent checks of those dependencies. And it doesn't mean no axioms: the proofs use a handful of standard-library assumptions, and the assumption audit names them: functional extensionality on dependent functions (two functions that agree everywhere are equal); two decidability principles that come with the standard library's real numbers, used wherever real analysis is, for example in the matrix proofs behind the game check and in the real-valued results of Sections 4, 12 and 21; and, in some theorems, the law of excluded middle (every proposition is true or false). The count, from the full audit: the full-corpus probe covers 5,997 named theorems across 341 files: 5,198 close under the global Coq context outright, and 799 use only Coq standard-library assumptions. Zero project-local axioms appear in any of the 5,997 dependency trees. The small machine's files, under minimal/, are inside that probe too, and every one of their 1,417 probed theorems comes back closed under Coq's own rules, with no axiom at all. Among them are the small machine, its version for any property language, the simpler host, the definition of Thiele-complete and its window theorem, the host machine with a register for every whole number, the numbers that write a guest program down, and the pigeonhole (Section 16), and the files that run Parts II and III on that machine and the diagonal on its programs (Sections 16.17 and 16.18). Those files also print the assumptions of their headline theorems. Both those printouts and the full audit report no added axioms for these results. That does not remove the hypotheses written in each theorem's statement. Only two small files under minimal/, MuCore.v and Napkin.v, sit outside the big audit, and they are built and checked on their own by bash scripts/verify_core.sh, which compiles each from a clean slate with coqc, fails on any Axioms: line, and expects ten closed theorems from MuCore.v and seven from Napkin.v. The universal program's own files, which lean on the vendored library's counter-machine blocks, sit in the kernel tree with the other link files, and they also end by printing their headline theorems' assumptions; so do the files of the priced host, the compiler from computable functions and the theorem for every computably presented Thiele machine. In plain words: every theorem in the audit either uses no axiom at all or uses only those standard-library axioms, none of mine. The hypotheses in each statement are a separate matter, and they stay. Those counts are of the sources at this edition. Add a theorem and the counts move. The shape, no axiom of mine anywhere, is what every edition has to keep.

PROPOSED:

Dependencies of the formal derivations

The companion formalization uses Coq's logic and, in some results, standard-library axioms: functional extensionality on dependent functions, two decidability principles for the real numbers, and excluded middle. The report in the reproduction instructions records these dependencies theorem by theorem. A theorem's own hypotheses are part of its statement and must be read there; an empty dependency list does not remove them.

This inventory helps someone inspect the formal derivations. It does not establish that the statements mean what the prose says, describe a physical device, or matter. Those questions belong to the arguments and, for claims about the world, to experiments.
