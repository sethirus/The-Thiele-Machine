BEDROCK — WHERE THE THREE ROUTES END
12 October 2026

The question. Is the toll a fact about computation, or a definition that is
true because the model was built so that it would be? Three routes could
settle that: a characterization (conditions stated outside the model force
it), a separation (something true here and false in an established
framework), and a prediction (a measurable consequence). This note pushes
each route to the point where proof stops, states what stands there with
its checked name, and says exactly what is left and why no further theorem
of the same kind can move it. Everything cited below is in the book and in
the checked sources unless marked NEW. The one addition is RetentionBound.v.

ROUTE 1 — CHARACTERIZATION: WHAT IS FORCED, AND BY WHAT

Forced by counting alone, no price anywhere:

  F1. Finite, permanent, flip ⇒ the raising move merges.
      Theorem thm:perm-flip-merges; permanent_flip_is_not_injective.
  F2. Drop permanence, keep finiteness: a move that raises the reading
      somewhere either merges or revokes it somewhere else. No third case.
      Theorem thm:merge-or-revoke; flip_merges_or_revokes.
  F3. The only moves every merge-pricing schedule must charge are the
      merging ones, with no finiteness needed.
      Theorem thm:forced-iff-merge; forced_priced_iff_merges.
  F4. The event is not special. F1 prices any yes/no reading that, once
      on, stays on; the latch theorem shows a base-driven permanent record
      is a latch over any base, and the event is a free choice.
      Observation thm:u11-latch; Corollary cor:u11-event-free.
  F5. NEW, checked today. Door one with a number. Any deterministic
      implementation with M states that keeps the history of a reset of an
      N-state family injectively through T repetitions needs
      M >= 1 + (T+1)(N-1). So a finite memory keeps such history for at
      most (M-1)/(N-1) repetitions, whatever its encoding, hidden state or
      clock, and after that some boundary step merges.
      The argument, with chairs. Take any of the N-1 live situations and
      follow its chair through T applications: T+1 chairs. If the path
      revisited a chair, undo the common steps (allowed, since no two
      visited chairs are sent to one place) and the starting chair would
      equal a chair reached after at least one step; but the start is live
      and every later chair is "nothing current". So the path has T+1
      distinct chairs. Two live situations' paths cannot touch: undo to the
      earlier time and either two situations share a start, or a live start
      sits on a reset chair. So the paths are disjoint: (T+1)(N-1) chairs.
      One more: where the "nothing current" situation's own path ends after
      T steps. If that chair were on a live path, the same undoing puts a
      live start on a reset chair or two starts on one chair. So it is new.
      For the 81 histories: seven bits (128 chairs) cannot survive one
      reset (161 needed); eight bits (256) survive two and not three (321).
      The same argument is typed out in
      research/structural-state/machine-preservation/RetentionBound.v and
      compiles with the standard library alone; that compilation checks
      the typing of the steps above, nothing more. The construction showing
      the bound is attained is in that directory's Result.txt, Section 7.

Forced by the three premises together, and by nothing less:

  F6. Finite + permanent + merge pricing ⇒ the toll.
      Observation thm:toll-derived; a2_from_merging_price_and_permanence.
  F7. Each premise is necessary, by a built counterexample:
        drop finiteness: the endless strip raises and keeps a flag with
        every move injective, at cost zero
        (unbounded_history_escapes; nec_f_history_infinite);
        drop permanence: the coin raises and lowers with one injective move
        at cost zero (revocable_certificate_escapes);
        drop the price: the stamp at cost zero is finite and permanent and
        merges, and the toll fails (free_merge_escapes).
      And the premises are jointly consistent: the stamp at cost one
      (priced_reset_satisfies_premises).
  F8. The three forms of the price premise (merge, halving, entropy) are
      one premise at the pair level and ordered in strength above it.
      Theorem thm:one-premise; one_premise_three_forms.

Where route 1 ends. Given a permanent yes/no reading on a finite machine
and a charge on merging, the price is forced, the premise set is minimal,
and the machine does not matter. What no theorem forces, and no theorem of
this kind could: which reading to make permanent (a theorem cannot choose
an event; F4 says every permanent reading is priced alike), and whether a
merge costs anything (a theorem cannot price a merge; that is door three).
The first is the pointer thesis, stated as a thesis with its refutations
listed. The second is physics, route 3.

ROUTE 2 — SEPARATION: NONE, AND WHY NONE IS POSSIBLE

A certification system is a transition system with a cost function and a
predicate satisfying one inequality. Drop the cost and it is an ordinary
deterministic machine. The book proves the inclusion in the hard direction
too: the small machine is two-counter universal, U runs every program of
the small machine on an ordinary counter host with the ledger carried, and
U_P runs every computably presented Thiele machine. The Scope section says
in so many words that no complexity-class separation follows from the
window collisions, and the research notes confirm the same at the level of
data structures: every bound there is inherited from or reduced to standard
computation (Pătraşcu–Thorup through the rollback reduction, union-find,
Myhill–Nerode). "Thiele machine" names a class of cost-annotated systems,
as "reversible" or "monotone" name classes, not a new computational power.

What does separate is the accounting, and it is provable because it is
small:

  S1. The toll is logically independent of the monitor safety property
      "no certify without an earned commit". A reference monitor enforces
      that property at cost zero (an unpriced small machine), and a toll
      system need not have an earned chain at all (cost one on every move,
      no check). The book's own line is that the difference from the
      monitor literature is that here the step enforces the price.
  S2. No window that shows a raising step and a quiet step alike can
      price the raise exactly. Observation thm:shadow-price.
  S3. The exact-check rule remembers which claim was checked, not what
      follows; the inference rule remembers what follows. They are
      different machines with different state counts (B_n bits against
      log2(B_n+1)), and neither is a compression of the other.
      research/structural-state/partition-preservation/Result.txt.

Where route 2 ends. There is no separation theorem to prove, because the
class is defined by an accounting constraint on ordinary machines. Any
claim of a computational separation would be false, and the book already
refuses it. The separation that exists is S1–S3: a rule about what is
charged and what a view can recover.

ROUTE 3 — PREDICTION: EXACTLY LANDAUER'S, UNDER LANDAUER'S CONDITIONS

The model's physical content is one named premise and its consequences:

  P1. Landauer's principle as a premise (prem:landauer); the heat floor of
      a permanent flip under an uncertain preparation (cor:heat-floor;
      permanent_flip_heat_floor); no floor from this premise for a known
      start (thm:known-state; known_state_flip_forces_no_heat); the toll
      from entropy pricing (thm:u04-entropy-toll).
  P2. The generic experiment has been done. Bérut, Arakelyan, Petrosyan,
      Ciliberto, Dillenschneider and Lutz, Nature 483 (2012): a colloidal
      particle in a modulated double well, a bit erased; the abstract says
      the mean dissipated heat saturates at the Landauer bound in the limit
      of long erasure cycles (record checked today through Europe PMC).
      That is "restore to one" from a 50/50 preparation: the stamp.
  P3. The model adds no prediction beyond Landauer. A device realizing a
      permanent yes/no record on finite memory, driven from an uncertain
      preparation, must hand over at least kT ln 2 per bit actually removed
      at the raising step. That is Landauer applied to a latch, not a new
      law, and the book says so. Nothing is predicted from the ledger
      alone: door one shows the same ledger at zero physical cost on an
      unbounded device, and F5 says how long a bounded device can imitate
      that before it must merge.
  P4. The dispute is named (Norton; Earman and Norton) and the book takes
      the sound horn deliberately: ordinary thermodynamics relabelled onto
      a ledger is all the physics it asks for.

Where route 3 ends. The only measurement that could break the model's
physical leg is a violation of Landauer's bound, which would be a result
about thermodynamics, not about this model. The only model-specific
experiment is a latch driven from an uncertain preparation, and that is
the Bérut experiment under another name. No experiment distinguishes a
Thiele machine from a correctly accounted ordinary machine, because the
model claims no such distinction.

THE ANSWER

What the model is: a conditional theorem (F6) with a minimal, independent,
separately refutable premise set (F7), invariant under the choice of
machine and event (F4), with one physical anchor (P1–P2), plus an
engineering construction, the small machine, in which the price is
enforced by the step rather than by a watcher (S1). What it is not: a new
law of computation, a new complexity class, or a new physical prediction.

"Did I fit the model?" The counting theorems never mention the machine; they
hold for any finite deterministic system with any permanent yes/no
observable. Each premise can be refused with a built counterexample, and
none is redundant. That is the standard a non-arbitrary definition meets:
not that it is forced by nothing, but that every assumption is visible,
minimal and separately refutable, and the conclusion does not depend on
the construction used to exhibit it. On that standard the toll passes. On
the standard "forced by nature with no premise" nothing passes, Landauer's
principle included, which is why the book keeps it a premise with its
critics named.

WHAT REMAINS, EXACTLY

  1. Which event deserves the permanent record: the pointer thesis. A
     claim about how records are copied and relied on, with its
     refutations listed in the book. Not closeable by proof.
  2. Whether merging costs anything in the world: Landauer under his
     conditions, measured in the generic case, philosophically disputed.
     Not closeable by proof.
  3. Whether there is a smaller start than a partition: the author's own
     open question.

These are the book's own "doesn't settle" list. This session adds none and
removes none. The gap felt as "maybe I just fitted the model" is not a gap
in the proofs; it is the location of the two premises, and both are marked.
