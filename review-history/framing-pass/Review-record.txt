RESULT
Forty revisions align the opening, short version, chapter transitions, implementation
discussion, proposal summaries and conclusion around structure held as state and
acted on by the step. Certification is presented as the worked record case. Its
mathematical results, and the separate pointer thesis proposing that event, retain
their actual statements and conditions.

The revision removes explicit competing directions to the reader: certification
as what the rest of the book is for, the certification result as the headline, the
flag as the one lamp the author set out to light, six instructions as the idea
itself, and record pricing as the one question the whole book is about.

The sorter and copier now explicitly hold an ordered list either way. Their
contrast concerns what each run did with that list. A checked relation and a record
of checking it are situated in the machine's operations rather than described as
the machine making a claim true.

The Minsky encoding and interpreter timings remain visible. The execution chapter
separates the ledger's charge from the work the representation takes to execute a
run. All benchmark tables, large running-time estimates and statements of what was
not executed remain. No claim of an efficient alternative is made. Changing the
base and composing machines now have their place in the opening map and conclusion.
