REVIEW
======

THE COMPLETED PARTITION COST STUDY
Research review for Devon Thiele — 12 October 2026

The question now has precise answers for two interfaces to the partition
model. The same established structure can have different computational
costs depending on whether a caller supplies a complete grouping or one
equality at a time. The permission policy matters just as much as the input.

THE TANGIBLE DISTINCTION

Check that cup A has the same mark as cup B. Then check that B has the same
mark as C. The equality A=C follows, although it was never checked directly.

An exact-check policy remembers which pair was checked. An inference policy
must recognize the connection through the checked chain. Your existing
partition machine uses exact matching; the inference policy studied here
is an explicit alternative. Both can retain the channel, historical record,
trap, actual-check requirement and three-mark minimum. They make different
promises about which claim may be committed.

THE ANSWERS

1. Minimal state can be characterized through the permission policy.

   For a finite sound closure policy, count its distinct active closed sets
   of claims, and add one state for no current check. The logarithm of that
   count is the exact abstract permission-bit requirement. Distinguishable
   next commitments prove necessity; storing the closure proves sufficiency.
   This is a state-count result, not a free algorithm for arbitrary inference.

2. Complete groupings have tight memory and time bounds.

   With n labels supplied per instruction, inferred permission uses
   Theta(n log n) structural bits and Theta(n) worst-case time for SET,
   CHECK and COMMIT. The implementation meets both bounds. CERTIFY captures
   an owned historical snapshot in constant structural time.

   Every grouping input can hide a decisive difference in its last unread
   entry. More memory cannot eliminate that worst-case input-reading cost.
   A connected-components calculation combines checks in linear time.

3. Pairwise claims reveal an unconditional computational barrier.

   Exact matching needs Theta(n^2) permission bits and permits constant-time
   CHECK and COMMIT within a fixed-key epoch. Inferred permission needs only
   Theta(n log n) structural bits, but answering whether an equality follows
   becomes incremental connectivity.

   Under the stated deterministic logarithmic-word model, making CHECK
   asymptotically faster than log n / log log n forces worst-case COMMIT
   time to n^(1-o(1)). In particular, constant-time updates cannot coexist
   with polylogarithmic worst-case inferred commitments.

   This is an application of the published Pătraşcu–Thorup lower bound,
   not a new lower bound or an unresolved conjecture. The report proves
   the transfer, including the fatal-trap issue: a trusted simulation logs
   and reverses a trial commitment, at constant-factor worst-case overhead.
   It does not grant the adversarial caller rollback access to the machine.

   The known optimal balanced worst-case bound is Theta(log n/log log n),
   attainable in O(n) words. The supplied pair implementation uses the
   simpler, fully checked O(log n) union-by-size algorithm. The more complex
   optimal implementation is cited, not claimed to have been built here.

WHAT WAS VERIFIED

Three implementations were checked against an independent mathematical
reference: complete-grouping inference, pair inference, and exact pair
matching. Exhaustive small-state checks, 24,300 random transition checks,
input lower-bound witnesses and historical-snapshot checks passed.

The rollback simulation answered 5,899 graph queries, including 478 negative
ones, with exact state restoration after every query. Scale checks reached
32,768 objects and matched the proved linear graph-work bound. Timings are
local observations, not the proof of the asymptotic results.

The ledger remains explicit. Unbounded accumulated bills need additional
memory and arithmetic time. A mark is not being equated with a CPU step,
heat, or entropy. Working storage and usable external history are accounted
for in the stated models.

WHAT THIS CONTRIBUTES

The model now has a worked resource analysis of how established structural
relations govern later actions. It distinguishes retaining a checked claim
from maintaining the consequences of many checks, and gives exact state
counts plus tight operation bounds in specified cases. It is independent
of the Small Machine's counters and interpreter overhead.

These results use classical mathematics and established complexity theorems.
Novelty has not been established. The study does not claim a new law of
computation or a complete frontier for every memory budget and language.
The worst-case rollback argument does not establish an amortized lower bound.

monitors.py         The three monitors and rollback simulation.

Run: python3 verify.py

represented as complete by this focused research package.


MEMORY AND COMPUTATIONAL COST: COMPLETE REPORT
==============================================

PROTECTED STRUCTURAL STATE: MEMORY, INPUT SIZE, AND INFERENCE TIME
Research note for Devon Thiele — 12 October 2026

PURPOSE AND STATUS

This completes the next bounded study of the existing partition example:
exact permission-state counts, an optimal complete-partition implementation,
pair-at-a-time implementations, and a worst-case lower-bound transfer that
handles fatal traps. The book and its original exact-check policy are not
changed. The inference policy is the explicit alternative analyzed in the
companion partition-preservation/Result.txt, not a silent reinterpretation
of the original same-claim requirement.

The source correspondence remains minimal/PartitionReading.v at commit
e4c234627651f1b12eb649148a051d5a252ed6b4. Mathematical arguments are stated
here in ordinary terms. monitors.py supplies executable implementations;
verify.py checks them against a separate relation-matrix reference.

The combinatorial tools and graph algorithms are classical. The dynamic
lower bounds are attributed to existing research. No open-problem solution,
new physical law, or original general lower-bound method is claimed.

1. THE CONTRACT BEING MEASURED

There are n named items. A grouping P says that any two items in the same
P-block have equal keys. Write P <= K if P refines the current key partition
K: every P-block fits inside a K-block. The discrete partition 0 separates
all items; the indiscrete partition 1 groups all items together.

CHECK P must test this assertion against the current key. A passing check
adds P to the current evidence. SET replaces the key and clears current
checks and the channel, while retaining the first historical record.

Two permission policies are compared:

  Exact:    COMMIT H requires H itself among the current checked partitions.
  Inferred: COMMIT H requires at least one current check and H <= J,
            where J is the join of the current checked partitions.

CERTIFY requires a channel. Its first success stores the committed claim
and the historical key; later successes retain that first record. A failure
traps permanently. A direct instruction on a trapped state preserves the
other fields and still adds its specified price. SET costs zero; the other
three instructions cost one, including failures. These are ledger marks,
not an assertion that the implementation uses one CPU instruction.

The adversarial caller selects instructions and their payloads. It cannot
alter monitor-owned state except through these instructions. Inputs are
available during the call, and may be reused or mutated afterward. No free
authenticated external transcript, stored evidence table, claim fingerprint,
or previously validated claim handle is supplied.

Memory counts all retained information used to make exact decisions and all
working storage in the algorithmic upper bounds. Describing it as protected
state assumes no additional usable history carrier. It is not a lower bound
for cryptographic schemes that outsource authenticated state under a changed
interface or permit computational error.

For runtime, use a deterministic word RAM with words of Theta(log(n+1)) bits,
with a sufficiently large constant to address the polynomial-size arrays.
Arithmetic and memory access on such words take constant time. Finite runs
of polynomial length fit their ledger in O(1) words. For longer runs, the
ledger qualification in Section 7 applies. We do not count a Python object
as a machine word or claim Python attains the abstract bit bounds exactly.

2. AN EXACT MEMORY FORMULA FOR A FIXED INFERENCE POLICY

Let L be a finite language of claims, and fix a current key K. Let L_K be
the claims sound for K. Let c be a sound closure policy on subsets of L:
it is extensive, monotone and idempotent, and every claim in c(T) follows
from the soundness assertions in T. Permission means T is nonempty and
H belongs to c(T).

Define

  M(K,c) = 1 + number of distinct sets c(T)
                   for nonempty subsets T of L_K.

The initial 1 represents no current check. The count is zero inside the
sum when L_K is empty, giving M=1.

Theorem C1. The permission component at fixed K has exactly M(K,c)
distinguishable states, and therefore needs ceil(log2 M(K,c)) bits in an
abstract fixed-width encoding. This counts permission state with the other
machine fields supplied, not the total cost of storing K, claims or a bill.

Proof. Store either the no-check state or the closed set c(T). The identity

  c(T union {P}) = c(c(T) union {P})

shows that an inserted successful check can be incorporated using the stored
closure alone. It follows from extensiveness and monotonicity in one direction
and from monotonicity and idempotence in the other. SET returns to no-check.
The COMMIT guard is exactly determined by this representation.

Different active closed sets differ on some claim H, whose COMMIT succeeds
in one and traps in the other. Every active closed set is nonempty by
extensiveness, so some COMMIT distinguishes it from no-check as well.
Thus all M states are necessary.

These distinctions can be reached with equal ledgers and instruction counts
when L_K is nonempty. Put t=|L_K|. For each nonempty T, SET K, check each
member, then repeat a member until t checks have occurred. For the no-check
state, perform t sound checks and then SET K. All have t+1 instructions,
ledger t, identical key, empty channel and historical record, and no trap.
No common supplied field carries the distinctions. End proof.

This formula characterizes memory through the claim language and inference
policy. It supplies no efficient ranking of its classes and no runtime
bound for an arbitrary c. A uniform implementation also needs an effective
description of the policy.

For all partitions at K=1, exact matching has M=2^(B_n), whereas inferred
permission has M=B_n+1. Here B_n is the number of partitions of n items.
The companion note proves these counts directly.

3. COMPLETE GROUPINGS: TIGHT SPACE AND TIME

In this interface SET, CHECK and COMMIT supply an array of n labels, one
per item. Every label is an integer in [0,n). Repeated or unused labels are
allowed. The array need not be canonically named. There is no trusted handle
identifying its partition. One array entry occupies one word.

Theorem C2. Under inferred permission, the structural monitor, including
working storage and owned historical snapshots, uses O(n log(n+1)) bits.
SET, CHECK and COMMIT take O(n) worst-case time. CERTIFY takes O(1) structural
time. The memory bound and the first three worst-case time bounds are
asymptotically optimal for this interface, for n>=2.

Construction. Retain the current key labels K, established partition J,
evidence-presence flag, optional channel, first historical record, and trap.
Arrays for K and committed claims are owned and immutable. Their references
may be captured by the historical record; later SET and COMMIT allocate new
arrays. Only a constant number of length-n arrays remain live.

Testing P <= Q takes one pass. For each P-label, remember the Q-label of
its first item. Every later item with that P-label must have the same
Q-label. The representative table has n entries.

To compute J join P, form a star inside each block of J and each block of
P. Each partition needs at most n-1 undirected star edges. Their union has
at most 2(n-1) edges. Its connected components are exactly J join P:
each star connects its original block, and graph paths express precisely
the equivalence closure of the two sets of equality relations.

An adjacency-list traversal labels these components in O(n) time and words.
There are at most 4(n-1) directed adjacency entries, each scanned once.
This avoids a logarithmic factor from performing separate union operations
when the whole grouping is already supplied as one batch.

CHECK first validates P <= K, then performs that join on success and sets
the evidence flag. COMMIT validates the flag and H <= J, then retains an
owned copy of H. SET copies its new K and resets current evidence and the
channel. CERTIFY captures the current immutable channel and key references
on first success. It does not copy n labels. Trap and ledger branches retain
the source behavior. All temporary arrays also have O(n) entries.

Correctness follows from the refinement test, the graph characterization
of the join, and inspection of the instruction rules in Section 1. Induction
on the trace preserves the key, permitted/refused actions, committed claim,
first historical record, trap, and every ledger increment. The stored join
summarizes the evidence; it does not reveal the original checked list.

Space lower bound. Distinct active partitions J have different refinement
sets, and hence some COMMIT separates them. No-check is separated from each
active state by COMMIT 0. At K=1 all B_n+1 states are reachable with equal
other fields, as in C1, so at least log2(B_n+1) bits are necessary.

For completeness, log2 B_n = Theta(n log n) requires no delicate asymptotic
formula. There are at most n^n labelings, giving the upper bound. For 2m<=n,
pair the first m items bijectively with the next m, leaving any remaining
items separate. There are m! different such partitions. The last floor(m/2)
factors of m! are at least m/2, yielding an Omega(n log n) logarithm when
m=floor(n/2). Thus the O(n log n) implementation matches the lower bound
up to constants, including its workspace. This does not claim exact
ceil(log2(B_n+1))-bit storage with the same runtime.

Input-time lower bound. Run CHECK on P=(0,1,...,n-1) with K discrete. It
must pass. If the algorithm does not read coordinate i, change only that
coordinate to the label of a different item j. The same observations now
hide an unsound equality, which must fail. Therefore every coordinate must
be read on the original input. For COMMIT, establish the discrete partition
first and use the same argument: the original claim is permitted and the
altered one is not. This proves Omega(n) probes for CHECK and COMMIT even
with additional persistent memory.

For SET, give those two key arrays in the same transient input buffer. If
coordinate i is unread, the retained states agree. A later CHECK asserting
i=j must then give the same answer, although it is sound only for the
altered key. Hence SET must also read every coordinate in the worst case.
Deferred inspection of caller-owned memory is excluded by the input contract.
Together with the construction, these are tight worst-case bounds. End proof.

Increasing the memory budget cannot improve the asymptotic O(n) bound for
these complete-array instructions. The minimum-space threshold is already
attainable together with minimum-order runtime. This settles this interface
without a further asymptotic memory-time trade-off. Smaller constants and
near-information-minimum encodings remain separate questions.

4. PAIR CLAIMS: EXPRESSIVENESS WITHOUT AN n-WORD PAYLOAD

The compact claim (i,j) asserts that two items have equal keys. As a partition
it has one two-item block and all other items separate. A self-pair is the
discrete partition 0; all self-pairs express the same claim. The language
therefore has t=n(n-1)/2+1 distinct claims.

CHECK(i,j) tests K[i]=K[j]. Under inferred permission, its effect on J is
to connect the components containing i and j. COMMIT(i,j) succeeds exactly
when at least one current check exists and those components coincide.
SET still takes a complete key array and starts a new evidence epoch.

Theorem C3. At fixed K=1, the exact pair policy needs exactly t permission
bits. The inferred pair policy needs ceil(log2(B_n+1)) permission bits as
an abstract state count, and has an O(n log n)-bit implementation.

Proof. Exact matching can independently remember every subset of the t
claims; a COMMIT on a differing member separates any two subsets. A t-bit
membership vector suffices. Inferred pair checks can generate any partition
by adding a spanning star within each desired block. Distinct partitions
disagree on some pair's connectivity. The active discrete partition is
generated by a self-pair and is distinct from no-check. Thus there are
exactly B_n+1 distinguishable permission states. End proof.

All inferred states can be reached with equal ledgers: use at most n-1
star edges, pad with self-pair checks to max(1,n-1) checks, and use an initial
SET. The no-check witness uses the same number of checks followed by SET.
The exact-policy equal-ledger witnesses are those in C1 with t checks.

Implemented bounds in a fixed-key epoch:

  Exact pairs:    Theta(n^2) permission bits; O(1) CHECK and COMMIT.
  Inferred pairs: Theta(n log n) structural bits; O(log n) CHECK and COMMIT.

The exact implementation packs the t flags into ordinary machine words,
using a triangular index for distinct pairs and one bit for the self-pair
claim. It never treats an n^2-bit integer operation as constant time.
Its implemented SET clears that array, costing O(n + n^2/log(n+1)); this
reset cost is not hidden inside the constant fixed-epoch operation bounds.

The inferred implementation uses a parent forest with union by component
size, without path compression. When a root becomes a child, its component
at least doubles, so parent depth is at most floor(log2 n). Two root walks
and a constant number of writes suffice per pair operation. It uses O(n)
words, with O(n) SET and O(1) CERTIFY. Its snapshots use owned immutable keys
and constant-size pair claims. Historical structural observations expand a
pair into a grouping only when requested; that output cost is not attributed
to the pair instruction itself.

Changing from exact to inferred permission is what makes this distinction:
remembering which equality was checked is different from deciding whether
an equality follows through a chain of checks. The difference is in the
same pair language, not in switching to a Minsky or RAM base machine.

5. FATAL TRAPS AND A VALID WORST-CASE REDUCTION

Classical incremental connectivity asks whether two vertices are connected
after edge insertions. A pair CHECK at K=1 inserts an edge into the established
equality graph. An inferred COMMIT asks connectivity. But a failed COMMIT
traps, whereas a graph data structure must survive negative answers.
Ignoring that difference would leave a gap in a lower-bound transfer.

Theorem C4. Suppose a deterministic inferred-pair monitor implemented on
the word RAM has worst-case times t_C for CHECK and t_M for COMMIT, with
all persistent state accounted for in its memory. It yields an incremental
connectivity data structure with O(t_C+1) insertion time and O(t_M+1) query
time. Temporary query storage increases by O(t_M+1) words. The construction
is valid in the polynomial-space, logarithmic-word regime relevant below.

Proof. Fix the key to 1 and begin with CHECK(0,0). This establishes an active
discrete partition without connecting distinct vertices. Translate an edge
insertion (i,j) to CHECK(i,j); it is always sound.

For a connectivity query, execute COMMIT(i,j) in a simulated transaction.
Before each simulated memory write, record its address and previous word
value. Preserve the constant-size control state as well; any larger stored
workspace is part of the simulated memory. Record whether the instruction
trapped, then undo every write in reverse order and restore the control
state. Return true precisely when it did not trap.

Each write incurs constant extra word-RAM work and one constant-size log
entry; reverse replay has the same order of cost. Multiple writes to one
address are logged separately and reversed in order. Allocator and ledger
state are included, not treated as free external effects. With O(t_M)
instruction steps, the extra storage and work are O(t_M).

The restored state is exactly the legal monitor state after the preceding
CHECK sequence. The next graph operation therefore starts untrapped with
the correct established partition. The monitor's worst-case bound applies
to every such query, so repeated restoration cannot invalidate the time
guarantee. This proves the reduction. End proof.

This is an algorithmic construction in a lower-bound argument. It gives no
untrusted caller permission to roll back a real monitor's protected state.
Unlogged hardware effects, physical irreversible operations and hidden
oracles are outside this RAM contract. All simulation memory is counted.

Why worst case matters: an amortized monitor may spend credit or improve its
representation during a query. Rollback can erase that improvement and let
the same expensive work recur. C4 therefore does NOT transfer an amortized
time guarantee. This note makes no inverse-Ackermann amortized lower-bound
claim for the fatal-query interface on the basis of this reduction.

6. THE RESULTING UNCONDITIONAL TIME BARRIER

The lower bounds in this section are established results of other authors,
applied through C4. They concern pairwise INFERRED permission in a fixed-key
epoch, deterministic exact decisions, polynomially addressable memory, and
words of Theta(log n) bits. They do not apply to exact pair membership.

Pătraşcu and Thorup, "Don't Rush into a Union: Take Time to Find Your Roots,"
Theorem 1, prove that incremental connectivity with worst-case insertion
time o(log n / log log n) needs worst-case query time n^(1-o(1)) in the
logarithmic-cell model. Their paper also records the balanced
O(log n / log log n) worst-case upper bound. [S1]

Applying C4 gives the following corollary for the monitor:

  t_CHECK = o(log n / log log n)
        implies t_COMMIT >= n^(1-o(1)).

In particular, constant-time pair CHECK cannot coexist with polylogarithmic
worst-case pair COMMIT under this inferred policy and computational model.
This is an unconditional computational restriction, not an OMv conjecture
and not a heat bound. The query-log overhead does not remove it: when query
time is already at least linear the conclusion is satisfied; otherwise the
additional storage remains polynomial and the simulation stays in the same
logarithmic-word model.

The classical Boolean union-find lower bound gives

  t_query = Omega(log n / log((t_update+2) log n)),

which in particular forces max(t_update,t_query)=Omega(log n/log log n).
Its applicability to Boolean answers is established by Kaplan, Shafrir and
Tarjan [S2]; the relationship to connectivity is explained in [S1, Section
1.4]. C4 transfers this worst-case restriction despite the monitor's traps.
The standard balanced connectivity construction supplies the matching
O(log n/log log n) upper bound by implementing CHECK as edge insertion
and COMMIT as connectivity testing, with constant additional monitor work.
It uses O(n) words [S5], so the balanced time optimum is achievable together
with asymptotically optimal O(n log n) structural bits.

Thus the optimal order of the balanced worst-case operation bound is
Theta(log n/log log n), with the usual asymptotic convention for large n.
The supplied implementation deliberately proves and attains the simpler
O(log n) bound; it does not implement the more elaborate literature-optimal
structure. No benchmark of the supplied code demonstrates the sharper bound.

These results do not identify the entire optimal memory/update/query frontier
for every intermediate budget, nor do they establish a new theorem for all
claim languages. C1 handles exact state counts for sound closure policies;
the time barrier here is specific to equality inference containing arbitrary
pair connectivity. A different language or permission rule needs its own
analysis. Additional memory alone cannot evade a bound that already permits
polynomially addressable memory in this model.

7. THE LEDGER AND THE LIMITS OF THE ACCOUNTING

The structural space bounds exclude an unbounded accumulated integer, but
the implementations retain and verify the actual ledger. After at most H
paid instructions, it requires O(log(H+1)) further bits. Distinct observable
bills require that many bits even when the structural state is unchanged.
For polynomial H it occupies O(1) logarithmic-size RAM words, as assumed
in the principal per-operation bounds.

For unrestricted H, exact increment with ordinary multiword binary storage
takes O(1+log(H+1)/log(n+1)) worst-case word operations and O(1) amortized
word operations over the increments. That cost must be added to a paid
instruction. Python's arbitrary-precision integer is not a constant-time
implementation of an unbounded ledger. The space is correspondingly
O(n log(n+1)+log(H+1)) for the inferred structural implementation.

No step in this analysis identifies ledger marks with comparisons, elapsed
time, energy, or entropy loss. The same charge can accompany different
runtime costs. Retaining a permanent historical record does not assert that
its claim remains true of a subsequently replaced key.

8. WHAT IS COMPLETED AND WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED

Completed here:

  - An exact permission-state formula for a finite sound closure policy.
  - Tight asymptotic space and complete-array operation times for partition
    inference, with a working implementation and independent checks.
  - Exact versus inferred state counts in the compact pair language.
  - Working exact-pair and inferred-pair monitors, including historical
    records, ledger, traps, self-pair evidence and resets.
  - A worst-case reduction that explicitly handles fatal COMMIT failures.
  - An unconditional inherited update/query barrier and the known balanced
    optimal order, with attribution and computational assumptions.

Not established: a new physical principle; novelty of the elementary
partition results; a new dynamic-data-structure lower bound; a complete
three-way frontier for every space budget and claim language; an amortized
lower bound for fatal queries via rollback; a whole-book audit; or an
implementation of the literature-optimal balanced pair structure.

The connection to the Thiele model is precise. These results preserve or
explicitly vary the rule connecting checked structure to permitted action,
while retaining the channel, historical record, trap and trace prices.
They do not elevate certification into the purpose of the entire model.

STRUCTURAL PRESERVATION: COMPANION PROOFS
=========================================

STRUCTURE, ESTABLISHMENT, AND EXECUTION COST
Research note for Devon Thiele — 12 October 2026

STATUS AND SCOPE

This note gives mathematical proofs about the partition example already in
The Thiele Machine. It does not propose another machine architecture. It asks
which information can be identified without changing the example's behavior.
The proofs stand independently of their finite executable checks.

The source correspondence is minimal/PartitionReading.v at repository commit
e4c234627651f1b12eb649148a051d5a252ed6b4. No manuscript or repository source
is changed. This is a focused result, not a completed audit of the book.

The result has three parts:

  1. The mathematical consequences of a collection of checked groupings have
     an exact summary: one partition, their join.
  2. That summary cannot in general preserve the existing rule about which
     groupings may be committed. A sharp family of examples needs t-1 bits
     to distinguish histories with identical mathematical consequences,
     where t is the number of available partition claims.
  3. Structural effects alone cannot determine the existing execution bill.
     Repeated successful checks can leave the structure unchanged while
     adding to the ledger. Paths or an accumulated ledger must retain this
     distinction.

These are consequences of explicit observation and action contracts. The
partition-lattice and distinguishable-history arguments are classical tools;
no claim of a new general lower-bound technique or open-problem solution is
made. The substantive application is to this existing structural example.

1. THE EXPERIMENT

Put three objects a, b, c on a table. Each carries a key, such as a color.
A grouping claims that objects placed in the same bucket have the same key.
The claim does not say that different buckets must have different keys.

Compare two runs when all three objects have the same key:

  Run A: check {a,b,c}; then check {a,b,c} again.
  Run B: check {a,b,c}; then check {a,b}|{c}.

Both runs have established the same mathematical consequences: all three
keys agree, which already implies agreement within any smaller bucket.
Both have performed two successful checks and paid two marks. Neither has
committed or raised a record.

Now request COMMIT {a,b}|{c}.

  Run A traps. Run B succeeds.

This is exactly the existing rule: COMMIT requires a matching partition in
the checked table. It does not merely ask whether that partition follows
logically from earlier checks. Renaming buckets does not matter; checking a
different partition does.

The example therefore distinguishes two questions:

  What follows from the established constraints?
  Which action has been earned under the specified history rule?

Their answers can differ without either being inconsistent. Treating them
as interchangeable changes the machine. This distinction is a policy in the
existing model, not a universal requirement that every computation must use.

2. THE MATHEMATICAL SETUP

Let X be a finite nonempty set of n items. A partition P is represented by its
equivalence relation E_P: x E_P y means x and y occupy the same bucket.
Write P <= Q when E_P is contained in E_Q. Thus P is finer than Q.

Bottom, denoted 0, separates every item. Top, denoted 1, puts all items into
one bucket. These symbols denote partitions, not instruction prices.

A key assignment k induces the partition K defined by equality of keys.
The grouping P is sound for k exactly when P <= K. Every partition K can
occur as a key partition: give distinct keys to its distinct blocks.

For a finite set T of checked partitions, define J(T) to be their join:
the equivalence closure of the union of their equivalence relations.
For T empty, J(T)=0. It is the least partition coarser than every member
of T. This is a mathematical operation, not a claim about its runtime.

The information expressed by T means only its soundness assertions. It does
not include the actual key assignment, the table's membership as a historical
fact, an external transcript, or the timing of the checks.

3. EXACT SUMMARY OF THE MATHEMATICAL CONSEQUENCES

Theorem 1. For any checked family T and proposed grouping H,

  for every key partition K,
    [P <= K for every P in T] implies H <= K

  if and only if H <= J(T).

Proof. The conjunction P <= K for all P in T is equivalent to J(T) <= K
by the defining property of the join. If H <= J(T), transitivity proves
the implication. Conversely choose K=J(T). It satisfies every assertion
in T, so the implication forces H <= J(T). Such a K is realizable by an
actual assignment of keys. This proves both directions. End proof.

Consequently the entire set of mathematical consequences is the set of
refinements of J(T). Two checked families have the same consequences if
and only if their joins agree. For the reverse direction, each join belongs
to its own refinement set, so equality of those sets gives both refinement
directions and hence equality of the partitions.

This generalizes the existing single-grouping entitlement result to any
finite collection of checked groupings. A chain example makes the join
visible: checking {a,b}|{c} and {a}|{b,c} establishes a=b and b=c, hence all
three keys agree. Neither individual grouping asserted the full result.

This is implication among the specified equality claims. It does not assert
that computation always contracts possibilities, or that a unique answer or
termination follows. SET can discard the checks against the previous key.

4. AN EXACT STRUCTURAL REPRESENTATION OF THE EXISTING RULES

Replace each grouping and key by its partition on X. Replace the checked
list by the SET of its distinct partitions. Retain:

  K: the current key partition;
  T: the set of partitions checked against the current key;
  C: an optional committed partition;
  R: an optional pair (partition, historical key partition);
  e: the trap flag;
  m: the accumulated ledger.

The permitted observations are structural: these canonical partitions and
checked membership, the channel, historical record, trap, and ledger.
Numerical bucket/key labels, checked-list order and duplicate multiplicity
are not separately observable. Action parameters are canonicalized in the
same way. This is a declared interface, not equality of raw source records.

For an untrapped state, the rules become:

  SET K':     K := K'; T := empty; C := none. Retain R. Cost 0.
  CHECK P:    if P <= K, add P to T; otherwise trap. Cost 1 either way.
  COMMIT P:   if P belongs to T, set C := P; otherwise trap. Cost 1.
  CERTIFY:    if C is none, trap; otherwise, if R is none, set R := (C,K).
              If R already exists, retain that first record. Cost 1.

On an already trapped state, only the ledger changes, by the action's cost.
In particular SET cannot recover a trapped state. Retaining the historical
record across SET does not make it a claim about the new current key.

Theorem 2. Canonicalization commutes with every step, preserves its price,
and therefore preserves every finite trace of the stated observations.

Proof. Equality of numerical keys becomes equality in K; the CHECK test is
exactly P <= K. The source COMMIT test searches for a grouping inducing the
same partition, which is exactly membership in T. Prepending a successful
check becomes set insertion. SET clears the same fields; the channel and
first historical record are transformed componentwise. Trap and ledger
updates agree in each branch, including already trapped states. This proves
the one-step identity. Induction on the action sequence proves the trace
statement. End proof.

This representation removes bucket names, list order and duplicates from
stored structural information. It retains the distinction between separately
checked partitions. Replacing T by J(T) alone is not an exact implementation
of these rules: the experiment in Section 1 disproves that possibility,
even when the other fields, including the ledger, are supplied.

If COMMIT were instead defined to accept any H <= J(T), the join WOULD be
sufficient for that guard. That would be an inference-permitting policy,
not a compression preserving the present exact-check policy. Neither
policy is established as universally preferable by this result.

5. A SHARP STATE REQUIREMENT WITH IDENTICAL CONSEQUENCES

Let L be a finite language of t DISTINCT partitions of X, including the
one-bucket partition 1. COMMIT may request any member of L. Consider all
histories starting from the same clean state with K=1 and ledger zero.

Theorem 3. There are 2^(t-1) reachable, pairwise distinguishable states with
the same current key, mathematical consequences, channel, historical record,
trap status, ledger and number of executed steps. An exact deterministic
implementation must have at least that many distinguishable retained states
at this boundary. With no other history carrier, this requires at least
t-1 bits. The bound is attained for this family by its membership vector.

Proof. For every subset U of L excluding 1, perform CHECK 1, then check
each member of U in a fixed order, then repeat CHECK 1 until exactly t
checks have been performed. All checks succeed because every partition
refines K=1. The resulting checked set is {1} union U.

All these states have K=1, C=none, R=none, e=false and m=t. Each has join
1 and therefore exactly the same mathematical consequences. The programs
can have the same length and a common next request; their earlier action
parameters must not be provided as a free external history at the boundary.

For U different from V, choose P in their symmetric difference. The common
next action COMMIT P succeeds for one state and traps for the other. If an
implementation assigned both histories the same complete retained state,
it could not produce different deterministic answers to that same request.
Thus all 2^(t-1) states must remain distinct. A representation with b bits
has at most 2^b states, so b >= t-1.

Conversely, one bit for membership of each partition other than 1 identifies
every state in this family, since membership of 1 is fixed. It answers every
COMMIT membership question on this family. This proves sharpness for the
family, not a total-memory optimum for all machine operations. End proof.

If L contains every partition of n items, t is the Bell number B_n:

  items n                 1     2     3     4     5
  partition claims B_n    1     2     5    15    52
  required bits B_n-1     0     1     4    14    51

For comparison, one semantic summary partition has only B_n possibilities;
its abstract identity can be encoded in ceil(log2 B_n) bits. This statement
is about distinguishable possibilities, not efficient encoding or update
time. On the particular lower-bound family the semantic summary is always
1 and itself requires no varying information at all.

The bound counts all retained information available for exact decisions.
It becomes a protected-memory bound only if no other usable history carrier
is available. An authenticated external table, recoverable action transcript
or trusted caller state must be counted or treated as a different interface.
The theorem supplies no time-space trade-off, randomized error bound, or
unconditional lower bound for arbitrary cryptographic outsourcing schemes.

Most importantly, it is a cost of enforcing THIS establishment rule, not an
inherent Bell-number memory requirement for truth, structure, or computation.
Allowing entailed commitments changes the rule and avoids this particular
distinguishability family. This is precisely why the interface matters.

6. WHY THE BILL CANNOT BE READ FROM STRUCTURAL EFFECTS

Remove m from the representation in Section 4, but keep the other fields.
Let s be a clean state where P is sound. One successful CHECK P and two
successive successful CHECK P instructions produce the same structural state:
same K, checked set {P}, empty channel and record, and no trap.
Their costs are 1 and 2. Thus no function of that final structural state
alone recovers the accumulated bill for these histories.

The same obstruction can be expressed at the level of transformation rules.
On states with fixed K where P is sound, CHECK P inserts P in a set and is
an idempotent structural map F: F composed with F equals F. An additive cost
on extensional maps with finite nonnegative integer or real values would
have to satisfy

  cost(F) = cost(F composed with F) = cost(F) + cost(F),

and hence cost(F)=0. It cannot assign the existing price 1 to that map.

Execution paths resolve this without a contradiction: [CHECK P] and
[CHECK P, CHECK P] are different paths, despite having the same structural
effect. Assign each executed edge its price and add along paths. Equivalently
retain an accumulated ledger as a separate state component. With the ledger
retained, the full CHECK transformation is not idempotent.

For fixed finite X the structural representation without m is finite, even
though arbitrarily many duplicate checks may be executed. A positive-cost
self-loop shows why an unbounded cumulative ledger cannot be reconstructed
as a state function on that finite structural representation.

More generally, an additive path cost descends through an identification of
paths if and only if identified paths have equal cost. Necessity follows
from well-definedness; sufficiency assigns each class its representatives'
common cost. To retain composition, the identification must also respect
composition. Positive reset prices therefore cannot be inferred merely by
calling structural maps morphisms and their costs a functor.

None of this proves a heat floor or a necessary positive price for CHECK.
The price here is the existing operational ledger rule. Physical dissipation
requires additional physical assumptions and uncertainty accounting.

7. INFERENCE POLICIES: A PRECISE ALTERNATIVE AND ITS PRICE

The source's exact-match rule is one possible establishment policy. A useful
comparison permits a commitment inferred from successful current checks.
This section analyzes that alternative; it does not install it in the book
or silently treat it as equivalent to the source.

There is a catch. The discrete partition 0 is sound for every key, without
any checks. Under the unqualified entailment guard H <= J(T), a clean
machine could COMMIT 0 and CERTIFY for two marks. Consequently replacing
the guard alone would invalidate the existing three-mark floor.

If a successful current check is required, retain an evidence-presence flag
A, which says T is nonempty. Permit COMMIT H only when A is true and
H <= J(T). A successful CHECK sets A=true and updates J by joining its
partition. SET resets A=false, J=0 and the channel. The historical record,
trap behavior and prices remain as specified earlier.

The flag cannot always be recovered from J: no checks and a successful
CHECK 0 both have join 0. But they must give different COMMIT 0 decisions
under this anchored policy. No claim of nontriviality follows from the flag;
CHECK 0 is still a successful, tautological check.

This construction extends beyond full entailment. Let L be a finite claim
language and let c map subsets of L to subsets of L. Require c to be:

  extensive:   T is contained in c(T);
  monotone:    T contained in U implies c(T) contained in c(U);
  idempotent:  c(c(T)) = c(T);
  sound:       every member of c(T) follows from the soundness claims in T.

These properties define a sound closure policy. The identity c(T)=T gives
exact matching. For partition claims, c(T)={H in L : H <= J(T)} gives full
semantic entailment. Soundness is relative to the stated interpretation of
the claims, not supplied merely by the other three algebraic properties.

Theorem 4. For the policy permitting COMMIT H exactly when T is nonempty
and H belongs to c(T), the pair (A,c(T)) is a sufficient online replacement
for T, with the other structural fields and priced actions retained.
It preserves the altered policy's behavior and soundness, and its minimum
cost to raise a first record from a clean zero-ledger state is still three,
whenever at least one claim can successfully be checked.

Proof. The closure axioms give

  c(T union {P}) = c(c(T) union {P}).

For one inclusion use T contained in c(T) and monotonicity. For the other,
both c(T) and {P} are contained in c(T union {P}); apply monotonicity and
idempotence. Thus CHECK updates the stored closure using only that closure
and its argument. SET resets it to c(empty) and clears A and the channel.
The COMMIT guard is determined by A and membership in the stored closure.
Other branches are unchanged. This proves step preservation as in Theorem 2.

Every member of T is sound for the current key. Soundness of c makes every
authorized commitment sound for that key too. The first historical record
therefore stores a sound claim and its historical key, even after later SETs.

The first successful CERTIFY needs a channel produced by COMMIT. That COMMIT
needs A=true, which requires an earlier successful CHECK since the latest
SET, if any. These are three distinct paid actions in order. Conversely,
CHECK P; COMMIT P; CERTIFY succeeds for a sound P because c is extensive.
It costs exactly three. End proof.

The provenance statement changes: an inferred committed claim need not have
been checked verbatim. It must follow from the actual collection of current
checks. This is an explicit policy difference, not the original same-claim
theorem under different notation. Theorem 4 supplies no computational time
bound for evaluating c, and not every inference mechanism is a closure of
this kind.

There is a sharp comparison for the language of all B_n partitions, with
the current key fixed to 1. Count only the retained component needed for
these permission decisions, with all other fields supplied and fixed in
the lower-bound experiment.

Theorem 5. The source's exact-match policy requires exactly 2^(B_n) possible
permission states, or B_n bits. The anchored full-entailment policy requires
exactly B_n+1 possible permission states, or ceil(log2(B_n+1)) bits. These
are abstract state counts and fixed-width encoding bounds, not update-time
bounds or total implementation memory bounds.

Proof for exact matching. Every subset T of the B_n claims is possible.
Distinct subsets differ on a COMMIT request. A membership vector attains
2^(B_n) states, establishing necessity and sufficiency.

To make the necessity hold with equal ledgers and equal action counts, put
t=B_n. For any nonempty T, first SET the key to 1, check every member of T,
then repeat one member until t checks have occurred. For T empty, check 1
t times and then SET the key to 1. All histories have t+1 actions, ledger t,
key 1, no channel or record, and no trap. Only the retained checked sets
vary. Thus the supplied common fields cannot encode their differences.

Proof for anchored entailment. If A=false, the only possible summary is
J=0. If A=true, J can be any of the B_n partitions. These B_n+1 states are
sufficient by Theorem 4. Each is reachable with the same common fields,
ledger and action count using the preceding construction with T empty or
T={J}. Two distinct active joins have different refinement sets, so some
COMMIT H distinguishes them. The inactive state rejects COMMIT 0, whereas
every active state accepts it. All states are therefore distinguishable.
End proof.

For three objects this comparison is five permission bits versus three;
for five objects it is fifty-two versus six. The difference is accounted
for by what the rule promises to remember: exact checking history or its
logical consequences plus the presence of a current check. Neither policy
receives a lower runtime or physical-energy bound from these state counts.
As in Theorem 3, external usable history must be counted or excluded before
calling this a protected-memory lower bound.

8. WHAT THIS SETTLES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

The partition model has an exact answer to a representation question:

  Mathematical consequences may be compressed to a join partition.
  Exact earned permissions may still require the distinct checked set.
  Execution costs require priced paths or a ledger when equal effects can
  arise from differently priced executions.

Those are three different equivalence relations on histories. A proposed
abstract representation must state which it preserves. If it identifies
histories separated by an allowed future observation, it changes the model.

This is independent of counter machines and numerical bucket names. It is
not a claim that standard computation cannot represent these distinctions;
the construction explicitly shows how to represent them. Nor does it derive
the choice of establishment policy or the positive ledger prices from the
partition geometry alone.

For sound closure policies, Theorem 4 answers how to preserve the connection
between actual checks and subsequent inferred actions. Theorem 5 gives a
tight state comparison for two such policies on this partition language.
The remaining time-space question requires a computational model for the
closure operation and claim representation; state counting alone does not
answer it. Any broader lower bound must also specify available external
history and the observations it promises to preserve.
