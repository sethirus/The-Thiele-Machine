STRONGER LOWER BOUNDS

An existing unconditional theorem now transfers directly to our monitor:
with n claims, Theta(log^4 n) objects and n polylog(n) words of total storage,
freshness queries taking o(log n/log log n) force worst-case writes taking
n^(1-o(1)). This result needs no OMv conjecture. Its difficult lower-bound
theorem is due to Clifford, Gronlund and Larsen (2015); our work supplies the
freshness reduction and checks its hypotheses. This is not a novelty claim.

Separately, at exactly n retained mutable bits, invalidating any fixed set of
k claim statuses sometimes requires Omega(ceil(k/w)) word writes, regardless
of the encoding. Packed contiguous ranges attain that bound. Extra retained
bits invalidate this particular unique-encoding argument.

CONCRETE MEMORY/TIME TRADE-OFF FOR INTERVAL POLICIES

Assume each write invalidates one contiguous interval in a supplied claim order.
Let w be the machine word width. Two implemented configurations give:

  Persistent bits             Record-check / write        Query
  n+O(w)                      O(1+n/w^2), worst-case        O(1)
  n+O(n/w+w*(1+log_w n))       O(1+log_w n), worst-case      same

For logarithmic words, the second row uses n+o(n) bits, including temporary
workspace. The first row's temporary workspace is O(w). These are upper bounds;
their optimality is not established. The full note gives a continuous choice
of block size and also records the simpler binary-tree construction.

Other useful consequences: laminar invalidation sets can be ordered into
intervals; known order maintenance removes the earlier lazy algorithm's
amortized spikes; disjoint-group choice dictionaries give another n+O(w)-bit
constant-operation family.

LIMIT REACHED IN THIS PASS

We have not proved a new literature-level classification, optimal interval
redundancy, or a full tight trade-off for arbitrary overlap. The recent Ko
preprint is a separate literature checkpoint, not an independently audited
foundation for these established findings. Its claimed balanced-case theorem
and the exact transfer are recorded in section 7 of Further-results.txt.
