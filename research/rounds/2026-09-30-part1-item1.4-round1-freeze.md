## Success and failure

PROVED requires a machine-checked TSV row for every one of the 8,156 physical input lines.
Each row is classified as matched, open-labeled, corrected, or nonclaim.
A substantive claim row records an exact Coq identity, definition,
primary literature source, executable artifact, or operational evidence, plus
a concise scope note. No row may be omitted. Every corrected claim must appear
in a separately checked correction ledger and in the resulting document diff.

The result must also pass the theorem-meaning audit, prose audit, citation
audit, document builds, claim-boundary tests, and an independent adversarial
read. Generated plaintext is rebuilt from source and is not hand edited.

REFUTED applies if a frozen document necessarily makes a false claim that
cannot be corrected while remaining the same document deliverable. PARTIAL
applies if any substantive claim lacks enough evidence for either matching or
an accurate open label. BLOCKED requires the exact obstacle and three genuine
strategies below to have failed. Editing a checker, omitting a difficult line,
or classifying substantive prose as syntax does not close the item.

## Strategies before BLOCKED

1. Direct binding: resolve the claim to the exact Coq declaration, executable
   artifact, primary source section, or recorded gate result and compare scope.
2. Constructive narrowing: retain the subject but rewrite the sentence to the
   strongest claim entailed by the available evidence, recording the original
   and replacement in the correction ledger.
3. Explicit openness: preserve the question or intended direction while
   labeling it conjectural, conditional, partial, synthetic, or interpretive.

These are three genuinely different strategies. Failure records must state why
each strategy could not make the line truthful.
