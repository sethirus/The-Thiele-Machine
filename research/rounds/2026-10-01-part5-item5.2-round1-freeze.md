# Part 5, Item 5.2, round 1 freeze: Necula PCC consumer core

Date: 2026-10-01

Authoritative sources: George C. Necula, “Proof-Carrying Code,” POPL 1997,
Sections 2--4, <https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/teaching/6.893/readings/necula-popl97.pdf>,
and Necula's description of the consumer pipeline at
<https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~necula/pcc.html>. The consumer publishes a
safety policy, derives a verification condition from machine code, and checks a
producer-supplied proof using a small trusted checker.

Frozen target SHA-256:
`ce53e86b4b8bd9a30c4341038986b20bad634b0e4b24024a1bf127190073599c`.
The exact propositions are `checker_accepts_iff_vc`,
`certificate_implies_vc`, and `unsafe_program_rejected` in
`NeculaPCCTarget.v`.

Interpretation calls: this is a finite memory-access instruction fragment. Its
policy is address-boundedness and its proof object mirrors the verification
condition. It does not model DEC Alpha, LF proof terms, the paper's VC
generator, control flow, or semantic execution safety. Those omissions must be
visible in the outcome.

Predicted outcome: the three exact propositions are PROVED, while Item 5.2 as a
model of Necula's complete system is PARTIAL. Success is assumption-free Coq
proofs plus that classification. Failure is calling the fragment the full PCC
system or claiming that the checker establishes a policy not encoded in its VC.
