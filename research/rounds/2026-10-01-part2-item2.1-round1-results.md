## Ground-truth result

The literal Round 4 form does not extend from a Boolean record to every
monotone multi-valued record. The frozen universal claim that one Boolean
latch carries every such record is false. A fully priced three-value chain
over a one-state base satisfies every `HonestGrowingExtension` premise, but
three record values cannot be decoded from the same base state and one bit.

What does extend is narrower. Every frozen honest growing record admits a
complete family of permanent threshold-update equations whose event functions
may read the threshold, base state, and current record value. The already
assumed strict-write pricing predicate is retained. Antisymmetry proves that
the complete family of lower thresholds determines the record value. Under
growth, pricing strict value changes is equivalent to pricing false-to-true
threshold changes.

For a fixed-length Boolean-vector representation, a pairwise-distinct
pointwise-monotone chain of `k`-bit vectors has at most `k + 1` members. Thus a
chain visiting `n` distinct values in this frozen representation requires at
least `n - 1` coordinates. This is a statement about monotone Boolean-vector
representations, not a lower bound for arbitrary encodings with a changing
base state.

## Hollowness checks

### `growing_record_decomposes`

- Definitional: FAILS substantive-strength check. The threshold event is
  constructed directly from the driving function, and the conclusion repeats
  the schedule premise. This is an exact representation lemma, not a new
  physical or computational theorem.
- Built in: the price schedule is built into `HonestGrowingExtension` and is
  only retained. Threshold permanence still uses the independent growth
  premise.
- Vacuity: `ThreeMachine`, `OneBase`, `ThreeCover`, and `three_honest` form a
  closed instance satisfying every premise.
- Swap test: the proof is polymorphic in the value type, partial order, base,
  cover, and record function. It uses their structure rather than a named
  event or machine.
- Adversarial read: PASS after correction. The first sentence incorrectly said
  the machine “factors into” base, thresholds, and schedule. The independent
  reader noted that no decoder or state isomorphism is proved and that the
  schedule is assumed. The corrected sentence above states only threshold
  update equations and retained pricing.

### `thresholds_determine_record`

- Definitional: not a restatement; the conclusion follows from reflexivity and
  antisymmetry of the frozen partial order.
- Built in: antisymmetry is an explicit partial-order law, but equality of all
  thresholds is not the conclusion supplied at the door.
- Vacuity: `ThreeOrder` is a closed three-element instance, and its lower
  threshold observations are concrete Booleans.
- Swap test: the proof quantifies over every frozen `BoolPartialOrder`, so it is
  independent of `Three` and of any named machine.
- Adversarial read: PASS. The sentence says exactly that equality of every
  lower-threshold observation determines equality of values.

### `record_price_iff_threshold_price`

- Definitional: not a restatement. One direction turns a threshold flip into
  unequal values; the other uses the new value as a threshold and invokes
  growth plus antisymmetry.
- Built in: growth is required, but neither pricing predicate is assumed by
  the theorem. Both sides explicitly carry the same ledger premise.
- Vacuity: the closed three-value instance grows, carries a monotone ledger,
  and charges both strict changes.
- Swap test: the proof is uniform in the machine, ordered value type, and
  record function.
- Adversarial read: PASS. The sentence matches the biconditional under exactly
  the frozen growth premise.

### `one_latch_suffices`

- Definitional: the refutation is not an unfolding. It uses the impossibility
  of decoding three distinct values from one fixed base state and one Boolean.
- Built in: the answer is not required by an inconsistent premise;
  `three_honest` closes every honesty obligation.
- Vacuity: the counterexample is the reachable run
  `three0 -> three1 -> three2 -> three2`, with ledger values `0, 1, 2, 2`.
- Swap test: the obstruction is cardinality, not the names of the constructors
  or machine. Any fixed base state with three distinguishable record values
  gives the same contradiction.
- Adversarial read: PASS. The sentence identifies exactly the frozen universal
  claim and the closed three-value, one-state counterexample.

### `chain_needs_bits`

- Definitional: not a restatement. The proof maps vectors to their number of
  true coordinates, proves those counts are distinct along the chain, and
  injects them into `0 .. k`.
- Built in: `NoDup` requires distinct vectors, but the `k + 1` bound is not a
  premise. Without distinctness, repeated vectors would refute the bound.
- Vacuity: for `k = 2`, `[false; false]`, `[true; false]`, and `[true; true]`
  are a distinct monotone chain attaining the bound.
- Swap test: the proof uses only Boolean-vector length, pointwise monotonicity,
  and distinctness; it is unrelated to a named machine or event.
- Adversarial read: PASS. The sentence is the exact `length vs <= S k`
  conclusion under the three frozen premises.
