## Success and failure

Item 1.1 succeeds only if all of the following hold:

1. The extractor reproduces the frozen source manifest and occurrence inventory byte for byte.
2. Every one of the 472 identities has exactly one class and a statement-based reason.
3. An acceptance test independently checks the frozen classification against the exact Coq declarations rather than trusting filename or theorem-name keywords.
4. For every predicted G identity, a Coq file specializes the exact theorem to a concrete non-certification reading or record system and compiles. Each premise is either discharged in Coq or remains visibly quantified in an evidence theorem whose outcome is labeled PARTIAL.
5. The four functor-body identities are checked through concrete functor instances, not silently exempted.
6. No cited proof occurrence, ambiguity, nonproof token, or unaddressable proof identity is silently skipped.

Item 1.1 fails if any frozen source or occurrence drifts, any cited proof identity is omitted or multiply classified, any prediction is contradicted by the exact declaration, any predicted G identity cannot be specialized to a non-certification reading without changing its exact type, or any row is accepted through a skip or exemption.

If a theorem remains conditional on an undischarged assumption, its evidence is PARTIAL under the plan's outcome rule. A conditional theorem is not promoted to an unconditional result by this classification. Classification as G says what the exact statement quantifies; it does not erase its premises.

## Strategies before BLOCKED

For a G specialization that does not close immediately, the required strategies are:

1. Directly specialize the declared theorem to a finite non-certification latch such as a word-validity, graph-allocation, meter, stamp, or history event, then discharge its premises by reduction and existing kernel lemmas.
2. Build a concrete abstract record or certification-system instance whose reading is that non-certification event, and prove the record laws separately before applying the theorem.
3. Instantiate the theorem through its enclosing module or functor with a minimal concrete model, unfold only the public interface predicates, and prove the resulting finite obligations by computation or explicit case analysis.

An identity is reported BLOCKED only after all three strategies have genuine recorded attempts and none yields closed evidence.
