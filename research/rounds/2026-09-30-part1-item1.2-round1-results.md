## Ground-truth result

Arbitrary latchability is not enough to preserve all 55 frozen translations.
A zero-cost graph-allocation event refutes the
generalized price and mu floors. A state-size event refutes the generalized
projection-hiding claims because the tested projections retain the registers
and memory from which that event is computed. A positive-cost checkpoint is an
injective writer of a latchable mu-threshold event, refuting the proposed
writer-merge generalization.

The state-size event is latchable on the raw `VMState` type using a 15-register
state. That witness is outside the intended fixed-size register invariant, so
those refutations do not establish an invariant-respecting counterexample.

The event-parametric results that do survive are the structural trace-writer
lemma, the finite-state permanence and merge consequences under their explicit
premises, exact pricing by a schedule defined from the event itself, projection
irredundancy, revocation exclusion, billed-core adequacy and honest extension,
conditional schedule uniqueness, reachable-simulation descent, representation
through an event-reflecting embedding, and the direct event certification
specification.

These proved rows are not equally substantive. Some are definitional, some
repeat a premise, and some assume the essential pricing, selector, cover, or
embedding structure. In particular, the reachable-simulation existence result
is conditional on a global representative satisfying a retraction law; this
round does not construct such a representative. The exact qualifications are
recorded per identity in the hollowness ledger.

## Wrong predictions

Two frozen predictions were wrong:

- `generalized_billed_schedule_equivalence` was predicted PROVED and is
  REFUTED.
- `generalized_surcharged_schedule_equivalence` was predicted PROVED and is
  REFUTED.

Both fail because their unchanged Thiele base must satisfy `schedule_priced`
for every substituted event. The graph-allocation event is latchable but can be
written by `PNEW` at zero cost, so the base machine fails the event-specific A2
condition. The conditional theorem `generalized_schedule_uniqueness` remains
PROVED because it explicitly assumes that condition.

## Method and scope

The acceptance test was written and observed failing before the proof source
and outcome ledger existed. Targeted guarded compilation then closed every
frozen proposition. The per-identity hollowness checks, prediction comparison,
and exact theorem names are recorded in
`2026-09-30-part1-item1.2-round1-evidence.tsv`.

This round makes no novelty claim, so it does not classify any result as
PROVED BUT KNOWN. Literature classification remains necessary if a later paper
revision claims novelty for one of these statements.
