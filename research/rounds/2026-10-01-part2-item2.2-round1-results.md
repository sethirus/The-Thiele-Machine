The first, second, and fourth results are closed under the global context.
The Casper accountable-conflict wrapper depends on
`Classical_Prop.classic`, inherited from the frozen Casper FFG proof, so R4
forbids calling that result PROVED. The prediction for that target was wrong.

Classification: literal permanent-record axis: OUTSIDE for any record with an
actual true-to-false transition; accountable-conflict generalization: INSIDE
when contradiction entails a stated penalty. Pricing revocation does not imply
pricing writes: the closed Boolean toggle countermodel charges true-to-false
and leaves false-to-true free.

The Casper theorem is narrower than the usual shorthand “revocable
finality.” Under the frozen FFG model, conflicting finalized blocks satisfying
the fork conditions imply a quorum whose votes satisfy a slashing condition.
This is not a transition-level deletion, a numeric execution charge, or proof
that a slash was executed. The concrete one-validator instance also shows
that writing a finalization record need not itself slash anyone.

## Hollowness checks

### `actual_revocation_excludes_permanence`

- Definitional: the contradiction is immediate from the two definitions, so
  this is a boundary lemma rather than a substantive discovery.
- Built in: actual revocation supplies the countertransition but does not
  assume non-permanence as a separate premise.
- Vacuity: Boolean negation has the concrete transition `true -> false`.
- Swap test: the proof is uniform in the state type, transition, and reading.
- Adversarial read: PASS.

### `revocation_price_does_not_price_writes`

- Definitional: the refutation uses a concrete asymmetric cost schedule.
- Built in: neither direction of pricing is assumed to imply the other.
- Vacuity: Boolean negation with cost one from `true` and zero from `false`
  both revokes and writes, satisfying exactly the claimed asymmetry.
- Swap test: renaming the Boolean states preserves the asymmetric-cost
  counterexample.
- Adversarial read: PASS.
 
### `casper_conflict_is_accountable`

- Definitional: this is a faithful wrapper around accountable safety, not a
  new Casper theorem.
- Built in: the fork conditions are premises; the slashable quorum is not.
- Vacuity: the underlying Casper development includes concrete settings, but
  this item does not construct a finalization fork.
- Swap test: the theorem is generic in `CasperSetting`, not tied to a named
  validator or hash.
- Adversarial read: PASS, with PARTIAL status because excluded middle remains
  an assumption.

### `casper_write_without_slashing`

- Definitional: the concrete vote, ancestry, finalization, and no-slashing
  obligations are proved separately.
- Built in: the one-validator setting does not assume the conclusion.
- Vacuity: `one_vote` is the closed witness.
- Swap test: this is intentionally a named real-model sanity instance; it is
  evidence about write pricing, not a generic theorem about all settings.
- Adversarial read: PASS.

The semantic test was observed red before the files existed and green after
the guarded Coq build. No novelty claim is made. The modeling calls were to
separate literal transition revocation from accountable conflict, refuse to
equate slashability with an executed slash, and mark the classical theorem
PARTIAL rather than hide its assumption.
