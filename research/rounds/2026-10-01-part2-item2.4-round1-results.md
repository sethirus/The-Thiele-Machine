`weak_base_equiv` is an equivalence relation on observed bases for a fixed
observation type after the Round 2 transitivity repair. It preserves the
caller-selected observations and halting while allowing
either one step to match zero or more steps on the other base. This explicitly
ignores instruction granularity through stuttering, but it does not choose a
canonical observation or prove any particular pair of machines equivalent.

The generic Round 4 transport theorem is PROVED but structurally hollow.
Round 4 already holds for every deterministic `BaseMachine`, so both sides of
the biconditional are proved independently and the cross-base premise is not
used. The repository's executable toy TM and unbounded VM semantics have
closed adapters and satisfy Round 4 separately.

The requested named four-way test is not complete. For RAM, the repository
contains prose and abstract RAM shadows but no executable transition
specification to adapt. For L, the adapter is closed in `CrossBaseGranularityL.v`: weak
call-by-value reduction is decidable, so a structural step function equals
`LRecursion.step` and the base stutters only on irreducible terms. Under R3
and R7, no prose adapter was substituted and the VM was not renamed as a RAM.

## BLOCKED records

### RAM adapter

Exact obstacle: there is no selected, cited, executable RAM state and step
specification in the repository. What is needed is the real RAM model required
by Part 5.3, with its source and interpretation ledger, followed by a
`BaseMachine` adapter.

Three distinct attempts:

1. Repository search found only unit-cost RAM prose and observer abstractions,
   not a state transition to prove commutation against.
2. The classical subset of the VM ISA was inspected as a possible adapter,
   but it is still the VM semantics and would make “RAM” a renaming shortcut.
3. A fresh ad hoc register machine was rejected because no actual
   specification and section-level interpretation had yet been frozen.

### L adapter

PROVED in `coq/kernel/foundation/CrossBaseGranularityL.v`, closed under the
global context. The earlier obstacle assumed a total function needs choice.
It does not: weak call-by-value reduction on L terms is decidable by
structure.

- `l_step_fun_correct`: `step s t <-> l_step_fun s = Some t`.
- `l_base_halted_iff_irreducible`: the base halts exactly on terms with no
  L step.
- `l_base_run_is_star` and `star_is_l_base_run`: base runs are exactly L
  reduction sequences.
- `round4_l_holds`: Round 4 on the L base.

Definitional: `l_step_fun` is defined by recursion on terms and proved equal
to the frozen inductive relation, not defined from it. Built in: no
hypothesis or field carries the conclusion. Vacuity: every term is a state
and every L reduction is a run. Swap: `round4_l_holds` is base-parametric,
the same structural hollowness disclosed above for TM and VM; the adapter
theorems are specific to L.

## Hollowness checks

### Equivalence laws

- Definitional: reflexivity and symmetry are direct; transitivity requires
  lifting weak one-step matches to finite runs and composing relations.
- Built in: the relation is defined symmetrically, so symmetry is close to
  built in; transitivity is not a field or premise.
- Vacuity: equality relates every base to itself, including the concrete TM
  and VM adapters.
- Swap test: all three laws are polymorphic in both bases and observations.
- Adversarial read: PASS, including the Round 2 transitivity sentence.

### `weak_equiv_preserves_round4`

- Definitional: not literally a definition, but hollow because the universal
  Round 4 theorem proves each side without the equivalence premise.
- Built in: the result comes from `uniqueness_round4_holds`, not from the new
  relation.
- Vacuity: reflexivity supplies related bases; TM and VM supply concrete base
  inhabitants.
- Swap test: the theorem is fully polymorphic and ignores base names.
- Adversarial read: PASS; the report explicitly discloses the unused premise.

### TM and VM adapters

- Definitional: the adapters use `step_tm` and `run_vm_u 1` respectively; the
  Round 4 conclusions reuse the universal theorem.
- Built in: satisfaction of Round 4 is inherited, not newly derived from their
  instruction semantics.
- Vacuity: every fixed program creates a concrete base; the initial predicates
  are inhabited.
- Swap test: the same theorem applies to either adapter, but no TM-to-VM weak
  equivalence is claimed.
- Adversarial read: PASS.

The tests were observed red before the definitions and results existed. All
six proved theorems are closed under the global context. The missing
transitivity obligation was caught before closeout, frozen in Round 2, and
proved without changing Round 1. No novelty claim is made.
