## Exact definitions and statements

The inherited universe is exactly the 55 identities classified C in `2026-09-30-part1-item1.1-round2-evidence.tsv`. For each identity, `2026-09-30-part1-item1.2-round1-targets.tsv` pins the source statement hash, one exact target proposition, the predicted outcome, and the translation applied.

The complete definitions and propositions are in `coq/kernel/foundation/EventGeneralizationTargets.v`. That file contains definitions only. It contains no theorem, lemma, proof, counterexample, axiom, admitted obligation, or result declaration.

The translation rules are:

1. A fixed Boolean certification flag or certificate-address predicate becomes an arbitrary `Reading` named `E`.
2. A named setter becomes the semantic predicate `event_writes E s i`, meaning exactly that `E` is false before the VM step and true afterward.
3. A trace event means exactly that `E` is false at the initial state and true after the entire trace.
4. A fixed record-carrying machine becomes the same machine with only its record reading replaced by `with_record`; transition, initial state, mu, and halt observation are unchanged.
5. A certification projection coordinate becomes the value of `E`; all other coordinates and all recoverability requirements remain unchanged.
6. The canonical unit price is rebuilt from `E` by charging one exactly on a false-to-true step. This tests the construction, not the existing certification schedule.
7. Conjuncts that do not mention certification remain in the target. In particular, the partition and certify-versus-PNEW bundles retain their original non-event clauses.
8. Every target quantifies `latchable E`, or its finite/record-machine analogue. Latchability includes permanence and an exhibited false-to-true write. It does not include pricing, mu growth, merging, or projection blindness.

No target is accepted as a generalization merely because a related generic theorem exists. Each of the 55 frozen source identities receives its own final outcome and evidence row.

## Success and failure

An identity is PROVED only if a closed Coq theorem has exactly its frozen target proposition and `Print Assumptions` reports no assumptions. An identity is REFUTED only if a closed Coq theorem proves the negation of its frozen target proposition, with an explicit event and witness where the negation requires one, and `Print Assumptions` reports no assumptions.

A predicted PROVED identity fails its prediction if its exact target is refuted or remains blocked. A predicted REFUTED identity fails its prediction if its exact target is proved or remains blocked. A prediction mismatch does not permit changing the target: the outcome and wrong prediction are recorded.

The item succeeds only when all 55 targets have exactly one permitted outcome, all PROVED and REFUTED results are assumption-free, the five hollowness checks are recorded per identity, the document audit uses the observed outcomes, and every gate passes. A source hash mismatch, omitted identity, renamed substitute, weakened proposition, hidden premise, or unrecorded target change is failure.

## Strategies before BLOCKED

Every target receives these genuinely different strategies before BLOCKED is permitted:

1. Direct parametric proof: unfold only the frozen event vocabulary, apply the latchability law or an existing generic theorem, and close the unchanged obligations.
2. Concrete finite countermodel: instantiate a non-certification reading on the actual VM or finite machine and prove the required write, permanence, price, mu, merge, or projection facts by computation and kernel lemmas.
3. Representation proof: rebuild the source argument over `with_record`, the event projection, or the event-preserving simulation record, and prove the result from transition-level equations rather than source theorem naming.

If all three fail, the BLOCKED record must state each attempted proof term or countermodel, its exact remaining goal, why it fails, and what additional lemma or premise would be required.

## Hollowness protocol

Before an outcome counts, its evidence row records all five checks required by R6:

1. Definitional: whether the conclusion merely unfolds a premise or definition.
2. Built in: whether the result is required by `latchable`, an event-bearing record, a projection definition, or another input interface.
3. Vacuity: a concrete satisfying instance for every premise-bearing result, including results proved by contradiction against a universal claim.
4. Swap: the concrete non-certification event used, or a second event instance demonstrating that the proof follows structure rather than the certification name.
5. Adversarial read: a separate statement-versus-sentence audit that rejects prose claiming more than the exact target.

Definitional or built-in results may be PROVED, but the final report and paper must call them hollow and may not count them as substantive evidence for the record-axis thesis.
