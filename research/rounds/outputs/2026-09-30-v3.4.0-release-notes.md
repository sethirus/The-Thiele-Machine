# The Thiele Machine v3.4.0

This draft records the completed theorem audit, structural and narrowing results, scoped readings of five specifications, partial cost-framework comparisons, and the physics and prior-work checks. The expanded ground-truth plan remains in progress.

## What carries the weight

- **Every machine can carry the record axis.** Take any deterministic machine and any event it reaches. The machine plus a latch on that event, charging one unit when the latch sets, is an honest extension of it (`latch_core_honest`). A reversible machine with unbounded memory can carry one and stay reversible (`history_latch_honest`). With finite memory it can't (`finite_reversible_cannot_write`).
- **The record can't be read back off the shadow.** On the VM, no function of the named windows returns certification or μ (`cert_not_function_of_forget`, `mu_not_function_of_bare_observable`).
- **On finite hardware, writing a permanent record merges states** (`permanent_flip_is_not_injective`), so an account that prices merges already prices the write (`a2_from_merging_price_and_permanence`).

A record that never switches off and whose update is driven by the base computation is a latch on one event (`uniqueness_round4_holds`). Both premises are essential: revocation and a hidden clock are counterexamples.

## The structural question, as it came out

- Uniqueness in the form the words suggest is false. A Thiele core that bills CPU time is adequate and runs the VM underneath, and it is not the same machine (`uniqueness_round1_refuted`, `uniqueness_round2_refuted`).
- Up to the price schedule it holds for machines that run the VM and record certification (`uniqueness_round3b_holds`). A state-dependent bill is just a schedule (`surcharged_core_equiv_mod_schedule`).
- Which permanent reading counts as the record is a separate free choice (`uniqueness_round3a_refuted`).
- Over any base, a record that never switches off and whose update is driven by the base computation is a latch on one event (`uniqueness_round4_holds`), and two such records are one axis (`uniqueness_round4_pair_holds`). A revocable record is not a latch (`toggle_not_latch`). A record run by a hidden clock is not driven by the computation (`clock_record_not_driven`).

## Narrowing

- A machine's own spread is priced along a run (`run_narrowing_priced_log`).
- An observer's knowledge can narrow for free (`observer_narrowing_can_be_free`), even read step by step (`demon_refutes_incremental`).
- Three states are enough for free step-by-step narrowing (`free_incremental_narrowing_with_three`), and two are not (`no_free_incremental_narrowing_below_three`).
- No Free Insight keeps its name. The insight it prices is certified insight.

## Five specifications, read through the record axis

| Specification | Record | Where a price sits | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ethereum, Prague execution spec | nonzero storage slot | on the write, at least 20000 gas; a slot set and cleared in one transaction costs 2300 (`persistent_write_priced`, `revoked_write_nearly_free`) | economic |
| Casper FFG | finalized block | on reversal, by slashing (`accountable_safety`, ported from the Casper team's Coq proofs; `conflicting_records_are_priced`, `finalization_without_slashing`) | economic |
| TPM 2.0, Library Version 185 | extended PCR, until the next reset | none; reversal is infeasible | cryptographic |
| Certificate Transparency, RFC 9162 | logged certificate | none; reversal is infeasible | cryptographic |
| Proof-carrying code (Necula) | none | none | outside the family |

Only Ethereum prices writing the record. Casper prices revoking it. TPM and CT hold permanence by structure. The convergence is on permanence, not on pricing the write.

## Cost frameworks

- A graded run carries its total cost as its grade (`run_graded_is_run`).
- The ledger is a writer (`run_writer_is_run_and_cost`).
- A2 and AARA's upper bound hold together exactly when pricing is exact and certification is permanent (`a2_and_aara_iff_exact`).
- Under A2, certifications never outnumber total cost (`flips_le_cost`).

None of these embeds the full semantics or type systems of those frameworks.

## Recursion

L, the weak call-by-value lambda calculus, has a second recursion theorem proved from its reduction rules (`second_recursion`, `L_recursion_theorem`), with Rice's theorem (`L_rice`) and the halting problem (`L_halting_undecidable`) following. L is not the 51-opcode VM, and the VM's own recursion theorem is still a premise of the substrate theorem.

## Open questions, as of 29 September 2026

- **The pointer question.** Which event a record latches, and why certification is the one to record. Nothing here settles it.
- **Whether permanence is always paid for at some level:** economic, cryptographic, or physical.
- **Which events every account has to price, beyond merges on finite hardware.**
- **A physical calibration of the ledger.** Landauer's principle is a named premise. It is not measured here. No device is known that stores an unrevocable certificate and meters the certifying step alone.
- **The VM's own recursion theorem.** It is a premise of the substrate theorem. The plan's target was a recursion theorem for the 12-instruction self-interpreter fragment. What is proved is the theorem for L.
- **A record with many values that only grows.** It needs its own proof beyond the pair result.
- **A record revocable at a price, the way Casper's is.** It needs its own entry test and sameness.
- **Machines on different bases, compared by cost-respecting simulations.** That is complexity theory's question.
- **Machines that choose at random.** They would need a distribution over latches.
- **Certificate Transparency in Coq.** The RFC 9162 Merkle tree hash and consistency proof are not modeled. Completeness of the RFC's iterative consistency check would take a dedicated formalization. Soundness needs collision resistance, which no result here models.
- **Concrete discrimination machines.** The RAM and reversible cases are covered by theorems over any base and any injective base. No concrete RAM or Janus interpreter is formalized.
