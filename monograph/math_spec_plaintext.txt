The Thiele Machine is an argument about what an account of computation should
preserve.
    Certification is one concrete witness of a broader investigation of structural state, observation,
and cost. The abstract laws, theorems, and executable realizations belong to that investigation.
Selected projections lose distinctions that the richer state can express; cost laws constrain
specified events under explicit hypotheses.
    A conventional encoding can preserve those distinctions. A projection that discards them
cannot recover them in general. The stronger proposal that these distinctions belong in a
necessary foundation of computation remains a research claim. Neither a physical cost metric nor
infinitely many independent geometric dimensions follows from the existence of many observables.
The status of each result is stated below.

                                            Abstract

This is the complete mathematical specification of the Thiele Machine: a formal model of
computation where structural information carries an explicit, monotone cost µ that starts at
zero and never decreases. Everything here is built from the machine-checked Coq corpus. You
can reconstruct the machine from what’s in this document alone.
    Coq establishes that conclusions follow from axioms. It does not establish that axioms model
physical reality. That distinction matters and I keep it explicit throughout. Every result is
tagged as one of three types: (S) structural theorems about the machine as a mathematical
object, (C) conditional derivations where a physics conclusion follows from a stated axiom, and
(R) consistency relations that check internal coherence but are not independent predictions.
    The core claims are structural (S): No Free Insight; µ-conservation; µ-hierarchy; µ-ledger
necessity (five-way independence classification); categorical separation; strict Turing√subsumption;
the classical CHSH bound (|S| ≤ 2); the algebraic Tsirelson bound (|S| ≤ 2 2); and the
biconditional with no project-local axioms column_contractive_iff_quantum_realizable.
The CHSH algebraic result is pure math. The Landauer connection is motivation. Geometry:
the discrete angle-defect identity under its incidence assumptions and the additional restriction
B = 3χ.
    The document also covers, with explicit epistemological tags, conditional derivations under
named physical axioms (Born rule under linearity, no-cloning, unitarity) and reference explo-
rations (4D simplicial-complex differential geometry, self-reference tower). These are marked (C)
or (R) and do not form part of the primary claim ledger. Two named trust boundaries discussed
here are: bsc_kami_compilation_trusted (Layer 2 Verilog semantics gap) and A_QM (physical
quantum axiom).

Chapter 1

Introduction

The Thiele Machine makes the cost of structure explicit. Ordinary step-counting assigns one
time unit per transition; it does not specify this structural-cost schedule. The Thiele Machine
assigns a specific cost µ to every operation that extracts or asserts structural information. That’s
the design. Here is where it goes.
    The central claim, proven formally: specified certification and structural-entitlement events
have the cost floors proved under their stated hypotheses. Combined with explicit physical
axioms (linearity, information conservation), that constraint produces structural results that
parallel quantum mechanics and thermodynamics. Each result’s status, whether a structural
theorem (S), a conditional derivation (C), or a consistency relation (R), is marked directly.
Nothing is hidden.
    The document is organized as:
   1. Foundations: State space, instruction set, operational semantics.
   2. Core Theorems: No Free Insight, µ-ledger necessity, µ-Chaitin, µ-conservation.
   3. Computation: Strict Turing subsumption, oracle pricing as instantiation of universal
      NFI, µ-complexity classes, µ-cost hierarchy.
   4. Symmetry & Conservation: Gauge action, Noether’s theorem, receipts.
   5. Quantum / CHSH algebra: Tsirelson bound (algebraic), CHSH classical bound,
      biconditional column-contractive ⇐⇒ NPA-realizable, three-tier observation taxonomy.
      Pure polynomial arithmetic, zero physics axioms.
   6. Conditional physics: Born rule (under linearity), unitarity, no-cloning, purification,
      Landauer (with second-law assumed as a record field), affine EFE family. Each is conditional
      on stated axioms in its own file.
   7. Discrete Geometry: Restricted angle-defect identity with B = 3χ (not classical Gauss-
      Bonnet); 4D simplicial complex and Einstein-tensor definitions (reference exploration only,
      vacuum case is a 0 = 0 consistency check).
   8. Meta-Theory: Three-layer Coq/Python/RTL observable contract, categorical structure.
   9. Predictions: Falsifiable predictions tied to specific Coq-verifiable claims.


1.1     Epistemological Framework
Formal verification (Coq) establishes that conclusions follow logically from axioms. It does not
establish that axioms model physical reality. This distinction is critical for evaluating the physics
claims in Parts V–VIII.

                                                 4
   Every result in this specification falls into one of three categories:

(S) Structural Theorems about the Thiele Machine as a mathematical object. Their conclu-
     sions follow from the definitions, explicit hypotheses, and reported library assumptions.
     Examples: µ-conservation, gauge invariance, confluence, halting undecidability, strict
     Turing subsumption.

(C) Conditional Theorems of the form “if axiom X holds, then physics result Y follows.” The
    logical inference is verified; whether axiom X holds in nature is an empirical question.
    Examples: Born rule uniqueness (conditional on linearity), complex necessity (conditional
    on 2D amplitudes), unitarity (conditional on information conservation).

(R) Consistency Algebraic identities or definitional equivalences that verify internal coherence
    but do not constitute independent predictions. Example: the Tsirelson cost definition.

    Each major theorem in Parts V–VIII is annotated with its category. A theorem marked (C)
or (R) is not diminished. Conditional derivations and consistency checks are valuable scientific
tools. But the reader should not mistake them for derivations from first principles without stated
assumptions. See Appendix B for the complete classification table.

Chapter 2

Notation and Conventions

This chapter exists because several symbols in this document are overloaded, and at least one
(S) is used well before it is defined. Read it once; it resolves ambiguities that would otherwise
require guessing from context.


2.1     Overloaded symbols
S, four distinct meanings. Disambiguated by context and, where ambiguity would be real,
     by explicit qualification:

        1. S (calligraphic): the state space.
        2. S (roman, in operational-semantics chapters): a particular VM state, an element of S.
        3. S(n) (applied to a natural number): the successor function on N, i.e. S(n) = n+1. This
           is Coq’s S constructor, and it is the reason cost expressions such as flen × 8 + S(cost)
           are bounded below by 1: S(n) ≥ 1 for every n ∈ N. This is the notation used
           from Chapter 4 onward, before its first formal appearance in the cost
           tables.
        4. S (in quantum chapters): the CHSH statistic, defined in Section 18.1. Real-valued.

G, three distinct meanings. All three occur, and two occur inside the same chapter on
     emergent geometry:

        1. G: the partition graph component of a VM state.
        2. G: Newton’s constant, fixed to 1/(8π) as a unit choice.
        3. Gµν : the Einstein tensor.

      Where the geometry chapter needs the partition graph it is written Gpart .

µ, ledger and index, and two different number systems. 1. µ: the cost ledger, a com-
          ponent of VM state. In the machine, µ ranges over N: it is a natural number, it is
          monotone non-decreasing under the step relation, and A2 gives it an integer floor.
        2. µ, ν as tensor indices in the geometry chapter (gµν , Tµν ). Unrelated to the ledger.
        3. µ in the quantum chapters (measurement cost, the no-cloning inequality, the µ/I
           ratio) is real-valued. This is a genuinely different object from the machine’s µ: there
           is no theorem in this development identifying them, and no coercion between them is
           claimed. Where the distinction matters the real-valued quantity is written µR .

Chapter 3

The State Space

3.1     Primitives
Let N = {0, 1, 2, . . . }. Let B = {0, 1}. Let Σ be a finite alphabet.


3.2     Regions and Modules
Definition 3.1 (Region). A Region R is a finite subset of N, canonically represented as a
deduplicated list preserving first-occurrence order: norm(L) = nodup(L) removes duplicate
elements while preserving the order of their first appearance. (The Python VM applies a stricter
canonical form via sorted(set(region)); the Coq specification uses nodup Nat.eq_dec which
does not sort.)

Definition 3.2 (Module). A Module M = (RM , AM ) consists of a normalized region RM ⊂ N
and a set of axioms AM ⊂ Σ∗ .


3.3     The Partition Graph
Definition 3.3 (Partition Graph). A partition graph G = (Nid , T ) where Nid ∈ N is the next
available module ID and T : {0, . . . , Nid − 1} ⇀ M is a finite partial function from IDs to
modules.

Axiom 3.1 (Well-Formedness). G is well-formed iff dom(T ) ⊆ {0, . . . , Nid − 1}.


3.4     Machine State
Definition 3.4 (VM State). The complete state is a 12-tuple:

                         S = (G, C, R, Mem, PC , µ, Tµ , err, ℓ, m, w, cert)

where G ∈ G is the partition graph, C ∈ N4 comprises the CSRs (certificate address, status,
error code, heap base), R ∈ NREG_COUNT is the register file, Mem ∈ NMEM_SIZE is main memory
(the kernel constants REG_COUNT and MEM_SIZE are parametric; the synthesized RTL fixes REG_
COUNT = 16 and MEM_SIZE = 128), PC ∈ N is the program counter, µ ∈ N is the µ-ledger,
Tµ ∈ N16 is the flattened 4 × 4 µ-tensor (row-major), err ∈ B is the global error flag (latching),
ℓ ∈ N is the logic-engine accumulator, m ∈ N is the machine status register (0 = Turing mode,
1 = Thiele mode), w ∈ N8 is the WitnessCount record (vm_witness): eight natural-number
counters wc_same_ab and wc_diff_ab for each setting pair (a, b) ∈ {00, 01, 10, 11}, and cert ∈ B
is the certified flag (vm_certified).

Chapter 4

Instruction Set and Cost Model

4.1     The Axiom Scheme A1–A6
“A2” is referred to throughout this document, and this is the scheme it belongs to. These are
conditions on Coq record types, not Axiom declarations. The assumption receipt distinguishes
project-local declarations from standard-library axioms and explicit theorem hypotheses. Sup-
plying an instance of the record is the only way to invoke the corresponding theorems, and the
51-opcode VM supplies them by construction.

4.1.1   The consolidated CertificationSystem (A1–A2)
A CertificationSystem is a tuple (Σ, I, step, cost, cert) where Σ is any state type, I any
instruction type, step : Σ × I → Σ, cost : I → N, and cert : Σ → B, subject to:

A1 (certification is observable). A total function cert : Σ → B exists. This holds by record
    existence: providing the field is providing the axiom.

A2 (certification is priced; the load-bearing one).

         ∀s ∈ Σ, ∀i ∈ I,    cert(s) = false ∧ cert(step(s, i)) = true =⇒ cost(i) ≥ 1.        (4.1)

      In Coq this is the field cs_cert_costs. Everything called “No Free Insight” in this
      document is A2 lifted from steps to traces by induction; the trace-level statement is
      genuinely proved, but its content is A2 plus “a sum of naturals containing a summand ≥1
      is ≥1”.

Remark 4.1 (A2 is a stipulation, and that is the intended reading). A2 is a condition a substrate
may or may not satisfy, not a discovered fact about computation. Nothing in this document
derives it; Section 21.2 derives it conditionally from a Landauer premise plus a named calibration
bridge, which relocates the stipulation rather than removing it. Claims of the form “the machine
proves certification must cost” should be read as “on any substrate satisfying A2, certification
costs”. That is what universal_nfi_any_substrate states, over an arbitrary state type and
an arbitrary instruction type.

4.1.2   The QuantitativeCertificationSystem extension (A3–A6)
The quantitative extension lifts the floor from ≥ 1 to ≥ K for an arbitrary threshold. It adds a
witness function W : Σ → N and a threshold K ∈ N, subject to:

A3 (cost bounds witness growth). ∀s, i : W (s) + cost(i) ≥ W (step(s, i)). Evidence cannot
    accumulate faster than it is paid for. Coq: qcs_cost_bounds_witness.

                                                9
A4 (certification requires the threshold). ∀s : cert(s) = true =⇒ W (s) ≥ K. Coq:
    qcs_cert_threshold_witness.

A5 (clean start). The initial state is uncertified, cert(s0 ) = false, and carries no witness,
    W (s0 ) = 0. Supplied as a hypothesis at use sites rather than as a record field.

A6 (threshold is the claim’s price). K is the parameter of the conclusion: a run from s0
     to a certified state has total cost ≥ K. Not an independent assumption; it names the
     quantity A3 and A4 telescope into.

Remark 4.2 (Two numbering systems, and which one this document uses). The kernel carries
a second, older numbering attached to the AbstractCertMachine record, in which the cost-on-
certification rule is called A4 and A2 names “cert starts unset”. The two systems agree on
content and disagree on labels. Throughout this document, “A2” means the cost-on-
certification rule in the consolidated CertificationSystem sense stated above. When
reading kernel sources that mention AbstractCertMachine, translate accordingly.


4.2     Instruction Categories
The instruction set I is organized into six families. Every instruction i carries an explicit cost
parameter ∆µi ∈ N.

4.2.1   Structural Operations
These modify the partition graph G:

  • PNEW(R, ∆µ): Create module with region R.

  • PSPLIT(m, L, R, ∆µ): Split module m into disjoint regions.

  • PMERGE(m1 , m2 , ∆µ): Merge two modules.

  • PDISCOVER(m, E, ∆µ): Discovery accounting for module m. The abstract specification
    attaches evidence E (list of axioms) to the module; the current hardware-aligned step
    advances PC and charges ∆µ without mutating the graph.

  • MDLACC(m, ∆µ): Minimum-description-length accumulate on module m.

4.2.2   Logical Operations
These interact with information content:

  • LASSERT(freg, creg, kind, flen, cost): Assert a formula read from vm_mem[R[freg]] (encoded-
    unit count flen, eight concrete bits per unit) against a witness package at vm_mem[R[creg]].
    In SAT mode the package contains one satisfying assignment and one falsifying assignment,
    stored back-to-back; ∆µ = flen × 8 + S(cost) ≥ 1.

  • LJOIN(c1reg, c2reg, ∆µ): Join certificates from two memory-resident strings addressed by
    registers.

  • REVEAL(m, n, cert, ∆µ): Reveal n bits of structure from m; cost is n + S(∆µ).

  • EMIT(m, p, ∆µ): Emit payload p from module m; cost is |p|bits + S(∆µ).




                                               10
4.2.3   Computational Operations (Reversible ALU)
Reversible register/memory operations:

  • XFER(dst, src, ∆µ): Copy register src to dst.

  • XOR_LOAD(dst, addr, ∆µ): Load memory word at addr.

  • XOR_ADD(dst, src, ∆µ): dst ← dst ⊕ src.

  • XOR_SWAP(a, b, ∆µ): Swap registers a and b.

  • XOR_RANK(dst, src, ∆µ): dst ← popcount(src).

4.2.4   Standard ALU and Control Flow
  • LOAD_IMM(dst, imm, ∆µ): Load 32-bit immediate into register.

  • LOAD(dst, addr, ∆µ): Load from main memory.

  • STORE(addr, src, ∆µ): Store to main memory.

  • ADD(dst, r1 , r2 , ∆µ): dst ← r1 + r2 (mod 264 ).

  • SUB(dst, r1 , r2 , ∆µ): dst ← r1 − r2 (mod 264 ).

  • JUMP(target, ∆µ): Unconditional jump.

  • JNEZ(r, target, ∆µ): Jump if R[r] ̸= 0.

  • CALL(target, ∆µ): Push return address, jump to target.

4.3     Cost Model
Every instruction i carries an explicit cost parameter ∆µi ∈ N. The function instruction_cost(i)
extracts this parameter. The cost is applied via apply_cost(s, i) = s.µ + instruction_cost(i).
Since ∆µi : N, the µ-ledger is monotonically non-decreasing by construction.
    The mandatory positive-cost class is: CERTIFY, MORPH_ASSERT, and LJOIN (each charges
S(∆µ) ≥ 1); EMIT (|payload|bits + S(∆µ)); REVEAL and READ_PORT (bits + S(∆µ)); and LASSERT
(flen × 8 + S(∆µ)). These cannot be zero-cost even with ∆µ = 0. The five CHSH_LASSERT family
instructions also charge S(∆µ). Other instructions charge their encoded ∆µ, which may be zero.

Theorem 4.1 (Least joint unit floor, (S)). For a finite family of event predicates on instructions,
the least natural-valued schedule satisfying a unit floor on each event is the indicator that at least
one event occurs. This is joint_floor_is_least. Independent Boolean coordinates can change
together for one unit under these rules; additivity requires an additional condition.


4.4     The CERTIFY instruction
CERTIFY is referred to throughout Chapters 6 onward and is defined here.

Definition 4.1 (CERTIFY (instr_certify, VMStep.v)). CERTIFY(∆µ) has the step semantics

                       pc 7→ pc + 1,      µ 7→ µ + S(∆µ),        cert 7→ true,                  (4.2)

with every other state component unchanged. Its cost is S(∆µ) ≥ 1, which is the concrete
instance of A2 (Section 4.1) for this ISA. It is the only rule in VMStep.v that sets cert := true.

Remark 4.3 (CERTIFY is unguarded; what that does and does not mean). The rule fires for
every state and every ∆µ: it carries no precondition, takes no proposition as an argument, and
checks nothing. Setting the certification bit is therefore always available, at a price of at least

                                                 12
1. Conversely, the instruction that does perform a decidable check on data, namely LASSERT,
which traps on failure, leaves both the certification CSR and cert unchanged.
    The honest description of the mechanism is therefore metering, not verification: the step rule
guarantees that flipping the bit is priced, not that the bit is only set when some claim holds.
Statements of the form “this machine makes stamping verified impossible” overstate it; what is
impossible is stamping it for free.
    The ISA does contain genuinely guarded instructions. MORPH_ASSERT requires the named
morphism to exist, and CHSH_LASSERT runs column_contractive_check_witness on actual
witness data and traps on failure, with a soundness bridge to PSD. A substrate in which the
certification bit may be set only when an invariant holds would let A2 be derived (the cost of
setting the bit becomes the cost of establishing the invariant) rather than stipulated. That is an
open direction, not a present result; see Remark 4.1.

Definition 10.1 (Gauge Shift). For δ ∈ Z, the gauge shift σδ : S → S maps S 7→ S[µ ←

Theorem 10.1 (Partial Group Action (KernelNoether.v)). The gauge shifts form a partial Z-

  1. Identity: σ0 (S) = S. Unconditional (z_action_identity).

  2. Composition: σa (σb (S)) = σa+b (S) provided µ(S) + b ≥ 0 and µ(S) + b + a ≥ 0 (z_

  3. Inverse: σ−n (σn (S)) = S provided µ(S) + n ≥ 0 (z_action_inverse).

Remark 10.1 (Why composition needs its guard). The guard on clause 2 is not decoration.
Take µ(S) = 0, b = −1, a = +1: σb clamps to µ = 0, so σa (σb (S)) has µ = 1; whereas
σa+b (S) = σ0 (S) = S has µ = 0. The two sides differ. Composition fails wherever an intermediate
shift underflows, which is why the mechanised theorem carries two nonnegativity hypotheses:
one for the intermediate state, one for the final state. Drop either and the counterexample above
goes through.

18.3.1    The adversarial search
Five confirmations settle nothing on their own. A criterion loose enough to accept anything

confirmations. It is the reported absence of exceptions after somebody went looking. coq/

the corner where the criterion is most likely to break rather than at systems resembling the five.
    The claim put at risk is the strong reading:

     (PO-STRONG) Every system that must resist forgery of a commitment has a
     commitment event that redundantly proliferates.

    A counterexample must satisfy three conditions fixed in advance: (C1) it must resist forgery
of a commitment, in the sense that its designers treat an unearned commitment as the primary
failure mode; (C2) it must be deployed and independently evolved, owing nothing to this
development; and (C3) its commitment event must fail redundantly_proliferating.
    (PO-STRONG) is false. Three candidates refute it, each machine-checked:

   • Deniable (designated-verifier) authentication (deniable_authentication_
     refutes_strong_criterion). The scheme is engineered so a third party cannot be
     convinced even given the whole transcript, because the designated verifier could have
     produced it. Non-transferability is the product requirement.
   • Symmetric-key authentication (mac_refutes_strong_criterion). Existential un-
     forgeability is the defining security notion of a MAC, and verification requires the shared
     secret, so no record reaches anyone else.
   • Object capabilities (capability_refutes_strong_criterion). Unforgeability here is
     unconditional, enforced by memory safety rather than by a cost or a hardness assumption.
     Nothing is paid, nothing is logged, and no observer outside the holder can tell.

    A public-log control (public_log_confirms) checks that the search can return confirms and
is not an instrument that only reports refutations; its rival effort event still fails to proliferate,
so the control discriminates rather than accepting everything.

MAC and a digital signature both resist forgery; only the signature proliferates. Deniable
authentication and certificate transparency both resist forgery; deniability actively prevents
third-party conviction while transparency actively requires it. Object capabilities and proof-
carrying code both make unearned authority impossible; the capability confines it to the holder
while the proof travels to every consumer. In each pair the proliferating member is the one
whose commitment must convince parties who were not present. The criterion that survives is
therefore:

     (PO-PUBLIC) A system whose commitment must convince third parties who did
     not witness its creation will proliferate records of the commitment event.

    The signature observer model makes the commitment visible in each observer fragment
and proves proliferation for that model. Its correspondence to deployed signatures remains a
modeling judgment. Public availability of a verifier does not imply that every observer retains a
record.

premises. The hardness construction has the weaker soundness guarantee stated in its theorem.
Neither these examples nor the factorization obstruction proves that only one route can involve
metering.

(M1) Forgery resistance forces metering. False. Signatures take the hardness route;

(M2) Forgery resistance forces record proliferation. False. Deniable authentication,

    first case that is the design goal.
(M3) Public commitments force record proliferation. Proposed. The chosen observer
    models satisfy it. Actual record proliferation needs independent evidence; public verifiability
    alone is insufficient.

any microstate-count function, and Scand : State → R any candidate entropy. Suppose for some
base ≥ 2 and some state s with L(s) ≥ 1:
                                                ′
   1. ledger-exponential: ω(s′ ) = baseL(s ) for all s′ ;

   2. ledger-linear: Scand (s′ ) = α · L(s′ ) for all s′ ;

   3. Boltzmann-form: Scand (s′ ) = kB ln ω(s′ ) whenever ω(s′ ) > 0.

Then α is forced:
                                           α = kB · ln(base).                                  (21.1)

    This is the sharpest honest statement of the limit on deriving physics from computation in
this framework, and it deserves to be read as a no-go, not as a bridge. The theorem quantifies
over any type, any integer ledger, and any exponential microstate count. It makes no reference
to the Thiele machine at all. What it says is that a dimensionless integer ledger determines
the entropy relation only up to two externally supplied scalars: the unit-conversion constant
kB (an empirical fact about thermodynamics) and the counting base (a structural fact about

                                                    50
the substrate’s degrees of freedom). Fix both and α follows; fix neither and the ledger does
not prefer any value. There is a one-parameter family of consistent (α, kB ) pairs, and choosing
among them is precisely the modelling input that the ledger cannot supply.
   Two consequences worth stating explicitly:

  • The kB ln 2 appearing in the Thiele instantiation (SecondLawBoltzmannWall.v, base = 2
    for binary cert-flips) is not derived from the machine. It is what you get after supplying
    kB and the base from outside.

  • Any claim of the form “this computational framework predicts a physical constant” is
    blocked by Theorem 21.4 unless it also says which two external scalars it assumed. This
    applies to the present development as much as to any other.

   Read this section before the conditional physics chapters, not after: it is the constraint they
operate under, and it is what keeps them honest.

                        self_ref(S) =⇒ ∃ Meta : dim(Meta) > dim(S)

22.2      The Thiele Manifold
Definition 22.1 (Thiele Manifold (ThieleManifold.v)). An infinite tower of systems {Ln }n∈N
with:

   • dim(Ln ) = 4 + n (level n has dimension 4 + n).

   • dim(Ln+1 ) > dim(Ln ) (strict enrichment).

   • Each level reasons about the level below.

   • Self-reference at level n is answered by level n + 1.

Theorem 22.2 (Projection to Spacetime (ThieleManifold.v)). The projection π4 collapses the
tower to dimension 4 (spacetime). For n > 0:

   • π4 is lossy: dim(Ln ) > 4.

   • µ-cost of projection = dim(Ln ) − 4 = n > 0.

Spacetime is the “shadow” of the full manifold.

Three-Layer Observable Agreement

Remark 23.1 (Agreement, not isomorphism). No isomorphism is constructed in this chapter,
and none is claimed: there is no pair of mutually inverse maps between the Coq, OCaml/Python,
and RTL layers, and no proof that either composite is the identity. What is established is
that each layer’s decoder satisfies a shared observable-projection contract, and that the three
projections agree on every tested input: transitivity of equality through a common projection.
    The distinction is not pedantic. Agreement on a projection is compatible with the layers
differing arbitrarily on everything the projection forgets; an isomorphism would forbid that.
Since the projection deliberately forgets internal representation, agreement is the right property
to want here. Isomorphism would be a stronger claim than the construction supports.

Theorem 23.1 (Observable Comparison Contract (ThreeLayerIsomorphism.v)). The active Coq
theorem packages the observable-state contract used for cross-layer comparison: if the chosen
Python and RTL decoders satisfy the same projection contract as the Coq layer, then the decoded
observable states agree on that projection for a shared trace τ .

decodePython (τ ) = decodeCoq (τ ) ∧ decodeRTL (τ ) = decodeCoq (τ ) =⇒ decodePython (τ ) = decodeRTL (τ )

This is a contract theorem over explicit observable projections, not an unconditional claim that
every current Python and RTL artifact is universally equal to Coq in all CI environments.

Categorical Structure

24.1     The Morphism Layer
The active implementation adds a concrete category directly to the machine state. This is not an
abstract analogy, it is an executable layer of 7 opcodes (0x27–0x2D) with formally verified laws.

24.1.1    Objects and Morphisms
The partition graph G carries two maps:

  • pg_modules : ModuleID ⇀ ModuleState, objects (partition modules)

  • pg_morphisms : MorphismID ⇀ MorphismState, arrows

    A MorphismState bundles: source module, target module, coupling data (list of (nat × nat)
pairs plus a label), and an identity flag.

24.1.2    The Seven Categorical Opcodes
  • MORPH(dst, src, tgt, idx, ∆µ): create morphism src → tgt, write morphism ID to regs[dst].
    Costs ∆µ.

  • COMPOSE(dst, f, g, ∆µ): compose morphisms f ; g when f.target = g.source, write new ID
    to regs[dst]. Costs ∆µ. Errors if endpoints mismatch.

  • MORPH_ID(dst, m, ∆µ): create identity morphism idm for module m (diagonal relation).
    Costs ∆µ.

  • MORPH_DELETE(f, ∆µ): remove morphism f . Cascades on module deletion via graph_
    cascade_delete_morphisms.

  • MORPH_ASSERT(f, prop, cert, ∆µ): cert-setter. Costs S(∆µ) ≥ 1 unconditionally, even for
    failed attempts (morphism does not exist). This is the No Free Insight law applied to
    relational claims.

  • MORPH_TENSOR(dst, f, g, ∆µ): tensor product f ⊗ g. Requires that the union modules
    src(f ) ∪ src(g) and tgt(f ) ∪ tgt(g) already exist in the graph.

  • MORPH_GET(dst, f, sel, ∆µ): read field from morphism f : selector 0 = source, 1 = target, 2
    = coupling length, 3 = is_identity.

24.1.3    Category Laws
Theorem 24.1 (Relational Composition is Associative (kernel/CategoryLaws.v), (S)). For any
three morphisms f : A → B, g : B → C, h : C → D with composable endpoints:

                                         (f ; g); h = f ; (g; h)

in the relational sense (coupling pairs of the composite). Zero Admitted.

Theorem 24.2 (Graph Composition Satisfies Laws (kernel/CategoryBridge.v), (S)). The
operation graph_compose_morphisms satisfies associativity and unitality. Additionally:

                         categorical_extension_nofi_consistent

MORPH and COMPOSE can carry ∆µ = 0 and are not in the mandatory-cost class. The NoFI
policy enters through MORPH_ASSERT: asserting that a built morphism chain exists and activating
the cert channel always costs S(∆µ) ≥ 1. Building the chain is free; certifying it never is.

Theorem 24.3 (Tensor Bifunctoriality (kernel/CategoryMonoidal.v), (S)). graph_tensor_morphisms
is a bifunctor: it satisfies the interchange law

                                  (f ⊗ g); (h ⊗ k) = (f ; h) ⊗ (g; k)

for composable, disjoint pairs.

24.1.4    The Categorical Separation Theorem
Theorem 24.4 (Categorical Separation (kernel/PartitionSeparation.v, §10), (S)). There exist
states s1 , s2 such that:

s1 .vm_regs = s2 .vm_regs ∧ s1 .vm_mem = s2 .vm_mem ∧ s1 .vm_mu = s2 .vm_mu ∧ s1 .vm_err = s2 .vm_err

but s1 .vm_graph.pg_morphisms ̸= s2 .vm_graph.pg_morphisms.

Proof. Construct s1 with pg_morphisms = [(0, ms)] for any morphism state ms, and s2 with
pg_morphisms = []. Arrange identical vm_regs, vm_mem, vm_mu, vm_err by choosing costs and
instruction sequences accordingly. Distinction by discriminate.

Remark 24.1 (Significance). A classical machine (exposing only registers, memory, and a cost
counter) cannot distinguish s1 from s2 . The Thiele Machine distinguishes them in one probe
instruction: MORPH_DELETE 0 succeeds on s1 and errors on s2 . The categorical layer records how
knowledge was derived, not just what the final values are.
    This is the machine’s claim to being a knowledge auditor: any party claiming to have verified
a structural relation must carry a µ-receipt ≥ the minimum construction cost. The NoFI theorem
supplies a cost floor under its trace hypotheses. It does not authenticate an externally supplied
receipt or prove arbitrary property text true.

Falsifiable Predictions

The honest falsifiable predictions are the structural theorems that compile or fail. The claims
below identify specific mathematical conditions that would constitute counterexamples to the
machine’s core theorems.


25.1     Falsify No Free Insight
Find a Thiele Machine program trace that starts with vm_certified = false and µ = 0,
ends with vm_certified = true, and ends with µ = 0. The Coq proof of certification_
requires_positive_mu would fail.


25.2     Falsify the Classical CHSH Bound
Exhibit a locally factorizable strategy achieving |S| > 2. This contradicts chsh_stat_
violation_not_local and also contradicts known mathematics.


25.3     Falsify the Biconditional
Exhibit E00 , E01 , E10 , E11 satisfying the three column-contractivity conditions but with a non-
PSD NPA moment matrix. The theorem column_contractive_iff_quantum_realizable says
no such example exists.


25.4     Falsify the µ-Hierarchy
Find k ≥ 1 and a trace reaching vm_certified = true and vm_mu ≥ k with total trace cost
< k. This contradicts mu_hierarchy_theorem.


25.5     What I Don’t Know
  • Physical interpretation of µ: Whether the µ ledger corresponds to thermodynamic
    entropy in a physical Thiele Machine is an open empirical question. The formal development
    is consistent with Landauer’s principle. The calibration hypothesis is named (mu_landauer_
    unruh_calibrated) and not proved in Coq.

  • Quantum mechanics connection: The mathematical equivalence between slice coher-
    ence and zero-marginal NPA realizability is a kernel-tier theorem. Whether a physical
    Thiele Machine can produce CHSH-violating statistics by exploiting quantum entanglement
    in hardware is an open empirical question outside the scope of this project.


                                               57
• Complexity implications: Whether µP ̸= µNP is an open question. The factored-search
  witness shows a concrete structural advantage but is not a class separation.

• Physical constants: No physical constant is derived from first principles. There is no
  derivation of Planck’s constant h; any relation tying an internal time-scale τµ to h would
  be circular by construction, defining τµ in terms of h and checking consistency rather than
  predicting h.

• The two +1s (OP-Plus-One): the +1 in triangle_angle’s denominator (PI · d_bc /
  (1 + d_ab + d_ac + d_bc), coq/kernel/curvature/MuGravity.v) versus the A2 cost-
  floor +1 on CERTIFY/LASSERT (coq/kernel/foundation/VMStep.v), same axiom, or
  unrelated regularisation? No kernel-internal theorem unifies them without circularity (coq/
  kernel/frontier/F3_PlusOneStructural.v); the symbolic correction term decays as
  −π/(3(3d + 1)) in the equilateral case (tools/triangle_angle_plus_one_analysis.py),
  regularisation-shaped rather than A2-quantum-shaped. Inconclusive. A positive structural
  identification (Case A, the geometric +1 is A2 in geometric form) would re-frame the
  calibrated regime’s asymptotic-only character as a structural consequence of A2; a positive
  non-identification (Case B) would license replacing S(. . . ) with (. . . ) in the denominator.
  Either is real categorical content. The kernel was not modified pending closure.

• F3 narrow non-vacuity (related to OP-Plus-One): a constructible finite VMState
  satisfying ∀m, calibration_residual(s, m) = 0 on a partition graph with at least one
  actual graph triangle and well_formed_triangulated holding. The symbolic search to
  N = 7 exhausts negatively. Whether the asymptotic-only character of calibration is a
  structural consequence of OP-Plus-One’s Case A, or just a search-depth artifact, is itself
  open.
