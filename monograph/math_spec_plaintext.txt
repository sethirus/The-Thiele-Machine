The Thiele Machine
 A Mathematical Reference


       Devon Thiele
Before the specification. I don’t do math by hand. I think in shapes, directions, and
connections, and my part of this work was to see where things had to fit, to direct, and to
refuse what didn’t fit. This document is the translation. Language models wrote the formal
statements and the proofs, and I read the statements to make sure they say what the shape
says. Coq checks the formal counterparts identified below; some on-page arguments have no Coq
counterpart here, as noted where they occur. The arguments that carry the results are in the
monograph, on the page; the formal versions here are their counterparts, and compiling them is
a tool for catching mistakes, not the reason to believe them. So nothing below is in my voice, on
purpose. It’s the reference layer: exact statements, exact premises, and where each one lives. If
you want the pictures, the monograph starts with them. If you want to know exactly what is
claimed, it’s here.
The Thiele Machine is an abstract model of computation, and an argument made
with it about what an account of computation should preserve.
    It is a machine in the sense a Turing machine is one: a mathematical object. No device is
meant. The model starts from a set of states, moves, a step, a price on each move, a record that
can be compared, and one rule, the toll: a move that takes the record out of the down-set of
where it stood pays at least one. Any system with those parts is a priced record system; a Thiele
machine is one whose record some run raises, from a designated start, and no move lowers. A
yes-or-no reading is the smallest record, and most of what follows is stated on it. A reading
no move carries out of its down-set is flat, and the toll asks nothing of it: a clock, a reversible
process and every ordinary deterministic machine, read through a reading their moves never
raise, are flat; each, once its states can be tested for equality and it has a run that leaves its
start, is the shadow of a member. Forget the prices and the record of any Thiele machine and
what is left is one of them; the results below are about what that forgetting throws away. The
latch, two states and one priced move, shows the definition can be met. It is not the model.
    The structural question is what a total deterministic process needs to raise a reading and keep
it: a merge along the run or endlessly fresh states. A physical price needs separate hypotheses.
Landauer bounds depend on the physical model and input distribution; finite-time entropy-
production bounds here concern specified thermal stochastic dynamics, not every physical state
change. Certification, a record that a claim was checked and closed, is the sharpest case. It
is one example of a wider question about structural state, observation and cost. A window, a
function from states to what can be seen of them (Chapter 5), can lose distinctions that the
full state keeps, such as the record and the ledger; the cost laws of Chapters 3 and 4 constrain
specified events under hypotheses each theorem names. A conventional encoding can preserve
those distinctions. A window that discards them cannot recover them in general. The stronger
proposal that these distinctions belong in a necessary foundation of computation is a conjecture.
The status of each result is stated below.

                                           Abstract

This is a mathematical reference for the Thiele Machine: a deterministic transition system with
instruction prices and a record that is a position in a preorder, with a toll on every step that
moves the record out of the down-set of where it stood, a record no step lowers, and a designated
start from which some run raises it. The one-bit record, a Boolean reading with its false-to-true
crossing, is the two-point instance, and most results are stated on it. An internal ledger is
an additional condition used by particular results. It states the model, its toll, the member
predicate, the smallest member and the history lift, then the results, each over the class its
premises name. Each result is stated with its premises and names the declaration that carries it.
    A formal proof shows that conclusions follow from premises. It does not show that premises
model physical reality. Every result is tagged as one of three types: (S) structural theorems
about the model or a stated machine, (C) conditional results that hold under a named premise
no machine fact discharges, and (R) refutations, where a theorem states the negation of a
proposed claim and a named construction supplies the counterexample. One proposal, the
pointer criterion of Chapter 9, has no proof; it carries a fourth tag, thesis.
    The structural results (S), by where they sit in the model:

  • The definition and the price in the move. The member predicate: the toll, permanence and
    a run that makes a distinction; a flat reading obeys the toll and is not a member; the latch
    is the smallest member; every system with a Boolean equality on states and a run that
    ends off its start is the shadow of a member. The floor is the toll over any order of records,
    and the universal floor on the one-bit instance. The least instruction price and the least
    price for the lift. On finite machines, the toll derived from merge pricing for permanent
    certificates. Shadow pricing: no price computed from a window that hides a crossing is
    exact.

  • What stays. A free move cannot touch a permanent reading. The record axis over any base
    is a latch, and a growing record decomposes into threshold latches.

  • What a window forgets. The coarsest observational identification, and the factorization of
    histories through it. Shadow pricing, and what a window can decide and price.

  • The room a kept distinction needs. On any state space a permanent flip merges or needs a
    fresh state every time, with the exact retention bound for a bounded memory, and the
    stochastic and unitary forms of the same count.

  • What no inside procedure decides. The structural shortcut wall over any substrate with a
    recursion theorem; the recursion theorem and Rice’s theorem (the logician Henry Rice,
    1953: no program decides a property of what programs do, unless it holds of every program
    or of none) for L.

That a flat reading, a toggle and a one-state system are not members, and the counterexamples
that show which premises do work, are refutations (R). Results stated over Landauer heat are
conditional (C) on the named premise landauer_heat.

Chapter 1

Introduction

A Thiele machine has states, moves, a price on each move and a record in a preorder; a move
that raises the record pays (the toll), no move lowers it, and some run from a designated start
raises it. A ledger, where a result uses one, is an additional condition, and a window that hides
the record and the ledger is the subject of Chapter 5. The chapters below state that rule, derive
its consequences, show which machines carry it, and prove where a window that hides the record
and the ledger loses information.
     Chapter 3 states the model, its toll, the member predicate, the smallest member, the history
lift and the universal floor. Chapter 4 derives the toll on finite machines from the price of
merging states and checks which premises are needed. Chapter 5 states what a window can
decide and price. Chapter 6 states the recursion and undecidability results, over any substrate
and for L. Chapter 7 states the record axis over any base and the latch decomposition of growing
records. Chapter 8 states the physical boundary. Chapter 9 states the models of external systems
and the pointer thesis.

Notation and Conventions

 • N is the natural numbers, B = {false, true}, R the reals.

 • A trace is a finite list of instructions. run(τ, s) folds the step function over τ from s.

 • (R, ≤) is the preorder of records and rec : S → R the record. In Coq it is ax_rec on an
   AxSys.

 • P is the record on the two-point order, a reading from states to B. In Coq it is cs_cert
   on a CertificationSystem.

 • c(i) ∈ N is the cost of instruction i; c(τ ) is the sum over a trace.

 • µ is the ledger. Where a state carries it, conservation means µ(step(s, i)) = µ(s) + c(i).

 • A window is a function from states to observations.

Chapter 3

Thiele Machines and the Universal
Floor

Definition 3.1 (Priced record system; AxSys in Coq). A priced record system is a tuple
(S, I, step, c, rec) with step : S × I → S, c : I → N and rec : S → R, where (R, ≤) is a preorder
given by a Boolean test. The record is a position in that order. A step from s by i leaves the
down-set when rec(step(s, i)) ̸≤ rec(s). The one rule is the toll, first named No Free Insight:

                        A2:    ∀s i, rec(step(s, i)) ̸≤ rec(s) =⇒ c(i) ≥ 1.

Coq: the record AxSys in coq/kernel/foundation/AxCore.v, with the toll as ax_a2. The
order carries no further assumption: not antisymmetry, not growth of the record along steps,
not finiteness. An internal ledger is not part of this definition or the next; the results that use
one say so.

Definition 3.2 (Thiele machine; ax_thiele in Coq). A Thiele machine is a priced record
system together with a set of designated starts starts ⊆ S such that

   • (toll) A2 holds;

   • (permanence) ∀s i, rec(s) ≤ rec(step(s, i));

   • (distinction) for some s0 ∈ starts and some trace τ , rec(run(τ, s0 )) ̸≤ rec(s0 ).

A reading is flat when rec(step(s, i)) ≤ rec(s) for every s and i. Flat is a property of the reading.
In the other direction, a system with a Boolean equality on its states and a run that ends
off its start is what some Thiele machine looks like through a window that drops the record
(Theorem 3.2). Coq: ax_thiele in coq/kernel/foundation/AxThiele.v, with the clauses
ax_a2, ax_grows and ax_distinction, and ax_flat for the flat reading. Every Thiele machine
has a move that costs at least 1 (thiele_has_a_priced_move) and has the floor over its order
of Theorem 3.3 (thiele_floor).

Theorem 3.1 (The smallest Thiele machine). (S) The latch has two states no < yes, one move
that sends both to yes at price 1, and the state as its reading. It is a Thiele machine with start
no (latch_is_thiele), and its move is not injective: both states land on one (latch_merges).
Every Thiele machine has two states with different readings (no_one_state_member); over the
two-point order every Thiele machine has a move that raises the reading from some no-state and
keeps every yes-state at yes (two_point_member_raising_move); on two states read as them-
selves every Thiele machine has the latch’s move (two_state_member_has_latch_move); and
the toggle, whose move swaps the two states, is not a Thiele machine (toggle_is_not_thiele).



                                                  4
Theorem 3.2 (The history lift). (S) Let M be any tuple (S, I, step, c, rec) of that shape, toll
or not, with a Boolean equality test on its states that agrees with equality, and x a state. The
history lift of M at x has states S × B, the moves of M , step ((s, b), i) 7→ (step(s, i), b ∨
[step(s, i) = x]), price 1 on every move, and reading (s, b) 7→ b (history_lift). Dropping
the bit returns the run of M (history_lift_shadow). If some run from a state s0 ̸= x
ends at x, the lift is a Thiele machine with start (s0 , false) (history_lift_is_thiele); so
every M with a run that ends on a state other than its start is the shadow of a Thiele machine
(moving_machine_is_a_shadow). Above every shadow sit two lifted states with different readings,
and no function of the shadow equals the reading (shadow_hides_history). Over a finite base the
least price list that meets the toll for the lift is 1 on every move that can land on x and 0 elsewhere,
the toll holds for a price list exactly when it is at least that list, and the lift priced by that list
is a Thiele machine (lift_can_raise_iff, lift_toll_iff_least, least_lift_is_thiele,
coq/audit/AxThieleLeast.v).

The flat reading, and computation as a shadow. A reading is flat when no step leaves
its down-set, so A2 holds with nothing to charge (flat_obeys_toll), no run of it leaves the
down-set of its start (flat_has_no_distinction), and it is not a Thiele machine for any choice
of starts (flat_is_not_thiele). A clock and a reversible process, each read through a reading
its moves never raise, are flat, and so is any deterministic transition system (S, I, step) given a
constant record: forget c and rec from any Thiele machine and what remains is flat. Flat is a
property of the reading, not of the machine: every system with a Boolean equality test on its
states and a run that ends on a state other than its start is the shadow of a Thiele machine
(Theorem 3.2). Ordinary computation is one flat shadow among others, and the theorems of
this reference are about what the forgetting throws away.

The one-bit instance. Take R = B with false < true (two_pre). The record is a reading
P : S → B, leaving the down-set is a false-to-true crossing, and A2 reads
                      ∀s i, P (s) = false ∧ P (step(s, i)) = true =⇒ c(i) ≥ 1.
Coq: the record CertificationSystem, with this instance of A2 as its field cs_cert_costs;
cs_axsys and axsys_cs translate each way. The name of that record does not restrict the
interpretation of P : it is an arbitrary Boolean reading, and neither permanence nor the occurrence
of both values is required. Most results below are stated on this instance, and each says so by
writing P . A certification system obeys the toll on this instance; it is a Thiele machine only
with permanence and a distinction added.
Theorem 3.3 (The floor is the toll). (S) For every priced record system, every run whose
final record is not at or below its starting record costs at least 1 exactly when A2 holds
(ax_floor_iff_a2), and under A2 every run costs at least the number of its steps that leave the
down-set (ax_cost_ge_exits). When the record never drops along a step, leaving the down-set
is a strict rise (ax_exit_is_strict_rise).
Proof. A one-step run is a step, so the floor gives the toll. For the converse, induct on the run.
If the first step stays in the down-set, the rest of the run ends outside the down-set of its own
start, by transitivity of the order, and the induction hypothesis pays. If the first step leaves the
down-set, A2 pays. Counting exits is the same induction.

Theorem 3.4 (Thresholds are one-bit machines). (S) For every point a of R, reading the
record only as the bit “a ≤ rec(s)” gives a one-bit priced record system, and it obeys its
toll whenever the original does (ax_view_a2); the original obeys A2 exactly when every such
threshold does (ax_a2_iff_views). When the order is antisymmetric, the thresholds determine
the record (bp_thresholds_determine). The one-bit floor of Theorem 3.9 is the two-point
instance (two_point_nfi).

Sliced interface. On the one-bit instance, a sliced system additionally has a surjection
π : S → B and a partition I = IH ⊔ IV . Write Fi (s) = step(s, i). For every i ∈ IH there is
hi : B → B with
                     π(Fi (s)) = hi (π(s)),     P (Fi (s)) = P (s),    c(i) = 0.
For every v ∈ IV , require π(Fv (s)) = π(s) and P (s) ≤ P (Fv (s)), ordering false below true. A2
still applies. These equations hold on all states of the stated domain; they imply permanence.
They do not require a product state space or a universal base.
     For a non-certification instance, use S = Z4 × {0, 1, 2}, π(b, k) = b and P (b, k) = [k = 2]. The
horizontal instruction R(b, k) = (b + 1 mod 4, k) costs zero; the vertical instruction V (b, k) =
(b, min(k + 1, 2)) costs one. Projection and reading preservation for R are immediate; V preserves
the projection, never lowers the reading, and pays one on its crossing from k = 1 to k = 2. It
also costs one at k = 2.

Valuations. A Boolean valuation need not itself be an autonomous two-state quotient:
coq/audit/ValuationBoundary.v gives a counterexample.


3.1     Free moves, least prices and observation
Theorem 3.5 (All readings and free moves). (S) For a state type with decidable
equality, fixed transitions and natural instruction prices, A2 holds for every Boolean
reading if and only if every zero-cost instruction is the identity on all states. Coq:
every_reading_toll_iff_free_moves_are_identity.
Proof. If a free move changes s to t, the decidable reading [x = t] is false at s and true at t,
contradicting A2. Conversely an identity move cannot raise any reading, and every positive
natural price is at least one.

   This distinguishes a chosen priced reading from arbitrary state predicates; it does not select
an autonomous base projection or a partition of moves.
Theorem 3.6 (Free reading preservation). (S) Under A2 and global permanence, every
zero-cost instruction preserves the reading. With the other sliced-interface laws fixed, global
permanence can replace horizontal reading preservation and vertical monotonicity. Coq:
free_reading_invariance.
Proof. A free move cannot raise the reading by A2 and cannot lower it by permanence. Conversely,
horizontal preservation and vertical monotonicity ensure that no instruction lowers the reading,
since the two classes exhaust the instruction set.

    The existing SlicedInterface uses the latter presentation; its derived permanence is not
an independent premise for removing a field.
Theorem 3.7 (Least instruction toll). (S) Fix the transition and reading, and supply a finite list
covering all states. For each instruction i, set p(i) = 1 if some state has a false-to-true crossing
under i, and p(i) = 0 otherwise. Then A2 holds for a natural schedule c exactly when p(i) ≤ c(i)
for every i. The same equivalence holds for the global finite-trace crossing floor. Consequently p
is the unique pointwise least valid schedule. Coq: toll_exactly_dominates_least_price;
trace_floor_exactly_dominates_least_price;
unique_least_valid_price.
Proof. Search the covering list. A crossing witness forces every valid instruction price to be
at least one. Without a witness, zero meets every demand for that instruction. The resulting
schedule is valid and no larger than any valid schedule. Antisymmetry proves uniqueness. A2
implies the trace floor by the first crossing, while one-step traces give its converse.

                                                  6
    The finite list supplies an effective search; it is not a minimization algorithm for arbitrary
infinite systems.

Theorem 3.8 (The coarsest observational identification). (S) For a total deterministic sys-
tem with specified observation o, let s ∼ t mean o(run(w, s)) = o(run(w, t)) for every fi-
nite word w, including the empty word. This is the unique coarsest observation-preserving,
step-stable equivalence. Any deterministic implementation preserving steps and observa-
tions must separate states that some future observation separates. The coarsest relations
are pointwise equivalent. Coq: future_equivalence_coarsest, coarsest_relation_unique,
encoding_cannot_merge_distinguishable_states.

Proof. Equality for each word gives the equivalence laws. The empty word gives current-output
preservation; prefixing a move gives step stability. Any stable output-preserving relation preserves
outputs along a word by induction, so is contained in ∼. An implementation merging a separated
pair would have to give two answers after the same continuation from one state.

    Uniqueness here is pointwise logical equivalence of relations.

Histories and maps. Give a pointed source machine an access word reaching each state.
A start- and step-preserving map into a pointed target exists exactly when equal source end-
points of any two words imply equal target endpoints. Necessity follows by mapping the
endpoints. For sufficiency, map a source state to the target endpoint of its access word; the
implication makes this independent of all other words reaching it. The empty word and ap-
pending a move give the required laws, and reachability forces pointwise uniqueness. Coq:
unique_factorization_iff_kernel_inclusion.
   If two reachable machines have the same initial output behavior and each is separated by
future observations, this implication follows by appending all continuations and using target
separation. With supplied access words for the first machine, the resulting map preserves outputs
and is onto by target reachability and injective by source separation. It is pointwise unique
(minimal_realizations_uniquely_isomorphic). This is behavioral minimality, not a claim
about smallest infinite cardinality.

Cost interfaces. The definition uses instruction costs c : I → N, so a trace’s cost is inde-
pendent of its initial state. Later source-charge results use a broader interface a : S → N
for one autonomous step, or ce : S × I → N with instructions. Their trace cost is the sum
of charges along the actual run. The same first-crossing proof gives a floor if each crossing
costs at least one, but instruction-priced theorems retain their stated signature and hypothe-
ses. In particular, a single instruction with one fixed price cannot charge one at entry and
zero at idle. The latter construction is the source-charge example paid_entry_free_idle in
minimal/AccountingBridge.v.

Theorem 3.9 (The universal floor). (S) In every one-bit system that obeys the toll, every
one-bit Thiele machine among them, if P (s0 ) = false and P (run(τ, s0 )) = true, then c(τ ) ≥ 1.
Coq: universal_nfi_any_substrate; it is also the two-point instance of the floor over any
order (two_point_nfi).

Proof. Induction on τ . Since P is false at the start and true at the end, some step in τ takes it
from false to true. By A2 that step costs at least 1; every other step costs at least 0.

Theorem 3.10 (Simulating systems). (S) Let a certification system, the base, be simulated by
another, the host: an embedding of base states into host states, a decoding of each base instruction
into a list of host instructions, a commuting step law, and certification reflected by the embedding
(record SimulatingCertificationSystem). A base trace from uncertified to certified costs at


                                                 7
least 1 in the base, the host run of its decoded trace ends certified, and that host run costs at
least 1. Coq: host_represents_simulating_cert_system.

    The commuting law compares endpoints of complete translated instruction blocks. The host’s
cost floor uses its own toll axiom. This theorem alone neither constrains intermediate block
states nor establishes an earned history for arbitrary host programs. Such guarantees require
preservation over the host’s permitted operations and initial states. The checked counterexample
in coq/audit/MacroBoundary.v separates these claims.

Theorem 3.11 (Quantitative form). (S) Extend a certification system with a witness w : S → N
and a threshold k such that each step raises w by at most its cost and every certified state has
w ≥ k (record QuantitativeCertificationSystem). A trace from a state with w = 0 to a
certified state costs at least k. Coq: universal_nfi_quantitative.

Theorem 3.12 (A2 is a restriction). (S) A cost-bearing system without A2
can switch certification on at zero cost, and A2 excludes exactly that. Coq:
honest_cost_tracking_strict_restriction, free_forgery_violates_A2.


3.2     A2 among cost frameworks
Theorem 3.13 (The toll as amortized cost). (S) A step system satisfies A2 if and only if
every step has non-negative amortized cost under the potential that is 1 on uncertified states
and 0 on certified ones; the universal floor is then the potential method’s telescoping bound.
Coq: a2_iff_nonnegative_amortized_cost, nfi_by_potential. Under A2 the number of
certification flips along a trace is at most its cost (flips_le_cost), and A2 together with
the matching upper-bound condition is equivalent to exact pricing of a permanent certificate
(a2_and_aara_iff_exact).

  These are local correspondences. The cost-framework families they touch (graded monads,
amortized resource analysis, cost translations, linear logic) are not embedded here.


3.3     Several floors and one charge
Theorem 3.14 (Joint floors). (S) For a finite family of events, the least cost schedule meet-
ing every unit floor charges one on a step where any event occurs and zero otherwise. Coq:
joint_floor_is_least. On pairs of Booleans, two independent coordinates that both rise in one
step are met by a single charge of 1 (independent_coordinates_joint_change_costs_one).

   Independent state coordinates do not by themselves force additive cost.

Theorem 3.15 (Exact pricing). (S) For trusted local-predicate pricing systems, a
schedule meets the certification floor and never overcharges exactly when it charges
on precisely the certification flips, one unit each, and nothing elsewhere.     Coq:
exact_commitment_pricing_characterization.

Chapter 4

Finite Machines and the Price of
Merging

Fix a state type S with a finite enumeration, instructions I, a step function and a reading P .
The certificate is permanent when no step switches it off. An instruction is injective when its
step function is.

Theorem 4.1 (A permanent flip merges). (S) On a finite state space with a permanent
certificate, an instruction that switches the certificate on at some state is not injective. Coq:
permanent_flip_is_not_injective.

Proof. If the instruction were injective it would map the certified states injectively into the
certified states, so onto them, leaving no certified image for the uncertified state it switches
on.

    This asserts non-injectivity of the instruction, not a collision at the entry destination. For
u∈/ H, f (u) ∈ H, and f (H) ⊆ H with |H| = K, the path u → h1 → · · · → hK → hK delays the
orbit’s collision until the (K + 1)st transition. The entry shares its destination with a source in
H exactly when f (u) ∈ f (H). Surjectivity of f |H is sufficient, not necessary, for this condition
at a particular entry. Instruction pricing charges every use of a non-injective instruction under
the merge-pricing premise, regardless of where its collision occurs.

Theorem 4.2 (A2 from merge pricing). (S) On a finite state space with
a permanent certificate, if every non-injective instruction costs at least 1,
A2 holds.      Coq:   a2_from_merging_price_and_permanence; the trace form is
permanent_certification_trace_floor, and an honest erasure account gives A2 too
(honest_erasure_accounting_implies_a2).

    Each premise is needed. Unbounded history walks past finiteness by paying in fresh states,
a new one every step (unbounded_history_escapes); a revocable certificate keeps nothing
(revocable_certificate_escapes); and an unpriced merge is a price list that skips the merge
(free_merge_escapes). On any state space, a raise that merges nothing sends the certified
states one-to-one into a proper part of themselves, so it needs infinitely many of them.

Theorem 4.3 (The squeeze bound). (S) On a finite state space with a permanent certifi-
cate, with m certified states and k uncertified states that one instruction switches on, the
instruction collapses at least k states (permanent_flips_collapse_at_least). Priced one
unit per halving of the number of distinct states, its cost c satisfies m + k ≤ 2c m, so, when
m ≥ 1, c ≥ log2 ((m + k)/m); in rounded-up logarithms, ⌈log2 (m + k)⌉ ≤ c + ⌈log2 m⌉. Coq:
permanent_flips_compression_bound, permanent_flips_log_bound.



                                                9
    These bounds are tight, and A2 is equivalent to the floor. For every system, A2 holds exactly
when every run from an uncertified state to a certified one costs at least 1 (nec_f_floor_iff_a2).
For every m ≥ 1 and c, a grid machine with m certified states and one move of cost c flips
2c m − m states, so the bound m + k ≤ 2c m of the theorem above is met with equality
(nec_f_squeeze_tight). Its rounded-logarithm form is strictly weaker: m = 3, k = 1 and cost
0 meet the rounded form and fail m + k ≤ 2c m (nec_f_rounded_log_form_weaker).
Theorem 4.4 (Which flips are forced). (S) When instructions have decidable equality, over all

Theorem 4.6 (Merge, or a fresh state every step: the dichotomy). (S) On any state space,

4.1     Entropy and heat
Theorem 4.17 (Entropy after a permanent step). (S) After a permanent step, any distri-
bution on the m certified states and the k flipping ones has Shannon entropy at most log2 m
(permanent_step_entropy_ceiling). From the uniform distribution the step removes at least
log2 ((m + k)/m) bits (permanent_flip_uniform_entropy_drop). An injective step removes
none (step_entropy_invariant_if_injective).

Theorem 4.18 (Heat). (C) Given the named premise landauer_heat, which states Lan-
dauer’s principle for a step and a distribution (Rolf Landauer argued at IBM in 1961 that a
step that cannot be run backwards requires a minimal heat generation; the premise reads this
as mean heat at least kB T ln 2 per bit of entropy the step removes, for the chosen physical
memory and its input distribution, as an average over that preparation rather than a bound
on each trial), the instruction has mean heat, when kB T ≥ 0, at least kB T ln((m + k)/m)
from the uniform distribution (permanent_flip_heat_floor), and, when kB T > 0, posi-
tive heat from any distribution that leaves every certified state and the flipping one possible
(permanent_flip_full_support_heat_positive). For a state known in advance, the premise
is equivalent to heat ≥ 0, so it supplies no positive lower bound; it does not determine actual
device heat, which depends on the physical process and preparation, and the ledger toll still
charges its unit on a known run, since merge pricing prices the instruction whatever input was
prepared (known_state_flip_forces_no_heat).

    The monograph adds, on the page and with no Coq counterpart here, a floor that comes
from the device rather than the premise. By the identity tying Ladyman’s reading of Landauer’s
principle to Sagawa’s, and the second law, a fixed device that merges K inputs produces at
least kB ln K of entropy, at least kB T ln K of dissipated work, on its worst input, however slowly
it runs and whatever anyone knows. So on a known start the premise sets no floor, and the
merging move still has one on its worst input.

Windows, Pricing and Narrowing

Theorem 5.1 (Window adequacy). (S) A query on states can be answered by a function of a win-
dow only if it is constant on the window’s fibers, the sets of states the window shows the same way
(decoding_requires_fiber_constancy). Given a representative for every view, fiber constancy
is equivalent to factoring through a decoder (selected_representatives_give_decoder).

Adequacy for a supplied operational model. Allow a relation of permitted succes-
sors, a chosen state view and required outputs. Supply a representative for each view and a
permitted successor for each instruction and underlying state. A deterministic decoder and
next-view rule exist exactly when equal views give equal current outputs and every permitted
successor under a common instruction has the same view from equal-view sources. This is
operational_adequacy_iff; it does not establish that the supplied relation describes a physi-
cal apparatus, or infer effective choices from existential ones. Observable branching from the
same state and instruction excludes an exact deterministic encoding preserving those outcomes
(branching_answers_exclude_exact_deterministic_encoding).

Selection and finite validation. For the unpointed two-state toggle, ev-
ery Boolean reading invariant under its state-exchange symmetry is constant
(toggle_symmetry_forces_constant_reading).         Distinguished starts or task meanings
are additional structure. For every finite horizon a unit-priced clock with a delayed reset
agrees through that horizon with a permanent reading, while both satisfy the actual toll
and only one is permanent (finite_observations_do_not_establish_permanence). These
results constrain conclusions from bare dynamics and bounded tests; they neither refute
every physical permanence model nor choose a universal physical description. Sources:
coq/audit/ObservationContract.v and coq/audit/IntrinsicBoundary.v.

Theorem 5.2 (Shadow pricing). (S) If a certifying step and a non-certifying step show the
same window before and after, no price computed from the window meets the certification floor
without charging the non-certifying step. Coq: shadow_cannot_price_exactly. A window that
shows the reading prices certification exactly (window_showing_reading_prices_exactly).

Theorem 5.3 (Retained history). (S) Keeping the previous state in a history makes every
deterministic step injective, and the state part of the new pair is still the original step’s result,
so the observed transition is unchanged. The history takes a fresh state every step; it walks past
the finiteness premise, not past what keeping takes. Coq: retained_history_step_injective,
history_simulates_observed_step.

Theorem 5.4 (Narrowing). (S) On a finite machine priced one unit per halving of the
number of distinct states, each halving of the machine’s own set of possible states along a
run is paid for (run_narrowing_priced_log). What an observer knows can shrink at zero

cost: a measurement that copies a hidden bit into a blank display forgets nothing, borrow-
ing the blank display (fresh states), and one admissible merge-priced cost assigns it zero
(observer_narrowing_can_be_free); wiping the display is a merge and costs at least one
(wipe_costs_at_least_one). The WIPE charge is an instruction-level bound over all its pos-
sible sources. On the particular correlated pair produced by copying, the hidden bit can retain
the entire distinction after WIPE. A charge of one is therefore not a claim that this preparation
loses one bit of joint entropy, or dissipates one corresponding heat unit.

Recursion and Undecidability

Definition 6.1 (Substrate). A substrate supplies states, programs, a partial run function, a
ledger that no step decreases, an encoding of programs into states with a decoder, behavioral
equivalence (equal runs from every state), a representability predicate on program transformers,
and the recursion theorem stated and proved by the logician Stephen Kleene in 1938, for
representable transformers, as a field (class Substrate). A shortcut predicate on programs
respects behavioral equivalence and has one accepted and one rejected canonical program (class
WithShortcutPredicate).

Theorem 6.1 (Structural undecidability). (S) In every substrate with a shortcut predicate, no
decider whose flip transformer is representable returns true exactly when the predicate holds.
Coq: structural_shortcut_undecidable.

Proof. Let D be such a decider and f (p) the rejected canonical program if D(p) is true and the
accepted one otherwise. The recursion theorem gives q behaviorally equivalent to f (q). If D(q)
is true, q behaves like the rejected program, so the predicate fails at q; if false, it holds. Either
way D is wrong at q.

   The natural-number substrate meets the recursion premise by construction, since its fixed-
point program copies the transformer’s output (nat_structural_shortcut_undecidable).

Theorem 6.2 (L). (S) For the weak call-by-value lambda calculus L, defined in
coq/kernel/foundation/LRecursion.v from its reduction rules, Kleene’s second recursion
theorem holds (second_recursion), every extensional nontrivial property of L terms is unde-
cidable (L_rice), and L halting is undecidable (L_halting_undecidable). L is the calculus
Yannick Forster and Gert Smolka presented as a model of computation in which computabil-
ity theory can be carried out in Coq (Interactive Theorem Proving, ITP 2017, LNCS 10499).
Yannick Forster, Fabian Kunze, Gert Smolka and Maximilian Wuttke later checked in Coq that
L and Turing machines simulate each other (Interactive Theorem Proving, ITP 2021). The
Turing-completeness of L is theirs and is not proved in this repository.

   The diagonal uses recursion and behavioral equivalence; cost plays no part. Deciding a
predicate over arbitrary programs is a different task from reading one field of a given state.

The Record Axis

Definition 7.1 (Bases and base-driven records). A base is a deterministic record-free machine:
states, a next function, initial states and halting (record BaseMachine). A record-carrying
machine has states, a next function, initial states, a record, a ledger and halting (record RCM).
A cover maps it onto a base step for step, preserving initial states and halting. The extension
is base-driven when its record’s next value depends only on the base state and itself, the
ledger never decreases, A2 holds, the record is permanent, and some reachable step writes it
(HonestBaseExtension).

Theorem 7.1 (The record axis is a latch). (S) Every base-driven record over every base
factors as a latch: there is a base event h such that the base state and the record evolve as
(b, r) 7→ (next(b), r ∨ h(b)). Coq: record_axis_is_latch_holds. Two permanent records factor
as two latches, each reading the other (record_pair_is_two_latches_holds).

Theorem 7.2 (Both premises do work). (R) A record that can switch back off is not a latch
(toggle_not_latch). A record switched on by a hidden clock is permanent and not driven by
the computation (clock_record_permanent, clock_record_not_driven).

Theorem 7.3 (Which machines carry the axis). (S) Every base carries it: the base plus
a latch on any event it reaches, charging one unit when the latch sets, is a base-driven
record (latch_core_honest). A reversible base with unbounded memory carries it reversibly
(history_latch_honest, history_latch_injective). With finite memory, a step that writes
a permanent record is not injective (finite_reversible_cannot_write).


7.1     Growing and probabilistic records
Theorem 7.4 (Growing records as families of latches). (S) A record whose next value
is a function of the base state and its current value, that only grows in a partial
order, whose ledger charges every strict change, and that changes at some reachable
step satisfies a complete family of threshold-latch equations and keeps its price schedule
(growing_record_decomposes_holds). A monotone chain of pairwise-distinct k-bit vectors
has at most k + 1 members (chain_needs_bits_holds).

Theorem 7.5 (Limits of one latch and a fixed schedule). (R) One Boolean latch does not
carry every growing record: a three-value chain over a one-state base is a counterexample
(one_latch_refuted). In a finite branching model a deterministic latch does not handle branch-
ing (deterministic_latch_handles_branching_refuted), and equal branch support and
charges do not determine the branch weights (schedule_determines_probabilities_refuted).

7.3     The record over any order

Theorem 7.6 (Latches, merges and windows on the axis). (S) A record that never drops
