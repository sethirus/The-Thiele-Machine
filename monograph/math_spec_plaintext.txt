Before the specification. I don’t do math by hand. I think in shapes, directions, and
connections, and my part of this work was to see where things had to fit, to direct, and to
refuse what didn’t fit. This document is the translation. Language models wrote the formal
statements and the proofs, and I read the statements to make sure they say what the shape
says. Coq checks the formal counterparts identified below; some on-page arguments have no Coq
counterpart here, as noted where they occur. The arguments that carry the results are in the
monograph, on the page; the formal versions here are their counterparts, and compiling them is
a tool for catching mistakes, not the reason to believe them. So nothing below is in my voice, on
purpose. It’s the reference layer: exact statements, exact premises, and where each one lives. If
you want the pictures, the monograph starts with them. If you want to know exactly what is
claimed, it’s here.
The Thiele Machine is an abstract model of computation, and an argument made
with it about what an account of computation should preserve.
    It is a machine in the sense a Turing machine is one: a mathematical object. No device is
meant. The model is a set of states, moves, a step, a price on each move, a record that can be
compared, and one rule, the toll: a move that takes the record out of the down-set of where it
stood pays at least one. Any system with those parts is a Thiele machine. A yes-or-no reading
is the smallest record, and most of what follows is stated on it. Forget the prices and the record
and what is left is an ordinary deterministic machine; the results below are about what that
forgetting throws away. A small two-counter machine whose commitments are earned is one
example, and a universal machine U is another. They show the definition can be met. They are
not the model.
    The structural question is what a total deterministic process needs to raise a reading and keep
it: a merge along the run or endlessly fresh states. A physical price needs separate hypotheses.
Landauer bounds depend on the physical model and input distribution; finite-time entropy-
production bounds here concern specified thermal stochastic dynamics, not every physical state
change. Certification, a record that a claim was checked and closed, is the sharpest case. It
is one example of a wider question about structural state, observation and cost. A window, a
function from states to what can be seen of them (Chapter 5), can lose distinctions that the
full state keeps, such as the record and the ledger; the cost laws of Chapters 3 and 4 constrain
specified events under hypotheses each theorem names. A conventional encoding can preserve
those distinctions. A window that discards them cannot recover them in general. The stronger
proposal that these distinctions belong in a necessary foundation of computation is a conjecture.
The status of each result is stated below.

                                             Abstract

This is a mathematical reference for the Thiele Machine: a deterministic transition system
with instruction prices and a record that is a position in a preorder, with a toll on every step
that moves the record out of the down-set of where it stood. The one-bit record, a Boolean
reading with its false-to-true crossing, is the two-point instance, and most results are stated on
it. Permanence and an internal ledger are additional conditions used by particular results. It
states the model, its toll and the universal floor, then the results that hold for every machine
meeting it, and gives one small example machine to show the definition can be met. Each result
is stated with its premises and names the declaration that carries it.
    A formal proof shows that conclusions follow from premises. It does not show that premises
model physical reality. Every result is tagged as one of three types: (S) structural theorems
about the model or a stated machine, (C) conditional results that hold under a named premise
no machine fact discharges, and (R) refutations, where a theorem states the negation of a
proposed claim and a named construction supplies the counterexample. One proposal, the
pointer criterion of Chapter 13, has no proof; it carries a fourth tag, thesis.
    The structural results (S), by where they sit in the model:

   • The definition and the price in the move. The floor is the toll over any order of records,
     and the universal floor on the one-bit instance. The least instruction price and the least
     price for the lift. On finite machines, the toll derived from merge pricing for permanent
     certificates. Shadow pricing: no price computed from a window that hides a crossing is
     exact.

   • What stays. A free move cannot touch a permanent reading. The record axis over any base
     is a latch, and a growing record decomposes into threshold latches. The small machine’s
     earned provenance, checker soundness and minimum price.

   • What a window forgets. The coarsest observational identification and the terminal lift.
     The window theorem for every Thiele-complete machine, with a gap of one, and for every
     certified Thiele-complete machine, with a gap of three.

   • The room a kept distinction needs. On any state space a permanent flip merges or needs a
     fresh state every time, with the exact retention bound for a bounded memory, and the
     stochastic and unitary forms of the same count.

   • Computation as the special case. Thiele completeness and certified Thiele completeness;
     the lift of every universal base to a certified Thiele-complete machine and its converse;
     composition; the universal machine U and the priced host’s program UP ; a verified compiler
     onto these machines; the recursion theorem and Rice’s theorem (the logician Henry Rice,
     1953: no program decides a property of what programs do, unless it holds of every program
     or of none) for L, and Rice’s theorem for the two-counter machine itself.

   • The world. The classical CHSH bound; the algebraic Tsirelson bound; and, on the zero-
     marginal slice, the equivalence of column contractivity with positive semidefiniteness of
     one fixed 5 × 5 NPA matrix.

The failure of every clock-style machine, the limits of lifting and of composition, and the necessity
of each clause of Thiele completeness are refutations (R). Results stated over Landauer heat are
conditional (C) on the named premise landauer_heat.

Chapter 1

Introduction

The Thiele Machine describes a computation by what it reads and writes, by a record of what
the computation established, and by a ledger of what establishing it cost, with a rule tying
the last two together: the step that raises the record pays. The chapters below state that rule,
derive its consequences, show which machines carry it, and prove where a window that hides the
record and the ledger loses information.
     Chapter 3 states the model, its toll and the universal floor. Chapter 4 derives the toll on
finite machines from the price of merging states and checks which premises are needed. Chapter 5
states what a window can decide and price. Chapter 6 defines the small machine. Chapter 7
defines Thiele completeness and certified Thiele completeness, states the window theorem for
each, lifts any universal base of any model of computation to a certified Thiele-complete machine,
composes certified Thiele-complete machines, and shows each clause is needed. Chapter 8
states the universal host, the universal machine U, the priced host’s program UP , a verified
compiler onto these machines and the extraction to OCaml. Chapter 9 states the recursion and
undecidability results, for L and for the two-counter machine itself. Chapter 10 states the record
axis over any base and the latch decomposition of growing records. Chapter 11 states the CHSH
and Tsirelson mathematics. Chapter 12 states the physical boundary. Chapter 13 states the
models of external systems and the pointer thesis.

Notation and Conventions

 • N is the natural numbers, B = {false, true}, R the reals.

 • A trace is a finite list of instructions. run(τ, s) folds the step function over τ from s.

 • (R, ≤) is the preorder of records and rec : S → R the record. In Coq it is ax_rec on an
   AxSys.

 • P is the record on the two-point order, a reading from states to B. In Coq it is cs_cert
   on a CertificationSystem.

 • c(i) ∈ N is the cost of instruction i; c(τ ) is the sum over a trace.

 • µ is the ledger. Where a state carries it, conservation means µ(step(s, i)) = µ(s) + c(i).

 • A window is a function from states to observations.

Chapter 3

Thiele Machines and the Universal
Floor

Definition 3.1 (Thiele machine; AxSys in Coq). A Thiele machine is a tuple (S, I, step, c, rec)
with step : S × I → S, c : I → N and rec : S → R, where (R, ≤) is a preorder given by a
Boolean test. The record is a position in that order. A step from s by i leaves the down-set
when rec(step(s, i)) ̸≤ rec(s). The one rule is the toll, first named No Free Insight:
                        A2:   ∀s i, rec(step(s, i)) ̸≤ rec(s) =⇒ c(i) ≥ 1.
Coq: the record AxSys in coq/kernel/foundation/AxCore.v, with the toll as ax_a2. The
order carries no further assumption: not antisymmetry, not growth of the record along steps,
not finiteness. Neither permanence nor an internal ledger is part of the definition; the results
that use them say so.

Computation as the special case. Forget c and rec and what remains is (S, I, step), a
deterministic transition system. Every such system is a Thiele machine: give it a constant record
and price zero on every instruction. No step leaves the down-set, so A2 holds. This is immediate
from the definition and has no separate Coq counterpart. The theorems of this reference are
about what that forgetting throws away.

The one-bit instance. Take R = B with false < true (two_pre). The record is a reading
P : S → B, leaving the down-set is a false-to-true crossing, and A2 reads
                     ∀s i, P (s) = false ∧ P (step(s, i)) = true =⇒ c(i) ≥ 1.
Coq: the record CertificationSystem, with this instance of A2 as its field cs_cert_costs;
cs_axsys and axsys_cs translate each way. The name of that record does not restrict the
interpretation of P : it is an arbitrary Boolean reading, and neither permanence nor the occurrence
of both values is required. Most results below are stated on this instance, and each says so by
writing P .
Theorem 3.1 (The floor is the toll). (S) For every Thiele machine, every run whose fi-
nal record is not at or below its starting record costs at least 1 exactly when A2 holds
(ax_floor_iff_a2), and under A2 every run costs at least the number of its steps that leave the
down-set (ax_cost_ge_exits). When the record never drops along a step, leaving the down-set
is a strict rise (ax_exit_is_strict_rise).
Proof. A one-step run is a step, so the floor gives the toll. For the converse, induct on the run.
If the first step stays in the down-set, the rest of the run ends outside the down-set of its own
start, by transitivity of the order, and the induction hypothesis pays. If the first step leaves the
down-set, A2 pays. Counting exits is the same induction.

                                                 5
Theorem 3.2 (Thresholds are one-bit machines). (S) For every point a of R, reading the
record only as the bit “a ≤ rec(s)” gives a one-bit Thiele machine, and it obeys its toll when-
ever the original does (ax_view_a2); the original obeys A2 exactly when every such threshold
does (ax_a2_iff_views). When the order is antisymmetric, the thresholds determine the
record (bp_thresholds_determine). The one-bit floor of Theorem 3.9 is the two-point in-
stance (two_point_nfi), and the four clauses of certified Thiele completeness in Chapter 7
are, one for one, the clauses of the same definition stated on the axis at the two-point order
(ax_tc_two_point_iff).

Sliced interface. On the one-bit instance, a sliced Thiele machine additionally has a surjection
π : S → B and a partition I = IH ⊔ IV . Write Fi (s) = step(s, i). For every i ∈ IH there is
hi : B → B with

                     π(Fi (s)) = hi (π(s)),     P (Fi (s)) = P (s),    c(i) = 0.

For every v ∈ IV , require π(Fv (s)) = π(s) and P (s) ≤ P (Fv (s)), ordering false below true. A2
still applies. These equations hold on all states of the stated domain; they imply permanence.
They do not require a product state space or a universal base.
     For a non-certification instance, use S = Z4 × {0, 1, 2}, π(b, k) = b and P (b, k) = [k = 2]. The
horizontal instruction R(b, k) = (b + 1 mod 4, k) costs zero; the vertical instruction V (b, k) =
(b, min(k + 1, 2)) costs one. Projection and reading preservation for R are immediate; V preserves
the projection, never lowers the reading, and pays one on its crossing from k = 1 to k = 2. It
also costs one at k = 2.

Structural generalization. For any deterministic base (B, J, d) and event E : B → B, put S =
B×B×N. Computing sends (b, r, l) to (d(b, j), r, l) at price zero; a mark sends it to (b, r∨E(b), l+1)
at price one. Projection to B has the section b 7→ (b, false, 0) and commutes with every computing
run. An event witness supplies a same-view pair with different readings. This preserves base
transitions, not a base’s previous price or reading. Coq: coq/audit/StructuralBridge.v; its
specialization to the repository’s tape model is coq/audit/TuringStructuralBridge.v. No
effectivity is assumed by this structural theorem. Ordinary encodings can retain the added
coordinates when effective presentations are supplied. A Boolean valuation need not itself be an
autonomous two-state quotient: coq/audit/ValuationBoundary.v gives a counterexample.


3.1     Free moves, least prices and the observable lift
Theorem 3.3 (All readings and free moves). (S) For a state type with decidable
equality, fixed transitions and natural instruction prices, A2 holds for every Boolean
reading if and only if every zero-cost instruction is the identity on all states. Coq:
every_reading_toll_iff_free_moves_are_identity.

Proof. If a free move changes s to t, the decidable reading [x = t] is false at s and true at t,
contradicting A2. Conversely an identity move cannot raise any reading, and every positive
natural price is at least one.

   This distinguishes a chosen priced reading from arbitrary state predicates; it does not select
an autonomous base projection or a partition of moves.

Theorem 3.4 (Free reading preservation). (S) Under A2 and global permanence, every
zero-cost instruction preserves the reading. With the other sliced-interface laws fixed, global
permanence can replace horizontal reading preservation and vertical monotonicity. Coq:
free_reading_invariance.


                                                  6
Proof. A free move cannot raise the reading by A2 and cannot lower it by permanence. Conversely,
horizontal preservation and vertical monotonicity ensure that no instruction lowers the reading,
since the two classes exhaust the instruction set.

    The existing SlicedInterface uses the latter presentation; its derived permanence is not
an independent premise for removing a field.

Theorem 3.5 (Least instruction toll). (S) Fix the transition and reading, and supply a finite list
covering all states. For each instruction i, set p(i) = 1 if some state has a false-to-true crossing
under i, and p(i) = 0 otherwise. Then A2 holds for a natural schedule c exactly when p(i) ≤ c(i)
for every i. The same equivalence holds for the global finite-trace crossing floor. Consequently p
is the unique pointwise least valid schedule. Coq: toll_exactly_dominates_least_price;
trace_floor_exactly_dominates_least_price;
unique_least_valid_price.

Proof. Search the covering list. A crossing witness forces every valid instruction price to be
at least one. Without a witness, zero meets every demand for that instruction. The resulting
schedule is valid and no larger than any valid schedule. Antisymmetry proves uniqueness. A2
implies the trace floor by the first crossing, while one-step traces give its converse.

    The finite list supplies an effective search; it is not a minimization algorithm for arbitrary
infinite systems.

Theorem 3.6 (The coarsest observational identification). (S) For a total deterministic sys-
tem with specified observation o, let s ∼ t mean o(run(w, s)) = o(run(w, t)) for every fi-
nite word w, including the empty word. This is the unique coarsest observation-preserving,
step-stable equivalence. Any deterministic implementation preserving steps and observa-
tions must separate states that some future observation separates. The coarsest relations
are pointwise equivalent. Coq: future_equivalence_coarsest, coarsest_relation_unique,
encoding_cannot_merge_distinguishable_states.

Proof. Equality for each word gives the equivalence laws. The empty word gives current-output
preservation; prefixing a move gives step stability. Any stable output-preserving relation preserves
outputs along a word by induction, so is contained in ∼. An implementation merging a separated
pair would have to give two answers after the same continuation from one state.

   Uniqueness here is pointwise logical equivalence of relations.

The comparison class. Fix a deterministic base (B, J, d), mark labels K, and events E :
K × B → B. An implementation has arbitrary state type X, computing and mark operations,
view q : X → B, reading r : X → B and ledger µ : X → N. Computing projects to d and costs
zero. Marks fix q, cost one and satisfy the exact latch equation r(Vk x) = r(x) ∨ E(k, q(x)).
Require A2, global permanence and exact accounting µ(Fi x) = µ(x) + c(i). An onto view is not
required here. The product lift has states (b, r, l), computing rule (d(b, j), r, l) and mark rule
(b, r ∨ E(k, b), l + 1). It satisfies every law of this class directly.

Theorem 3.7 (Terminal lift and reachable minimal realization). (S) Every implementation in
this class has a unique coordinate-preserving map to the product lift, namely x 7→ (q(x), r(x), µ(x)).
It commutes with all steps and runs. Its kernel is precisely future observational equivalence for
observation (q, r, µ). From a start with reading false and ledger zero, it maps onto all reachable
product states from the corresponding start. If reachable implementation states are separated by
future observations, this map is injective there, and is the unique start- and step-preserving
map on reachable states. Equality of current triples is equivalent to equality of all future obser-
vations. Coq: canonical_satisfies_contract, lift_terminal_over_exact_coordinates,


                                                  7
projection_preserves_runs,     observable_equivalence_exactly_projection_kernel,
reachable_minimal_realization.

Proof. The coordinates force the map. For computing steps the view follows d, the free-reading
theorem preserves r, and accounting preserves µ. For marks the exact latch and accounting
laws give the product mark rule. Induction gives the run equation. Equal projected triples
have equal future triples by deterministic product evolution; conversely the empty word already
distinguishes unequal triples. Run any word reaching a product state on the implementation to
obtain a preimage. Behavioral separation then turns equal projected triples into equal reachable
states. Any map preserving start and steps is forced on an endpoint by a word reaching it.

    This is a bijection on reachable states, not a choice-based construction of an inverse function
or an effective minimization algorithm. Observing only (q, r) defines a different equivalence from
observing the ledger too.

Histories and the direction of the map. Finite instruction lists, with start [] and steps
appending one instruction, have exactly one start- and step-preserving evaluation into any
pointed machine: the empty list fixes the start and appending fixes each next value. For
the preceding implementation, evaluation followed by projection equals evaluation in the lift
(trace_extension_factorization). Thus histories map to implementations, and implemen-
tations map to the lift. The reverse arrow need not exist. Take a Boolean base toggled by a
free move and a mark event always true. Add to the lift a counter increased only by free moves.
All contract laws still hold. Two free moves return every lift state to itself, while increasing
the extra counter by two. No map from the lift can commute with those free moves, even on
reachable states. Coq: no_map_even_on_reachable_states.
    More generally, give a pointed source machine an access word reaching each state. A
start- and step-preserving map into a pointed target exists exactly when equal source end-
points of any two words imply equal target endpoints. Necessity follows by mapping the
endpoints. For sufficiency, map a source state to the target endpoint of its access word; the
implication makes this independent of all other words reaching it. The empty word and ap-
pending a move give the required laws, and reachability forces pointwise uniqueness. Coq:
unique_factorization_iff_kernel_inclusion.
    If two reachable machines have the same initial output behavior and each is separated by
future observations, this implication follows by appending all continuations and using target
separation. With supplied access words for the first machine, the resulting map preserves outputs
and is onto by target reachability and injective by source separation. It is pointwise unique
(minimal_realizations_uniquely_isomorphic). This is behavioral minimality, not a claim
about smallest infinite cardinality.

Theorem 3.8 (Least unit schedule for the lift). (S) If every mark event has a base-state
witness where it holds, the lift’s zero/one schedule is pointwise least among all natural instruction
schedules satisfying A2 for its fixed dynamics and reading, over arbitrary base types. Coq:
canonical_unit_price_is_least_when_events_possible.

Proof. Computing preserves the reading, so zero suffices. For each mark take a witness base
state with reading false; the mark raises it and forces price at least one. The existing zero/one
schedule satisfies A2, so is least.

   Witnesses need not be reachable from a selected start. An identically false mark event
permits price zero under A2; unit accounting then adds a demand. These characterizations fix
the base, events, observations and price unit. They determine the realization relative to that
contract.



                                                 8
Cost interfaces. The definition uses instruction costs c : I → N, so a trace’s cost is inde-
pendent of its initial state. Later source-charge results use a broader interface a : S → N
for one autonomous step, or ce : S × I → N with instructions. Their trace cost is the sum
of charges along the actual run. The same first-crossing proof gives a floor if each crossing
costs at least one, but instruction-priced theorems retain their stated signature and hypothe-
ses. In particular, a single instruction with one fixed price cannot charge one at entry and
zero at idle. The latter construction is the source-charge example paid_entry_free_idle in
minimal/AccountingBridge.v.

Theorem 3.9 (The universal floor). (S) In every one-bit Thiele machine, if P (s0 ) = false
and P (run(τ, s0 )) = true, then c(τ ) ≥ 1. Coq: universal_nfi_any_substrate; it is also the
two-point instance of the floor over any order (two_point_nfi).

Proof. Induction on τ . Since P is false at the start and true at the end, some step in τ takes it
from false to true. By A2 that step costs at least 1; every other step costs at least 0.

Theorem 3.10 (Simulating systems). (S) Let a base Thiele machine be simulated by a host:
an embedding of base states into host states, a decoding of each base instruction into a list of
host instructions, a commuting step law, and certification reflected by the embedding (record
SimulatingCertificationSystem). A base trace from uncertified to certified costs at least 1 in
the base, the host run of its decoded trace ends certified, and that host run costs at least 1. Coq:
host_represents_simulating_cert_system.

    The commuting law compares endpoints of complete translated instruction blocks. The host’s
cost floor uses its own toll axiom. This theorem alone neither constrains intermediate block
states nor establishes an earned history for arbitrary host programs. Such guarantees require
preservation over the host’s permitted operations and initial states. The checked counterexamples
in coq/audit/MacroBoundary.v and coq/audit/PaidBypassBoundary.v separate these claims;
the latter extends the host with a hypothetical instruction, not an instruction of the original
host.

Theorem 3.11 (Quantitative form). (S) Extend a Thiele machine with a witness w : S → N
and a threshold k such that each step raises w by at most its cost and every certified state has
w ≥ k (record QuantitativeCertificationSystem). A trace from a state with w = 0 to a
certified state costs at least k. Coq: universal_nfi_quantitative.

Theorem 3.12 (A2 is a restriction). (S) A cost-bearing system without A2
can switch certification on at zero cost, and A2 excludes exactly that. Coq:
honest_cost_tracking_strict_restriction, free_forgery_violates_A2.


3.2     A2 among cost frameworks
Theorem 3.13 (The toll as amortized cost). (S) A step system satisfies A2 if and only if
every step has non-negative amortized cost under the potential that is 1 on uncertified states
and 0 on certified ones; the universal floor is then the potential method’s telescoping bound.
Coq: a2_iff_nonnegative_amortized_cost, nfi_by_potential. Under A2 the number of
certification flips along a trace is at most its cost (flips_le_cost), and A2 together with
the matching upper-bound condition is equivalent to exact pricing of a permanent certificate
(a2_and_aara_iff_exact).

  These are local correspondences. The cost-framework families they touch (graded monads,
amortized resource analysis, cost translations, linear logic) are not embedded here.

3.3    Several floors and one charge
Theorem 3.14 (Joint floors). (S) For a finite family of events, the least cost schedule meet-
ing every unit floor charges one on a step where any event occurs and zero otherwise. Coq:
joint_floor_is_least. On pairs of Booleans, two independent coordinates that both rise in one
step are met by a single charge of 1 (independent_coordinates_joint_change_costs_one).

   Independent state coordinates do not by themselves force additive cost.

Theorem 3.15 (Exact pricing). (S) For trusted local-predicate pricing systems, a
schedule meets the certification floor and never overcharges exactly when it charges
on precisely the certification flips, one unit each, and nothing elsewhere.     Coq:
exact_commitment_pricing_characterization.

Chapter 4

Finite Machines and the Price of
Merging

Fix a state type S with a finite enumeration, instructions I, a step function and a reading P .
The certificate is permanent when no step switches it off. An instruction is injective when its
step function is.

Theorem 4.1 (A permanent flip merges). (S) On a finite state space with a permanent
certificate, an instruction that switches the certificate on at some state is not injective. Coq:
permanent_flip_is_not_injective.

Proof. If the instruction were injective it would map the certified states injectively into the
certified states, so onto them, leaving no certified image for the uncertified state it switches

    This asserts non-injectivity of the instruction, not a collision at the entry destination. For
u∈/ H, f (u) ∈ H, and f (H) ⊆ H with |H| = K, the path u → h1 → · · · → hK → hK delays the

at a particular entry. Instruction pricing charges every use of a non-injective instruction under
the merge-pricing premise, regardless of where its collision occurs.

Theorem 4.6 (Merge, or a fresh state every step: the dichotomy). (S) On any state space,

Theorem 4.17 (Entropy after a permanent step). (S) After a permanent step, any distri-

(permanent_step_entropy_ceiling). From the uniform distribution the step removes at least

Theorem 4.18 (Heat). (C) Given the named premise landauer_heat, which states Lan-
dauer’s principle for a step and a distribution (Rolf Landauer argued at IBM in 1961 that a
step that cannot be run backwards requires a minimal heat generation; the premise reads this
as mean heat at least kB T ln 2 per bit of entropy the step removes, for the chosen physical
memory and its input distribution, as an average over that preparation rather than a bound
on each trial), the instruction has mean heat, when kB T ≥ 0, at least kB T ln((m + k)/m)
from the uniform distribution (permanent_flip_heat_floor), and, when kB T > 0, posi-
tive heat from any distribution that leaves every certified state and the flipping one possible
(permanent_flip_full_support_heat_positive). For a state known in advance, the premise
is equivalent to heat ≥ 0, so it supplies no positive lower bound; it does not determine actual
device heat, which depends on the physical process and preparation, and the ledger toll still
charges its unit on a known run, since merge pricing prices the instruction whatever input was
prepared (known_state_flip_forces_no_heat).

    The monograph adds, on the page and with no Coq counterpart here, a floor that comes
from the device rather than the premise. By the identity tying Ladyman’s reading of Landauer’s
principle to Sagawa’s, and the second law, a fixed device that merges K inputs produces at
least kB ln K of entropy, at least kB T ln K of dissipated work, on its worst input, however slowly
it runs and whatever anyone knows. So on a known start the premise sets no floor, and the
merging move still has one on its worst input.

Theorem 5.1 (Window adequacy). (S) A query on states can be answered by a function of a win-
dow only if it is constant on the window’s fibers, the sets of states the window shows the same way
(decoding_requires_fiber_constancy). Given a representative for every view, fiber constancy
is equivalent to factoring through a decoder (selected_representatives_give_decoder).

Adequacy for a supplied operational model. Allow a relation of permitted succes-
sors, a chosen state view and required outputs. Supply a representative for each view and a
permitted successor for each instruction and underlying state. A deterministic decoder and
next-view rule exist exactly when equal views give equal current outputs and every permitted
successor under a common instruction has the same view from equal-view sources. This is
operational_adequacy_iff; it does not establish that the supplied relation describes a physi-
cal apparatus, or infer effective choices from existential ones. Observable branching from the
same state and instruction excludes an exact deterministic encoding preserving those outcomes
(branching_answers_exclude_exact_deterministic_encoding).

Selection and finite validation. For the unpointed two-state toggle, ev-
ery Boolean reading invariant under its state-exchange symmetry is constant
(toggle_symmetry_forces_constant_reading).         Distinguished starts or task meanings
are additional structure. For every finite horizon a unit-priced clock with a delayed reset
agrees through that horizon with a permanent reading, while both satisfy the actual toll
and only one is permanent (finite_observations_do_not_establish_permanence). These
results constrain conclusions from bare dynamics and bounded tests; they neither refute
every physical permanence model nor choose a universal physical description. Sources:
coq/audit/ObservationContract.v and coq/audit/IntrinsicBoundary.v.

shows the reading prices certification exactly (window_showing_reading_prices_exactly).

Theorem 5.4 (Narrowing). (S) On a finite machine priced one unit per halving of the
number of distinct states, each halving of the machine’s own set of possible states along a
run is paid for (run_narrowing_priced_log). What an observer knows can shrink at zero

This chapter builds one machine that meets the definition, to show it can be met. Nothing in the
rest of the model depends on its details. The small machine is defined in minimal/EarnedCore.v
on the Coq standard library alone.
Definition 6.1 (State). A core has two counters a, b ∈ N, their versions va , vb ∈ N, a program
counter pc ∈ N whose lines are numbered from 1 (a value with no instruction, 0 included, is a
stop), a table of facts, a commitment channel holding at most one fact, and a trap latch. A fact
is a triple (property, counter, version); the properties are “is zero”, “is even” and “is at least
n”, each with a checker eval and a meaning holds tied by eval_iff. A state is a core with
a ledger µ ∈ N and a flag P ∈ B. The table holds at most 16 facts. The start state for (a, b)
initializes those counter values without executing a write; both versions are zero, the line is 1,
the table and channel are empty, the trap and flag are down, and µ = 0.
Definition 6.2 (Instructions and costs). INC c adds one to counter c. DEC c j subtracts one and
jumps to j if c > 0, and otherwise falls through. HALT stops. CHECK p c evaluates p on counter c
and, if it holds and the table has room, writes the fact (p, c, vc ). COMMIT p c succeeds only if
the fact (p, c, vc ) is in the table, and then sets the channel to it. CERTIFY succeeds only if the
channel names a fact; success preserves the channel and advances the line. Every failure sets the
trap latch, after which no instruction changes anything but the ledger. Every write to a counter
adds one to its version and preserves the table and channel. CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY
cost 1; the others cost 0. The ledger adds the cost of every executed instruction, and the flag
rises exactly on a passing CERTIFY.
Theorem 6.1 (Universality). (S) The INC/DEC fragment is the two-counter machine step
for step, and a program halts on the small machine exactly when it halts as a two-counter
program. Coq: simulation_run, halting_correspondence. Its halting problem is undecidable,
by reduction from the vendored two-counter result (earned_core_halting_undecidable). The
Turing-universality of two-counter machines is a theorem of Marvin Minsky, who studied how bare
a machine can be and still compute what a Turing machine computes (Annals of Mathematics
74, 1961; Computation: Finite and Infinite Machines, Prentice-Hall, 1967). It is cited and not
re-proved here; the undecidability of two-counter halting used above is checked in Coq in the
vendored Coq Library of Undecidability Proofs of Yannick Forster, Dominique Larchey-Wendling
and coauthors.
Theorem 6.2 (The toll). (S) The ledger is the exact sum of executed costs
(mu_conservation_trace); only CERTIFY raises the flag (only_certify_certifies); the
flag never drops (cert_permanent); A2 holds (a2); and a run from an uncertified state to a
certified one pays at least 1 (nfi_floor).

Definition 7.1 (Machine). A machine is a state type, a move type, a step, a cost per move and
a record P : S → B (record machine). It is the one-bit instance of the definition in Chapter 3.
   The toll and a universal base alone are a low bar: a two-counter machine that
charges 1 for every move has a universal base and obeys the toll for any record
(clock_weakly_thiele_complete).
Definition 7.2 (Thiele complete). A machine is Thiele-complete when it can be read through a
universal base (as in clause (a) below, without clean starts), a split of its moves into computing
moves and marks, and a ledger, such that: computing moves never change the record and the
record once up stays up; every loaded start has the record down; computing moves cost 0, every
other move costs exactly 1, and the ledger grows by the cost of each move; and some run ends
with the record up from one loaded start and down from another. Nothing in it says what the
record is about. Coq: neutral_complete in minimal/NeutralComplete.v.
Theorem 7.1 (Thiele-complete machines). (S) Every Thiele-complete machine obeys the toll
and raises its record only by a mark costing exactly 1 (nc_is_weak, nc_raise_is_mark); every
run from a state with the record down that ends with it up costs at least 1 (nc_floor_one); its
window hides its record and its ledger, with a gap of one (nc_hides_record, nc_hides_ledger).
A clock and a machine whose record never rises are not Thiele-complete (clock_not_nc,
silent_not_nc), and each clause is needed (drop_not_nc, frozen_not_nc, paid_all_not_nc).
Every universal base plus marks on an event that separates two loaded starts is Thiele-complete
(every_base_lifts_neutrally, nlift_neutral_complete). A machine is certified Thiele-
complete through an interface exactly when it is Thiele-complete through the same interface read
without its claims and its loaded starts are clean, its record is earned and its non-vacuity clause
holds (tc_iff_nc_plus_certification); the inclusion is strict (nc_strictly_weaker).
Definition 7.3 (Certified Thiele complete). A machine is certified Thiele-complete when its
moves can be read as base moves and three record moves, CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY of
a claim, over a type of claims with a meaning on states, a checker, a notion of the thing a claim
is about being unchanged, a set of clean starts and a ledger, such that:
(a) Universal base. Every two-counter instruction has a base move that, from every live state,
    acts on a window of the state exactly as the instruction acts on a two-counter configuration
    and leaves the state live; every two-counter start has a clean live state showing it (its loaded
    start), base moves never change the record, and the record once up stays up.
(b) Earned record. A clean start has the record down. Every run from a clean start that ends
    with the record up contains a passing CHECK of some claim, then a COMMIT of the same
    claim with the thing it is about unchanged in between, then the CERTIFY that raises the
    record. A passing check means its claim holds, and a claim that holds keeps holding while
    the thing it is about is unchanged.

                                                20
(c) Exact prices. Base moves cost 0, each record move costs exactly 1, and the ledger grows by
    the cost of each move.

(d) Non-vacuity. Some claim is true at some loaded start and false at another, and the bare
    chain CHECK, COMMIT, CERTIFY on it raises the record from a loaded start exactly
    when the claim holds there.

Coq: thiele_complete in minimal/ThieleComplete.v.

   The claim language is fixed first and the definition is taken over it; the interface may not
name the meanings, since checker soundness would then hold for nothing.

The Universal Machine

Definition 8.1 (Universal Thiele machine). Fix a class C of Thiele machines, each with its
runs from given start states. A universal Thiele machine for C is a Thiele machine H, the host,
with one program P fixed for the whole class, a way to load any guest M in C with any start s0
into a start state of H, and a way to read a guest’s state off the host’s registers, such that:
(1) H is Thiele-complete, and its computing moves are its counter moves;
(2) the run of P from the loaded state halts exactly when M ’s run from s0 stops, and then
    shows the observable part of M ’s last state, as the class names it, its reading kept as a latch,
    and its ledger up to the surcharge;
(3) the run passes, in order, through host states that show the observable part of M ’s states
    along its run, at which the host’s record equals M ’s reading kept as a latch and the host’s
    ledger equals M ’s ledger plus a surcharge, never negative and bounded by a number fixed
    for the class;
(4) the rise is the program’s: from every untrapped state, a record instruction selected by P
    either traps, or else leaves the flag up exactly when it was already up or the current line is
    marked as raising by P ;
(5) H is a register host: its counter moves are INC, DEC and HALT on registers, its record
    moves leave the registers alone and go to the next line or trap, and which move comes next
    depends only on the line and whether the trap is up;
(6) each guest is loaded with the record down, no trap, a fixed line and the guest written into
    the registers, and the guest’s state is read off the registers in a coding fixed with the class,
    before any guest is named.
A certified universal Thiele machine also has its host certified Thiele-complete with clean loads,
and its record, whenever it is up, standing on the host’s own check of the guest’s own justification,
as the class supplies it.
    U and UP are certified universal Thiele machines, by the theorems of this chapter put
together. For U the surcharge is 0, the guest’s justification is its chain claim, and the observable
part is the guest’s two counters; in order U matches everything but the guest’s version numbers.
For UP the surcharge is at most 2 when the guest’s reading starts at no and exactly 3 when it
starts at yes, the guest’s justification is its reading, and the observable part is the guest’s whole
state. UP ’s program, read on a register host with no claims and no checker, is UN ; it meets
the uncertified definition with the same surcharge (UN_neutral_universal). Surcharge 0 for
every guest whose reading starts at no, across a universal class, is reached on that host by UP ’s
compiler with one TICK in place of the three-act chain at the first yes; the monograph argues it,
and it isn’t formalised.

Recursion and Undecidability

Definition 9.1 (Substrate). A substrate supplies states, programs, a partial run function, a
ledger that no step decreases, an encoding of programs into states with a decoder, behavioral
equivalence (equal runs from every state), a representability predicate on program transformers,
and the recursion theorem stated and proved by the logician Stephen Kleene in 1938, for
representable transformers, as a field (class Substrate). A shortcut predicate on programs
respects behavioral equivalence and has one accepted and one rejected canonical program (class
WithShortcutPredicate).

Theorem 9.1 (Structural undecidability). (S) In every substrate with a shortcut predicate, no
decider whose flip transformer is representable returns true exactly when the predicate holds.
Coq: structural_shortcut_undecidable.

Proof. Let D be such a decider and f (p) the rejected canonical program if D(p) is true and the
accepted one otherwise. The recursion theorem gives q behaviorally equivalent to f (q). If D(q)
is true, q behaves like the rejected program, so the predicate fails at q; if false, it holds. Either
way D is wrong at q.

   The natural-number substrate meets the recursion premise by construction, since its fixed-
point program copies the transformer’s output (nat_structural_shortcut_undecidable).

Theorem 9.2 (L). (S) For the weak call-by-value lambda calculus L, defined in
coq/kernel/foundation/LRecursion.v from its reduction rules, Kleene’s second recursion
theorem holds (second_recursion), every extensional nontrivial property of L terms is un-
decidable (L_rice), and L halting is undecidable (L_halting_undecidable). L is the calcu-
lus Yannick Forster and Gert Smolka presented as a model of computation in which com-
putability theory can be carried out in Coq (Interactive Theorem Proving, ITP 2017, LNCS
10499). Yannick Forster, Fabian Kunze, Gert Smolka and Maximilian Wuttke later checked
in Coq that L and Turing machines simulate each other (Interactive Theorem Proving, ITP
2021). The Turing-completeness of L is not re-proved in this repository’s own files: it
comes from the models-equivalence theorem of the vendored Coq Library of Undecidability
Proofs (vendor/coq-undecidability/theories/Synthetic/Models_Equivalent.v), which is
checked with this build.

Theorem 9.3 (The complement of two-counter halting). (S) The complement of
two-counter halting is undecidable, composed from the vendored library’s reductions
(MM2_HALTING_compl_undec).

   The diagonal uses recursion and behavioral equivalence; cost plays no part. Deciding a
predicate over arbitrary programs is a different task from reading one field of a given state.

The Record Axis

Definition 10.1 (Bases and base-driven records). A base is a deterministic record-free machine:
states, a next function, initial states and halting (record BaseMachine). A record-carrying
machine has states, a next function, initial states, a record, a ledger and halting (record RCM).
A cover maps it onto a base step for step, preserving initial states and halting. The extension
is base-driven when its record’s next value depends only on the base state and itself, the
ledger never decreases, A2 holds, the record is permanent, and some reachable step writes it
(HonestBaseExtension).

Theorem 10.1 (The record axis is a latch). (S) Every base-driven record over every base
factors as a latch: there is a base event h such that the base state and the record evolve as
(b, r) 7→ (next(b), r ∨ h(b)). Coq: record_axis_is_latch_holds. Two permanent records factor
as two latches, each reading the other (record_pair_is_two_latches_holds).

Theorem 10.2 (Both premises do work). (R) A record that can switch back off is not a latch
(toggle_not_latch). A record switched on by a hidden clock is permanent and not driven by
the computation (clock_record_permanent, clock_record_not_driven).

Theorem 10.3 (Which machines carry the axis). (S) Every base carries it: the base plus
a latch on any event it reaches, charging one unit when the latch sets, is a base-driven
record (latch_core_honest). A reversible base with unbounded memory carries it reversibly
(history_latch_honest, history_latch_injective). With finite memory, a step that writes
a permanent record is not injective (finite_reversible_cannot_write).


10.1     Growing and probabilistic records
Theorem 10.4 (Growing records as families of latches). (S) A record whose next

step satisfies a complete family of threshold-latch equations and keeps its price schedule

has at most k + 1 members (chain_needs_bits_holds).

Theorem 10.5 (Limits of one latch and a fixed schedule). (R) One Boolean latch does not

Theorem 11.2 (Algebraic Tsirelson bound). (S) A rational correlator satisfying √    the

13.1     The pointer criterion
The pointer-observable criterion proposes that the event a step rule is forced to price is
singled out by redundant record proliferation across observers. Twelve candidate events
over seven observer maps proliferate as stated (twelve_candidate_measurements_checked).
The MAC-labelled model does not proliferate (mac_model_not_proliferating); apply-
ing that to a real system depends on the observer model.               A two-observer tog-
gle game with consensus, authenticity and coordinator-free updates revokes its event
(toggle_game_refutes_strong_pointer_necessity), so those properties do not force per-
manence.     Durable views do imply permanence, by making persistence a premise
(durable_consensus_implies_permanence). The external consequence survey contains five
narrow countermodels, each for a point a governing source already addresses, and five stronger
