Before the specification. I don’t do math by hand. I think in shapes, directions, and
connections, and my part of this work was to see where things had to fit, to direct, and to refuse
what didn’t fit. This document is the translation. Language models wrote the formal statements
and the proofs, Coq checked every one of them, and I read the statements to make sure they say
what the shape says. So nothing below is in my voice, on purpose. It’s the reference layer: exact
statements, exact premises, and where each one lives. If you want the pictures, the monograph
starts with them. If you want to know exactly what is claimed, it’s here.
The Thiele Machine is an abstract model of computation, and an argument made
with it about what an account of computation should preserve.
   It is a machine in the sense a Turing machine is one: a mathematical object, not a device.
The model is the abstract CertificationSystem record and the Substrate interface. The small
machine of minimal/EarnedCore.v, a two-counter machine whose commitments are earned, is
one instance; the universal machine U built on it is another. They are witnesses, not the model.
   Certification is one concrete witness of a broader investigation of structural state, observation,
and cost. Selected windows lose distinctions that the richer state can express; cost laws
constrain specified events under explicit hypotheses. A conventional encoding can preserve
those distinctions. A window that discards them cannot recover them in general. The stronger
proposal that these distinctions belong in a necessary foundation of computation is a conjecture.
The status of each result is stated below.

                                           Abstract

This is a mathematical reference for the Thiele Machine: a model of computation in which
a step that raises a certification record must pay into a ledger that only grows. It presents
the abstract interfaces, the main theorems, and the definitions of the small machine from the
machine-checked Coq corpus. The Coq source is the complete definition; this document states
each result with its premises and names the declaration that carries it.
    Coq establishes that conclusions follow from premises. It does not establish that premises
model physical reality. Every result is tagged as one of three types: (S) structural theorems
about the model or a stated machine, (C) conditional results that hold under a named premise
no machine fact discharges, and (R) refutations, where a theorem states the negation of a
proposed claim and a named construction supplies the counterexample.
    The core claims are structural (S): No Free Insight for every certification system; on finite
machines, the toll derived from merge pricing for permanent certificates; the window and pricing
results; the small machine’s earned provenance, checker soundness and minimum price; Thiele
completeness of the small machine and the failure of every clock-style machine; the window
theorem for every Thiele-complete machine; the universal machine U; the record axis over any
base; the recursion theorem and Rice’s theorem for L; the classical CHSH bound; the algebraic
Tsirelson bound; and, on the zero-marginal slice, the equivalence of column contractivity with
positive semidefiniteness of one fixed 5 × 5 NPA matrix. Results stated over Landauer heat are
conditional (C) on the named premise landauer_heat.

Chapter 1

Introduction

A computation is usually described by what it reads and writes. The Thiele Machine adds a
record of what the computation established and a ledger of what establishing it cost, and a rule
tying the two together: the step that raises the record pays. The chapters below state that rule,
derive its consequences, show which machines carry it, and prove where a window that hides the
record and the ledger loses information.
    Chapter 3 states the model and No Free Insight. Chapter 4 derives the toll on finite machines
from the price of merging states. Chapter 5 states what a window can decide and price. Chapter 6
defines the small machine. Chapter 7 defines Thiele completeness and states the window theorem.
Chapter 8 states the universal host and the universal machine U. Chapter 9 states the recursion
and undecidability results. Chapter 10 states the record axis over any base. Chapter 11 states
the CHSH and Tsirelson mathematics. Chapter 12 states the physical boundary. Chapter 13
states the models of external systems and the pointer conjecture.

Chapter 2

Notation and Conventions

 • N is the natural numbers, B = {false, true}, R the reals.

 • A trace is a finite list of instructions. run(τ, s) folds the step function over τ from s.

 • P is the certification reading, a function from states to B. In Coq it is cs_cert on a
   CertificationSystem.

 • c(i) ∈ N is the cost of instruction i; c(τ ) is the sum over a trace.

 • µ is the ledger. Where a state carries it, conservation means µ(step(s, i)) = µ(s) + c(i).

 • A window is a function from states to observations.

Chapter 3

Certification Systems and No Free
Insight

Definition 3.1 (Certification system). A certification system is a tuple (S, I, step, c, P ) with
step : S × I → S, c : I → N, P : S → B, satisfying
                  A2:   ∀s i, P (s) = false ∧ P (step(s, i)) = true =⇒ c(i) ≥ 1.
Coq: the record CertificationSystem, with A2 as its field cs_cert_costs.
Theorem 3.1 (No Free Insight). (S) In every certification system, if P (s0 ) = false and
P (run(τ, s0 )) = true, then c(τ ) ≥ 1. Coq: universal_nfi_any_substrate.
Proof. Induction on τ . Since P is false at the start and true at the end, some step in τ takes it
from false to true. By A2 that step costs at least 1; every other step costs at least 0.

Theorem 3.2 (Simulating systems). (S) Let a base certification system be simulated by a host:
an embedding of base states into host states, a decoding of each base instruction into a list of
host instructions, a commuting step law, and certification reflected by the embedding (record
SimulatingCertificationSystem). A base trace from uncertified to certified costs at least 1 in
the base, the host run of its decoded trace ends certified, and that host run costs at least 1. Coq:
host_represents_simulating_cert_system.
Theorem 3.3 (Quantitative form). (S) Extend a certification system with a witness w : S → N
and a threshold k such that each step raises w by at most its cost and every certified state has
w ≥ k (record QuantitativeCertificationSystem). A trace from a state with w = 0 to a
certified state costs at least k. Coq: universal_nfi_quantitative.
Theorem 3.4 (A2 is a restriction). (S) A cost-bearing system without A2 can switch certification
on for free, and A2 excludes exactly that. Coq: honest_cost_tracking_strict_restriction,
free_forgery_violates_A2.


3.1     A2 among cost frameworks
Theorem 3.5. (S) A step system satisfies A2 if and only if every step has non-negative amortized
cost under the potential that is 1 on uncertified states and 0 on certified ones; No Free Insight
is then the potential method’s telescoping bound. Coq: a2_iff_nonnegative_amortized_cost,
nfi_by_potential. Under A2 the number of certification flips along a trace is at most its cost
(flips_le_cost), and A2 together with the matching upper-bound condition is equivalent to
exact pricing of a permanent certificate (a2_and_aara_iff_exact).
  These are local correspondences, not embeddings of the cited cost-framework families (graded
monads, amortized resource analysis, cost translations, linear logic).

3.2    Several floors and one charge
Theorem 3.6 (Joint floors). (S) For a finite family of events, the least cost schedule meet-
ing every unit floor charges one on a step where any event occurs and zero otherwise. Coq:
joint_floor_is_least. On pairs of Booleans, two independent coordinates that both rise in one
step are met by a single charge of 1 (independent_coordinates_joint_change_costs_one).

   Independent state coordinates do not by themselves force additive cost.

Theorem 3.7 (Exact pricing). (S) For trusted local-predicate pricing systems, a schedule
that charges exactly one on the steps its predicate marks meets the certification floor and
never overcharges exactly when its predicate marks precisely the certification flips. Coq:
exact_commitment_pricing_characterization.

Chapter 4

Finite Machines and the Price of
Merging

Fix a state type S with a finite enumeration, instructions I, a step function and a reading P .
The certificate is permanent when no step switches it off. An instruction is injective when its
step function is.

Theorem 4.1 (A permanent flip merges). (S) On a finite state space with a permanent
certificate, an instruction that switches the certificate on at some state is not injective. Coq:
permanent_flip_is_not_injective.

Proof. If the instruction were injective it would map the certified states injectively into the
certified states, so onto them, leaving no certified image for the uncertified state it switches
on.

Theorem 4.2 (A2 from merge pricing). (S) On a finite state space with
a permanent certificate, if every non-injective instruction costs at least 1,
A2 holds.      Coq:   a2_from_merging_price_and_permanence; the trace form is
permanent_certification_trace_floor, and an honest erasure account gives the same bill
(honest_erasure_accounting_implies_a2).

   Each premise is needed. Unbounded history escapes (unbounded_history_escapes), a re-
vocable certificate escapes (revocable_certificate_escapes), and an unpriced merge escapes
(free_merge_escapes).

Theorem 4.3 (The size of the bill). (S) With m certified states and k uncerti-
fied states that one instruction switches on, the instruction collapses at least k states
(permanent_flips_collapse_at_least). Priced one unit per halving of the number of dis-
tinct states, it costs at least log2 ((m + k)/m). Coq: permanent_flips_log_bound.

Theorem 4.4 (Which flips are forced). (S) Over all schedules that price merges, an instruction
is forced to have positive price if and only if it merges two states (forced_priced_iff_merges).
An instruction that switches a reading on either merges or switches it off somewhere else
(flip_merges_or_revokes). So every permanent-record write is forced, and the only free flips
are revocable ones.

Theorem 4.5 (Refutation). (R) “The forced events are exactly the permanent-record
writes” is false: a three-state instruction flips, revokes, and merges at once. Coq:
forced_price_without_permanent_record.

4.1    Entropy and heat
Theorem 4.6. (S) After a permanent step, any distribution on the m certified states and the k
flipping ones has Shannon entropy at most log2 m (permanent_step_entropy_ceiling).
From the uniform distribution the step removes at least log2 ((m + k)/m) bits
(permanent_flip_uniform_entropy_drop).            An injective step removes none
(step_entropy_invariant_if_injective).

Theorem 4.7 (Heat). (C) Given the named premise landauer_heat, which states Lan-
dauer’s principle for a step and a distribution, the flip dissipates at least kB T ln((m +
k)/m) from the uniform distribution (permanent_flip_heat_floor), and positive heat
from any distribution that leaves every certified state and the flipping one possible
(permanent_flip_full_support_heat_positive). For a state known in advance, the premise
is equivalent to heat ≥ 0, so it forces no heat (known_state_flip_forces_no_heat).


4.2    An eight-state instance
Example 4.1. Four program slots and a certification flag make eight states. An ad-
vance step is injective (fnext_injective); the certify step and every jump merge states
(fcertify_merges). The cost rule prices exactly the merges, so A2 holds there as a theo-
rem (fin_a2_from_merging_price).


4.3    No intrinsic joule value
Theorem 4.8 (Refutation). (R) For every state type and natural-number ledger, two real-
valued scales agree on the ledger and differ in energy; the ledger fixes no energy unit. Coq:
mu_has_no_intrinsic_joule_value. Choosing the scale kB T ln 2 gives one ledger unit that
energy by definition (calibrated_mu_landauer_energy); the scale is a premise, not a measure-
ment.

Chapter 5

Windows, Pricing and Narrowing

Theorem 5.1 (Window adequacy). (S) A query on states can be answered by a function of a win-
dow only if it is constant on the window’s fibers, the sets of states the window shows the same way
(decoding_requires_fiber_constancy). Given a representative for every view, fiber constancy
is equivalent to factoring through a decoder (selected_representatives_give_decoder).

Theorem 5.2 (Shadow pricing). (S) If a certifying step and a non-certifying step show the
same window before and after, no price computed from the window meets the certification floor
without charging the non-certifying step. Coq: shadow_cannot_price_exactly. A window that
shows the reading prices certification exactly (window_showing_reading_prices_exactly).

Theorem 5.3 (Retained history). (S) Keeping the previous state in a history makes
every deterministic step injective without changing its observed transition. Coq:
retained_history_step_injective.

Theorem 5.4 (Narrowing). (S) On a finite machine that prices merges, each halving of the
machine’s own set of possible states along a run is paid for (run_narrowing_priced_log).
What an observer knows can shrink for free: a measurement that copies a hidden bit
into a blank display forgets nothing, and one admissible merge-priced cost assigns it zero
(observer_narrowing_can_be_free); wiping the display is a merge and costs at least one
(wipe_costs_at_least_one).

Chapter 6

The Small Machine

The small machine is defined in minimal/EarnedCore.v on the Coq standard library alone.

Definition 6.1 (State). A core has two counters a, b ∈ N, their versions va , vb ∈ N, a program
counter pc ≥ 1, a table of facts, a commitment channel holding at most one fact, and a trap
latch. A fact is a triple (property, counter, version); the properties are “is zero”, “is even” and
“is at least n”, each with a checker eval and a meaning holds tied by eval_iff. A state is a
core with a ledger µ ∈ N and a flag P ∈ B. The table holds at most 16 facts. The start state for
(a, b) has no facts, an empty channel, the flag down and µ = 0.

Definition 6.2 (Instructions and costs). INC c adds one to counter c. DEC c j subtracts one
and jumps to j if c > 0, and otherwise falls through. HALT stops. CHECK p c evaluates p on
counter c and, if it holds and the table has room, writes the fact (p, c, vc ). COMMIT p c succeeds
only if the fact (p, c, vc ) is in the table, and then sets the channel to it. CERTIFY succeeds only if
the channel names a fact. Every failure sets the trap latch, after which no instruction changes
anything but the ledger. Every write to a counter adds one to its version. CHECK, COMMIT
and CERTIFY cost 1; the others cost 0. The ledger adds the cost of every executed instruction,
and the flag rises exactly on a passing CERTIFY.

Theorem 6.1 (Universality). (S) The INC/DEC fragment is the two-counter machine step
for step, and a program halts on the small machine exactly when it halts as a two-counter
program. Coq: simulation_run, halting_correspondence. Its halting problem is undecidable,
by reduction from the vendored two-counter result (earned_core_halting_undecidable). The
Turing-universality of two-counter machines is Minsky’s theorem, cited.

Theorem 6.2 (The toll). (S) The ledger is the exact sum of executed costs
(mu_conservation_trace); only CERTIFY raises the flag (only_certify_certifies); the
flag never drops (cert_permanent); A2 holds (a2); and a run from an uncertified state to a
certified one pays at least 1 (nfi_floor).

Theorem 6.3 (Earned provenance). (S) From a clean start, every certified run contains a
passing CHECK of some claim, then a passing COMMIT of the same claim with that counter
untouched in between, then CERTIFY. Coq: earned_certification_provenance.

Theorem 6.4 (Checker soundness and no forging). (S) On a run from a clean start, a fact
whose counter has not been written since its check names a true claim about that counter
(checker_soundness), and every fact in the table was written by a passing CHECK of exactly
that claim (no_forging).

Theorem 6.5 (Price). (S) From a clean start a certified run costs at least 3
(certified_run_min_cost), and a three-instruction program pays exactly 3 (min_cost_tight).

Theorem 6.6 (A check that can fail). (S) The program CHECK “a is zero”, COMMIT,
CERTIFY certifies from a start with a = 0, paying exactly 3, and from any other start
traps with the flag down, for every number of steps. Coq: earned_run_check_can_fail,
earned_run_refused_forever.

Theorem 6.7 (Links). (S) The small machine is a certification system with the trace floor
(earned_core_floor), and as a record-carrying machine its record moves as a latch on one base
event (earned_core_is_latch).

Remark 6.1. minimal/EarnedGeneric.v states the same machine over any property
language with an exact checker; sorted lists are one instance (sorted_cs_sound).
minimal/EarnedMulti.v has a counter for every natural number and is the host machine
of Chapter 8.

Chapter 7

Thiele Completeness

Definition 7.1 (Machine, weak completeness). A machine is a state type, a move type, a step,
a cost per move and a record P : S → B (record machine). It is weakly Thiele-complete when it
is a certification system whose base is universal.
   The weak notion is nearly empty: a two-counter machine that charges 1 for every move
meets it for any record (clock_weakly_thiele_complete).
Definition 7.2 (Thiele complete). A machine is Thiele-complete when its moves can be read
as base moves and three record moves, CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY of a claim, over a
type of claims with a meaning on states, a checker, a notion of the thing a claim is about being
unchanged, a set of clean starts and a ledger, such that:
(a) Universal base. Every two-counter instruction has a base move that acts on a window of the
    state exactly as the instruction acts on a two-counter configuration, every two-counter start
    has a clean live state showing it, base moves never change the record, and the record once
    up stays up.

(b) Earned record. A clean start has the record down. Every run from a clean start that ends
    with the record up contains a passing CHECK of some claim, then a COMMIT of the same
    claim with the thing it is about unchanged in between, then the CERTIFY that raises the
    record. A passing check means its claim holds, and a claim that holds keeps holding while
    the thing it is about is unchanged.

(c) Exact toll. Base moves cost 0, each record move costs exactly 1, and the ledger grows by the
    cost of each move.

(d) Non-vacuity. Some claim is true at some clean start and false at another, and the bare chain
    CHECK, COMMIT, CERTIFY on it raises the record from a clean start exactly when the
    claim holds there.
Coq: thiele_complete in minimal/ThieleComplete.v.
Theorem 7.1 (Consequences). (S) In every Thiele-complete machine: the ledger
counts the record moves (ledger_counts_record_moves);        a certificate costs at
least 3 (certificate_costs_three); a committed claim holds when committed
(committed_claim_holds); some check fails (check_can_fail); some run certifies
(some_run_certifies); the move raising the record from a clean start is a CERTIFY
(only_certify_raises); and some move costs 0 (complete_has_free_move).
Theorem 7.2 (Machines that meet it). (S) The small machine is Thiele-
complete (earned_core_thiele_complete), and so are the machine over any
property language with an exact checker and a property true of one value and

                                               11
false of another (earned_generic_thiele_complete)             and    the   sorted-list   machine
(sorted_machine_thiele_complete).

Theorem 7.3 (Machines that fail it). (R) Every clock-style machine, with any record, is
weakly Thiele-complete and not Thiele-complete (clock_not_thiele_complete); so is a clock
whose record is a latch on its configuration (latch_clock_not_thiele_complete). A machine
with a free base and one paid move that raises a latch meets clauses (a) and (c) and fails (b)
(one_move_record_excluded, paid_latch_not_thiele_complete). A machine whose record
never rises meets (a), (b) and (c) and fails (d) (silent_not_thiele_complete).


7.1    The window theorem
Theorem 7.4 (Every Thiele-complete machine hides its record). (S) For every Thiele-complete
machine there are a clean start and two runs from it, the second made of base moves only,
that end at the same base window; the first ends certified with its ledger at least 3 above the
start, and the second ends uncertified with its ledger unchanged (complete_two_runs). Hence
no function of the base window recovers the record (complete_hides_record) or the ledger
(complete_hides_ledger) on every reachable state (thiele_complete_hides).

Proof. Clause (d) and clause (b) give a clean start and a certifying run. The base is universal,
so base moves alone reach the window the certifying run ends at; base moves change neither the
record nor the ledger.

    Every Thiele-complete machine is a certification system with the trace floor, and its window
is blind to its reading (thiele_complete_floor, complete_cs_window_blind).

The Universal Machine

8.1     The universal host
Theorem 8.1. (S) The host of minimal/UniversalThiele.v runs any small-machine pro-
gram as a guest. Its mirror of the guest’s flag always equals that flag (record_agreement).
The mirror rises only in a guest step that passed CERTIFY, charged in that same host step
(no_free_host_certification_step, toll_enforced_by_host_step). The host is a certifi-
cation system with the trace floor (host_nfi).

    A machine from elsewhere enters through the two-counter encoding; its computation and the
toll are kept, its own price list is not.


8.2     U
U is one fixed program of the machine of minimal/EarnedMulti.v, with the single property
of minimal/UniversalCodes.v, made of the small machine’s own instruction kinds and with
no guest step built into anything (coq/kernel/foundation/UniversalLayout.v). For a small-
machine program P and a start (x, y), gm is the guest after m steps and hn is U after n steps
from the loaded state.

Theorem 8.2 (Simulation). (S) For every m there is n ≥ m with hn related to gm by the
simulation relation, or both machines have stopped. Coq: U_simulation.

Theorem 8.3 (Halting and output). (S) The guest halts if and only if U halts
(universal_halting); at halting, U’s first two registers hold the guest’s counters, and the
trap latches, ledgers and flags are equal (universal_output).

Theorem 8.4 (The flag is earned again). (S) The guest’s flag ever rises if and only if U’s flag
ever rises (universal_flag_iff). A raised flag of U stands on U’s own passing CHECK, its
own COMMIT of the same claim with the slot untouched, and the CERTIFY that raised it; when
checked, the slot held the guest’s claim and value, and the guest’s own run contains its chain on
the same claim (universal_earned).

Theorem 8.5 (The ledger). (S) At matching points U’s ledger equals the guest’s, and every ledger
of U lies between the guest’s ledger at some m and at m + 1. Coq: universal_ledger_exact.

Theorem 8.6. (S) The machine U runs on, read with INC and DEC as its base and CHECK,
COMMIT and CERTIFY as its record moves, is Thiele-complete (universal_thiele_complete).
Its halting problem is undecidable (interp_halting_undecidable).

Recursion and Undecidability

Definition 9.1 (Substrate). A substrate supplies states, programs, a partial run function, a
ledger that no step decreases, an encoding of programs into states with a decoder, behavioral
equivalence (equal runs from every state), a representability predicate on program transformers,
and Kleene’s recursion theorem for representable transformers as a field (class Substrate). A
shortcut predicate on programs respects behavioral equivalence and has one accepted and one
rejected canonical program (class WithShortcutPredicate).

Theorem 9.1 (Structural undecidability). (S) In every substrate with a shortcut predicate, no
decider whose flip transformer is representable returns true exactly when the predicate holds.
Coq: structural_shortcut_undecidable.

Proof. Let D be such a decider and f (p) the rejected canonical program if D(p) is true and the
accepted one otherwise. The recursion theorem gives q behaviorally equivalent to f (q). If D(q)
is true, q behaves like the rejected program, so the predicate fails at q; if false, it holds. Either
way D is wrong at q.

   The natural-number substrate meets the recursion premise by construction, since its fixed-
point program copies the transformer’s output (nat_structural_shortcut_undecidable).

Theorem 9.2 (L). (S) For the weak call-by-value lambda calculus L, defined in
coq/kernel/foundation/LRecursion.v from its reduction rules, Kleene’s second recursion the-
orem holds (second_recursion), every extensional nontrivial property of L terms is undecidable
(L_rice), and L halting is undecidable (L_halting_undecidable). The Turing-completeness of
L is cited from Forster and Smolka, not re-proved.

Theorem 9.3. (S) The complement of two-counter halting is undecidable, composed from the
vendored library’s reductions (MM2_HALTING_compl_undec).

   The diagonal uses recursion and behavioral equivalence; cost plays no part. Deciding a
predicate over arbitrary programs is a different task from reading one field of a given state.

The Record Axis

Definition 10.1 (Bases and honest extensions). A base is a deterministic record-free machine:
states, a next function, initial states and halting (record BaseMachine). A record-carrying
machine has states, a next function, initial states, a record, a ledger and halting (record
RCM). A cover maps it onto a base step for step, preserving initial states and halting. The
extension is honest when its record’s next value depends only on the base state and itself, the
ledger never decreases, A2 holds, the record is permanent, and some reachable step writes it
(HonestBaseExtension).

Theorem 10.1 (The record axis is a latch). (S) Every honest extension of every base factors
as a latch: there is a base event h such that the base state and the record evolve as (b, r) 7→
(next(b), r ∨ h(b)). Coq: record_axis_is_latch_holds. Two permanent records factor as two
latches, each reading the other (record_pair_is_two_latches_holds).

Theorem 10.2 (Both premises do work). (R) A record that can switch back off is not a latch
(toggle_not_latch). A record switched on by a hidden clock is permanent and not driven by
the computation (clock_record_not_driven).

Theorem 10.3 (Which machines carry the axis). (S) Every base carries it: the base plus
a latch on any event it reaches, charging one unit when the latch sets, is an honest exten-
sion (latch_core_honest). A reversible base with unbounded memory carries it reversibly
(history_latch_honest). With finite memory, a step that writes a permanent record is not
injective (finite_reversible_cannot_write).


10.1     Growing and probabilistic records
Theorem 10.4. (S) A growing record in a partial order satisfies a complete family of threshold-
latch equations and keeps its price schedule (growing_record_decomposes_holds). A monotone
chain of pairwise-distinct k-bit vectors has at most k + 1 members (chain_needs_bits_holds).

Theorem 10.5. (R) One Boolean latch does not carry every growing record: a
three-value chain over a one-state base is a counterexample (one_latch_refuted).
In a finite branching model a deterministic latch does not handle branching
(deterministic_latch_handles_branching_refuted), and equal branch support and charges
do not determine the branch weights (schedule_determines_probabilities_refuted).

Chapter 11

CHSH and Tsirelson

The results in this chapter are mathematics about correlators and trial counts. No machine is
fixed.

Theorem 11.1 (Classical bound). (S) A factorizable correlator has |S| ≤ 2
(factorizable_CHSH_classical_bound); trial counts consistent with one deterministic local
strategy cannot exceed the classical bound (chsh_stat_violation_not_local).

Theorem 11.2 (Algebraic Tsirelson bound). (S) A rational correlator satisfying √    the
absolute-value bounds and four selected 3 × 3 polynomial minors has |S| ≤ 2 2
(algebraically_coherent_tsirelson_general). The bound also follows from row constraints
and Cauchy–Schwarz (tsirelson_from_row_bounds) and is reached (tsirelson_tight). No
Hilbert space is used.

Theorem 11.3 (The biconditional). (S) On the zero-marginal slice, the column-contractivity
conditions on E00 , E01 , E10 , E11 hold if and only if the zero-marginal 5 × 5 NPA moment matrix
is positive semidefinite. Coq: column_contractive_iff_npa_psd.

Theorem 11.4 (The integer check). (S) If the integer column check accepts eight trial
counts, the zero-marginal NPA matrix built from their correlators is positive semidefinite
(column_contractive_check_witness_npa_psd), and a PSD matrix of that form satisfies the
Tsirelson bound (npa_psd_implies_tsirelson_bound). The check is sound in this direction
only; a rejection is not proved to mean the conditions fail.

Theorem 11.5 (Elliptope). (S) The CHSH correlator quantum set is an existential completion
of the zero-marginal NPA matrix; points of it satisfy the Tsirelson bound (elliptope_tsirelson),
and the PR box is outside it (pr_box_not_elliptope). An integer membership check is sound
into realizability (elliptope_check_full_sound).

   No theorem here identifies a correlator with a physical experiment or proves that any device
produces CHSH-violating statistics.

The Physical Boundary

Theorem 12.1 (Calorimeter). (S) In a two-state calorimeter protocol, a canonical reset from
half-excited to ground satisfies a discrete master equation exactly, and at fixed Hamiltonian the
bath receives ∆/2 (canonical_reset_heat_exact).

Theorem 12.2 (Refutation). (R) Population dynamics do not fix heat: two gaps with identical
dynamics release different heat (master_equation_does_not_fix_heat_scale).

   Ruling a gap out needs a thermal premise such as local detailed balance, or device evidence.
Converting the ledger to energy requires thermal and device premises; the ledger has no intrinsic
energy unit (Chapter 4).

External Systems and the Pointer
Conjecture

Each model below is narrow. None is a full security, consensus, database or audit-log theorem.

   • RFC 9162. The iterative inclusion and consistency checks of the specification re-
     ject the boundary cases and accept the specification’s examples under symbolic digests
     (rfc9162_inclusion_boundary_safe, rfc9162_consistency_boundary_safe). Colli-
     sion resistance and signed-tree-head authenticity are cryptographic premises, not proved.

   • Proof-carrying code. A toy checker accepts exactly when the address-bound verification
     condition holds (pcc_checker_accepts_iff_vc). It mirrors the roles of Necula’s system;
     it is not that system.

   • TPM quotes. A bare sign/verify interface does not imply quote authenticity
     (tpm_interface_authenticity_refuted).

   • Casper FFG. Two finalized blocks on conflicting branches imply a slashable quorum
     (accountable_safety). This is the known accountable-safety theorem.

    The addressed-RAM and reversible-update files prove only their named fragments. The
cost-semantics correspondences are not full cost-framework embeddings.


13.1     The pointer criterion
The pointer-observable criterion proposes that the event a step rule is forced to price is
singled out by redundant record proliferation across observers. Twelve candidate events
over six observer maps proliferate as stated (twelve_candidate_measurements_checked).
The MAC-labelled model does not proliferate (mac_model_not_proliferating); apply-
ing that to a real system depends on the observer model.                  A two-observer tog-
gle game with consensus, authenticity and coordinator-free updates revokes its event
(toggle_game_refutes_strong_pointer_necessity), so those properties do not force per-
manence.      Durable views do imply permanence, by making persistence a premise
(durable_consensus_implies_permanence). The external consequence survey contains five
narrow statements already known from their governing specifications and five stronger statements
without enough protocol or hardware semantics; no surveyed consequence is simultaneously
record-axis dependent, novel, and ready for use. The pointer criterion is a conjecture.
