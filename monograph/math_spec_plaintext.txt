Before the specification. I don’t do math by hand. I think in shapes, directions, and
connections, and my part of this work was to see where things had to fit, to direct, and to refuse
what didn’t fit. This document is the translation. Language models wrote the formal statements
and the proofs, Coq checked every one of them, and I read the statements to make sure they say
what the shape says. So nothing below is in my voice, on purpose. It’s the reference layer: exact
statements, exact premises, and where each one lives. If you want the pictures, the monograph
starts with them. If you want to know exactly what is claimed, it’s here.
The Thiele Machine is an abstract model of computation, and an argument made
with it about what an account of computation should preserve.
    It is a machine in the sense a Turing machine is one: a mathematical object, not a device. The
51-opcode VM summarized in the chapters below is one build of that model; its complete checked
definition is the Coq source. It is not the model. The model is the abstract CertificationSystem
and Substrate interfaces; the VM is one instance of each. The VM prices commitments and
does not check that they were earned. A second, minimal machine, minimal/EarnedCore.v, is
a two-counter machine whose commitments are earned (Remark 4.3).
    Certification is one concrete witness of a broader investigation of structural state, observation,
and cost. The abstract laws, theorems, and executable realizations belong to that investigation.
Selected projections lose distinctions that the richer state can express; cost laws constrain
specified events under explicit hypotheses.
    A conventional encoding can preserve those distinctions. A projection that discards them
cannot recover them in general. The stronger proposal that these distinctions belong in a
necessary foundation of computation is a conjecture. Neither a physical cost metric nor infinitely
many independent geometric dimensions follows from the existence of many observables. The
status of each result is stated below.

                                             Abstract

This is a mathematical reference for the Thiele Machine: a formal model of computation
where structural information carries an explicit, monotone cost µ that starts at zero and never
decreases. It presents the abstract interfaces, the main theorems, and selected semantics from
the machine-checked Coq corpus. The complete 51-opcode operational definition is the Coq
source; this document alone does not enumerate enough semantics to reconstruct it.
    Coq establishes that conclusions follow from axioms. It does not establish that axioms model
physical reality. That distinction is kept explicit throughout. Every result is tagged as one of three
types: (S) structural theorems about the machine as a mathematical object, (C) conditional
derivations where a physics conclusion follows from a stated axiom, and (R) consistency relations
that check internal coherence but are not independent predictions.
    The core claims are structural (S): No Free Insight; µ-conservation; µ-hierarchy; µ-ledger
necessity (five-way independence classification); categorical separation; strict Turing subsump-
tion of instruction
         √           sets; the classical CHSH bound (|S| ≤ 2); the algebraic Tsirelson bound
(|S| ≤ 2 2); and, on the zero-marginal slice, the equivalence of column contractivity with
positive semidefiniteness of one fixed 5 × 5 NPA matrix, with no project-local axioms column_
contractive_iff_npa_psd. The CHSH algebraic result is pure math. The Landauer connection
is motivation. Geometry: the discrete angle-defect identity under its incidence assumptions and
the additional restriction B = 3χ, as statements about partition graphs in general; on states the
VM reaches, module regions are disjoint and these statements hold only in a degenerate form
(Remark 19.3).
    The document also covers, with explicit epistemological tags, conditional derivations under
named physical axioms (Born rule under linearity, no-cloning, unitarity) and reference explo-
rations (4D simplicial-complex differential geometry, self-reference tower). These are marked (C)
or (R) and do not form part of the primary claim ledger. Two named trust boundaries discussed
here are: bsc_kami_compilation_trusted (Layer 2 Verilog semantics gap) and A_QM (physical
quantum axiom).

Chapter 1

Introduction

The Thiele Machine makes the cost of structure explicit. Ordinary step-counting assigns one
time unit per transition; it does not specify this structural-cost schedule. The Thiele Machine
assigns a specific cost µ to every operation that extracts or asserts structural information. The
chapters below lay out the consequences.
    The central claim, proven formally: specified certification and structural-entitlement events
have the cost floors proved under their stated hypotheses. Alongside that core, the specification
records conditional results in which explicitly supplied premises (linearity, an information-
conservation inequality, a second-law record field) yield statements with the shape of results
in quantum mechanics and thermodynamics. In most of them µ enters as a named cost field,
and the supplied premises do the work. They are marked (C): conditional on those premises,
not derived from the ledger alone. Each result’s status, whether a structural theorem (S), a
conditional derivation (C), or a consistency relation (R), is marked directly.
    The document is organized as:

  1. Foundations: State space, instruction set, operational semantics.

  2. Core Theorems: No Free Insight, µ-ledger necessity, µ-Chaitin, µ-conservation.

  3. Computation: Strict Turing subsumption of instruction sets, oracle pricing as instantia-
     tion of universal NFI, µ-complexity classes, µ-cost hierarchy.

  4. Symmetry & Conservation: Gauge shifts of µ, a Noether-style invariance, receipts.

  5. Quantum / CHSH algebra: Tsirelson bound (algebraic), CHSH classical bound,
     biconditional column-contractive ⇐⇒ PSD of the zero-marginal NPA matrix, three-tier
     observation taxonomy. Pure polynomial arithmetic, zero physics axioms.

  6. Conditional physics: Born rule (under linearity), unitarity, no-cloning, purification,
     Landauer (with second-law assumed as a record field), affine EFE family. Each is conditional
     on stated axioms in its own file.

  7. Discrete Geometry: Restricted angle-defect identity with B = 3χ (not classical Gauss-
     Bonnet); 4D simplicial complex and Einstein-tensor definitions (reference exploration only,
     vacuum case is a 0 = 0 consistency check).

  8. Meta-Theory: Three-layer Coq/Python/RTL observable contract, categorical structure.

  9. Predictions: Falsifiable predictions tied to specific Coq-verifiable claims.

1.1     Epistemological Framework
Formal verification (Coq) establishes that conclusions follow logically from axioms. It does not
establish that axioms model physical reality. This distinction is critical for evaluating the physics
claims in Parts V–VIII.
    Every result in this specification falls into one of three categories:

(S) Structural Theorems about the model and the kernel as mathematical objects. Their con-
     clusions follow from the definitions, explicit hypotheses, and reported library assumptions.
     Examples: µ-conservation, gauge invariance, strict Turing subsumption of instruction
     sets, and, for the calculus L, confluence of value reduction (star_value_confluent) and
     halting undecidability (L_halting_undecidable).

(C) Conditional Theorems of the form “if axiom X holds, then physics result Y follows.” The
    logical inference is verified; whether axiom X holds in nature is an empirical question.
    Examples: Born rule uniqueness (conditional on linearity), complex necessity (conditional
    on 2D amplitudes), unitarity (conditional on information conservation).

(R) Consistency Algebraic identities or definitional equivalences that verify internal coherence
    but do not constitute independent predictions. Example: the Tsirelson cost definition.

    Each major theorem in Parts V–VIII is annotated with its category. A theorem marked (C)
or (R) is not diminished. Conditional derivations and consistency checks are valuable scientific
tools. But the reader should not mistake them for derivations from first principles without stated
assumptions. See Appendix B for the complete classification table.

Chapter 2

Notation and Conventions

This chapter exists because several symbols in this document are overloaded, and at least one
(S) is used well before it is defined. Read it once; it resolves ambiguities that would otherwise
require guessing from context.


2.1     Overloaded symbols
S, four distinct meanings. Disambiguated by context and, where ambiguity would be real,
     by explicit qualification:

        1. S (calligraphic): the state space.
        2. S (roman, in operational-semantics chapters): a particular VM state, an element of S.
        3. S(n) (applied to a natural number): the successor function on N, i.e. S(n) = n+1. This
           is Coq’s S constructor, and it is the reason cost expressions such as flen × 8 + S(cost)
           are bounded below by 1: S(n) ≥ 1 for every n ∈ N. This is the notation used
           from Chapter 4 onward, before its first formal appearance in the cost
           tables.
        4. S (in quantum chapters): the CHSH statistic, defined in Section 18.1. Real-valued.

G, three distinct meanings. All three occur, and two occur inside the same chapter on
     emergent geometry:

        1. G: the partition graph component of a VM state.
        2. G: Newton’s constant, fixed to 1/(8π) as a unit choice.
        3. Gµν : the Einstein tensor.

      Where the geometry chapter needs the partition graph it is written Gpart .

µ, ledger and index, and two different number systems. 1. µ: the cost ledger, a com-
          ponent of VM state. In the machine, µ ranges over N: it is a natural number, it is
          monotone non-decreasing under the step relation, and A2 gives it an integer floor.
        2. µ, ν as tensor indices in the geometry chapter (gµν , Tµν ). Unrelated to the ledger.
        3. µ in the quantum chapters (measurement cost, the no-cloning inequality, the µ/I
           ratio) is real-valued. This is a genuinely different object from the machine’s µ: there
           is no theorem in this development identifying them, and no coercion between them is
           claimed. Where the distinction matters the real-valued quantity is written µR .

Chapter 3

The State Space

3.1     Primitives
Let N = {0, 1, 2, . . . }. Let B = {0, 1}. Let Σ be a finite alphabet.


3.2     Regions and Modules
Definition 3.1 (Region). A Region R is a finite subset of N, canonically represented as a
deduplicated list preserving first-occurrence order: norm(L) = nodup(L) removes duplicate
elements while preserving the order of their first appearance. (The Python VM applies a stricter
canonical form via sorted(set(region)); the Coq specification uses nodup Nat.eq_dec which
does not sort.)

Definition 3.2 (Module). A Module M = (RM , AM , TM ) consists of a normalized region
RM ⊂ N, a set of axioms AM ⊂ Σ∗ , and a per-module 4 × 4 µ-tensor TM (16 natural-number
entries, row-major; module_mu_tensor).


3.3     The Partition Graph
Definition 3.3 (Partition Graph). A partition graph G = (Nid , T , Nmor , F) where Nid ∈ N is
the next available module ID, T : {0, . . . , Nid − 1} ⇀ M is a finite partial function from IDs to
modules, Nmor ∈ N is the next available morphism ID, and F is a finite partial function from
morphism IDs below Nmor to morphisms (a source module, a target module, and coupling data).

Definition 3.4 (Well-Formedness). G is well-formed iff dom(T ) ⊆ {0, . . . , Nid − 1}, dom(F) ⊆
{0, . . . , Nmor − 1}, and the source and target of every morphism in F are in dom(T ) (well_
formed_graph).


3.4     Machine State
Definition 3.5 (VM State). The complete state is a 12-tuple:

                         S = (G, C, R, Mem, PC , µ, Tµ , err, ℓ, m, w, cert)

where G ∈ G is the partition graph, C ∈ N4 comprises the CSRs (certificate address, status,
error code, heap base), R ∈ NREG_COUNT is the register file, Mem ∈ NMEM_SIZE is main memory
(the kernel fixes REG_COUNT = 16 and MEM_SIZE = 128, and some kernel proofs are stated
parametrically in them; register and memory words are truncated to 64 bits), PC ∈ N is the
program counter, µ ∈ N is the µ-ledger, Tµ ∈ N16 is the flattened 4 × 4 µ-tensor (row-major),


                                                  8
err ∈ B is the global error flag (latching), ℓ ∈ N is the logic-engine accumulator, m ∈ N is the
machine status register (0 = Turing mode, 1 = Thiele mode), w ∈ N8 is the WitnessCount record
(vm_witness): eight natural-number counters wc_same_ab and wc_diff_ab for each setting pair
(a, b) ∈ {00, 01, 10, 11}, and cert ∈ B is the certified flag (vm_certified).

Chapter 4

Instruction Set and Cost Model

4.1     The Axiom Scheme A1–A6
“A2” is referred to throughout this document, and this is the scheme it belongs to. These are
conditions on Coq record types, not Axiom declarations. The assumption receipt distinguishes
project-local declarations from standard-library axioms and explicit theorem hypotheses. Sup-
plying an instance of the record is the only way to invoke the corresponding theorems, and the
51-opcode VM supplies them by construction.

4.1.1   The consolidated CertificationSystem (A1–A2)
A CertificationSystem is a tuple (Σ, I, step, cost, cert) where Σ is any state type, I any
instruction type, step : Σ × I → Σ, cost : I → N, and cert : Σ → B, subject to:

A1 (certification is observable). A total function cert : Σ → B exists. This holds by record
    existence: providing the field is providing the axiom.

A2 (certification is priced; the load-bearing one).

         ∀s ∈ Σ, ∀i ∈ I,    cert(s) = false ∧ cert(step(s, i)) = true =⇒ cost(i) ≥ 1.        (4.1)

      In Coq this is the field cs_cert_costs. Everything called “No Free Insight” in this
      document is A2 lifted from steps to traces by induction; the trace-level statement is
      genuinely proved, but its content is A2 plus “a sum of naturals containing a summand ≥1
      is ≥1”.

Remark 4.1 (A2 is a stipulation, and that is the intended reading). A2 is a condition a substrate
may or may not satisfy, not a discovered fact about computation. Nothing in this document
derives it; Section 21.3 derives it conditionally from a Landauer premise plus a named calibration
bridge, which relocates the stipulation rather than removing it. Claims of the form “the machine
proves certification must cost” should be read as “on any substrate satisfying A2, certification
costs”. That is what universal_nfi_any_substrate states, over an arbitrary state type and
an arbitrary instruction type.

4.1.2   The QuantitativeCertificationSystem extension (A3–A6)
The quantitative extension lifts the floor from ≥ 1 to ≥ K for an arbitrary threshold. It adds a
witness function W : Σ → N and a threshold K ∈ N, subject to:

A3 (cost bounds witness growth). ∀s, i : W (s) + cost(i) ≥ W (step(s, i)). Evidence cannot
    accumulate faster than it is paid for. Coq: qcs_cost_bounds_witness.

                                               10
A4 (the witness is nondecreasing). ∀s, i : W (s) ≤ W (step(s, i)). It does not follow from
    A3, which bounds growth from above. It is a separate field of the extended record (qcsf_
    witness_nondecreasing) and the floor does not use it.

A5 (certification requires the threshold). ∀s : cert(s) = true =⇒ W (s) ≥ K. Coq:
    qcs_cert_threshold_witness.

A6 (clean start). The initial state carries no witness, W (s0 ) = 0. Supplied as a hypothesis at

4.2     Instruction Categories
The instruction set I is organized into six families. Every instruction i carries an explicit cost
parameter ∆µi ∈ N.

4.2.1   Structural Operations
These modify the partition graph G:

  • PNEW(R, ∆µ): Claim the range of data memory that starts at the first address of norm(R)
    and has |norm(R)| addresses (pnew_region). If a module already owns exactly that range,
    the graph is unchanged; otherwise a fresh module with no axioms takes it.

  • PSPLIT(m, L, R, ∆µ): Remove module m mod 64 and the arrows that touch it, and add
    two fresh modules: the first ⌊n/2⌋ addresses of its range and the rest. L and R are accepted
    and ignored.

  • PMERGE(m1 , m2 , ∆µ): Remove modules m1 mod 64 and m2 mod 64 and the arrows that
    touch them, and add one fresh module whose range is the two ranges joined.

  • PDISCOVER(m, E, ∆µ): Discovery accounting for module m. The abstract specification
    attaches evidence E (list of axioms) to the module; the hardware-aligned step advances
    PC and charges ∆µ without mutating the graph.

  • MDLACC(m, ∆µ): Advance the PC and charge ∆µ; nothing else changes.

PNEW succeeds only when a module number is free (Nid + 1 ≤ 64), every claimed address is
below MEM_SIZE = 128, and the range overlaps no module except one whose range it is (pnew_
ok). PSPLIT needs two free module numbers (module_room). PMERGE needs one free number
and two ranges that, laid end to end in one order or the other, form one range (pmerge_ok).
When the check fails the step traps: err ← true, the error code is set, PC ← LASSERT_TRAP_PC,
the cost is charged, and the graph is unchanged. From a well-formed graph with no modules and
a module counter at most 64, every reachable graph has pairwise disjoint, contiguous regions
inside data memory, at most 64 modules, and every module number below 64, so reading a
module number modulo 64 changes no live number (vm_reachable_partition_in_bounds).



                                               11
4.2.2   Logical Operations
These interact with information content:

  • LASSERT(freg, creg, kind, flen, cost): Assert a formula read from vm_mem[R[freg]] (encoded-
    unit count flen, eight priced bits per unit; the factor 8 is a pricing convention) against a
    witness package at vm_mem[R[creg]]. In SAT mode the package contains one satisfying
    assignment and one falsifying assignment, stored back-to-back; ∆µ = flen × 8 + S(cost) ≥ 1.
    The step succeeds only when flen matches the header length of the formula in memory
    (lassert_hw_flen); a mismatch traps like a failed check.

  • LJOIN(c1reg, c2reg, ∆µ): Advance the PC and charge S(∆µ). It neither reads nor compares
    the certificate strings.

  • REVEAL(m, n, cert, ∆µ): Add n to the µ-tensor entry at index m mod 16, advance the PC,
    and charge n + S(∆µ). The certificate string is not examined.

  • EMIT(m, p, ∆µ): Emit payload p from module m; cost is |p|bits + S(∆µ).

4.2.3   Computational Operations
Register/memory operations:

  • XFER(dst, src, ∆µ): Copy register src to dst.

  • XOR_LOAD(dst, addr, ∆µ): Load memory word at addr (a plain load; no XOR is involved).

  • XOR_ADD(dst, src, ∆µ): dst ← dst ⊕ src.

  • XOR_SWAP(a, b, ∆µ): Swap registers a and b.

  • XOR_RANK(dst, src, ∆µ): dst ← popcount(src).

4.2.4   Standard ALU and Control Flow
  • LOAD_IMM(dst, imm, ∆µ): Load the immediate, truncated to 64 bits, into register.

  • LOAD(dst, addr, ∆µ): Load from main memory.

  • STORE(addr, src, ∆µ): Store to main memory.

  • ADD(dst, r1 , r2 , ∆µ): dst ← r1 + r2 (mod 264 ).

  • SUB(dst, r1 , r2 , ∆µ): dst ← r1 − r2 (mod 264 ).

  • JUMP(target, ∆µ): Unconditional jump.

  • JNEZ(r, target, ∆µ): Jump if R[r] ̸= 0.

  • CALL(target, ∆µ): Push return address, jump to target.

  • RET(∆µ): Pop return address, jump back.

These 7 opcodes (0x27–0x2D) implement the categorical layer over partition modules. See

   • MORPH(dst, src, tgt, idx, ∆µ): Create morphism src → tgt; write morphism ID to regs[dst].

   • COMPOSE(dst, f, g, ∆µ): Compose f ; g when f.tgt = g.src; errors on type mismatch.

machine is the law, not the instruction set.

control flow, 7 I/O and tensor, 4 partition operations, 3 logic and certification, 8 witness and

4.3     Cost Model
Every instruction i carries an explicit cost parameter ∆µi ∈ N. The function instruction_cost(i)
extracts this parameter. The cost is applied via apply_cost(s, i) = s.µ + instruction_cost(i).
Since ∆µi : N, the µ-ledger is monotonically non-decreasing by construction.
    The mandatory positive-cost class is: CERTIFY, MORPH_ASSERT, and LJOIN (each charges
S(∆µ) ≥ 1); EMIT (|payload|bits + S(∆µ)); REVEAL and READ_PORT (bits + S(∆µ)); and LASSERT
(flen × 8 + S(∆µ)). These cannot be zero-cost even with ∆µ = 0. The five CHSH_LASSERT family
instructions also charge S(∆µ). Other instructions charge their encoded ∆µ, which may be zero.

Theorem 4.1 (Least joint unit floor, (S)). For a finite family of event predicates on instructions,
the least natural-valued schedule satisfying a unit floor on each event is the indicator that at least
one event occurs. This is joint_floor_is_least. Independent Boolean coordinates can change
together for one unit under these rules; additivity requires an additional condition.


4.4     The CERTIFY instruction
CERTIFY is referred to throughout Chapters 6 onward and is defined here.

Definition 4.1 (CERTIFY (instr_certify, VMStep.v)). CERTIFY(∆µ) has the step semantics

                       pc 7→ pc + 1,      µ 7→ µ + S(∆µ),        cert 7→ true,                  (4.2)

with every other state component unchanged. Its cost is S(∆µ) ≥ 1, which is the concrete
instance of A2 (Section 4.1) for this ISA. It is the only rule in VMStep.v that sets cert := true.

Remark 4.3 (CERTIFY is unguarded; what that does and does not mean). The rule fires for
every state and every ∆µ: it carries no precondition, takes no proposition as an argument, and
checks nothing. Setting the certification bit is therefore always available, at a price of at least
1. Conversely, the instruction that does perform a decidable check on data, namely LASSERT,
which traps on failure, leaves both the certification CSR and cert unchanged.
    The description of the mechanism is therefore metering, not verification: the step rule
guarantees that flipping the bit is priced, not that the bit is only set when some claim holds.
Statements of the form “this machine makes stamping verified impossible” overstate it; what is
impossible is stamping it for free.
    The ISA does contain genuinely guarded instructions. MORPH_ASSERT requires the named mor-
phism to exist, and CHSH_LASSERT runs column_contractive_check_witness on actual witness
data and traps on failure, with a soundness bridge to PSD. A substrate with a guarded certifica-
tion bit is formalised separately, on the Coq standard library alone, in minimal/EarnedCore.v:
a two-counter machine with a table of checked facts and three priced instructions. CHECK writes
a fact (property, counter, the counter’s current version) only when the property holds; COMMIT
succeeds only on such a fact at the counter’s current version; CERTIFY succeeds only after a com-
mitment. From a clean start every certified run contains a passing check, a passing commitment
of the same claim with the counter untouched in between, and a passing certification (earned_
certification_provenance); a fact whose counter has not been written since its check names
a true claim (checker_soundness); every certified run costs at least 3 (certified_run_min_
cost). Its prices are still flat, one per instruction, so A2 holds there by the price list and is not
derived from the cost of establishing the claim; see Remark 4.1.

5.1     Transition Function

   1. PC ′ = PC + 1 for non-control-flow instructions; JUMP, JNEZ, CALL, and RET set PC

Theorem 8.4 (µ-Cost Hierarchy (kernel/MuHierarchyTheorem.v), (S)). For every k ≥ 1, the

sum equals the trace cost (run_vm_mu_conservation).
    Corollary (mu_hierarchy_no_upper_bound): no fixed µ-budget suffices for all levels. The
µ-dimension is unbounded and non-collapsible. Zero Admitted.

Remark 8.2 (µ-Hierarchy as a P-vs-NP analogue, not a P-vs-NP result). Theorem 8.4 is the
µ-analogue of the classical time hierarchy theorem in shape. Its content is lighter. The level of
a certificate is defined by the operand δ of the CERTIFY that issued it, and the cost schedule
charges δ + 1 for that instruction, so the floor comes from the schedule, not from a search or
diagonal argument. It is not a statement about classical P versus NP, which this project does
not touch. In the certified-structure setting it makes one thing precise: issuing a certificate
at level k costs µ ≥ k, because the schedule charges δ + 1 for the CERTIFY that sets the level.
It says nothing about how hard the certified thing was to find. The hierarchy is strict and
unbounded, with zero Admitted, no oracle, and no diagonalization. The lower-bound proof is

8.4     Intrinsic level hierarchy
Definition 8.2 (Intrinsic level (coq/IntrinsicLevelHierarchy.v)). A final state s is intrinsically

Remark 8.3 (Relation to Theorem 8.4). Theorem 8.6 is the state-level counterpart of the trace-

10.1     The Partial Z-Action (Gauge Group)
Definition 10.1 (Gauge Shift). For δ ∈ Z, the gauge shift σδ : S → S maps S 7→ S[µ ←
toNat(µ + δ)], preserving all other fields.
   Note the truncation. Since µ ranges over N, the Coq definition z_gauge_shift computes
Z.to_nat (Z.of_nat vm_mu + delta), and Z.to_nat clamps negatives to zero. So σδ is a total
function on states but is not injective, and the shifts do not form a Z-action on all of S.
Theorem 10.1 (Partial Group Action (KernelNoether.v)). The gauge shifts form a partial Z-
action on states; equivalently, a genuine Z-action on the sub-poset of states where no intermediate
shift underflows:
  1. Identity: σ0 (S) = S. Unconditional (z_action_identity).
  2. Composition: σa (σb (S)) = σa+b (S) provided µ(S) + b ≥ 0 and µ(S) + b + a ≥ 0 (z_
     action_composition, which carries both hypotheses).
  3. Inverse: σ−n (σn (S)) = S provided µ(S) + n ≥ 0 (z_action_inverse).
Remark 10.1 (Why composition needs its guard). The guard on clause 2 is not decoration.
Take µ(S) = 0, b = −1, a = +1: σb clamps to µ = 0, so σa (σb (S)) has µ = 1; whereas
σa+b (S) = σ0 (S) = S has µ = 0. The two sides differ. Composition fails wherever an intermediate
shift underflows, which is why the mechanised theorem carries two nonnegativity hypotheses:
one for the intermediate state, one for the final state. Drop either and the counterexample above
goes through.

18.3      The Pointer-Observable Criterion
coq/kernel/frontier/PointerObservable.v formalizes the schema behind the metering con-
jecture. An ecosystem (Ecosystem) is a state type carrying n observers, each holding a fragment
map over states; an event E redundantly proliferates (redundantly_proliferating) iff
every observer’s fragment records E; and E is the unique pointer among a designated list of
rival events (unique_pointer_among) iff E proliferates and no listed rival does. Uniqueness is
relative to the named rivals on purpose, absolute uniqueness is false for free. A toy replicated-
ledger instance shows the definitions are satisfiable and discriminating: the certification event is
the unique pointer among the toy’s two meterable events (toy_cert_unique_pointer), a sanity
instance only.
     PointerObservableReductions.v instantiates the schema at models of the five metering
disciplines of the reductions tier: proof-of-stake finality, gas metering, TEE attestation, certificate
transparency, and proof-carrying verification. In each model every observer returns the selected
flag, and a named rival flag is omitted by construction; all five are one construction with the
fields renamed. The metered event is proved the unique pointer among its named rivals in
each model (PoS_model_unique_pointer, Gas_model_unique_pointer, TEE_model_unique_
pointer, CT_model_unique_pointer, PCC_model_unique_pointer), and the conjunction is
exported as five_labeled_models_have_selected_pointer, which closes under the global
Coq context, no axioms at all, stdlib or otherwise.
     Scope: these theorems verify that the five formalized ecosystems have the claimed pointer
structure. No protocol semantics, deployment, or security property occurs in their statements.
Whether the real disciplines have that observer structure is a modeling claim argued in prose;
if it holds, it is evidence for the pointer-observable conjecture, not a proof of it. No theorem
derives a forced event from these models.
     RecordProliferationSurvey.v extends the comparison to twelve named candidate events
drawn from the proof-of-stake, gas, attestation, transparency, proof-carrying, MAC, and
signature observer maps. The conjunction twelve_candidate_measurements, proved as
twelve_candidate_measurements_checked, states the declared Boolean classifications. In
one three-observer ecosystem (swap_ecosystem), swapped_event_is_pointer_checked proves
that second_event is the unique pointer among [first_event]. The MAC-labelled event is
non-proliferating in the specified observer map. Neither the label’s cryptographic significance

nor the observer map’s faithfulness to a deployed system occurs in the theorem statement; the
real-system reading is therefore model-dependent.
    The proposed route from distributed agreement to permanence is refuted in EcosystemGame.v.
The predicate strong_pointer_necessity assumes at least one observer, agreement, Boolean

18.3.1    The adversarial search
Five confirmations settle nothing on their own. A criterion loose enough to accept anything ac-
cepts five things just as easily. coq/kernel/frontier/PointerObservableCounterexamples.v
tests the criterion in the corner where it is most likely to break, on systems that do not resemble
the five.
    The claim put at risk is the strong reading:

     (PO-STRONG) Every system that must resist forgery of a commitment has a
     commitment event that redundantly proliferates.

A counterexample must satisfy three conditions: (C1) it must resist forgery of a commitment, in
the sense that its designers treat an unearned commitment as the primary failure mode; (C2) it
must be deployed and independently evolved, owing nothing to this development; and (C3) its
commitment event must fail redundantly_proliferating.
    Each candidate has two halves. (C1) and (C2) are facts about a real design and are argued
in prose. The Coq half fixes an observer map and computes (C3) for it. The file contains no
definition of forgery resistance, EUF-CMA security, deniability, memory safety, metering, or
deployment, so the Coq results alone neither refute nor confirm a security claim.
    Three candidates argue against (PO-STRONG), using a two-part argument rather
than an unconditional machine theorem. For each, the non-proliferating observer map is checked
in Coq and its faithfulness to the design is argued in prose:

   • Deniable (designated-verifier) authentication (deniable_authentication_model_
     not_proliferating). The scheme is engineered so a third party cannot be convinced
     even given the whole transcript, because the designated verifier could have produced it.
     Non-transferability is the product requirement.

   • Symmetric-key authentication (mac_model_not_proliferating). Existential unforge-
     ability is the defining security notion of a MAC, and verification requires the shared secret,
     so no record reaches anyone else.

   • Object capabilities (capability_model_not_proliferating). Unforgeability here is
     unconditional, enforced by memory safety rather than by a cost or a hardness assumption.
     Nothing is paid, nothing is logged, and no observer outside the holder can tell.

A public-log control (public_log_model_proliferating) checks that the search can return
confirms and is not an instrument that only reports refutations; its rival effort event also fails
to proliferate (public_log_effort_not_proliferating), so the control discriminates rather
than accepting everything.
    If those prose correspondences are accepted, the boundary they trace is not the one (PO-
STRONG) guessed at. A MAC and a digital signature both resist forgery; only the signature

                                                39
proliferates. Deniable authentication and certificate transparency both resist forgery; deniability
actively prevents third-party conviction while transparency actively requires it. Object capabili-
ties and proof-carrying code both make unearned authority impossible; the capability confines it
to the holder while the proof travels to every consumer. In each pair the proliferating member is
the one whose commitment must convince parties who were not present. The surviving candidate
criterion is therefore:

     (PO-PUBLIC) A system whose commitment must convince third parties who did
     not witness its creation will proliferate records of the commitment event.

The signature observer model makes the commitment visible in each observer fragment and
proves proliferation for that model. Its correspondence to deployed signatures is a modeling
judgment. Public availability of a verifier does not imply that every observer retains a record.
   The verifier constructions give three sufficient evidence interfaces under their respective
premises: a full-state transcript, an exact commitment-bit contract, and a reported response.
Neither these examples nor the factorization obstruction proves that only one route can involve
metering.
   Three statements travel together in loose prose and come apart under these candidates:

(M1) Forgery resistance forces metering. False, on the prose argument. Signatures
    take the hardness route; object capabilities achieve unforgeability unconditionally through
    memory safety, with no cost, no record, and no assumption.

(M2) Forgery resistance forces record proliferation. False, on the same argument. Deni-
    able authentication, symmetric MACs, and capabilities all resist forgery while proliferating
    nothing, and in the first case that is the design goal.

(M3) Public commitments force record proliferation. Proposed. The chosen observer
    models satisfy it. Actual record proliferation needs independent evidence; public verifiability
    alone is insufficient.

    The metering claim supported here is exact. Under supplied colliding transcripts, no verifier
that is sound and complete for vm_mu = 1 factors through the classical projection (V_does_
not_factor_through_classical). The pricing statement is carried by No Free Insight, not by
this theorem. It is narrower than “forgery resistance,” and it is the version this development
supports.

Remark 21.1 (Applicability failure of the full-ISA physical premises). landauer_dissipation_premises_incon

Remark 23.1 (Agreement, not isomorphism). No isomorphism is constructed in this chapter,
and none is claimed: there is no pair of mutually inverse maps between the Coq, OCaml/Python,
and RTL layers, and no proof that either composite is the identity. What is established is
that any two implementations satisfying a shared observable-projection contract agree on the
projected fields after a shared trace: transitivity of equality through a common projection. The
Coq layer satisfies the contract (coq_full_wire_spec). That the Python and RTL artifacts
satisfy it is a premise, not a Coq result.
    The distinction is not pedantic. Agreement on a projection is compatible with the layers
differing arbitrarily on everything the projection forgets; an isomorphism would forbid that.
Since the projection deliberately forgets internal representation, agreement is the right property
to want here. Isomorphism would be a stronger claim than the construction supports.

Theorem 23.1 (Observable Comparison Contract (ThreeLayerIsomorphism.v)). The file states
an observable-state contract used for cross-layer comparison. An implementation satisfies
FullWireSpec when each of twelve projected output fields of one step equals the corresponding field

that agree on all twelve projected fields at the start agree on all of them after a shared step
(full_state_single_step_bisimulation) and after a shared trace τ (full_state_trace_

23.2      The CPU Against the Kernel
The refinement theorems relate the Kami model of the CPU to vm_apply under stated premises,
and the two machines differ in ways those premises carry. The CPU’s data words and its ledger
register are 32 bits wide, and the ledger wraps at 232 ; the kernel’s words are 64 bits and its ledger
is an unbounded natural number. Each refinement theorem assumes the counters it touches fit
in 32 bits (cpu_preconditions asks for µ < 231 ). The CPU traps with a named error code on
checks the kernel does not make: LOAD, STORE, their heap forms, CALL and RET outside the active
module’s range; a PDISCOVER whose declared cost is below its second operand; a ledger below the
tensor total; malformed rich-format fields. The refinement theorems cover only steps on which
none of these fires. Partition capacity and range are checked by both, with the same outcome.
Retirement under the CPU’s own scheduler is proved for every admitted instruction (admitted_
retires) and, from reset, for runs of admitted instructions (fsm_retirement_refinement). The
board-top gate-level simulation, the SymbiYosys properties, and the board/loader equivalence
check, which is conditional on the CPU interface and leaves the CPU and its RAM out, run
outside Coq; no physical board has run the design.

Categorical Structure

24.1     The Morphism Layer
The active implementation adds a concrete category directly to the machine state. This is not an
abstract analogy, it is an executable layer of 7 opcodes (0x27–0x2D) with formally verified laws.

24.1.1    Objects and Morphisms
The partition graph G carries two maps:

  • pg_modules : ModuleID ⇀ ModuleState, objects (partition modules)

  • pg_morphisms : MorphismID ⇀ MorphismState, arrows

    A MorphismState bundles: source module, target module, coupling data (list of (nat × nat)
pairs plus a label), and an identity flag.

24.1.2    The Seven Categorical Opcodes
  • MORPH(dst, src, tgt, idx, ∆µ): create morphism src → tgt, write morphism ID to regs[dst].
    Costs ∆µ.

  • COMPOSE(dst, f, g, ∆µ): compose morphisms f ; g when f.target = g.source, write new ID
    to regs[dst]. Costs ∆µ. Errors if endpoints mismatch.

  • MORPH_ID(dst, m, ∆µ): create identity morphism idm for module m (diagonal relation).
    Costs ∆µ.

  • MORPH_DELETE(f, ∆µ): remove morphism f . Cascades on module deletion via graph_
    cascade_delete_morphisms.

  • MORPH_ASSERT(f, prop, cert, ∆µ): cert-setter. Costs S(∆µ) ≥ 1 unconditionally, even for
    failed attempts (morphism does not exist). This is the No Free Insight law applied to
    relational claims.

  • MORPH_TENSOR(dst, f, g, ∆µ): tensor product f ⊗ g. Requires that the union modules
    src(f ) ∪ src(g) and tgt(f ) ∪ tgt(g) already exist in the graph.

  • MORPH_GET(dst, f, sel, ∆µ): read field from morphism f : selector 0 = source, 1 = target, 2
    = coupling length, 3 = is_identity.

Exact scope
The record-axis theorems have an exact scope: a permanent record driven by the base computation
is a latch on an event, a finite permanent write is non-injective, and the VM’s named projections
do not recover its record. Certification is an instance; no theorem derives a unique event choice.
    Finite monotone multi-valued records admit threshold-latch decompositions, but the corre-
sponding single-latch, revocable, and probabilistic uniqueness statements are false. The guest
VM has a direct Rice reduction, numeric decoding, verified fuel-bounded dispatch, semantic
s-m-n specialization, and its own recursion theorem (vm_guest_recursion_theorem_closed):


                                                    73
every program transformer that a guest program computes on program codes has a fixed point
that matches it in all four final registers and in µ on every input, halting or not. The evaluator in
that proof is extracted to the lambda calculus L, compiled to a Minsky machine, and executed by
guest code. For the full VM, a fixed point for every map on programs up to equal thousand-step
runs is false. The 51-opcode VM prices commitments without checking that they were earned;
the minimal two-counter machine earns them. The geometry theorems are about partition
graphs in general; on states the VM reaches, module regions are disjoint and calibration holds
only with no modules. The two-state calorimeter computes exact heat and proves that identical
population dynamics do not determine the Hamiltonian scale. Converting µ to energy requires
thermal and device premises; µ has no intrinsic energy unit.
    The RFC 9162 file checks iterative inclusion and consistency control flow and examples, not
cryptographic collision resistance or signed-tree-head authenticity. The PCC, addressed-RAM,
reversible-update, and TPM-interface files prove only their named fragment or countermodel
propositions. The local cost-semantics correspondences are not full cost-framework embeddings.
    The twelve-event observer-map bundle and swap theorem are closed. Its MAC-labelled
model is non-proliferating, but applying that statement to a real system depends on the observer
model. The two-observer toggle game refutes the claim that consensus, exact observation,
and coordinator-free evolution force permanence; the durability theorem supplies persistence
as a premise, and the VM certification game satisfies it (vm_certification_is_permanent_
consensus). The external consequence survey contains five narrow statements already known
from their governing specifications and five stronger statements without enough protocol or
hardware semantics. No surveyed consequence is simultaneously record-axis dependent, novel,
and ready for use. The pointer criterion is a conjecture.
