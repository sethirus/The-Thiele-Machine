\noindent\textbf{Before the specification.} I don't do math by hand. I think in shapes, directions, and connections, and my part of this work was to see where things had to fit, to direct, and to refuse what didn't fit. This document is the translation. Language models wrote the formal statements and the proofs, Coq checked every one of them, and I read the statements to make sure they say what the shape says. \medskip

\noindent\textbf{The Thiele Machine is an abstract model of computation, and an argument made with it about what an account of computation should preserve.}

It is a machine in the sense a Turing machine is one: a mathematical object, not a device. The 51-opcode VM summarized in the chapters below is one build of that model; its complete checked definition is the Coq source. It is not the model. The model is the abstract \texttt{CertificationSystem} and \texttt{Substrate} interfaces; the VM is one instance of each. The VM prices commitments and does not check that they were earned. A second, minimal machine, \texttt{minimal/\allowbreak{}EarnedCore.v}, is a two-counter machine whose commitments are earned (Remark~\ref{rem:certify_unguarded}).

Certification is one concrete witness of a broader investigation of structural state, observation, and cost. The abstract laws, theorems, and executable realizations belong to that investigation. Selected projections lose distinctions that the richer state can express; cost laws constrain specified events under explicit hypotheses.

A conventional encoding can preserve those distinctions. A projection that discards them cannot recover them in general. The stronger proposal that these distinctions belong in a necessary foundation of computation is a conjecture. Neither a physical cost metric nor infinitely many independent geometric dimensions follows from the existence of many observables. The status of each result is stated below.

\begin{abstract}
This is a mathematical reference for the Thiele Machine: a formal model of computation where structural information carries an explicit, monotone cost $\mu$ that starts at zero and never decreases. It presents the abstract interfaces, the main theorems, and selected semantics from the machine-checked Coq corpus. The complete 51-opcode operational definition is the Coq source; this document alone does not enumerate enough semantics to reconstruct it.

Coq establishes that conclusions follow from axioms. It does \emph{not} establish that axioms model physical reality. That distinction is kept explicit throughout. Every result is tagged as one of three types: \textbf{(S)}~structural theorems about the machine as a mathematical object, \textbf{(C)}~conditional derivations where a physics conclusion follows from a stated axiom, and \textbf{(R)}~consistency relations that check internal coherence but are not independent predictions.

The core claims are structural (S): No Free Insight; $\mu$-conservation; $\mu$-hierarchy; $\mu$-ledger necessity (five-way independence classification); categorical separation; strict Turing subsumption of instruction sets; the classical CHSH bound ($|S| \leq 2$); the algebraic Tsirelson bound ($|S| \leq 2\sqrt{2}$); and, on the zero-marginal slice, the equivalence of column contractivity with positive semidefiniteness of one fixed $5\times 5$ NPA matrix, with no project-local axioms \texttt{column\_\allowbreak{}contractive\_\allowbreak{}iff\_\allowbreak{}npa\_\allowbreak{}psd}. The CHSH algebraic result is pure math. The Landauer connection is motivation. Geometry: the discrete angle-defect identity under its incidence assumptions and the additional restriction $B=3\chi$, as statements about partition graphs in general; on states the VM reaches, module regions are disjoint and these statements hold only in a degenerate form (Remark~\ref{rem:f3_no_topology}).

The document also covers, with explicit epistemological tags, conditional derivations under named physical axioms (Born rule under linearity, no-cloning, unitarity) and reference explorations (4D simplicial-complex differential geometry, self-reference tower). These are marked (C) or (R) and do not form part of the primary claim ledger. Two named trust boundaries discussed here are: \texttt{bsc\_\allowbreak{}kami\_\allowbreak{}compilation\_\allowbreak{}trusted} (Layer 2 Verilog semantics gap) and \texttt{A\_\allowbreak{}QM} (physical quantum axiom).
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Introduction}
The Thiele Machine makes the cost of structure explicit. Ordinary step-counting assigns one time unit per transition; it does not specify this structural-cost schedule. The Thiele Machine assigns a specific cost $\mu$ to every operation that extracts or asserts structural information. The chapters below lay out the consequences.

The central claim, proven formally: \emph{specified certification and structural-entitlement events have the cost floors proved under their stated hypotheses}. Alongside that core, the specification records conditional results in which explicitly supplied premises (linearity, an information-conservation inequality, a second-law record field) yield statements with the shape of results in quantum mechanics and thermodynamics. In most of them $\mu$ enters as a named cost field, and the supplied premises do the work. They are marked \textbf{(C)}: conditional on those premises, not derived from the ledger alone. Each result's status, whether a structural theorem~\textbf{(S)}, a conditional derivation~\textbf{(C)}, or a consistency relation~\textbf{(R)}, is marked directly.

The document is organized as:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Foundations}: State space, instruction set, operational semantics.
    \item \textbf{Core Theorems}: No Free Insight, $\mu$-ledger necessity, $\mu$-Chaitin, $\mu$-conservation.
    \item \textbf{Computation}: Strict Turing subsumption of instruction sets, oracle pricing as instantiation of universal NFI, $\mu$-complexity classes, $\mu$-cost hierarchy.
    \item \textbf{Symmetry \& Conservation}: Gauge shifts of $\mu$, a Noether-style invariance, receipts.
    \item \textbf{Quantum / CHSH algebra}: Tsirelson bound (algebraic), CHSH classical bound, biconditional column-contractive $\iff$ PSD of the zero-marginal NPA matrix, three-tier observation taxonomy. Pure polynomial arithmetic, zero physics axioms.
    \item \textbf{Conditional physics}: Born rule (under linearity), unitarity, no-cloning, purification, Landauer (with second-law assumed as a record field), affine EFE family. Each is conditional on stated axioms in its own file.
    \item \textbf{Discrete Geometry}: Restricted angle-defect identity with $B=3\chi$ (not classical Gauss-Bonnet); 4D simplicial complex and Einstein-tensor definitions (reference exploration only, vacuum case is a $0=0$ consistency check).
    \item \textbf{Meta-Theory}: Three-layer Coq/Python/RTL observable contract, categorical structure.
    \item \textbf{Predictions}: Falsifiable predictions tied to specific Coq-verifiable claims.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Epistemological Framework}

Formal verification (Coq) establishes that conclusions follow logically from axioms. It does \textbf{not} establish that axioms model physical reality. This distinction is critical for evaluating the physics claims in Parts~V--VIII.

Every result in this specification falls into one of three categories:

\begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{(S) Structural}] Theorems about the model and the kernel as mathematical objects. Their conclusions follow from the definitions, explicit hypotheses, and reported library assumptions. \emph{Examples}: $\mu$-conservation, gauge invariance, strict Turing subsumption of instruction sets, and, for the calculus L, confluence of value reduction (\texttt{star\_\allowbreak{}value\_\allowbreak{}confluent}) and halting undecidability (\texttt{L\_\allowbreak{}halting\_\allowbreak{}undecidable}).

    \item[\textbf{(C) Conditional}] Theorems of the form ``if axiom $X$ holds, then physics result $Y$ follows.'' The logical inference is verified; whether axiom $X$ holds in nature is an empirical question. \emph{Examples}: Born rule uniqueness (conditional on linearity), complex necessity (conditional on 2D amplitudes), unitarity (conditional on information conservation).

    \item[\textbf{(R) Consistency}] Algebraic identities or definitional equivalences that verify internal coherence but do not constitute independent predictions. \emph{Example}: the Tsirelson cost definition.
\end{description}

Each major theorem in Parts~V--VIII is annotated with its category. A theorem marked \textbf{(C)} or \textbf{(R)} is not diminished. Conditional derivations and consistency checks are valuable scientific tools. But the reader should not mistake them for derivations from first principles without stated assumptions. See Appendix~B for the complete classification table.

\section{Overloaded symbols}

\begin{description}
  \item[$S$, four distinct meanings.] Disambiguated by context and, where
    ambiguity would be real, by explicit qualification:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\Sspace$ (calligraphic): the \emph{state space}.
      \item $S$ (roman, in operational-semantics chapters): a \emph{particular
        VM state}, an element of $\Sspace$.
      \item $S(n)$ (applied to a natural number): the \emph{successor
        function} on $\N$, i.e.\ $S(n) = n+1$. This is Coq's \texttt{S}
        constructor, and it is the reason cost expressions such as
        $\mathit{flen}\times 8 + S(\mathit{cost})$ are bounded below by $1$:
        $S(n) \geq 1$ for every $n \in \N$. \textbf{This is the notation used
        from Chapter~\ref{ch:isa} onward, before its first formal
        appearance in the cost tables.}
      \item $S$ (in quantum chapters): the \emph{CHSH statistic}, defined in
        Section~\ref{sec:chsh_statistic}. Real-valued.
    \end{enumerate}
  \item[$G$, three distinct meanings.] All three occur, and two occur inside
    the same chapter on emergent geometry:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item $G$: the \emph{partition graph} component of a VM state.
      \item $G$: \emph{Newton's constant}, fixed to $1/(8\pi)$ as a unit choice.
      \item $G_{\mu\nu}$: the \emph{Einstein tensor}.
    \end{enumerate}
    Where the geometry chapter needs the partition graph it is written
    $G_{\mathrm{part}}$.
  \item[$\mu$, ledger and index, and two different number systems.]
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\mu$: the \emph{cost ledger}, a component of VM state. In the
        machine, \textbf{$\mu$ ranges over $\N$}: it is a natural number, it is
        monotone non-decreasing under the step relation, and A2 gives it an
        integer floor.
      \item $\mu, \nu$ as \emph{tensor indices} in the geometry chapter
        ($g_{\mu\nu}$, $T_{\mu\nu}$). Unrelated to the ledger.
      \item $\mu$ in the \textbf{quantum chapters} (measurement cost, the
        no-cloning inequality, the $\mu/I$ ratio) is \textbf{real-valued}.
        This is a genuinely different object from the machine's $\mu$: there
        is no theorem in this development identifying them, and no coercion
        between them is claimed. Where the distinction matters the real-valued
        quantity is written $\mu_{\mathbb{R}}$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{description}

\chapter{The State Space}

\section{Primitives}
Let $\N = \{0,1,2,\dots\}$. Let $\B = \{0,1\}$. Let $\Sigma$ be a finite alphabet.

\section{Regions and Modules}
\begin{definition}[Region]
A \textbf{Region} $R$ is a finite subset of $\N$, canonically represented as a deduplicated list preserving first-occurrence order: $\mathrm{norm}(L) = \mathrm{nodup}(L)$ removes duplicate elements while preserving the order of their first appearance. (The Python VM applies a stricter canonical form via \texttt{sorted(set(region))}; the Coq specification uses \texttt{nodup Nat.eq\_\allowbreak{}dec} which does not sort.)
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Module]
A \textbf{Module} $M = (R_M, A_M, T_M)$ consists of a normalized region $R_M \subset \N$, a set of axioms $A_M \subset \Sigma^*$, and a per-module $4\times 4$ $\mu$-tensor $T_M$ (16 natural-number entries, row-major; \texttt{module\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}tensor}).
\end{definition}

\section{The Partition Graph}
\begin{definition}[Partition Graph]
A partition graph $G = (N_{id}, \mathcal{T}, N_{mor}, \mathcal{F})$ where $N_{id} \in \N$ is the next available module ID, $\mathcal{T}: \{0,\dots,N_{id}-1\} \rightharpoonup \Mspace$ is a finite partial function from IDs to modules, $N_{mor} \in \N$ is the next available morphism ID, and $\mathcal{F}$ is a finite partial function from morphism IDs below $N_{mor}$ to morphisms (a source module, a target module, and coupling data).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Well-Formedness]
$G$ is well-formed iff $\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{T}) \subseteq \{0,\dots,N_{id}-1\}$, $\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{F}) \subseteq \{0,\dots,N_{mor}-1\}$, and the source and target of every morphism in $\mathcal{F}$ are in $\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{T})$ (\texttt{well\_\allowbreak{}formed\_\allowbreak{}graph}).
\end{definition}

\section{Machine State}
\begin{definition}[VM State]
The complete state is a 12-tuple:
\[ S = (G, C, R, \mathit{Mem}, \mathit{PC}, \mu, T_\mu, \mathit{err}, \ell, m, w, \mathit{cert}) \]
where $G \in \Gspace$ is the partition graph, $C \in \N^4$ comprises the CSRs (certificate address, status, error code, heap base), $R \in \N^{\texttt{REG\_\allowbreak{}COUNT}}$ is the register file, $\mathit{Mem} \in \N^{\texttt{MEM\_\allowbreak{}SIZE}}$ is main memory (the kernel fixes \texttt{REG\_\allowbreak{}COUNT}~$=16$ and \texttt{MEM\_\allowbreak{}SIZE}~$=128$, and some kernel proofs are stated parametrically in them; register and memory words are truncated to 64 bits), $\mathit{PC} \in \N$ is the program counter, $\mu \in \N$ is the $\mu$-ledger, $T_\mu \in \N^{16}$ is the flattened $4\times 4$ $\mu$-tensor (row-major), $\mathit{err} \in \B$ is the global error flag (latching), $\ell \in \N$ is the logic-engine accumulator, $m \in \N$ is the machine status register ($0$~=~Turing mode, $1$~=~Thiele mode), $w \in \N^8$ is the WitnessCount record (\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}witness}): eight natural-number counters \texttt{wc\_\allowbreak{}same\_\allowbreak{}ab} and \texttt{wc\_\allowbreak{}diff\_\allowbreak{}ab} for each setting pair $(a,b) \in \{00,01,10,11\}$, and $\mathit{cert} \in \B$ is the certified flag (\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified}).
\end{definition}

\chapter{Instruction Set and Cost Model}
\label{ch:isa}

\section{The Axiom Scheme A1--A6}
\label{sec:axiom_scheme}

``A2'' is referred to throughout this document, and this is the scheme it
belongs to. These are \emph{conditions on Coq record types}, not
\texttt{Axiom} declarations. The assumption receipt distinguishes project-local declarations from standard-library axioms and explicit theorem hypotheses. Supplying an instance of the record is the only way to
invoke the corresponding theorems, and the 51-opcode VM supplies them by
construction.

\subsection{The consolidated \texttt{CertificationSystem} (A1--A2)}

A \textbf{CertificationSystem} is a tuple
$(\Sigma, \Ispace, \mathrm{step}, \mathrm{cost}, \mathrm{cert})$ where
$\Sigma$ is any state type, $\Ispace$ any instruction type,
$\mathrm{step} : \Sigma \times \Ispace \to \Sigma$,
$\mathrm{cost} : \Ispace \to \N$, and
$\mathrm{cert} : \Sigma \to \mathbb{B}$, subject to:

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{A1} (certification is observable).] A total function
    $\mathrm{cert} : \Sigma \to \mathbb{B}$ exists. This holds \emph{by record
    existence}: providing the field is providing the axiom.
  \item[\textbf{A2} (certification is priced; \emph{the load-bearing one}).]
    \begin{equation}
      \forall s \in \Sigma,\ \forall i \in \Ispace,\quad
      \mathrm{cert}(s) = \mathrm{false} \ \wedge\
      \mathrm{cert}(\mathrm{step}(s,i)) = \mathrm{true}
      \ \implies\ \mathrm{cost}(i) \geq 1 .
    \end{equation}
    In Coq this is the field \texttt{cs\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}costs}. Everything called ``No
    Free Insight'' in this document is A2 lifted from steps to traces by
    induction; the trace-level statement is genuinely proved, but its content
    is A2 plus ``a sum of naturals containing a summand ${\geq}1$ is
    ${\geq}1$''.
\end{description}

\begin{remark}[A2 is a stipulation, and that is the intended reading]
\label{rem:a2_stipulated}
A2 is a condition a substrate may or may not satisfy, not a discovered fact
about computation. Nothing in this document derives it; Section~\ref{sec:f1}
derives it \emph{conditionally} from a Landauer premise plus a named
calibration bridge, which relocates the stipulation rather than removing it.
Claims of the form ``the machine proves certification must cost'' should be
read as ``on any substrate satisfying A2, certification costs''. That is
what \texttt{universal\_\allowbreak{}nfi\_\allowbreak{}any\_\allowbreak{}substrate} states, over an arbitrary state
type and an arbitrary instruction type.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The \texttt{QuantitativeCertificationSystem} extension (A3--A6)}

The quantitative extension lifts the floor from $\geq 1$ to $\geq K$ for an
arbitrary threshold. It adds a witness function
$W : \Sigma \to \N$ and a threshold $K \in \N$, subject to:

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{A3} (cost bounds witness growth).]
    $\forall s, i:\ W(s) + \mathrm{cost}(i) \geq W(\mathrm{step}(s,i))$.
    Evidence cannot accumulate faster than it is paid for. Coq:
    \texttt{qcs\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}bounds\_\allowbreak{}witness}.
  \item[\textbf{A4} (the witness is nondecreasing).]
    $\forall s, i:\ W(s) \leq W(\mathrm{step}(s,i))$. It does not follow from
    A3, which bounds growth from above. It is a separate field of the extended
    record (\texttt{qcsf\_\allowbreak{}witness\_\allowbreak{}nondecreasing}) and the
    floor does not use it.
  \item[\textbf{A5} (certification requires the threshold).]
    $\forall s:\ \mathrm{cert}(s) = \mathrm{true} \implies W(s) \geq K$. Coq:
    \texttt{qcs\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}threshold\_\allowbreak{}witness}.
  \item[\textbf{A6} (clean start).] The initial state carries no witness,
    $W(s_0) = 0$. Supplied as a hypothesis at use sites rather than as a
    record field. With A3 and A5 it gives the conclusion: a run from $s_0$ to a
    certified state has total cost $\geq K$.
\end{description}

\begin{remark}[Two numbering systems, and which one this document uses]
\label{rem:two_numberings}
The record \texttt{AbstractCertMachine} has its own labels A1--A4, in which
\textbf{A4} is the cost-on-certification rule and \textbf{A2} is ``cert starts
unset''. The labels A3--A6 above follow \texttt{QuantitativeNoFI.v}.
\textbf{Throughout this document, ``A2'' means the cost-on-certification rule
in the consolidated \texttt{CertificationSystem} sense stated above.} When
reading kernel sources that mention \texttt{AbstractCertMachine}, translate
accordingly.
\end{remark}

\section{Instruction Categories}
The instruction set $\Ispace$ is organized into six families. Every instruction $i$ carries an explicit cost parameter $\Delta\mu_i \in \N$.

\subsection{Structural Operations}
These modify the partition graph $G$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\texttt{PNEW}(R, \Delta\mu)$: Claim the range of data memory that starts at the first address of $\mathrm{norm}(R)$ and has $|\mathrm{norm}(R)|$ addresses (\texttt{pnew\_\allowbreak{}region}). If a module already owns exactly that range, the graph is unchanged; otherwise a fresh module with no axioms takes it.
    \item $\texttt{PSPLIT}(m, L, R, \Delta\mu)$: Remove module $m \bmod 64$ and the arrows that touch it, and add two fresh modules: the first $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ addresses of its range and the rest. $L$ and $R$ are accepted and ignored.
    \item $\texttt{PMERGE}(m_1, m_2, \Delta\mu)$: Remove modules $m_1 \bmod 64$ and $m_2 \bmod 64$ and the arrows that touch them, and add one fresh module whose range is the two ranges joined.
    \item $\texttt{PDISCOVER}(m, E, \Delta\mu)$: Discovery accounting for module $m$. The abstract specification attaches evidence $E$ (list of axioms) to the module; the hardware-aligned step advances PC and charges $\Delta\mu$ without mutating the graph.
    \item $\texttt{MDLACC}(m, \Delta\mu)$: Advance the PC and charge $\Delta\mu$; nothing else changes.
\end{itemize}
PNEW succeeds only when a module number is free ($N_{id} + 1 \le 64$), every claimed address is below \texttt{MEM\_\allowbreak{}SIZE}~$=128$, and the range overlaps no module except one whose range it is (\texttt{pnew\_\allowbreak{}ok}). PSPLIT needs two free module numbers (\texttt{module\_\allowbreak{}room}). PMERGE needs one free number and two ranges that, laid end to end in one order or the other, form one range (\texttt{pmerge\_\allowbreak{}ok}). When the check fails the step traps: $\mathit{err} \gets \mathrm{true}$, the error code is set, $\mathit{PC} \gets \texttt{LASSERT\_\allowbreak{}TRAP\_\allowbreak{}PC}$, the cost is charged, and the graph is unchanged. From a well-formed graph with no modules and a module counter at most 64, every reachable graph has pairwise disjoint, contiguous regions inside data memory, at most 64 modules, and every module number below 64, so reading a module number modulo 64 changes no live number (\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}reachable\_\allowbreak{}partition\_\allowbreak{}in\_\allowbreak{}bounds}).

\subsection{Logical Operations}
These interact with information content:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\texttt{LASSERT}(\mathit{freg}, \mathit{creg}, \mathit{kind}, \mathit{flen}, \mathit{cost})$: Assert a formula read from \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mem}[\texttt{R[freg]}] (encoded-unit count $\mathit{flen}$, eight priced bits per unit; the factor $8$ is a pricing convention) against a witness package at \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mem}[\texttt{R[creg]}]. In SAT mode the package contains one satisfying assignment and one falsifying assignment, stored back-to-back; $\Delta\mu = \mathit{flen} \times 8 + S(\mathit{cost}) \geq 1$. The step succeeds only when $\mathit{flen}$ matches the header length of the formula in memory (\texttt{lassert\_\allowbreak{}hw\_\allowbreak{}flen}); a mismatch traps like a failed check.
    \item $\texttt{LJOIN}(\mathit{c1reg}, \mathit{c2reg}, \Delta\mu)$: Advance the PC and charge $S(\Delta\mu)$. It neither reads nor compares the certificate strings.
    \item $\texttt{REVEAL}(m, n, \mathit{cert}, \Delta\mu)$: Add $n$ to the $\mu$-tensor entry at index $m \bmod 16$, advance the PC, and charge $n + S(\Delta\mu)$. The certificate string is not examined.
    \item $\texttt{EMIT}(m, p, \Delta\mu)$: Emit payload $p$ from module $m$; cost is $|p|_{\mathrm{bits}} + S(\Delta\mu)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Computational Operations}
Register/memory operations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\texttt{XFER}(\mathit{dst}, \mathit{src}, \Delta\mu)$: Copy register $\mathit{src}$ to $\mathit{dst}$.
    \item $\texttt{XOR\_\allowbreak{}LOAD}(\mathit{dst}, \mathit{addr}, \Delta\mu)$: Load memory word at $\mathit{addr}$ (a plain load; no XOR is involved).
    \item $\texttt{XOR\_\allowbreak{}ADD}(\mathit{dst}, \mathit{src}, \Delta\mu)$: $\mathit{dst} \gets \mathit{dst} \oplus \mathit{src}$.
    \item $\texttt{XOR\_\allowbreak{}SWAP}(a, b, \Delta\mu)$: Swap registers $a$ and $b$.
    \item $\texttt{XOR\_\allowbreak{}RANK}(\mathit{dst}, \mathit{src}, \Delta\mu)$: $\mathit{dst} \gets \mathrm{popcount}(\mathit{src})$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Standard ALU and Control Flow}
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\texttt{LOAD\_\allowbreak{}IMM}(\mathit{dst}, \mathit{imm}, \Delta\mu)$: Load the immediate, truncated to 64 bits, into register.
    \item $\texttt{LOAD}(\mathit{dst}, \mathit{addr}, \Delta\mu)$: Load from main memory.
    \item $\texttt{STORE}(\mathit{addr}, \mathit{src}, \Delta\mu)$: Store to main memory.
    \item $\texttt{ADD}(\mathit{dst}, r_1, r_2, \Delta\mu)$: $\mathit{dst} \gets r_1 + r_2 \pmod{2^{64}}$.
    \item $\texttt{SUB}(\mathit{dst}, r_1, r_2, \Delta\mu)$: $\mathit{dst} \gets r_1 - r_2 \pmod{2^{64}}$.
    \item $\texttt{JUMP}(\mathit{target}, \Delta\mu)$: Unconditional jump.
    \item $\texttt{JNEZ}(r, \mathit{target}, \Delta\mu)$: Jump if $R[r] \neq 0$.
    \item $\texttt{CALL}(\mathit{target}, \Delta\mu)$: Push return address, jump to target.
    \item $\texttt{RET}(\Delta\mu)$: Pop return address, jump back.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Auxiliary Operations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\texttt{CHSH\_\allowbreak{}TRIAL}(x, y, a, b, \Delta\mu)$: Run a CHSH correlation trial; $x,y,a,b \in \{0,1\}$ required.
    \item $\texttt{CHECKPOINT}(\mathit{label}, \Delta\mu)$: Execution checkpoint (charges $\mu$).
    \item $\texttt{READ\_\allowbreak{}PORT}(\mathit{dst}, \mathit{ch}, \mathit{val}, \mathit{bits}, \Delta\mu)$: Read from I/O channel (positive-cost class).
    \item $\texttt{WRITE\_\allowbreak{}PORT}(\mathit{ch}, \mathit{src}, \Delta\mu)$: Write to I/O channel.
    \item $\texttt{HEAP\_\allowbreak{}LOAD}(\mathit{dst}, \mathit{addr}, \Delta\mu)$: Load from heap-relative address.
    \item $\texttt{HEAP\_\allowbreak{}STORE}(\mathit{addr}, \mathit{src}, \Delta\mu)$: Store to heap-relative address.
    \item $\texttt{HALT}(\Delta\mu)$: Advance the PC and charge $\Delta\mu$; execution ends only when the PC leaves the program or the fuel runs out.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Categorical Morphism Operations}
These 7 opcodes (0x27--0x2D) implement the categorical layer over partition modules. See Chapter~\ref{ch:categorical} for laws and proofs.
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\texttt{MORPH}(\mathit{dst}, \mathit{src}, \mathit{tgt}, \mathit{idx}, \Delta\mu)$: Create morphism $\mathit{src} \to \mathit{tgt}$; write morphism ID to $\texttt{regs}[\mathit{dst}]$.
    \item $\texttt{COMPOSE}(\mathit{dst}, f, g, \Delta\mu)$: Compose $f;g$ when $f.\mathrm{tgt} = g.\mathrm{src}$; errors on type mismatch.
    \item $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ID}(\mathit{dst}, m, \Delta\mu)$: Create identity morphism $\mathrm{id}_m$ (diagonal relation).
    \item $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}DELETE}(f, \Delta\mu)$: Remove morphism $f$; cascades when modules are split/merged.
    \item $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ASSERT}(f, \mathit{prop}, \mathit{cert}, \Delta\mu)$: \textbf{Cert-setter.} Charges $S(\Delta\mu) \geq 1$ unconditionally. Even failed attempts (morphism does not exist) incur cost $1$. This is the strongest form of the NoFI law: the machine refuses to let even a doomed attempt at certifying a relational claim slip through for free.
    \item $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}TENSOR}(\mathit{dst}, f, g, \Delta\mu)$: Tensor product $f \otimes g$ (requires the union modules to exist, the source regions of $f$ and $g$ to be disjoint, and the target regions to be disjoint).
    \item $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}GET}(\mathit{dst}, f, \mathit{sel}, \Delta\mu)$: Read morphism metadata: selector $0=\mathrm{src}$, $1=\mathrm{tgt}$, $2=\mathrm{coupling\ length}$, $3=\mathrm{is\_\allowbreak{}identity}$.
\end{itemize}

\emph{A reminder before the machinery: this ISA is scaffolding. It is one way to build the substrate, not the substrate itself. Burn all 51 opcodes and the machine is exactly as real, because the machine is the law, not the instruction set.}

The active implementation exposes a 51-opcode ISA: 14 arithmetic/data, 8 memory and control flow, 7 I/O and tensor, 4 partition operations, 3 logic and certification, 8 witness and certification (\texttt{CHSH\_\allowbreak{}TRIAL}, \texttt{CERTIFY}, \texttt{REVEAL}, \texttt{CHSH\_\allowbreak{}LASSERT}, and the four $Q_{1+AB}$ check variants), 7 categorical morphism operations ($14+8+7+4+3+8+7=51$). The CPU implements 47 of the 51. The four $Q_{1+AB}$ variants are in \texttt{kami\_\allowbreak{}step}, the Gallina model of the hardware step, whose step agrees with the kernel's, and the CPU has no opcode for them; they run in the kernel, the extracted runner and the Python VM. This condensed mathematical specification presents the core instruction families relevant to the kernel semantics and cost model. Every instruction carries an explicit $\Delta\mu$ parameter; the function $\texttt{instruction\_\allowbreak{}cost}$ extracts it.

\section{Cost Model}
Every instruction $i$ carries an explicit cost parameter $\Delta\mu_i \in \N$. The function $\texttt{instruction\_\allowbreak{}cost}(i)$ extracts this parameter. The cost is applied via $\texttt{apply\_\allowbreak{}cost}(s, i) = s.\mu + \texttt{instruction\_\allowbreak{}cost}(i)$. Since $\Delta\mu_i : \N$, the $\mu$-ledger is monotonically non-decreasing by construction.

The mandatory positive-cost class is: $\texttt{CERTIFY}$, $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ASSERT}$, and $\texttt{LJOIN}$ (each charges $S(\Delta\mu) \geq 1$); $\texttt{EMIT}$ ($|\mathit{payload}|_{\mathrm{bits}} + S(\Delta\mu)$); $\texttt{REVEAL}$ and $\texttt{READ\_\allowbreak{}PORT}$ ($\mathit{bits} + S(\Delta\mu)$); and $\texttt{LASSERT}$ ($\mathit{flen} \times 8 + S(\Delta\mu)$). These cannot be zero-cost even with $\Delta\mu = 0$. The five \texttt{CHSH\_\allowbreak{}LASSERT} family instructions also charge $S(\Delta\mu)$. Other instructions charge their encoded $\Delta\mu$, which may be zero.

\begin{theorem}[Least joint unit floor, \textbf{(S)}]
For a finite family of event predicates on instructions, the least natural-valued schedule satisfying a unit floor on each event is the indicator that at least one event occurs. This is \texttt{joint\_\allowbreak{}floor\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}least}. Independent Boolean coordinates can change together for one unit under these rules; additivity requires an additional condition.
\end{theorem}

\section{The \texttt{CERTIFY} instruction}
\label{sec:certify_def}

\texttt{CERTIFY} is referred to throughout Chapters~\ref{ch:nofi} onward and is
defined here.

\begin{definition}[\texttt{CERTIFY} {\normalfont (\texttt{instr\_\allowbreak{}certify}, VMStep.v)}]
$\texttt{CERTIFY}(\Delta\mu)$ has the step semantics
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{pc} \mapsto \mathrm{pc}+1,\qquad
\mu \mapsto \mu + S(\Delta\mu),\qquad
\mathrm{cert} \mapsto \mathrm{true},
\end{equation}
with every other state component unchanged. Its cost is $S(\Delta\mu) \geq 1$,
which is the concrete instance of A2 (Section~\ref{sec:axiom_scheme}) for this
ISA. It is the \emph{only} rule in \texttt{VMStep.v} that sets
$\mathrm{cert} := \mathrm{true}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[\texttt{CERTIFY} is unguarded; what that does and does not mean]
\label{rem:certify_unguarded}
The rule fires for every state and every $\Delta\mu$: it carries no
precondition, takes no proposition as an argument, and checks nothing. Setting
the certification bit is therefore always \emph{available}, at a price of at
least $1$. Conversely, the instruction that does perform a decidable check on
data, namely \texttt{LASSERT}, which traps on failure, leaves both the
certification CSR and $\mathrm{cert}$ unchanged.

The description of the mechanism is therefore \emph{metering}, not
verification: the step rule guarantees that flipping the bit is priced, not
that the bit is only set when some claim holds. Statements of the form ``this
machine makes stamping \emph{verified} impossible'' overstate it; what is
impossible is stamping it \emph{for free}.

The ISA does contain genuinely guarded instructions.
\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ASSERT} requires the named morphism to exist, and
\texttt{CHSH\_\allowbreak{}LASSERT} runs \texttt{column\_\allowbreak{}contractive\_\allowbreak{}check\_\allowbreak{}witness} on
actual witness data and traps on failure, with a soundness bridge to PSD. A substrate with a guarded certification bit is formalised separately, on the
Coq standard library alone, in \texttt{minimal/\allowbreak{}EarnedCore.v}: a
two-counter machine with a table of checked facts and three priced
instructions. \texttt{CHECK} writes a fact (property, counter, the counter's
current version) only when the property holds; \texttt{COMMIT} succeeds only
on such a fact at the counter's current version; \texttt{CERTIFY} succeeds
only after a commitment. From a clean start every certified run contains a
passing check, a passing commitment of the same claim with the counter
untouched in between, and a passing certification
(\texttt{earned\_\allowbreak{}certification\_\allowbreak{}provenance}); a fact
whose counter has not been written since its check names a true claim
(\texttt{checker\_\allowbreak{}soundness}); every certified run costs at least
$3$ (\texttt{certified\_\allowbreak{}run\_\allowbreak{}min\_\allowbreak{}cost}).
Its prices are still flat, one per instruction, so A2 holds there by the
price list and is not derived from the cost of establishing the claim; see
Remark~\ref{rem:a2_stipulated}. A host on the same machine,
in \texttt{minimal/\allowbreak{}UniversalThiele.v}, runs any of its programs
as a guest, keeps a mirror equal to the guest's certified flag
(\texttt{record\_\allowbreak{}agreement}), and raises the mirror only in a
guest step that passed \texttt{CERTIFY}, charged in that step
(\texttt{toll\_\allowbreak{}enforced\_\allowbreak{}by\_\allowbreak{}host\_\allowbreak{}step});
a machine from elsewhere enters through the two-counter encoding with its
computation and toll kept but not its own prices.
\end{remark}

\chapter{Operational Semantics}

\section{Transition Function}
The transition $\delta: \Sspace \times \Ispace \to \Sspace$ is defined by:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mathit{PC}' = \mathit{PC} + 1$ for non-control-flow instructions; JUMP, JNEZ, CALL, and RET set PC to the target address; HALT advances PC like any other non-control-flow instruction.
    \item $\mu' = \mu + \Delta\mu_i$.
    \item $\mathit{err}' = \mathit{err} \lor \mathrm{fail}(S, i)$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Traces and Execution}
\begin{definition}[Trace]
A trace $\tau = [i_0, \dots, i_k] \in \Ispace^*$ is a finite sequence of instructions.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Execution]
$\mathrm{Run}([], S) = S$; \quad $\mathrm{Run}(i::\tau', S) = \mathrm{Run}(\tau', \delta(S,i))$. This is \texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}instrs} in \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}NecessityOfMuLedger.v}, a fold of \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}apply} over the list. The bounded executor \texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}vm} (\texttt{SimulationProof.v}) takes a fuel bound, fetches the instruction at the current PC, and may skip or repeat instructions. Theorems stated with a fuel argument use \texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}vm} or \texttt{trace\_\allowbreak{}run} (\texttt{RevelationRequirement.v}); the abstract-substrate theorems fold over the list directly.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Unique descended values, \textbf{(S)}]
Let $V(t)$ be evaluation of a VM instruction list from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state}, and let $T(t)$ be target evaluation from a fixed basepoint. Then
\[
\bigl(\forall t,u,\ V(t)=V(u)\Rightarrow T(t)=T(u)\bigr)
\]
is equivalent to each reachable VM state having exactly one target value represented by its traces. This is \texttt{trace\_\allowbreak{}descent\_\allowbreak{}unique\_\allowbreak{}value\_\allowbreak{}iff}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive reachable simulation, \textbf{(S)}]
Supply a function $r$ selecting an instruction list for each VM state, satisfying $V(r(V(t)))=V(t)$ for every trace $t$. A map preserving the basepoint, steps on reachable states, and certification exists exactly when target evaluation respects equal VM outcomes and target certification agrees with VM certification on every trace. The map is $s\mapsto T(r(s))$ and is pointwise unique on reachable states. This is \texttt{reachable\_\allowbreak{}simulation\_\allowbreak{}exists\_\allowbreak{}iff} together with \texttt{reachable\_\allowbreak{}simulation\_\allowbreak{}unique}. A selector exists: \texttt{reachable\_\allowbreak{}trace\_\allowbreak{}representative} returns, for each state, the first trace in an enumeration of all traces that evaluates to it, found by search with the standard-library axiom \texttt{sig\_\allowbreak{}forall\_\allowbreak{}dec} (\texttt{reachable\_\allowbreak{}trace\_\allowbreak{}representative\_\allowbreak{}correct}). It is a search, not a program that rebuilds a trace from the state. No extension of the step law to unreachable states is asserted.
\end{theorem}

The certification quotient gives an explicit inhabited target. Conversely, the trace-history target satisfies A2 and certification agreement but fails descent: the empty trace and \texttt{JUMP 0 0} reach the same VM state while leaving distinct histories.

\section{Selected Instruction Semantics}

\textbf{PNEW}: Claim the range \texttt{pnew\_\allowbreak{}region}$(R)$. If a module already owns exactly that range, return it and leave the graph unchanged; otherwise add a module with the next module ID, no axioms, and that range. The step traps instead when no module number is free, the range runs past data memory, or it overlaps another module's range (\texttt{pnew\_\allowbreak{}ok}).

\textbf{REVEAL}: Add $\mathit{bits}$ to the $\mu$-tensor entry at index (module $\bmod\ 16$); the CSRs are unchanged and the certificate $\mathit{cert}$ is not examined. Cost: $\mathit{bits} + S(\Delta\mu)$.

\textbf{LASSERT}: Read formula from \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mem}[\texttt{R[formula\_\allowbreak{}addr\_\allowbreak{}reg]}] (encoded-unit count \texttt{formula\_\allowbreak{}len}, eight priced bits per unit, a pricing convention) and witness package from \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mem}[\texttt{R[cert\_\allowbreak{}addr\_\allowbreak{}reg]}]. \texttt{cert\_\allowbreak{}kind = true} (SAT): verify using a satisfying model plus a falsifying countermodel (dual-witness). \texttt{cert\_\allowbreak{}kind = false} (UNSAT): the kernel step always fails at this path, \texttt{lassert\_\allowbreak{}check\_\allowbreak{}ok} returns \texttt{false} unconditionally; UNSAT certificates are not checked, and LRAT checking is not invoked in the step semantics. Success also requires \texttt{formula\_\allowbreak{}len} to match the header length of the formula in memory (\texttt{lassert\_\allowbreak{}hw\_\allowbreak{}flen}). If the check succeeds, the step advances the PC with no trap and no error latch, and no certification state changes; if it fails, or the length does not match, the PC jumps to \texttt{LASSERT\_\allowbreak{}TRAP\_\allowbreak{}PC} and $\mathit{err} \gets \mathrm{true}$. LASSERT does not mutate the partition graph's axiom set; cert-channel activation happens through the subsequent CERTIFY or MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ASSERT. Cost: $\mathtt{formula\_\allowbreak{}len} \times 8 + S(\mathtt{mu\_\allowbreak{}delta})$.

\chapter{No Free Insight}
\label{ch:nofi}

The central theorem: structural insight is never free.

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[Receipt Predicate]
$P: \mathcal{O} \to \B$ is a computable function on observation histories.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Strength Ordering]
$P_1 \le P_2$ iff $\forall o.\; P_1(o)=1 \implies P_2(o)=1$.
$P_1 < P_2$ iff $P_1 \le P_2$ and $\exists o.\; P_2(o)=1 \wedge P_1(o)=0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Certification]
Trace $\tau$ \textbf{certifies} $P$ iff execution succeeds ($\mathit{err}=0$), the supra-certificate flag is set, and the output satisfies $P$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structure Addition]
$\mathrm{HasStructureAddition}(\tau)$ is true iff some step of the bounded run of $\tau$ changes the certification CSR \texttt{csr\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}addr} from zero to non-zero (\texttt{has\_\allowbreak{}structure\_\allowbreak{}addition}). Membership of an instruction in $\tau$ is not execution. The opcode list \texttt{cert\_\allowbreak{}addr\_\allowbreak{}setterb} (REVEAL, EMIT, LJOIN, LASSERT, MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ASSERT) is a positive-cost policy class and a superset of what writes the CSR.
\end{definition}

\section{The Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[No Free Insight {\normalfont (NoFreeInsight\_\allowbreak{}Theorem.v)}]
\label{thm:nfi}
Let $P_{\mathrm{strong}} < P_{\mathrm{weak}}$. If a trace $\tau$ starting from a clean state $s_0$ certifies $P_{\mathrm{strong}}$, then:
\[ \mathrm{Certified}(\tau, P_{\mathrm{strong}}) \implies \mathrm{HasStructureAddition}(\tau) \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
The statement is parametric: \texttt{NoFreeInsight\_\allowbreak{}Theorem.v} states it for any system satisfying the \texttt{NO\_\allowbreak{}FREE\_\allowbreak{}INSIGHT\_\allowbreak{}SYSTEM} module type interface, by applying the interface's no-free-insight field. The interface requires four contract obligations (not Coq \texttt{Axiom} declarations, they are proved separately for the kernel instantiation): (1)~$\mu$-monotonicity, (2)~a certification specification linking \texttt{certifies} to execution, (3)~a no-free-insight contract stating that strict strengthening requires structure addition, and (4)~that the system has a notion of clean start. The theorem file itself contains zero \texttt{Axiom}, zero \texttt{Admitted}. The load-bearing proof is the kernel instantiation (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}nofi/\allowbreak{}Instance\_\allowbreak{}Kernel.v}). There the strictness hypothesis $P_{\mathrm{strong}} < P_{\mathrm{weak}}$ is not used. The content is: from \texttt{csr\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}addr} $=0$, a run to a certified state contains a step that changes \texttt{csr\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}addr} from zero to non-zero. It is evidence that certification requires structure addition, not that strengthening does. The predicate-level form that uses strictness is \texttt{strengthening\_\allowbreak{}requires\_\allowbreak{}structure\_\allowbreak{}addition} (\texttt{NoFreeInsight.v}), with premises \texttt{strictly\_\allowbreak{}stronger} $P_{\mathrm{strong}}\ P_{\mathrm{weak}}$, initial \texttt{csr\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}addr} $=0$, and a certified state after \texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}vm} with a fuel bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Cost of Insight]
From an initial state with \texttt{csr\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}addr} $=0$, a run of \texttt{thiele\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}machine} that ends with \texttt{csr\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}addr} $\neq 0$ raises $\mu$ by at least $1$ (\texttt{partition\_\allowbreak{}refinement\_\allowbreak{}nonfree}, \texttt{PartitionRefinementNoFI.v}).
\end{corollary}

\section{$\mu$-Ledger Necessity}

\begin{theorem}[Observation adequacy, \textbf{(S)}]
For observation $o:S\to V$ and query $q:S\to A$, the existence of a decoder $d:V\to A$ satisfying $d(o(s))=q(s)$ implies
\[
o(s)=o(t)\ \Longrightarrow\ q(s)=q(t).
\]
Given a section $r:V\to S$ with $o(r(v))=v$, this condition is also sufficient: $d=q\circ r$. These are \texttt{decoding\_\allowbreak{}requires\_\allowbreak{}fiber\_\allowbreak{}constancy} and \texttt{selected\_\allowbreak{}representatives\_\allowbreak{}give\_\allowbreak{}decoder}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[$\mu$-Ledger Necessity and Complete Independence Classification {\normalfont (NecessityOfMuLedger.v, kernel/NecessityAbstract.v)}, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:mu_ledger_necessity}
Five independence results are formally proven, zero Admitted:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mu \perp (\texttt{mem}, \texttt{regs}, \texttt{pc})$: no strict-classical oracle recovers $\mu$ \hfill \texttt{turing\_\allowbreak{}ram\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}necessity}
\item $\texttt{certified} \perp (\texttt{mem}, \texttt{regs}, \texttt{pc})$: no strict-classical oracle recovers certification \hfill \texttt{turing\_\allowbreak{}ram\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}necessity}
\item $\texttt{certified} \perp (\texttt{mem}, \texttt{regs}, \texttt{pc}, \mu)$: knowing $\mu$ does not determine certification \hfill \texttt{cost\_\allowbreak{}model\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}necessity}
\item $\mu \perp (\texttt{mem}, \texttt{regs}, \texttt{pc}, \texttt{certified})$: knowing certification does not determine $\mu$ \hfill \texttt{cert\_\allowbreak{}model\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}necessity}
\item $\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}graph} \perp (\texttt{mem}, \texttt{regs}, \texttt{pc}, \mu, \texttt{certified})$: the categorical layer is independent of the full $\mu$-ledger shadow \hfill \texttt{graph\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}recoverable\_\allowbreak{}from\_\allowbreak{}P\_\allowbreak{}full}
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Three-component independence} (\texttt{thiele\_\allowbreak{}state\_\allowbreak{}three\_\allowbreak{}component\_\allowbreak{}independence}): $\mu$, \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified}, and \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}graph} are the three provably independent non-classical state components. Each of $\mu$, \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified}, and \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}graph} is irrecoverable from the specific projections listed in items 1 to 5 above. Not proved: recovery of $\mu$ or \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified} from a projection that also includes \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}graph}.

\textbf{Non-droppability, not representation minimality} (\texttt{P\_\allowbreak{}full\_\allowbreak{}complete\_\allowbreak{}neither\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}nor\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}droppable}): $(\texttt{mem}, \texttt{regs}, \texttt{pc}, \mu, \texttt{certified})$ is complete for returning $\mu$ and certification, and neither named coordinate can be dropped from this projection while retaining its corresponding answer. No ordering over arbitrary encodings is defined, so this is not a smallest-representation theorem. Graph structure is a third independent axis.

\textbf{Universality} (\texttt{mu\_\allowbreak{}ledger\_\allowbreak{}necessity\_\allowbreak{}universal}): The separation holds from \emph{every} VMState, not only from crafted zero-state witnesses. From any reachable state, \texttt{CERTIFY 0} and \texttt{PNEW [] 0} produce identical strict classical shadows but different $\mu$. Certification also differs when the source state is uncertified.

\textbf{Trace closure} (\texttt{po1\_\allowbreak{}trace\_\allowbreak{}necessity}): For any common prefix program of any length, the two witness extensions maintain equal strict shadows but strictly different $\mu$. No accumulation of classical computation closes the gap.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
Each separation uses explicit witnesses where equal projected inputs force equal oracle outputs, while the witness states require unequal outputs, a contradiction. Strict shadow equality: both \texttt{CERTIFY 0} and \texttt{PNEW [] 0} advance \texttt{pc} by 1 and preserve \texttt{mem}/\texttt{regs}. $\mu$ divergence: CERTIFY charges $S(0)=1$; PNEW charges 0. Graph separation uses \texttt{categorical\_\allowbreak{}separation} from \texttt{PartitionSeparation.v}: two states with identical $P_{\text{full}}$ but different \texttt{pg\_\allowbreak{}morphisms}. Universality follows because \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}apply\_\allowbreak{}certify\_\allowbreak{}strict\_\allowbreak{}shadow} and \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}apply\_\allowbreak{}pnew\_\allowbreak{}strict\_\allowbreak{}shadow} hold for arbitrary source states.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Latent $\mu$: Trace Cost for a Fixed Schedule {\normalfont (NecessityOfMuLedger.v §7)}, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:latent_mu}
The $\mu$-cost accumulated by any computation is a property of the instruction sequence, not of any tracking system. Four results, all zero Admitted:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{shadow\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}computation\_\allowbreak{}intrinsic}: For any program \texttt{prog} and any starting state $s$:
\[
(\texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}instrs}\ s\ \texttt{prog}).\mu = s.\mu + \texttt{trace\_\allowbreak{}total\_\allowbreak{}cost}(\texttt{prog})
\]
The increment is determined entirely by the instruction sequence, independent of vm\_\allowbreak{}graph, vm\_\allowbreak{}regs, vm\_\allowbreak{}mem, vm\_\allowbreak{}certified, and the starting $\mu$.

\item \texttt{shadow\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}delta\_\allowbreak{}universal}: Two machines running the same program from any two starting states accumulate the same additional $\mu$. The cost is written in the program before execution begins.

\item \texttt{shadow\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}unique\_\allowbreak{}accounting}: Any accounting $M$ that increments by the schedule's cost on every instruction (\texttt{instruction\_\allowbreak{}consistent}) and satisfies $M(s) = 0$ at the starting state assigns exactly \texttt{trace\_\allowbreak{}total\_\allowbreak{}cost} to the result. Replace the Thiele Machine's $\mu$-counter with any other such accounting and you get the same number.

\item \texttt{shadow\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}inevitable}: Any program containing an instruction with positive declared cost accumulates strictly positive $\mu$ from any starting state. This conclusion requires a positive-cost instruction.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Interpretation.} Given a trace and this cost schedule, its sum is determined even if the observer does not retain the ledger. This does not establish a physical bill for every classical computation. A full encoding may retain the ledger; the irrecoverability theorem concerns the specified projection.
\end{theorem}

\section{Partition Refinement and No Free Insight}

PNEW, PSPLIT, and PMERGE are not cert-setters and can carry $\Delta\mu = 0$:

    \item \texttt{partition\_\allowbreak{}structural\_\allowbreak{}ops\_\allowbreak{}can\_\allowbreak{}be\_\allowbreak{}free}: All three can execute with $\Delta\mu = 0$.
    \item \texttt{partition\_\allowbreak{}structural\_\allowbreak{}trace\_\allowbreak{}cannot\_\allowbreak{}certify}: A trace containing only PNEW, PSPLIT, and PMERGE, at any costs, leaves \texttt{csr\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}addr} where it started.

\chapter{The $\mu$-Chaitin Theorem}

A Chaitin-style bound denominated in $\mu$-currency. ``Proves $k$ bits'' is an abstract predicate supplied by an instance of the interface; the theorem is not a statement about provability in a fixed formal system.

\begin{theorem}[$\mu$-Chaitin {\normalfont (MuChaitinTheory\_\allowbreak{}TraceLocal.v)}]
For any instance $T$ of the interface \texttt{MU\_\allowbreak{}CHAITIN\_\allowbreak{}TRACE\_\allowbreak{}SYSTEM}, the number of bits $k$ the theory can certify is bounded by:
\[ k \le |T| + c \]
where $|T|$ is the description length of the theory and $c$ is a fixed overhead constant (\texttt{proves\_\allowbreak{}bits\_\allowbreak{}bounded\_\allowbreak{}by\_\allowbreak{}description\_\allowbreak{}trace\_\allowbreak{}local}). The proof chain: $k \le \mathrm{payload} \le \mu\text{-info} \le \mathrm{budget} \le |T| + c$. The description-length budget is an obligation of the interface, not derived. Pricing is required only of the instructions on the theory's own traces (\texttt{priced\_\allowbreak{}on\_\allowbreak{}traces}), and the $\mu$ payment is derived from the kernel's certification theorem.
\end{theorem}

The interface \texttt{MuChaitinTheory\_\allowbreak{}Interface} (with \texttt{MuChaitinTheory\_\allowbreak{}Theorem.v}) requires every instruction to be priced. The VM's cost schedule does not meet that (\texttt{current\_\allowbreak{}schedule\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}globally\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}priced}), so that interface has no VM instance. \section{Strict Containment}

\begin{theorem}[Turing $\subsetneq$ Thiele, as instruction sets {\normalfont (kernel/Subsumption.v)}]
The \texttt{Kernel} instruction language is \textbf{properly extended} by its sighted programs:
\[ \exists\, p.\; \mathrm{sighted}(p) \wedge \neg\mathrm{turing}(p) \]
There exist \texttt{Kernel} programs that are not Turing programs. The witness is the one-instruction program \texttt{[H\_\allowbreak{}ClaimTapeIsZero 0]}. The file stands alone: nothing in it mentions \texttt{VMState}, \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}step}, \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu}, or \texttt{instruction\_\allowbreak{}cost}. The separation is syntactic: the instruction language is larger. It is not a claim that the Thiele model computes any function a Turing machine cannot. The extra structure is new content in what a program can say, not in what it can compute.
\end{theorem}

Turing completeness of the VM instruction set is a separate result. \texttt{TuringCompletenessISA.v} compiles a 2-counter Minsky machine to five opcodes executed through \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}apply}, within the 64-bit word bound. \texttt{thiele\_\allowbreak{}strictly\_\allowbreak{}extends\_\allowbreak{}turing} (\texttt{ProperSubsumption.v}) simulates every Turing computation in a local \texttt{Thiele\_Config} record with cost certificates. \texttt{TuringClassicalEmbedding.v} takes the classical opcode fragment as the Turing notion, with the identity embedding into the Thiele programs; it does not simulate \texttt{KernelTM.v} inside that fragment.
\section{Oracle Cost Lower Bound}
\begin{theorem}[Oracle pricing self-consistency (conceptual; not separately formalized)]
Inside the No Free Insight framework, the universal lower bound on certification cost (\texttt{universal\_\allowbreak{}nfi\_\allowbreak{}any\_\allowbreak{}substrate} in \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}nfi/\allowbreak{}UniversalCertificationCost.v}) immediately implies that any sound oracle satisfying the one-step rule \texttt{cs\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}costs} (cost $\ge 1$ on any uncertified-to-certified transition) charges $\ge 1$ on any trace that takes the oracle's certification from false to true. The justification for charging $\mu > 0$ on oracle queries comes from NoFI, which is an accounting law, not a physical one: gaining certified information about undecidable propositions has positive $\mu$-cost.
\end{theorem}
\section{$\mu$-budget predicates}

\begin{definition}[$\mu$-budget predicates {\normalfont (kernel/MuComplexity.v)}]
Let $p_{\mathrm{fuel}}, p_{\mu} : \N \to \N$ be numeric bounds and $\mathrm{size} : \mathrm{VMState} \to \N$ a size function. Polynomiality is not required.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{error\_\allowbreak{}free\_\allowbreak{}preservation\_\allowbreak{}budget} means that every input has an error-free trace within the length and $\mu$ bounds, and that $f$ has equal values on the initial and final states whenever $\mu$ increases.
    \item \texttt{positive\_\allowbreak{}input\_\allowbreak{}certification\_\allowbreak{}budget} means that every input satisfying $f$ has a trace within those bounds ending with \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified = true}.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Zero-$\mu$ traces satisfy the preservation budget {\normalfont (kernel/MuComplexity.v)}, \textbf{(S)}]
\texttt{zero\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}traces\_\allowbreak{}satisfy\_\allowbreak{}preservation\_\allowbreak{}budget}: if every input has a bounded, error-free, zero-$\mu$ trace, the preservation budget holds with $\mu$-bound zero for every Boolean state predicate. Its positive-$\mu$ antecedent never holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
These predicates supply neither a uniform solver with a correct output nor a sound verifier rejecting negative inputs. They do not define decision or verification complexity classes. The implication between them is named as a premise, not proved. The factored-search witness in \texttt{StructuralAdvantage.v} gives a concrete program-level $\mu$-cost comparison: a program whose instruction costs sum to $18$ (\texttt{sighted\_\allowbreak{}program\_\allowbreak{}total\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}eighteen}) and whose iteration count is $2N$, against $N^2$ for the blind program. The VM runs are proved for every $N$ by loop invariants: from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state} the blind program on the last of $N^2$ cells stops with $N^2$ passes and $\mu = 0$, and the sighted program stops with $2N$ passes and $\mu = 18$; at a price of $\lambda$ passes per unit of $\mu$ the sighted run is cheaper exactly when $2N + 18\lambda < N^2$ (\texttt{time\_\allowbreak{}tax\_\allowbreak{}theorem}). That is not a class separation.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[$\mu$-Cost Hierarchy {\normalfont (kernel/MuHierarchyTheorem.v)}, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:mu-hierarchy}
For every $k \geq 1$, the $\mu$-dimension admits a strict hierarchy:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Achievability}: there exists a trace from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state} with $\mu$-cost exactly $k$ that is \emph{level-$k$ certified}, the run executes a \texttt{CERTIFY}~$\delta$ instruction with $S(\delta) \geq k$ and ends with \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified = true}.
  \item \textbf{Lower bound}: any level-$k$ certified trace from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state} must have spent at least $k$ units of $\mu$-cost.
\end{enumerate}
Formally (\texttt{mu\_\allowbreak{}hierarchy\_\allowbreak{}theorem}): for every $k \geq 1$, the witness trace $[\texttt{instr\_\allowbreak{}certify}(k{-}1)]$ (cost $= S(k{-}1) = k$) executes a level-$k$ \texttt{CERTIFY} from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state}; conversely, the trace-side predicate \texttt{level\_\allowbreak{}k\_\allowbreak{}certified} forces $\texttt{trace\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}cost} \geq k$ via \texttt{level\_\allowbreak{}k\_\allowbreak{}certification\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}floor}, since the \texttt{CERTIFY}'s declared cost appears in the executed-instruction ledger and the ledger sum equals the trace cost (\texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}vm\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}conservation}).

\textbf{Corollary} (\texttt{mu\_\allowbreak{}hierarchy\_\allowbreak{}no\_\allowbreak{}upper\_\allowbreak{}bound}): no fixed $\mu$-budget suffices for all levels. The $\mu$-dimension is unbounded and non-collapsible. Zero Admitted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[$\mu$-Hierarchy as a P-vs-NP analogue, not a P-vs-NP result]
Theorem~\ref{thm:mu-hierarchy} is the $\mu$-analogue of the classical time hierarchy theorem in shape. Its content is lighter. The level of a certificate is defined by the operand $\delta$ of the \texttt{CERTIFY} that issued it, and the cost schedule charges $\delta + 1$ for that instruction, so the floor comes from the schedule, not from a search or diagonal argument. It is not a statement about classical P versus NP, which this project does not touch. In the certified-structure setting it makes one thing precise: issuing a certificate at level $k$ costs $\mu \geq k$, because the schedule charges $\delta + 1$ for the \texttt{CERTIFY} that sets the level. It says nothing about how hard the certified thing was to find. The hierarchy is strict and unbounded, with zero \texttt{Admitted}, no oracle, and no diagonalization. The lower-bound proof is trace-side: a level-$k$ certified run executed a specific \texttt{CERTIFY}~$\delta$ with $S(\delta) \geq k$, and \texttt{executed\_\allowbreak{}instruction\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}recorded} plus \texttt{ledger\_\allowbreak{}sum\_\allowbreak{}contains\_\allowbreak{}lower\_\allowbreak{}bound} place that cost in the ledger sum, which \texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}vm\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}conservation} equates with the trace's $\mu$-cost.
\end{remark}

\section{Intrinsic level hierarchy}

\begin{definition}[Intrinsic level {\normalfont (coq/IntrinsicLevelHierarchy.v)}]
A final state $s$ is \emph{intrinsically certifiable at level $\geq k$}, written $\texttt{level\_\allowbreak{}intrinsic\_\allowbreak{}at\_\allowbreak{}least}(k, s)$, iff every $\texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}vm}$ trace from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state} that ends at $s$ with \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified = true} executes at least $k$ cert-setter instructions. Cert-setter here is the positive-cost class \texttt{is\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}setter} (REVEAL, EMIT, LJOIN, LASSERT, READ\_\allowbreak{}PORT, CERTIFY, MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ASSERT, and the \texttt{CHSH\_\allowbreak{}LASSERT} family), not only instructions that write the CSR:
\begin{multline*}
\forall \mathit{fuel}\,\mathit{trace}.\;
\texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}vm}(\mathit{fuel},\mathit{trace},\texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state})=s \\
{}\land\; s.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified}=\texttt{true}
\;\Rightarrow\; \texttt{cert\_\allowbreak{}events\_\allowbreak{}in}(\mathit{fuel},\mathit{trace},\texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state})\geq k.
\end{multline*}
The level is a property of the \emph{final state}, not of any particular trace reaching it.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic level forces $\mu$ {\normalfont (coq/IntrinsicLevelHierarchy.v::level\_\allowbreak{}intrinsic\_\allowbreak{}forces\_\allowbreak{}mu)}]
If $\texttt{level\_\allowbreak{}intrinsic\_\allowbreak{}at\_\allowbreak{}least}(k, s)$ holds and $s.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified} = \texttt{true}$, then for every trace reaching $s$ from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state}, $s.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu} \geq k$. Zero Admitted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Strict separation {\normalfont (coq/IntrinsicLevelHierarchy.v::level\_\allowbreak{}k\_\allowbreak{}plus\_\allowbreak{}1\_\allowbreak{}forces\_\allowbreak{}more\_\allowbreak{}than\_\allowbreak{}k\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}events)}]
\label{thm:intrinsic-strict-separation}
For every $k \geq 0$, if $\texttt{level\_\allowbreak{}intrinsic\_\allowbreak{}at\_\allowbreak{}least}(k+1, s)$ holds and $s.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified} = \texttt{true}$, then every trace reaching $s$ executes strictly more than $k$ cert-setter instructions. Zero Admitted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Theorem~\ref{thm:mu-hierarchy}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-strict-separation} is the state-level counterpart of the trace-level $\mu$-hierarchy. The trace-level version (\texttt{mu\_\allowbreak{}hierarchy\_\allowbreak{}theorem}, \texttt{level\_\allowbreak{}k\_\allowbreak{}certification\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}floor}) defines level on the trace, a trace is level-$k$ certified iff it executes a \texttt{CERTIFY}~$\delta$ with $S(\delta) \geq k$. The state-level version defines level on the final state, every trace reaching it requires at least $k$ cert-setter executions. The two are complementary entry points to the same hierarchy: the trace-level theorem gives the achievability witness ($[\texttt{instr\_\allowbreak{}certify}(k{-}1)]$ from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state}); the state-level theorem gives the lower bound on $\mu$ for any state intrinsically requiring $k$ cert-events. Both theorems are closed under the global context.
\end{remark}

\section{Verifier model and the bare-setting impossibility}

\begin{definition}[Bare verifier {\normalfont (coq/VerifierModel.v)}]
The bare-setting transcript type is $\texttt{BareTranscript} := \texttt{list StrictClassicalState}$. A bare verification problem is a record \texttt{BareVerificationProblem} carrying
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}honest\_\allowbreak{}claim} : $\texttt{VMState} \to \texttt{Prop}$;
    \item \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}explains} : $\texttt{VMState} \to \texttt{BareTranscript} \to \texttt{Prop}$ (the transcript-honestly-witnessed-by-state relation);
    \item \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}cheap\_\allowbreak{}budget}, \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}recompute\_\allowbreak{}cost} : $\N$.
\end{itemize}
A bare verifier is a record \texttt{BareVerifier} with $\texttt{bv\_\allowbreak{}decide} : \texttt{BareTranscript} \to \texttt{bool}$ and $\texttt{bv\_\allowbreak{}cost} : \texttt{BareTranscript} \to \N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Soundness, completeness, cheapness {\normalfont (coq/VerifierModel.v)}]
For a problem $P$ and verifier $V$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}sound}$(P, V) := \forall t.\; V(t) = \texttt{true} \Rightarrow \forall s.\; \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}explains}(P, s, t) \Rightarrow \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}honest\_\allowbreak{}claim}(P, s)$.
    \item \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}complete}$(P, V) := \forall s\, t.\; \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}honest\_\allowbreak{}claim}(P, s) \Rightarrow \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}explains}(P, s, t) \Rightarrow V(t) = \texttt{true}$.
    \item \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}cheap}$(P, V) := \forall t.\; \texttt{bv\_\allowbreak{}cost}(V, t) \leq \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}cheap\_\allowbreak{}budget}(P)$.
\end{itemize}
The soundness condition is \emph{strong}: the claim must hold for every state explaining the transcript, not merely for some.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bare-setting impossibility {\normalfont (coq/VerifierImpossibility.v::bare\_\allowbreak{}setting\_\allowbreak{}no\_\allowbreak{}sound\_\allowbreak{}complete\_\allowbreak{}verifier)}]
\label{thm:bare-verifier-impossibility}
There is no bare verifier simultaneously \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}sound} and \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}complete} on the verification problem $\texttt{mu\_\allowbreak{}eq\_\allowbreak{}one\_\allowbreak{}problem}$ whose claim is $\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu}(s) = 1$ and whose \texttt{bare\_\allowbreak{}explains} relation holds for the two witness states from \texttt{coq/NecessityOfMuLedger.v} against the shared strict-shadow trace.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\texttt{po1\_\allowbreak{}state\_\allowbreak{}A}$ (the cost-1 \texttt{CERTIFY 0} result, $\mu = 1$) and $\texttt{po1\_\allowbreak{}state\_\allowbreak{}B}$ (the cost-0 \texttt{PNEW [] 0} result, $\mu = 0$) project to identical strict-shadow traces. Completeness applied to $A$'s honest run forces $V$ to accept the shared transcript. Strong soundness applied to the same accepted transcript at the explanation by $B$ forces $\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu}(B) = 1$, contradicting $\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu}(B) = 0$. \texttt{Print Assumptions} returns \texttt{Closed under the global context}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Three sufficient interfaces {\normalfont (coq/VerifierEscape\_\allowbreak{}\{Substrate,Hardness,Interaction\}.v)}]
\label{thm:three_escapes}
Each kernel theorem below exhibits a constant-cost verifier that simultaneously satisfies soundness and completeness on the $\mu$-sensitive claim by augmenting the transcript with non-classical structure:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{substrate\_\allowbreak{}escape\_\allowbreak{}succeeds}, transcript is $\texttt{list VMState}$; verifier reads $\mu$ directly.
    \item \texttt{commitment\_\allowbreak{}contract\_\allowbreak{}verifier}, transcript is $\texttt{BareTranscript} \times \texttt{bool}$ (commitment bit); the \texttt{CommitmentBitContract} supplies exact agreement between the bit and the claim for every explaining state. The verifier reads the bit at abstract unit cost. No cryptographic security or runtime bound is proved.
    \item \texttt{interactive\_\allowbreak{}escape\_\allowbreak{}succeeds}, transcript is $\texttt{BareTranscript} \times \N$ (response); verifier checks the response against the claim.
\end{itemize}
Each closes under the global context with zero \texttt{Admitted}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Factorisation impossibility {\normalfont (coq/VerifierExhaustiveness.v::V\_\allowbreak{}does\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}factor\_\allowbreak{}through\_\allowbreak{}classical)}]
\label{thm:verifier-factorisation}
Let $T$ be a transcript type, $\pi : T \to \texttt{BareTranscript}$ a projection, and $\mathsf{explains}_T$ an explanation relation. Given supplied colliding transcripts $t_A,t_B : T$ such that $\pi(t_A)=\pi(t_B)$, where $t_A$ explains the fixed $\mu=1$ witness and $t_B$ explains the fixed $\mu=0$ witness, any verifier sound and complete for the named claim satisfies $\neg\,\texttt{factors\_classical}\;\pi\;V$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[The verifier corollary]
The collision and both explanation premises are hypotheses, not conclusions. The theorem does not construct a collision for arbitrary $T$ or $\pi$, and it does not classify all verifier designs. The full-state transcript, exact commitment-bit contract, and reported response above are three sufficient interfaces. The commitment-bit theorem is not a computational-hardness result. The substrate channel is the option the structural axis makes available; hardness and interaction are the two routes classical cryptography and complexity already used, and here the hardness route appears as the exact commitment-bit contract.
\end{remark}

\chapter{$\mu$-Conservation and Receipt Integrity}

\section{The $\mu$-Ledger Identity}

\begin{theorem}[$\mu$-Ledger Conservation {\normalfont (MuLedgerConservation.v)}]
For any bounded execution \texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}vm}~$\mathit{fuel}~\tau~s$:
\[ \mu_{\mathrm{final}} = \mu(s) + \sum_{j=0}^{k} \mathrm{cost}(i_j) \]
where $i_0,\dots,i_k$ are the instructions the run executes (\texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}vm\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}conservation}). The sum is over executed instructions, so it equals the sum over the list $\tau$ only for straight-line code; for the list fold \texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}instrs} the increment is \texttt{trace\_\allowbreak{}total\_\allowbreak{}cost}$(\tau)$. Every instruction increases $\mu$ by exactly its declared cost. No axioms, no admits.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Monotonicity]
$\mu$ never decreases: $\mu(s) \le \mu(\mathrm{Run}(\tau, s))$ for all $\tau$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Irreversibility Bound]
$\mathrm{irreversible\_\allowbreak{}bits}(\tau) \le \mu_{\mathrm{final}} - \mu_{\mathrm{init}}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[$\mu$ Decomposition {\normalfont (MuLedgerConservation.v)}]
Given a per-instruction split $\mathrm{cost}(i) = \mathrm{blind}(i) + \mathrm{sighted}(i)$, supplied as a premise (\texttt{mu\_\allowbreak{}component\_\allowbreak{}split}), a bounded run satisfies $\mu_{\mathrm{final}} = \mu(s) + \mu_{\mathrm{blind}} + \mu_{\mathrm{sighted}}$, where each component is summed over the executed instructions (\texttt{bounded\_\allowbreak{}run\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}decomposition}). The theorem does not decide which instructions are blind or sighted.
\end{theorem}

\section{Receipt Integrity}

\begin{definition}[Receipt]
A receipt $r = (\mathrm{step}, \mathrm{instr}, \mu_{\mathrm{pre}}, \mu_{\mathrm{post}}, h_{\mathrm{pre}}, h_{\mathrm{post}})$ records one transition.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Receipt Integrity {\normalfont (ReceiptIntegrity.v)}]
A valid receipt chain has final $\mu$ equal to $\mu_{\mathrm{init}} + \sum \mathrm{costs}$, the stepwise sum of the chain's instruction costs (\texttt{valid\_\allowbreak{}chain\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}equals\_\allowbreak{}computation}). It does not attest that a run happened. Any receipt claiming $\Delta\mu \neq \mathrm{cost}(\mathrm{instr})$ fails validation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Inconsistent-Increment Rejection {\normalfont (ReceiptIntegrity.v)}]
A receipt with $\mu_{\mathrm{post}} = \mu_{\mathrm{pre}} + d$ and $d \neq \texttt{instruction\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}delta}(\mathrm{instr})$ fails \texttt{receipt\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}consistent} (\texttt{forged\_\allowbreak{}receipt\_\allowbreak{}fails\_\allowbreak{}validation}). This is soundness of the validation predicate, not unforgeability: the state hash is a natural number, and no cryptographic hash or adversary model is defined. Overflow values ($\mu > 2^{31}-1$) are rejected at the receipt layer.
\end{theorem}

\chapter{Gauge Shifts of the Ledger and a Noether-Style Invariance}

\section{The Partial $\Z$-Action (Gauge Group)}

\begin{definition}[Gauge Shift]
For $\delta \in \Z$, the gauge shift $\sigma_\delta: \Sspace \to \Sspace$ maps $S \mapsto S[\mu \gets \mathrm{toNat}(\mu + \delta)]$, preserving all other fields.

Note the truncation. Since $\mu$ ranges over $\N$, the Coq definition
\texttt{z\_\allowbreak{}gauge\_\allowbreak{}shift} computes \texttt{Z.to\_\allowbreak{}nat (Z.of\_\allowbreak{}nat vm\_\allowbreak{}mu + delta)},
and \texttt{Z.to\_\allowbreak{}nat} \emph{clamps negatives to zero}. So $\sigma_\delta$ is a
total function on states but is \emph{not} injective, and the shifts do not
form a $\Z$-action on all of $\Sspace$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Partial Group Action {\normalfont (KernelNoether.v)}]
The gauge shifts form a \emph{partial} $\Z$-action on states; equivalently, a
genuine $\Z$-action on the sub-poset of states where no intermediate shift
underflows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Identity}: $\sigma_0(S) = S$. Unconditional
      (\texttt{z\_\allowbreak{}action\_\allowbreak{}identity}).
    \item \textbf{Composition}: $\sigma_a(\sigma_b(S)) = \sigma_{a+b}(S)$
      \emph{provided} $\mu(S) + b \ge 0$ \emph{and} $\mu(S) + b + a \ge 0$
      (\texttt{z\_\allowbreak{}action\_\allowbreak{}composition}, which carries both hypotheses).
    \item \textbf{Inverse}: $\sigma_{-n}(\sigma_n(S)) = S$ provided
      $\mu(S) + n \ge 0$ (\texttt{z\_\allowbreak{}action\_\allowbreak{}inverse}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Why composition needs its guard]
The guard on clause~2 is not decoration. Take $\mu(S) = 0$, $b = -1$, $a = +1$:
$\sigma_b$ clamps to $\mu = 0$, so $\sigma_a(\sigma_b(S))$ has $\mu = 1$;
whereas $\sigma_{a+b}(S) = \sigma_0(S) = S$ has $\mu = 0$. The two sides differ.
Composition fails wherever an intermediate shift underflows, which is why the
mechanised theorem carries \emph{two} nonnegativity hypotheses: one for the
intermediate state, one for the final state. Drop either and the counterexample
above goes through.
\end{remark}

\section{Gauge Invariance}

\begin{definition}[Observable]
$\mathrm{Obs}(S)$ extracts the partition region structure, ignoring $\mu$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Gauge Invariance {\normalfont (KernelNoether.v)}]
$\mathrm{Obs}(\sigma_\delta(S)) = \mathrm{Obs}(S)$ for every $\delta$ and $S$. It holds by unfolding: no separately named theorem carries it. \texttt{Observable\_\allowbreak{}partition} ignores \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu}; this says nothing about every possible observation of the VM.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Noether-Style Orbit Invariance {\normalfont (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}curvature/\allowbreak{}KernelNoether.v})}]
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Forward} (\texttt{noether\_\allowbreak{}forward}): If every field other than \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu} is equal (the partition observable, graph, registers, memory, CSRs, PC, $\mu$-tensor, error flag, logic accumulator, machine status, witness counters, and certified flag), the two states lie on the same gauge orbit: some $\delta$ gives $\sigma_\delta(S_1) = S_2$.
    \item \textbf{Gauge Invariance}: Shifting $\mu$ does not change \texttt{Observable\_\allowbreak{}partition}. This is definitional: \texttt{z\_\allowbreak{}gauge\_\allowbreak{}shift} only modifies the \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu} field, and \texttt{Observable\_\allowbreak{}partition} reads only \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}graph}. The equality is discharged by unfolding; no named theorem carries it.
    \item \textbf{Step Equivariance} (\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}step\_\allowbreak{}orbit\_\allowbreak{}equiv}): For one \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}step} and $\delta$ with $0 \le \mu(S_1) + \delta$, a step $S_1 \to S_2$ under instruction $i$ gives a step from $\sigma_\delta(S_1)$ to $\sigma_\delta(S_2)$ under $i$. Without the positivity premise the equivariance fails ($\mu = 10$, cost $5$, $\delta = -12$).
\end{enumerate}
The $\mu$-ledger is a gauge degree of freedom for the partition observable: its \emph{absolute} value does not enter it, and only \emph{differences} $\Delta\mu$ carry meaning there. The ``Noether'' label is a deliberate analogy: the result follows from the record structure (separate fields for $\mu$ and graph), not from a deep symmetry principle.

\begin{remark}[Gauge freedom and absolute $\mu$-claims]
\label{rem:gauge_vs_absolute}
Gauge freedom has to be squared with the results that assert absolute values, most directly the verifier argument, which needs $\mathrm{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu}(s) = 1$ to be a
well-posed claim, and the $\mu$-hierarchy, which needs ``cost exactly $k$''. If
only differences carried meaning, neither statement would have content.

Every absolute $\mu$-claim in this document is \emph{relative to}
\texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state}, where $\mu = 0$ by definition. Read
$\mathrm{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu}(s) = 1$ as $\Delta\mu = 1$ along a run from
\texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state} to $s$; $\mu$-conservation
(\texttt{run\_\allowbreak{}vm\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}conservation}) is what licenses the reading, since it
makes the final ledger equal to the trace cost.

The scope is worth naming precisely: $\sigma_\delta$ is defined on
\emph{arbitrary} states, not on orbits rooted at \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state}, so the
gauge group as formalised is larger than the equivalence the absolute claims
need. Nothing in the development quotients by it. The theorems live on \emph{reachable} states, where the base point pins the
gauge, and the gauge chapter describes a symmetry of the state \emph{type}
rather than of the reachable dynamics. Note also that
\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}step\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}monotonic} makes $\mu$ non-decreasing along runs, which
already breaks the $\Z$-symmetry on trajectories: the dynamics single out a
direction the gauge action does not respect.
\end{remark}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[$\mu$-Monotonicity {\normalfont (KernelNoether.v)}]

\chapter{The Born Rule}

\begin{definition}[Bloch Sphere Probabilities]
For a state with purity vector $(x,y,z)$:
\[ P(|0\rangle) = \frac{1+z}{2}, \qquad P(|1\rangle) = \frac{1-z}{2} \]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Measurement $\mu$-Cost]
\[ \mathrm{Cost}(x,y,z) = \frac{1 - (x^2 + y^2 + z^2)}{2} \]
This is the linear entropy. For pure states ($r^2=1$), cost $= 0$. For mixed states ($r^2 < 1$), cost $> 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of the Born Rule among Linear Rules {\normalfont (BornRule.v)}, \textbf{(C)}]
The Born rule $P(|0\rangle) = (1+z)/2$ is the \textbf{unique} value of outcome~$0$ for a probability assignment satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Non-negativity on the Bloch ball ($x^2+y^2+z^2 \le 1$) and normalization: $P \ge 0$, $P(|0\rangle) + P(|1\rangle) = 1$.
    \item Eigenstate consistency: $P(0,0,1) = 1$, $P(0,0,-1) = 0$ for outcome~$0$.
    \item Linearity in $z$ on the Bloch ball: $P(x,y,z) = az + b$ for some $a,b$.
\end{enumerate}
Proof: Boundary conditions force $a = 1/2$, $b = 1/2$. No other solution exists. Outcome~$1$ then follows by normalization (\texttt{linear\_\allowbreak{}implies\_\allowbreak{}born}, \texttt{valid\_\allowbreak{}linear\_\allowbreak{}rule\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}born\_\allowbreak{}with\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}side\_\allowbreak{}condition}).

\textbf{Note:} The Coq theorem also lists a \texttt{measurement\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}nonnegative} hypothesis, but it is \textbf{unused} in the proof. The hypothesis $\texttt{measurement\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}nonnegative}~P$ unfolds to ``measurement cost $\ge 0$ for valid Bloch vectors,'' which is directly from the definition true for all states and does not constrain $P$. The linearity assumption (item~3) does all the work, it assumes the functional form $P = az+b$ and solves a 2-equation system. This is less ``deriving the Born rule from $\mu$-accounting'' and more ``the Born rule is the unique linear rule consistent with eigenstates.''
\end{theorem}

\chapter{Unitarity}

\begin{definition}[Evolution]
An evolution $E$ maps Bloch vectors $(x,y,z) \mapsto (x',y',z')$ with associated $\mu$-cost. The cost is a real field of the record. Nothing in the file computes it from the map.
\end{definition}

The predicate \texttt{radius\_\allowbreak{}preserving} means preservation of squared Bloch radius on the unit ball. The file does not prove that such a map comes from a Hilbert-space operator. Two premises are supplied, not derived: \texttt{respects\_\allowbreak{}info\_\allowbreak{}conservation} (radius loss is at most $\mu$) and \texttt{purity\_\allowbreak{}nonincreasing} (radius gain is at most $\mu$).

\begin{theorem}[Zero Cost and Radius Preservation {\normalfont (Unitarity.v)}, \textbf{(C)}]
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Under the conservation premise, $\mu = 0 \implies r'^2 \ge r^2$ for inputs in the unit ball (purity cannot decrease at zero cost). Proven as \texttt{zero\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}preserves\_\allowbreak{}purity}.

Proven as \texttt{nonunitary\_\allowbreak{}requires\_\allowbreak{}mu}: the proof specializes the conservation hypothesis at the witness, then \texttt{lra}.
    \item Under both premises, $\mu = 0$ implies \texttt{radius\_\allowbreak{}preserving}. Proven as \texttt{zero\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}preserves\_\allowbreak{}radius}: the two premises give $r'^2 \ge r^2$ and $r'^2 \le r^2$.
    \item The converse, radius preservation $\Rightarrow \mu = 0$, is \emph{stated} in the file but not proven as a theorem. It appears as the \texttt{Definition} \texttt{radius\_\allowbreak{}preserving\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}cost}. Because $\mu$ is a free field of the record, it could not be proved for every evolution.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Assume the conservation premise, a supplied bound \texttt{satisfies\_\allowbreak{}lindblad\_\allowbreak{}bound}~$E~\gamma$ with $\gamma > 0$, and a witness that the pure state $(1,0,0)$ loses exactly $\gamma$ of squared radius. Then $\mu \ge \gamma$ (\texttt{lindblad\_\allowbreak{}requires\_\allowbreak{}mu}). In this record, that loss is paid for. \chapter{Purification}

\begin{theorem}[Two-Point Decomposition of a Bloch State {\normalfont (Purification.v)}, \textbf{(S)}]
The file calls this the purification principle. It proves the spectral fact that a purification would use. It does not construct a purifying state on a larger system. Every mixed state $(x,y,z)$ with $r^2 = x^2+y^2+z^2 \le 1$ admits eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ with:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 = 1$, \quad $0 \le \lambda_i \le 1$.
    \item $(\lambda_1 - \lambda_2)^2 = r^2$.
    \item Construction: $\lambda_1 = (1 + r)/2$, $\lambda_2 = (1 - r)/2$.
\end{itemize}
Pure states ($r=1$) need no external reference (deficit $= 0$). The maximally mixed state ($r=0$) has deficit $= 1$.
\end{theorem}

\chapter{No-Cloning}

A cloning operation here is a record of four real numbers: input information $I$, output informations $I_1, I_2$, and a cost $\mu$. The chapter's results are arithmetic on those fields under one supplied premise. They use no Hilbert space, channel, or fidelity between states.

\begin{theorem}[No Perfect Cloning at Zero Declared Cost {\normalfont (NoCloning.v)}, \textbf{(C)}]
Let $I = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ be the information content (purity). For a cloning operation with outputs of information $I_1, I_2$ and $\mu$-cost $\mu$, assume the conservation premise (\texttt{respects\_\allowbreak{}conservation}, supplied, not derived):
\[ I_1 + I_2 \le I + \mu \]
If $I > 0$ and $I_1 = I_2 = I$, then $\mu \neq 0$ (\texttt{no\_\allowbreak{}cloning\_\allowbreak{}from\_\allowbreak{}conservation}). For a pure input, $\mu \ge 1$ (\texttt{no\_\allowbreak{}cloning\_\allowbreak{}bloch}). \texttt{unitary\_\allowbreak{}cannot\_\allowbreak{}clone} links this to the Unitarity chapter. Under that chapter's two premises and $\mu = 0$, one evolution's output copied into both fields cannot satisfy the premise.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Approximate Cloning {\normalfont (NoCloning.v)}, \textbf{(C)}]
Under the same premise, with $I > 0$ and $I_i = f_i I$ for scale factors $0 \le f_i \le 1$: $f_1 + f_2 \le 1 + \mu/I$. At zero cost: $f_1 + f_2 \le 1$. The file calls $f_1, f_2$ fidelities. They are scale factors on the information field, not the fidelity between quantum states.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[No Deletion at Zero Declared Cost {\normalfont (NoCloning.v)}, \textbf{(C)}]
Perfect deletion also requires $\mu \ge I$ (dual of no-cloning), under the deletion record's own supplied premise $I_{\mathrm{out}} + \mu \ge I_1 + I_2$, with $I_{\mathrm{out}} = I_1 = I_2 > 0$ (\texttt{no\_\allowbreak{}deletion\_\allowbreak{}without\_\allowbreak{}cost}).
\end{theorem}

\chapter{Slice Coherence and the Zero-Marginal NPA Matrix: The Biconditional}
\label{ch:biconditional}

This algebraic result has no project-local axioms or admitted lemmas; its assumption receipt includes standard classical-real and functional-extensionality axioms.

\begin{definition}[Column Contractivity (Slice Coherence)]
\section{The CHSH statistic $S$ and its witness counts}
\label{sec:chsh_statistic}

The central quantum object of this part is defined here, since it is used
throughout without ever having been given as a function of machine state.

\begin{definition}[Correlator from witness counts {\normalfont (CHSHStatisticalBridge.v)}]
A VM state carries a \texttt{WitnessCounts} record in $\N^8$, holding for each
setting pair $(x,y) \in \{0,1\}^2$ the tallies
$\mathrm{same}_{xy}$ and $\mathrm{diff}_{xy}$. Writing
$N_{xy} = \mathrm{same}_{xy} + \mathrm{diff}_{xy}$, the empirical correlator is
\begin{equation}
E_{xy} =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\mathrm{same}_{xy} - \mathrm{diff}_{xy}}{N_{xy}} & N_{xy} > 0,\\[2ex]
0 & N_{xy} = 0 .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The $N_{xy}=0$ branch is a definitional convention (no trials, no evidence), not
a claim that uncorrelated is the right default.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[CHSH statistic {\normalfont (\texttt{chsh\_\allowbreak{}S}, TsirelsonFromMu.v)}]
\begin{equation}
S = E_{00} + E_{01} + E_{10} - E_{11} \in \mathbb{R} .
\end{equation}
Note the sign on the last term, and note that in
\texttt{CHSHStatisticalBridge.v} the same statistic is assembled with the
$(1,1)$ arguments reversed, giving $E^{*}_{11} = (\mathrm{diff}_{11} -
\mathrm{same}_{11})/N_{11}$, so that all four terms may be added. The two
presentations agree.

The classical bound is $|S| \leq 2$ and the Tsirelson bound is
$|S| \leq 2\sqrt{2}$. Both are proved here as \emph{algebraic} facts with no
physics axioms. Where the development states the Tsirelson bound in squared
form it appears as $S^2 \leq 8$, which is the same bound: $\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt2$.
\end{definition}

A correlation tuple $(E_{00}, E_{01}, E_{10}, E_{11})$ is \textbf{slice-coherent} (equivalently, column-contractive; the predicate tests slice membership rather than truthfulness, and the name records that) if:
\begin{align}
1 - E_{00}^2 - E_{10}^2 &\geq 0 \quad \text{(condition 1)} \\
1 - E_{01}^2 - E_{11}^2 &\geq 0 \quad \text{(condition 2)} \\
(1-E_{00}^2-E_{10}^2)(1-E_{01}^2-E_{11}^2) &\geq (E_{00}E_{01}+E_{10}E_{11})^2 \quad \text{(condition 3)}
\end{align}
These are exactly the PSD conditions on the $5 \times 5$ zero-marginal NPA moment matrix.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Slice Coherence $\iff$ NPA PSD {\normalfont (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}quantum/\allowbreak{}QuantumPartitionPSD.v}, corollary \texttt{column\_\allowbreak{}contractive\_\allowbreak{}iff\_\allowbreak{}npa\_\allowbreak{}psd})}, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:thiele_honest_iff_qr}
\texttt{column\_\allowbreak{}contractive\_\allowbreak{}iff\_\allowbreak{}npa\_\allowbreak{}psd}:
\[
\text{slice\_\allowbreak{}coherent}(\mathbf{E}) \iff \text{npa\_\allowbreak{}psd}(\mathbf{E})
\]
where \emph{slice\_\allowbreak{}coherent} means the three column-contractivity conditions hold, and \emph{npa\_\allowbreak{}psd} means the $5\times5$ zero-marginal NPA moment matrix is positive semidefinite.

\textbf{Forward direction} (\texttt{zero\_\allowbreak{}marginal\_\allowbreak{}npa\_\allowbreak{}column\_\allowbreak{}contractive\_\allowbreak{}implies\_\allowbreak{}psd} in \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}nfi/\allowbreak{}MuLedgerQuantumBridge.v}): column-contractive $\Rightarrow$ NPA PSD. Proved by completing squares: the quadratic form splits into three squares and a $2\times2$ block that is PSD by the three conditions.

\textbf{Reverse direction} (\texttt{npa\_\allowbreak{}psd\_\allowbreak{}implies\_\allowbreak{}column\_\allowbreak{}contractive} in \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}quantum/\allowbreak{}QuantumPartitionPSD.v}): NPA PSD $\Rightarrow$ column-contractive. Proved by specializing the PSD condition at the test vectors $(0,-E_{00},-E_{10},1,0)$, $(0,-E_{01},-E_{11},0,1)$, and the family $(0,-(E_{00}t+E_{01}),-(E_{10}t+E_{11}),t,1)$, obtaining a non-negative quadratic in $t$, then closing via \texttt{quadratic\_\allowbreak{}nonneg\_\allowbreak{}discriminant}.

This biconditional has no project-local axioms; its assumption report includes standard-library real-number and functional-extensionality assumptions. No physics assumptions. Pure polynomial arithmetic. The zero-marginal NPA framework is the orthogonal-observables slice of the quantum elliptope ($\rho_{AA}=\rho_{BB}=0$), not the full elliptope.

Key consequences:
\begin{itemize}
\item PR box $(E_{00}=E_{01}=E_{10}=1, E_{11}=-1)$ fails condition 1 immediately ($1-1-1=-1<0$). Direct arithmetic; no separate theorem needed.
\item \texttt{state\_\allowbreak{}column\_\allowbreak{}contractive\_\allowbreak{}implies\_\allowbreak{}tsirelson} (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}nfi/\allowbreak{}MuLedgerQuantumBridge.v}) directly proves slice-coherent $\Rightarrow$ $|S|^2 \leq 8$ (equivalently $|S| \leq 2\sqrt{2}$); its proof composes \texttt{state\_\allowbreak{}column\_\allowbreak{}contractive\_\allowbreak{}implies\_\allowbreak{}npa\_\allowbreak{}gram} with \texttt{npa\_\allowbreak{}psd\_\allowbreak{}implies\_\allowbreak{}tsirelson\_\allowbreak{}bound}. The Cauchy--Schwarz inner step (PSD $\Rightarrow$ row bounds $\Rightarrow$ $|S|^2 \leq 8$) is \texttt{tsirelson\_\allowbreak{}from\_\allowbreak{}row\_\allowbreak{}bounds} in \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}quantum/\allowbreak{}TsirelsonGeneral.v}. The contrapositive ($|S| > 2\sqrt{2}$ $\Rightarrow$ not slice-coherent) follows immediately. The converse is false and is not claimed.
\item Turing-classical correlations $(1,0,1,0)$ satisfy $|S|=2$ but fail condition 1: $1-1-1=-1<0$. A Turing machine can output this correlation; the slice gate rejects it as slice-incoherent (the full-elliptope gate of the next section accepts it, the point is inside the elliptope, outside the zero-marginal slice). Direct arithmetic from \texttt{column\_\allowbreak{}contractive\_\allowbreak{}iff\_\allowbreak{}npa\_\allowbreak{}psd}.
\end{itemize}
Zero Admitted. Zero named hypotheses.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The adversarial search}
\label{sec:pointer_counterexamples}

Five confirmations settle nothing on their own. A criterion loose enough to accept anything accepts five things just as easily. \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}frontier/\allowbreak{}PointerObservableCounterexamples.v} tests the criterion in the corner where it is most likely to break, on systems that do not resemble the five.

The claim put at risk is the strong reading:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{(PO-STRONG)} Every system that must resist forgery of a commitment has a commitment event that redundantly proliferates.
\end{quote}
A counterexample must satisfy three conditions: (C1) it must resist forgery of a commitment, in the sense that its designers treat an unearned commitment as the primary failure mode; (C2) it must be deployed and independently evolved, owing nothing to this development; and (C3) its commitment event must fail \texttt{redundantly\_\allowbreak{}proliferating}.

Each candidate has two halves. (C1) and (C2) are facts about a real design and are argued in prose. The Coq half fixes an observer map and computes (C3) for it. The file contains no definition of forgery resistance, EUF-CMA security, deniability, memory safety, metering, or deployment, so the Coq results alone neither refute nor confirm a security claim.

\textbf{Three candidates argue against (PO-STRONG)}, using a two-part argument rather than an unconditional machine theorem. For each, the non-proliferating observer map is checked in Coq and its faithfulness to the design is argued in prose:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Deniable (designated-verifier) authentication} (\texttt{deniable\_\allowbreak{}authentication\_\allowbreak{}model\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}proliferating}). The scheme is engineered so a third party cannot be convinced even given the whole transcript, because the designated verifier could have produced it. Non-transferability is the product requirement.
    \item \textbf{Symmetric-key authentication} (\texttt{mac\_\allowbreak{}model\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}proliferating}). Existential unforgeability is the defining security notion of a MAC, and verification requires the shared secret, so no record reaches anyone else.
    \item \textbf{Object capabilities} (\texttt{capability\_\allowbreak{}model\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}proliferating}). Unforgeability here is unconditional, enforced by memory safety rather than by a cost or a hardness assumption. Nothing is paid, nothing is logged, and no observer outside the holder can tell.
\end{itemize}
A public-log control (\texttt{public\_\allowbreak{}log\_\allowbreak{}model\_\allowbreak{}proliferating}) checks that the search can return \emph{confirms} and is not an instrument that only reports refutations; its rival effort event also fails to proliferate (\texttt{public\_\allowbreak{}log\_\allowbreak{}effort\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}proliferating}), so the control discriminates rather than accepting everything.

If those prose correspondences are accepted, the boundary they trace is not the one (PO-STRONG) guessed at. A MAC and a digital signature both resist forgery; only the signature proliferates. Deniable authentication and certificate transparency both resist forgery; deniability actively prevents third-party conviction while transparency actively requires it. Object capabilities and proof-carrying code both make unearned authority impossible; the capability confines it to the holder while the proof travels to every consumer. In each pair the proliferating member is the one whose commitment must convince parties who were not present. The surviving candidate criterion is therefore:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{(PO-PUBLIC)} A system whose commitment must convince third parties who did not witness its creation will proliferate records of the commitment event.
\end{quote}
The signature observer model makes the commitment visible in each observer fragment and proves proliferation for that model. Its correspondence to deployed signatures is a modeling judgment. Public availability of a verifier does not imply that every observer retains a record.

The verifier constructions give three sufficient evidence interfaces under their respective premises: a full-state transcript, an exact commitment-bit contract, and a reported response. Neither these examples nor the factorization obstruction proves that only one route can involve metering.

Three statements travel together in loose prose and come apart under these candidates:
\begin{description}
    \item[(M1)] \textbf{Forgery resistance forces metering.} \textbf{False}, on the prose argument. Signatures take the hardness route; object capabilities achieve unforgeability unconditionally through memory safety, with no cost, no record, and no assumption.
    \item[(M2)] \textbf{Forgery resistance forces record proliferation.} \textbf{False}, on the same argument. Deniable authentication, symmetric MACs, and capabilities all resist forgery while proliferating nothing, and in the first case that is the design goal.
    \item[(M3)] \textbf{Public commitments force record proliferation.} \textbf{Proposed.} The chosen observer models satisfy it. Actual record proliferation needs independent evidence; public verifiability alone is insufficient.
\end{description}

The metering claim supported here is exact. Under supplied colliding transcripts, no verifier that is sound and complete for $\mathrm{vm\_mu}=1$ factors through the classical projection (\texttt{V\_\allowbreak{}does\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}factor\_\allowbreak{}through\_\allowbreak{}classical}). The pricing statement is carried by No Free Insight, not by this theorem. It is narrower than ``forgery resistance,'' and it is the version this development supports.

\end{theorem}

\section{Discrete Geometry: A Restricted Angle-Defect Identity}
\label{sec:gauss_bonnet}

The result is a conditional combinatorial identity with an assigned equilateral angle model. No physical interpretation is needed, but its graph, incidence, and boundary-count hypotheses are essential.

The formal statement is in \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}curvature/\allowbreak{}DiscreteGaussBonnet.v}.

\begin{theorem}[Restricted Angle-Defect Identity, \textbf{(S)}]
For a partition graph $g$ satisfying \texttt{well\_\allowbreak{}formed\_\allowbreak{}triangulated}, assign $\pi/3$ to every triangle corner. The predicate includes $3F=2I+B$, $\sum_v\deg(v)=3F$, and $B=3\chi$, where $\chi=V-E+F$; the edge definitions give $E=I+B$. With $\deg(v)$ counting incident triangles, define
\[
D(g)=\sum_v(2\pi-\deg(v)\pi/3).
\]
Then $D(g)=5\pi\chi(g)$. The definition uses $2\pi$ at every vertex, including boundary vertices.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Hypotheses and relation to Gauss--Bonnet]
The incidence equations yield $D=2\pi V-\pi F=2\pi\chi+\pi B$. The extra hypothesis $B=3\chi$ supplies the coefficient $5\pi$. It is not a general property of planar triangulations with boundary: a quadrilateral disk divided into two triangles has $B=4$ and $\chi=1$ and is excluded. The predicate also does not itself provide a planar embedding or a proof that the graph is a topological disk.

For a triangulated surface with boundary cycles, the usual combined interior-curvature and boundary-turning sum uses $2\pi$ at interior vertices and $\pi$ at boundary vertices. Since boundary vertices and edges then have equal counts, this correction subtracts $\pi B$ and gives $2\pi\chi$. Establishing the appropriate surface conditions and boundary-vertex definition is required for that statement. The file formalizes the restricted identity only.

Thus the proof establishes a valid identity for its defined quantity under its stated restrictions. It neither derives those restrictions from computation nor establishes a physical curvature law or an angle scale from $\mu$-cost.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Cross-link in MuGravity Vocabulary}

\begin{theorem}[Graph-Laplacian Sum-Zero Identity {\normalfont (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}frontier/\allowbreak{}MuLaplacianSum.v})}, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:total_mu_laplacian_zero}
For every \texttt{VMState} $s$:
\[
\sum_{m \in \mathrm{pg\_\allowbreak{}modules}(\mathrm{vm\_\allowbreak{}graph}\,s)} \mathrm{mu\_\allowbreak{}laplacian}(s, m) = 0.
\]
Proof by edge-pair antisymmetry. \texttt{nat\_\allowbreak{}list\_\allowbreak{}disjoint} is symmetric, hence \texttt{modules\_\allowbreak{}adjacent\_\allowbreak{}by\_\allowbreak{}region} is symmetric, hence \texttt{edge\_\allowbreak{}weight} is symmetric; \texttt{mu\_\allowbreak{}gradient} is antisymmetric by definition; the double sum cancels under the Fubini swap. No well-formedness, no calibration. Standalone graph-theoretic fact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Calibrated Total Angle Defect Is Zero {\normalfont (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}frontier/\allowbreak{}PartitionTopologyCrossLink.v})}, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:f3_narrow}
For every \texttt{VMState} $s$ at universal local calibration ($\forall m, \texttt{calibration\_\allowbreak{}residual}(s, m) = 0$):
\[
\sum_{m \in \mathrm{pg\_\allowbreak{}modules}(\mathrm{vm\_\allowbreak{}graph}\,s)} \mathrm{geometric\_\allowbreak{}angle\_\allowbreak{}defect}(s, m) = 0.

The proof composes two kernel facts: \texttt{calibration\_\allowbreak{}residual\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}iff} (geometry $\leftrightarrow$ $\mu$-ledger at every module) and \texttt{total\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}laplacian\_\allowbreak{}zero} (Theorem~\ref{thm:total_mu_laplacian_zero}). They do not supply VM counter-instances establishing independence of the premises.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Why this is not the topology version]
\label{rem:f3_no_topology}
The discrete angle-defect identity does not imply $\sum_m \mathrm{mu\_\allowbreak{}laplacian} = 5 \cdot \chi$. That inference would require identifying two distinct angle-defect quantities: \texttt{DiscreteGaussBonnet.angle\_\allowbreak{}defect} (degree $\times$ $\pi/3$, equilateral) and \texttt{MuGravity.geometric\_\allowbreak{}angle\_\allowbreak{}defect} (distance-weighted, $\pi \cdot d_{bc}/(1 + d_{ab}+d_{ac}+d_{bc})$). They are not equal in general: \texttt{strong\_\allowbreak{}bridge\_\allowbreak{}counterexample} (in the same file) is a scalar computation: for every $d \geq 1$ the formulas $2\pi - 6\pi d/(1 + 3d)$ and $2\pi - 6\pi/3$ differ. The cross-link makes no claim about the topology invariant. Discrete Gauss-Bonnet remains a kernel theorem; it is simply not load-bearing for this cross-link.

\textbf{Non-vacuity fails on reachable states.} Adjacency (\texttt{modules\_\allowbreak{}adjacent\_\allowbreak{}by\_\allowbreak{}region}) means two module regions meet, and on every state reachable from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state} the regions are pairwise disjoint. So no two modules are adjacent, every module has no neighbours and no triangles, and the face-graph triangle count is $0$ (\texttt{reachable\_\allowbreak{}no\_\allowbreak{}adjacent\_\allowbreak{}modules}); the calibration premise holds on a reachable state exactly when it has no modules (\texttt{reachable\_\allowbreak{}calibrated\_\allowbreak{}iff\_\allowbreak{}no\_\allowbreak{}modules}). A well-formed triangulated reachable graph with $F$ faces has no interior edge, $3F$ vertices, $3F$ edges and Euler characteristic $F$ (\texttt{reachable\_\allowbreak{}triangulated\_\allowbreak{}isolated}), and one PNEW from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state} reaches such a graph with $F = 1$ (\texttt{reachable\_\allowbreak{}triangulated\_\allowbreak{}exists}). The geometry theorems are therefore theorems about partition graphs in general; the machine reaches only these degenerate cases. \texttt{module\_\allowbreak{}triangles} in \texttt{MuGravity.v} requires pairwise-adjacent neighbors, an actual graph triangle, which excludes tree-shaped pseudo-witnesses.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Metric Tensor from $\mu$-Costs}

\begin{definition}[Computational Metric {\normalfont (MetricFromMuCosts.v, RiemannTensor4D.v)}]
The metric tensor at vertex $v$ is the per-module $4\times 4$ $\mu$-tensor of module $v$, read as a real matrix:
\begin{equation}
g_{\mu\nu}(v) = \texttt{module\_\allowbreak{}tensor\_\allowbreak{}entry}\ s\ v\ (\mu \bmod 4)\ (\nu \bmod 4)
\end{equation}
(\texttt{full\_\allowbreak{}metric\_\allowbreak{}at\_\allowbreak{}vertex}, aliased as \texttt{metric\_\allowbreak{}component}). Its entries are natural numbers, and different vertices can carry different tensors. \texttt{MetricFromMuCosts.v} also defines two scalar quantities from the structural mass $\text{mass}(v) = \texttt{module\_\allowbreak{}structural\_\allowbreak{}mass}(s, v)$, the length of the module's region plus the length of its axiom list, a natural number. The diagonal metric:
\begin{equation}
\texttt{metric\_\allowbreak{}at\_\allowbreak{}vertex}(s,v,\mu,\nu) = \begin{cases}
\text{mass}(v) & \text{if } \mu \bmod 4 = \nu \bmod 4 \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and the edge length $\ell(s, m_1, m_2) = \text{mass}(m_1) + \text{mass}(m_2)$. When the tensor at $v$ is the isotropic diagonal with entries $\text{mass}(v)$, the two metrics agree (\texttt{full\_\allowbreak{}metric\_\allowbreak{}compat\_\allowbreak{}diagonal}).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The inverse metric]
\label{rem:metric_underspecified}
A tensor with zero determinant has no inverse, so ``raising an index'' is not available there; this includes the all-zero tensor of the vacuum configuration. \texttt{MetricFromMuCosts.v} defines \texttt{inverse\_\allowbreak{}metric\_\allowbreak{}at\_\allowbreak{}vertex} by Cramer's rule. The curvature operators of \texttt{RiemannTensor4D.v} do not use it: they take the identity as the inverse metric, a declared convention of the discrete operators.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
Uniform module tensors (\texttt{uniform\_\allowbreak{}module\_\allowbreak{}tensor}: every module carries the same $4\times 4$ tensor) make the metric position-independent, and the Christoffel, Riemann, and Einstein tensors below vanish. Differing structural masses make \texttt{metric\_\allowbreak{}at\_\allowbreak{}vertex} position-dependent (\texttt{Kernel.\allowbreak{}EinsteinEquations4D.\allowbreak{}non\_\allowbreak{}uniform\_\allowbreak{}mass\_\allowbreak{}produces\_\allowbreak{}curvature}). No theorem concludes nonzero curvature.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Discrete Differential Geometry}

\begin{definition}[Discrete Derivative {\normalfont (RiemannTensor4D.v)}]
For a scalar field $f: V \to \mathbb{R}$ and direction $\mu$:
\begin{equation}
\partial_\mu f(v) = f(w) - f(v)
\end{equation}
where $w$ is the first vertex, in the vertex-list order of $\mathcal{K}$, joined to $v$ by an edge of direction $\mu$ or an undirected edge. If there is no such vertex, $\partial_\mu f(v) = 0$. There is no averaging over neighbours.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Christoffel Symbols {\normalfont (RiemannTensor4D.v)}]
The connection coefficients are:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}(v) = \frac{1}{2} \Bigl(\partial_\mu g_{\nu\rho}(v) + \partial_\nu g_{\mu\rho}(v) - \partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu}(v)\Bigr)
\end{equation}
There is no inverse metric and no sum over a fourth index: the identity inverse is the declared convention of the discrete operators.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform Module Tensors Have Zero Connection {\normalfont (EinsteinEquations4D.v: \texttt{flat\_\allowbreak{}spacetime\_\allowbreak{}christoffel\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}general})}, \textbf{(S)}]

\begin{equation}
\texttt{uniform\_\allowbreak{}module\_\allowbreak{}tensor}\ s \quad \implies \quad \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}(v) = 0
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Uniform module tensors $\implies$ position-independent metric $\implies$ $\partial_\mu g_{\nu\sigma} = 0$ $\implies$ $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Curvature Tensors}

\begin{definition}[Riemann Curvature Tensor {\normalfont (RiemannTensor4D.v)}]
The Riemann curvature tensor measures the failure of parallel transport:
\begin{align}
R^\rho_{\sigma\mu\nu}(v) &= \partial_\mu \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma}(v) - \partial_\nu \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma}(v) \nonumber \\
&\quad + \sum_\lambda \Bigl(\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}(v)\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma}(v) - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}(v)\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}(v)\Bigr)
\end{align}
where $\lambda$ ranges over the vertices of $\mathcal{K}$ (\texttt{sc4d\_\allowbreak{}vertices}), not over the four coordinate indices.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ricci Tensor {\normalfont (RiemannTensor4D.v)}]
Contraction of the Riemann tensor:
\begin{equation}
R_{\mu\nu}(v) = \sum_\rho R^\rho_{\mu\rho\nu}(v)
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ ranges over the vertices of $\mathcal{K}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ricci Scalar {\normalfont (RiemannTensor4D.v)}]
Complete contraction, with the identity as inverse metric:
\begin{equation}
R(v) = \sum_{\mu,\nu} \delta_{\mu\nu} R_{\mu\nu}(v)
\end{equation}
where $\mu,\nu$ range over the vertices of $\mathcal{K}$. The variants \texttt{local\_\allowbreak{}ricci\_\allowbreak{}tensor\_\allowbreak{}4d} and \texttt{local\_\allowbreak{}ricci\_\allowbreak{}scalar\_\allowbreak{}4d} (\texttt{EinsteinEquations4D.v}) contract over the four indices $0,1,2,3$ instead.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Einstein Tensor {\normalfont (RiemannTensor4D.v)}]
The Einstein tensor:
\begin{equation}
G_{\mu\nu}(v) = R_{\mu\nu}(v) - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu}(v) R(v)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform Module Tensors Have Zero Curvature {\normalfont (EinsteinEquations4D.v: \texttt{flat\_\allowbreak{}spacetime\_\allowbreak{}riemann\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}general}, \texttt{flat\_\allowbreak{}spacetime\_\allowbreak{}einstein\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}general})}, \textbf{(S)}]

\subsection{Stress-Energy Tensor}

\begin{remark}[The components below are named by analogy, not by physics]
\label{rem:stress_energy_misnomer}
The three definitions that follow assign the letters $T_{00}$, $T_{0i}$,
$T_{ij}$ to expressions in \texttt{module\_\allowbreak{}structural\_\allowbreak{}mass}. The names are
borrowed from the relativistic stress-energy tensor; the objects are not those
quantities, and three specific mismatches should be stated plainly rather than
left for a reader to discover:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{$T_{00}$ is not an energy density.} It is set equal to
    $\mathrm{mass}(v)$, a dimensionless integer attached to a vertex. An energy
    density is energy per unit volume; there is no volume element anywhere in
    this construction, and no conversion constant. Compare
    Section~\ref{sec:dimensional_gap}: a dimensionless integer ledger cannot
    produce a dimensional constant, which is exactly the obstruction here.
  \item \textbf{$T_{0i}$ is not a momentum density.} Momentum density is
    $\rho v^i$, mass density times velocity. The definition below is
    $\partial_i T_{00}$, the spatial \emph{gradient} of the energy-density
    slot. That is a different object with different index structure and
    different dimensions; a gradient of a density is not a flux. There is no
    velocity field in this model for it to be built from.
  \item \textbf{$T_{ij} = \mathrm{mass}\cdot g_{ij}$ silently fixes an
    equation of state.} The spatial block is the energy-density slot times the
    metric. Where $g_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ it is an isotropic block with pressure
    equal to energy density, $p = \rho$: a \emph{stiff fluid}, the maximally
    rigid equation of state consistent with causality in the relativistic
    setting. Nothing in the model motivates that choice; it is a definitional
    convenience, and it should not be read as a derived result about the
    matter content.
\end{enumerate}

None of this affects the vacuum theorem, where every component is $0$ because
$\mathrm{mass} \equiv 0$. They matter for how the section is read.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[$T_{00}$ slot: vertex mass {\normalfont (EinsteinEquations4D.v)}]
The $00$ component is set to the computational mass. Per
Remark~\ref{rem:stress_energy_misnomer} this is not an energy density:
\begin{equation}
T_{00}(v) = \text{mass}(v)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$T_{0i}$ slot: spatial gradient of $T_{00}$ {\normalfont (EinsteinEquations4D.v)}]
The mixed components are set to the spatial derivative of the $00$ slot. Per
Remark~\ref{rem:stress_energy_misnomer} this is a gradient, \emph{not} a
momentum density:
\begin{equation}
T_{0i}(v) = T_{i0}(v) = \partial_i T_{00}(v)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$T_{ij}$ slot: energy density times the metric {\normalfont (EinsteinEquations4D.v)}]
The spatial block is the $00$ slot times the metric at $v$. Per
Remark~\ref{rem:stress_energy_misnomer}, where $g_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ this is
the stiff-fluid equation of state $p = \rho$; it is imposed here by
definition, not derived:
\begin{equation}
T_{ij}(v) = \text{mass}(v)\, g_{ij}(v)
\end{equation}
The off-diagonal entries $T_{ij}$, $i \neq j$, vanish when the metric at $v$ is diagonal (\texttt{diagonal\_\allowbreak{}metric\_\allowbreak{}at}, \texttt{stress\_\allowbreak{}off\_\allowbreak{}diagonal\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}isotropic}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Stress-Energy Tensor {\normalfont (EinsteinEquations4D.v)}]
Combining all components:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mu\nu}(s, \mathcal{K}, v) = \begin{cases}
T_{00}(v) & \text{if } \mu = \nu = 0 \\
T_{0i}(v) & \text{if } \mu = 0, \nu = i \text{ or } \mu = i, \nu = 0 \\
T_{ij}(v) & \text{if } \mu = i \neq 0, \nu = j \neq 0
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\section{The Dimensional Gap: Why Ledgers Cannot Fix Constants}
\label{sec:dimensional_gap}

\begin{theorem}[Dimensional Gap {\normalfont (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}thermodynamic/\allowbreak{}DimensionalGapTheorem.v})}, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:dimensional_gap}
Let $\mathrm{State}$ be any type, $L : \mathrm{State} \to \N$ any dimensionless
integer ledger, $\omega : \mathrm{State} \to \N$ any microstate-count function,
and $S_{\mathrm{cand}} : \mathrm{State} \to \mathbb{R}$ any candidate entropy. Suppose
for some $\mathrm{base} \geq 2$ and some state $s$ with $L(s) \geq 1$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{ledger-exponential}: $\omega(s') = \mathrm{base}^{L(s')}$ for all $s'$;
    \item \textbf{ledger-linear}: $S_{\mathrm{cand}}(s') = \alpha \cdot L(s')$ for all $s'$;
    \item \textbf{Boltzmann-form}: $S_{\mathrm{cand}}(s') = k_B \ln \omega(s')$ whenever $\omega(s') > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\alpha$ is \emph{forced}:
\begin{equation}
\alpha = k_B \cdot \ln(\mathrm{base}).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

This is the limit on deriving physics from computation in this framework, and
it should be read as a \textbf{no-go}, not as a bridge. The theorem quantifies
over \emph{any} type, \emph{any} integer ledger, and \emph{any} exponential
microstate count. It makes no reference to any Thiele machine at all. It proves
$\alpha = k_B \ln(\mathrm{base})$ from the three stated premises. The reading
of the file is that the unit-conversion constant $k_B$ (an empirical fact about
thermodynamics) and the counting base (a structural fact about the substrate's
degrees of freedom) are supplied from outside. Fix both and $\alpha$ follows.
The ledger alone does not prefer a value of $\alpha$, and choosing among the
consistent $(\alpha, k_B)$ pairs is the modelling input that the ledger cannot
supply. That reading is not itself a Coq theorem.

Two consequences worth stating explicitly:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The $k_B \ln 2$ appearing in the Thiele instantiation
    (\texttt{SecondLawBoltzmannWall.v}, base $= 2$ for binary cert-flips) is
    obtained after supplying $k_B$ and the base from outside: the file proves
    $\alpha = k_B \ln 2$ for a base-2 candidate under the Boltzmann premise.
  \item Any claim of the form ``this computational framework predicts a
    physical constant'' is blocked by Theorem~\ref{thm:dimensional_gap} unless
    it also says which two external scalars it assumed. This applies to the
    this development as much as to any other.
\end{itemize}

Read this section before the conditional physics chapters, not after: it is
the constraint they operate under.

\section{The Thiele Manifold}

\begin{definition}[Thiele Manifold {\normalfont (ThieleManifold.v)}]

\begin{itemize}

\chapter{Three-Layer Observable Agreement}

\begin{remark}[Agreement, not isomorphism]
No isomorphism is constructed in this chapter, and none is claimed: there is no
pair of mutually inverse maps between the Coq, OCaml/Python, and RTL layers, and
no proof that either composite is the identity. What is established is that any
two implementations satisfying a shared observable-projection contract agree on
the projected fields after a shared trace: transitivity of equality through a
common projection. The Coq layer satisfies the contract
(\texttt{coq\_\allowbreak{}full\_\allowbreak{}wire\_\allowbreak{}spec}). That the Python and RTL artifacts satisfy

the layers differing arbitrarily on everything the projection forgets; an
isomorphism would forbid that. Since the projection deliberately forgets
internal representation, agreement is the right property to want here. Isomorphism would be a stronger claim than the construction supports.

The remaining 10 (PNEW, PSPLIT, PMERGE, MORPH, MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ID, MORPH\_\allowbreak{}DELETE, MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ASSERT, MORPH\_\allowbreak{}GET, COMPOSE, MORPH\_\allowbreak{}TENSOR) carry Qed proofs under preconditions that differ by opcode. MORPH\_\allowbreak{}DELETE and MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ASSERT need \texttt{morph\_\allowbreak{}table\_\allowbreak{}wf}; MORPH\_\allowbreak{}GET, COMPOSE, and MORPH\_\allowbreak{}TENSOR need \texttt{extended\_\allowbreak{}hw\_\allowbreak{}invariant}, the conjunction of \texttt{pt\_\allowbreak{}well\_\allowbreak{}formed}, \texttt{morph\_\allowbreak{}table\_\allowbreak{}wf}, \texttt{coupling\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}empty}, \texttt{coupling\_\allowbreak{}wf}, and \texttt{coupling\_\allowbreak{}desc\_\allowbreak{}safe}; MORPH adds that invariant to module bounds and nonzero sizes; MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ID needs \texttt{coupling\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}empty}, a module bound, and a nonzero size; PNEW needs a nonempty normalized region, a module counter at most 64, and zero module tensors at the fresh slot; PSPLIT and PMERGE need \texttt{pt\_\allowbreak{}well\_\allowbreak{}formed} plus module-number, size, and fresh-slot conditions. Four of the invariant components are preserved by every \texttt{kami\_\allowbreak{}step}: \texttt{morph\_\allowbreak{}table\_\allowbreak{}wf\_\allowbreak{}kami\_\allowbreak{}step\_\allowbreak{}preserved}, \texttt{coupling\_\allowbreak{}desc\_\allowbreak{}safe\_\allowbreak{}kami\_\allowbreak{}step\_\allowbreak{}preserved}, \texttt{coupling\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}empty\_\allowbreak{}kami\_\allowbreak{}step\_\allowbreak{}preserved}, and \texttt{coupling\_\allowbreak{}wf\_\allowbreak{}kami\_\allowbreak{}step\_\allowbreak{}preserved}. The last takes \texttt{coupling\_\allowbreak{}desc\_\allowbreak{}safe} as a side hypothesis (in addition to \texttt{coupling\_\allowbreak{}wf}). No preservation theorem exists for \texttt{pt\_\allowbreak{}well\_\allowbreak{}formed} or for the operand-level premises, which remain per-instruction preconditions.
\end{remark}

\section{The CPU Against the Kernel}

The refinement theorems relate the Kami model of the CPU to \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}apply} under stated premises, and the two machines differ in ways those premises carry. The CPU's data words and its ledger register are 32 bits wide, and the ledger wraps at $2^{32}$; the kernel's words are 64 bits and its ledger is an unbounded natural number. Each refinement theorem assumes the counters it touches fit in 32 bits (\texttt{cpu\_\allowbreak{}preconditions} asks for $\mu < 2^{31}$). The CPU traps with a named error code on checks the kernel does not make: \texttt{LOAD}, \texttt{STORE}, their heap forms, \texttt{CALL} and \texttt{RET} outside the active module's range; a \texttt{PDISCOVER} whose declared cost is below its second operand; a ledger below the tensor total; malformed rich-format fields. The refinement theorems cover only steps on which none of these fires. Partition capacity and range are checked by both, with the same outcome. Retirement under the CPU's own scheduler is proved for every admitted instruction (\texttt{admitted\_\allowbreak{}retires}) and, from reset, for runs of admitted instructions (\texttt{fsm\_\allowbreak{}retirement\_\allowbreak{}refinement}). The board-top gate-level simulation, the SymbiYosys properties, and the board/loader equivalence check, which is conditional on the CPU interface and leaves the CPU and its RAM out, run outside Coq; no physical board has run the design.

\section{Named Trust Boundaries}

Two trust boundaries are named in Coq. The other downstream pipeline edges (extraction, the Kami printer, project text transforms, synthesis, place and route) are likewise trusted or tested rather than proved. They are not Admitted proofs. They are explicit, auditable limits of the formal claim.

\begin{description}
\item[\texttt{bsc\_\allowbreak{}kami\_\allowbreak{}compilation\_\allowbreak{}trusted} (Layer 2, Verilog semantics gap).] The Bluespec SystemVerilog compiler generates Verilog from the Kami hardware specification. The correctness of the BSC compilation step, that the generated Verilog implements exactly the Kami spec, is trusted, not proved in Coq. This is documented in \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}hardware\_\allowbreak{}bridge/\allowbreak{}VerilogRTLCorrespondence.v}, where \texttt{Definition bsc\_\allowbreak{}kami\_\allowbreak{}compilation\_\allowbreak{}trusted} is the conjunction of two abstract propositions, \texttt{kami\_\allowbreak{}pretty\_\allowbreak{}printer\_\allowbreak{}trusted} and \texttt{bluespec\_\allowbreak{}compiler\_\allowbreak{}trusted}. The Section Variable \texttt{rtl\_\allowbreak{}step\_\allowbreak{}correct} states that an abstract RTL step agrees with \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}apply} on projected state; this is the residual hypothesis that every theorem in the section quantifies over. An instance for generated Verilog additionally needs a model of that artifact and a semantic correspondence proof. The boundary is empirically backed by the cosim suite (\texttt{tests/\allowbreak{}test\_\allowbreak{}verilog\_\allowbreak{}cosim.py}, 38 tests, including \texttt{test\_\allowbreak{}all\_\allowbreak{}47\_\allowbreak{}opcodes\_\allowbreak{}in\_\allowbreak{}cosim} which checks that every opcode appears) and the parametrized cross-layer fuzz suites (\texttt{tests/\allowbreak{}test\_\allowbreak{}fuzz\_\allowbreak{}random\_\allowbreak{}programs.py} and \texttt{tests/\allowbreak{}test\_\allowbreak{}cross\_\allowbreak{}layer\_\allowbreak{}adversarial\_\allowbreak{}fuzz.py}).

\item[\texttt{A\_\allowbreak{}QM} (physical quantum axiom).] No theorem proves that physical hardware implementing the kernel produces CHSH-violating statistics by exploiting quantum entanglement in hardware. The \texttt{column\_\allowbreak{}contractive\_\allowbreak{}iff\_\allowbreak{}npa\_\allowbreak{}psd} biconditional (Theorem~\ref{thm:thiele_honest_iff_qr}) requires no physical axiom; it is pure algebra. The physical quantum axiom \texttt{A\_\allowbreak{}QM} (declared as a \texttt{Context} parameter in \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}PhysicsConditionalClosure.v}) is the separate named hypothesis that the chosen quantum CHSH experimental predicate admits a PSD completion with free within-party cross moments. It does not appear in the kernel-tier proof of the biconditional; it is the bridge used in conditional theorems such as \texttt{master\_\allowbreak{}tsirelson\_\allowbreak{}conditional}.
\end{description}
\chapter{Categorical Structure}
\label{ch:categorical}

\section{The Morphism Layer}

The active implementation adds a concrete category directly to the machine state. This is not an abstract analogy, it is an executable layer of 7 opcodes (0x27--0x2D) with formally verified laws.

\subsection{Objects and Morphisms}

The partition graph $G$ carries two maps:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\texttt{pg\_\allowbreak{}modules} : \mathrm{ModuleID} \rightharpoonup \mathrm{ModuleState}$, objects (partition modules)
    \item $\texttt{pg\_\allowbreak{}morphisms} : \mathrm{MorphismID} \rightharpoonup \mathrm{MorphismState}$, arrows
\end{itemize}

A $\mathrm{MorphismState}$ bundles: source module, target module, coupling data (list of $(nat \times nat)$ pairs plus a label), and an identity flag.

\subsection{The Seven Categorical Opcodes}

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\texttt{MORPH}(dst, src, tgt, idx, \Delta\mu)$: create morphism $src \to tgt$, write morphism ID to $\texttt{regs}[dst]$. Costs $\Delta\mu$.
    \item $\texttt{COMPOSE}(dst, f, g, \Delta\mu)$: compose morphisms $f ; g$ when $f.\mathrm{target} = g.\mathrm{source}$, write new ID to $\texttt{regs}[dst]$. Costs $\Delta\mu$. Errors if endpoints mismatch.
    \item $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ID}(dst, m, \Delta\mu)$: create identity morphism $\mathrm{id}_m$ for module $m$ (diagonal relation). Costs $\Delta\mu$.
    \item $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}DELETE}(f, \Delta\mu)$: remove morphism $f$. Cascades on module deletion via \texttt{graph\_\allowbreak{}cascade\_\allowbreak{}delete\_\allowbreak{}morphisms}.
    \item $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ASSERT}(f, \mathit{prop}, \mathit{cert}, \Delta\mu)$: cert-setter. Costs $S(\Delta\mu) \geq 1$ unconditionally, even for failed attempts (morphism does not exist). This is the No Free Insight law applied to relational claims.
    \item $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}TENSOR}(dst, f, g, \Delta\mu)$: tensor product $f \otimes g$. Requires that the union modules $\mathrm{src}(f) \cup \mathrm{src}(g)$ and $\mathrm{tgt}(f) \cup \mathrm{tgt}(g)$ already exist in the graph.
    \item $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}GET}(dst, f, \mathit{sel}, \Delta\mu)$: read field from morphism $f$: selector 0 = source, 1 = target, 2 = coupling length, 3 = is\_\allowbreak{}identity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Category Laws}

\begin{theorem}[Relational Composition is Associative, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:compose_assoc}
In \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}category/\allowbreak{}CategoryLaws.v}, for any three coupling lists $r_1, r_2, r_3$ (\texttt{relational\_\allowbreak{}compose\_\allowbreak{}assoc}):
\[ (r_1 ; r_2) ; r_3 \equiv r_1 ; (r_2 ; r_3) \]
where $\equiv$ is extensional equivalence of the composite coupling pairs, not equality of lists. Zero Admitted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Coupling Composition Satisfies Laws, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:graph_compose_assoc}
In \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}category/\allowbreak{}CategoryBridge.v}, the coupling-pair composite of looked-up morphisms is associative up to $\equiv$ (\texttt{morph\_\allowbreak{}graph\_\allowbreak{}compose\_\allowbreak{}assoc}, \texttt{morph\_\allowbreak{}compose\_\allowbreak{}assoc\_\allowbreak{}coupling}). Diagonal couplings are units up to $\equiv$ under domain and codomain premises: $\mathrm{diag}(R) ; f \equiv f$ when every pair of $f$ starts in $R$ (\texttt{morph\_\allowbreak{}id\_\allowbreak{}left\_\allowbreak{}coupling}), and $f ; \mathrm{diag}(R) \equiv f$ when every pair ends in $R$ (\texttt{morph\_\allowbreak{}id\_\allowbreak{}right\_\allowbreak{}coupling}). These are statements about \texttt{relational\_\allowbreak{}compose} on coupling-pair lists; the laws for arrows stored by \texttt{graph\_\allowbreak{}compose\_\allowbreak{}morphisms} are Theorem~\ref{thm:stored_arrow_laws}. Additionally:
\[ \texttt{categorical\_\allowbreak{}extension\_\allowbreak{}nofi\_\allowbreak{}consistent} \]
MORPH and COMPOSE can carry $\Delta\mu = 0$ and are not in the mandatory-cost class. The NoFI policy enters through $\texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}ASSERT}$: asserting that a built morphism chain exists and activating the cert channel always costs $S(\Delta\mu) \geq 1$. Building the chain is free; certifying it never is.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Category laws on stored arrows {\normalfont (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}category/\allowbreak{}CategoryBridge.v})}, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:stored_arrow_laws}
The composite that \texttt{COMPOSE} stores carries the identity flag exactly when both factors carry it, and the composite of two identity arrows is stored as an identity arrow with empty coupling data and cost $0$ (\texttt{graph\_\allowbreak{}compose\_\allowbreak{}identities\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}identity}). Call two stored arrows equivalent when they have the same source, target and identity flag and, if not identities, equivalent couplings. Then on a well-formed graph, composing $f;k$ then $h$ and composing $f$ with $k;h$ store equivalent arrows (\texttt{graph\_\allowbreak{}compose\_\allowbreak{}assoc\_\allowbreak{}stored}), and an identity arrow $i$ with equal source and target is a left and a right unit up to equivalence (\texttt{graph\_\allowbreak{}compose\_\allowbreak{}left\_\allowbreak{}identity\_\allowbreak{}stored}, \texttt{graph\_\allowbreak{}compose\_\allowbreak{}right\_\allowbreak{}identity\_\allowbreak{}stored}). From a graph with no arrows, every reachable graph stores each identity arrow with equal endpoints and no pairs (\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}reachable\_\allowbreak{}identity\_\allowbreak{}arrows\_\allowbreak{}canonical}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Tensor Interchange {\normalfont (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}category/\allowbreak{}CategoryMonoidal.v})}, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:tensor_bifunctor}
Take the tensor of coupling lists to be list append $\mathbin{+\!\!+}$. For coupling lists $p_f, p_g, p_{f'}, p_{g'}$ whose cross composites are empty ($p_f ; p_{g'} \equiv [\,]$ and $p_g ; p_{f'} \equiv [\,]$), the interchange law holds (\texttt{tensor\_\allowbreak{}bifunctor}):
\[ (p_f \mathbin{+\!\!+} p_g) ; (p_{f'} \mathbin{+\!\!+} p_{g'}) \equiv (p_f ; p_{f'}) \mathbin{+\!\!+} (p_g ; p_{g'}) \]
up to $\equiv$. It is a statement about coupling-list append, not about \texttt{graph\_\allowbreak{}tensor\_\allowbreak{}morphisms} as a graph operation. \texttt{Kernel.\allowbreak{}CategoryMonoidal.\allowbreak{}monoidal\_\allowbreak{}coherence} (associativity and units of \texttt{coupling\_\allowbreak{}tensor}) is a separate theorem.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The Categorical Separation Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Categorical Separation {\normalfont (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}foundation/\allowbreak{}PartitionSeparation.v}, §10)}, \textbf{(S)}]
\label{thm:categorical_separation}
There exist states $s_1, s_2$ such that:
\[
\begin{aligned}
& s_1.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}regs} = s_2.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}regs} \;\wedge\;
s_1.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mem}  = s_2.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mem}  \;\wedge\;
s_1.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu}   = s_2.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}mu}   \;\wedge\; \\
& s_1.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}pc}   = s_2.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}pc}   \;\wedge\;
s_1.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}err}  = s_2.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}err}  \;\wedge\;
s_1.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified} = s_2.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified}
\end{aligned}
\]
but $s_1.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}graph}.\texttt{pg\_\allowbreak{}morphisms} \neq s_2.\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}graph}.\texttt{pg\_\allowbreak{}morphisms}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Two explicit states that differ only in $\texttt{pg\_\allowbreak{}morphisms}$: $s_1$ holds one identity morphism (source and target $0$), $s_2$ holds none. Every other field is identical. Distinction by $\texttt{discriminate}$, on the list constructor mismatch (cons against nil). \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Significance]
A classical observer, one that sees only registers, memory, $\mu$, PC, error flag, and certification status, cannot distinguish $s_1$ from $s_2$ (\texttt{classical\_\allowbreak{}observer\_\allowbreak{}cannot\_\allowbreak{}separate}). \texttt{shadow\_\allowbreak{}strictly\_\allowbreak{}lossy} (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}witness/\allowbreak{}ShadowProjection.v}) gives two states with the same shadow, different morphism lists, and a graph-preserving probe that retains the distinction. No Coq statement says what \texttt{MORPH\_\allowbreak{}DELETE}~$0$ does on these two states. The categorical layer records \emph{how} knowledge was derived, not just \emph{what} the final values are.

This is the machine's claim to being a \emph{knowledge auditor}: a certifying step must have paid at least $1$ (\texttt{certification\_\allowbreak{}requires\_\allowbreak{}positive\_\allowbreak{}mu}). The NoFI theorem supplies a cost floor under its trace hypotheses. It does not authenticate an externally supplied receipt or prove arbitrary property text true.
\end{remark}
\chapter{Falsifiable Predictions}

The falsifiable predictions are the structural theorems that compile or fail. The claims below identify specific mathematical conditions that would constitute counterexamples to the machine's core theorems.

\section{Falsify No Free Insight}

Find a kernel step \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}apply}~$s~i$ that takes \texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certified} from false to true, or \texttt{csr\_\allowbreak{}cert\_\allowbreak{}addr} from zero to non-zero, without raising $\mu$ by at least $1$. The Coq proof of \texttt{certification\_\allowbreak{}requires\_\allowbreak{}positive\_\allowbreak{}mu} would fail. The trace form is the trace-level No Free Insight theorem (\texttt{universal\_\allowbreak{}nfi\_\allowbreak{}any\_\allowbreak{}substrate}).

\section{Falsify the Classical CHSH Bound}

Exhibit witness counts with $|S| > 2$ that are consistent with one deterministic local strategy. This contradicts \texttt{chsh\_\allowbreak{}stat\_\allowbreak{}violation\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}local}. Exhibit a factorizable correlator with $|S| > 2$ and it contradicts \texttt{fine\_\allowbreak{}theorem} (\texttt{MinorConstraints.v}); either also contradicts known mathematics.

\section{Falsify the Biconditional}

Exhibit $E_{00}, E_{01}, E_{10}, E_{11}$ satisfying the three column-contractivity conditions but with a non-PSD NPA moment matrix. The theorem \texttt{column\_\allowbreak{}contractive\_\allowbreak{}iff\_\allowbreak{}npa\_\allowbreak{}psd} says no such example exists.

\section{Falsify the $\mu$-Hierarchy}

Find $k \geq 1$ and a trace from \texttt{init\_\allowbreak{}state} that is \texttt{level\_\allowbreak{}k\_\allowbreak{}certified} with \texttt{trace\_\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}cost} $< k$. This contradicts \texttt{mu\_\allowbreak{}hierarchy\_\allowbreak{}theorem}.

\section{Boundaries and conjectures}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Physical interpretation of $\mu$}: The two-state calorimeter protocol fixes populations, a Hamiltonian, a discrete master equation, and exact heat, but proves that the dynamics alone do not fix the Hamiltonian scale. No theorem identifies the $\mu$ ledger with thermodynamic entropy in a physical device. The calibration hypothesis is named (\texttt{mu\_\allowbreak{}landauer\_\allowbreak{}unruh\_\allowbreak{}calibrated}) and not proved in Coq.
\item \textbf{Quantum mechanics connection}: The mathematical equivalence between slice coherence and PSD of the zero-marginal NPA matrix is a kernel-tier theorem. No theorem identifies the VM with physical entanglement hardware or proves that such hardware produces CHSH-violating statistics.
\item \textbf{Complexity implications}: No $\mu$P $\neq$ $\mu$NP class-separation theorem is claimed. The factored-search witness proves a concrete structural advantage under its stated model.
\item \textbf{Physical constants}: No physical constant is derived from first principles. There is no derivation of Planck's constant $h$; any relation tying an internal time-scale $\tau_\mu$ to $h$ would be circular by construction, defining $\tau_\mu$ in terms of $h$ and checking consistency rather than predicting $h$.
\item \textbf{The two $+1$s}: the \texttt{+1} in \texttt{triangle\_\allowbreak{}angle}'s denominator (\texttt{PI · d\_\allowbreak{}bc /\allowbreak{} (1 + d\_\allowbreak{}ab + d\_\allowbreak{}ac + d\_\allowbreak{}bc)}, \texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}curvature/\allowbreak{}MuGravity.v}) versus the A2 cost-floor \texttt{+1} on CERTIFY/LASSERT (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}foundation/\allowbreak{}VMStep.v}), same axiom, or unrelated regularisation? No kernel theorem identifies them (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}frontier/\allowbreak{}TriangleAnglePlusOne.v}). That file proves \texttt{instr\_certify\_cost\_is\_successor}, which is \texttt{instruction\_\allowbreak{}cost (instr\_\allowbreak{}certify cost) = S cost} and says nothing about \texttt{triangle\_\allowbreak{}angle}, and \texttt{triangle\_\allowbreak{}angle\_\allowbreak{}plus\_\allowbreak{}one\_\allowbreak{}correction\_\allowbreak{}decays}: the correction term decays as $-\pi / (3(3d+1))$ in the equilateral case (\texttt{tools/\allowbreak{}triangle\_\allowbreak{}angle\_\allowbreak{}plus\_\allowbreak{}one\_\allowbreak{}analysis.py} computes it symbolically). The kernel uses the stated denominator; no identification theorem is claimed.
\item \textbf{Non-vacuity of the calibrated angle-defect theorem}: refuted. Calibration at every module is impossible on a well-formed triangulated partition graph whose vertex links are connected and whose module identifiers are distinct (\texttt{connected\_triangulation\_not\_calibrated}); the hypotheses are met by a concrete state (\texttt{calibration\_obstruction\_hypotheses\_satisfiable}). The headline of \texttt{PartitionTopologyCrossLink.v} is therefore vacuous on such graphs, and on states the VM reaches it is vacuous for every graph with a module (\texttt{reachable\_\allowbreak{}calibrated\_\allowbreak{}iff\_\allowbreak{}no\_\allowbreak{}modules}). No theorem derives the asymptotic behavior from the $+1$.
\item \textbf{Cross-base equivalence} (\texttt{coq/\allowbreak{}kernel/\allowbreak{}foundation/\allowbreak{}CrossBaseGranularity*.v}): Base adapters for the Turing machine
(\texttt{tm\_\allowbreak{}base}), the VM (\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}base}), a Cook--Reckhow unit-cost
RAM (\texttt{ram\_\allowbreak{}base}), and L (\texttt{l\_\allowbreak{}base}) exist, and each satisfies
\texttt{record\_\allowbreak{}axis\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}latch\_\allowbreak{}on} (\texttt{record\_\allowbreak{}axis\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}latch\_\allowbreak{}on\_\allowbreak{}tm\_\allowbreak{}holds}, \texttt{record\_\allowbreak{}axis\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}latch\_\allowbreak{}on\_\allowbreak{}vm\_\allowbreak{}holds},
\texttt{record\_\allowbreak{}axis\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}latch\_\allowbreak{}on\_\allowbreak{}ram\_\allowbreak{}holds}, \texttt{record\_\allowbreak{}axis\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}latch\_\allowbreak{}on\_\allowbreak{}l\_\allowbreak{}holds}); \texttt{record\_\allowbreak{}axis\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}latch\_\allowbreak{}on} holds for every base, so these instances do not distinguish the bases. The substantive adapter facts are that the L and RAM bases are faithful (\texttt{l\_\allowbreak{}step\_\allowbreak{}fun\_\allowbreak{}correct}, \texttt{star\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}l\_\allowbreak{}base\_\allowbreak{}run}). The relation
\texttt{weak\_\allowbreak{}base\_\allowbreak{}equiv} is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, and it carries no
cost. Cost-respecting simulations between different bases are not constructed,
and no \texttt{weak\_\allowbreak{}base\_\allowbreak{}equiv} between two different bases is proved.
\item \textbf{Cost-framework comparison}: The graded, writer, potential, and
counting theorems are local correspondences. Full semantics-preserving
embeddings of the four cost-framework families have not been constructed.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Exact scope}

The record-axis theorems have an exact scope: a permanent record driven by the
base computation is a latch on an event, a finite permanent write is
non-injective, and the VM's named projections do not recover its record.
Certification is an instance; no theorem derives a unique event choice.

Finite monotone multi-valued records admit threshold-latch decompositions, but
the corresponding single-latch, revocable, and probabilistic uniqueness
statements are false. The guest VM has a direct Rice reduction, numeric
decoding, verified fuel-bounded dispatch, semantic s-m-n specialization, and
its own recursion theorem (\texttt{vm\_guest\_recursion\_theorem\_closed}):
every program transformer that a guest program computes on program codes has a
fixed point that matches it in all four final registers and in $\mu$ on every
input, halting or not. The evaluator in that proof is extracted to the lambda
calculus L, compiled to a Minsky machine, and executed by guest code. For the
full VM, a fixed point for every map on programs up to equal thousand-step runs
is false. The 51-opcode VM prices commitments without checking that they were
earned; the minimal two-counter machine earns them. The geometry theorems are
about partition graphs in general; on states the VM reaches, module regions are
disjoint and calibration holds only with no modules. The
two-state calorimeter computes exact heat and proves that identical population
dynamics do not determine the Hamiltonian scale. Converting $\mu$ to energy
requires thermal and device premises; $\mu$ has no intrinsic energy unit.

The RFC 9162 file checks iterative inclusion and consistency control flow and
examples, not cryptographic collision resistance or signed-tree-head
authenticity. The PCC, addressed-RAM, reversible-update, and TPM-interface
files prove only their named fragment or countermodel propositions. The local
cost-semantics correspondences are not full cost-framework embeddings.

The twelve-event observer-map bundle and swap theorem are closed. Its
MAC-labelled model is non-proliferating, but applying that statement to a real
system depends on the observer model. The two-observer toggle game refutes the
claim that consensus, exact observation, and coordinator-free evolution force
permanence; the durability theorem supplies persistence as a premise, and the VM
certification game satisfies it (\texttt{vm\_\allowbreak{}certification\_\allowbreak{}is\_\allowbreak{}permanent\_\allowbreak{}consensus}). The
external consequence survey contains five
narrow statements already known from their governing specifications and five
stronger statements without enough protocol or hardware semantics. No
surveyed consequence is simultaneously record-axis dependent, novel, and ready
for use. The pointer criterion is a conjecture.
