\noindent\textbf{The Thiele Machine is an abstract model of computation, and an argument made with it about what an account of computation should preserve.}

It is a machine in the sense a Turing machine is one: a mathematical object, not a device. The model is the abstract \texttt{CertificationSystem} record and the \texttt{Substrate} interface. The small machine of \texttt{minimal/EarnedCore.v}, a two-counter machine whose commitments are earned, is one instance; the universal machine U built on it is another. They are witnesses, not the model.

Certification is one concrete witness of a broader investigation of structural state, observation, and cost. Selected windows lose distinctions that the richer state can express; cost laws constrain specified events under explicit hypotheses. A conventional encoding can preserve those distinctions. A window that discards them cannot recover them in general. The stronger proposal that these distinctions belong in a necessary foundation of computation is a conjecture. The status of each result is stated below.

\begin{abstract}
This is a mathematical reference for the Thiele Machine: a model of computation in which a step that raises a certification record must pay into a ledger that only grows. It presents the abstract interfaces, the main theorems, and the definitions of the small machine from the machine-checked Coq corpus. The Coq source is the complete definition; this document states each result with its premises and names the declaration that carries it.

Coq establishes that conclusions follow from premises. It does \emph{not} establish that premises model physical reality. Every result is tagged as one of three types: \textbf{(S)}~structural theorems about the model or a stated machine, \textbf{(C)}~conditional results that hold under a named premise no machine fact discharges, and \textbf{(R)}~refutations, where a theorem states the negation of a proposed claim and a named construction supplies the counterexample.

The core claims are structural (S): No Free Insight for every certification system; on finite machines, the toll derived from merge pricing for permanent certificates; the window and pricing results; the small machine's earned provenance, checker soundness and minimum price; Thiele completeness of the small machine and the failure of every clock-style machine; the window theorem for every Thiele-complete machine; the universal machine U; the record axis over any base; the recursion theorem and Rice's theorem for L; the classical CHSH bound; the algebraic Tsirelson bound; and, on the zero-marginal slice, the equivalence of column contractivity with positive semidefiniteness of one fixed $5\times 5$ NPA matrix. Results stated over Landauer heat are conditional (C) on the named premise \texttt{landauer\_heat}.
\end{abstract}

A computation is usually described by what it reads and writes. The Thiele Machine adds a record of what the computation established and a ledger of what establishing it cost, and a rule tying the two together: the step that raises the record pays. \chapter{Notation and Conventions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\N$ is the natural numbers, $\B = \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$, $\Reals$ the reals.
  \item A \emph{trace} is a finite list of instructions. $\mathrm{run}(\tau, s)$ folds the step function over $\tau$ from $s$.
  \item $\cert$ is the certification reading, a function from states to $\B$. In Coq it is \texttt{cs\_cert} on a \texttt{CertificationSystem}.
  \item $\cost(i) \in \N$ is the cost of instruction $i$; $\cost(\tau)$ is the sum over a trace.
  \item $\mu$ is the ledger. Where a state carries it, conservation means $\mu(\mathrm{step}(s,i)) = \mu(s) + \cost(i)$.
  \item A \emph{window} is a function from states to observations.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Certification Systems and No Free Insight}\label{ch:nfi}

\begin{definition}[Certification system]
A certification system is a tuple $(S, I, \mathrm{step}, \cost, \cert)$ with $\mathrm{step} : S \times I \to S$, $\cost : I \to \N$, $\cert : S \to \B$, satisfying
\[
\textbf{A2:}\quad \forall s\, i,\ \cert(s) = \mathrm{false} \wedge \cert(\mathrm{step}(s,i)) = \mathrm{true} \implies \cost(i) \ge 1.
\]
Coq: the record \texttt{CertificationSystem}, with A2 as its field \texttt{cs\_cert\_costs}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[No Free Insight]\label{thm:nfi} \textbf{(S)}
In every certification system, if $\cert(s_0) = \mathrm{false}$ and $\cert(\mathrm{run}(\tau, s_0)) = \mathrm{true}$, then $\cost(\tau) \ge 1$.
Coq: \texttt{universal\_nfi\_any\_substrate}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induction on $\tau$. Since $\cert$ is false at the start and true at the end, some step in $\tau$ takes it from false to true. By A2 that step costs at least $1$; every other step costs at least $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Simulating systems] \textbf{(S)}
Let a base certification system be simulated by a host: an embedding of base states into host states, a decoding of each base instruction into a list of host instructions, a commuting step law, and certification reflected by the embedding (record \texttt{SimulatingCertificationSystem}). A base trace from uncertified to certified costs at least $1$ in the base, the host run of its decoded trace ends certified, and that host run costs at least $1$.
Coq: \texttt{host\_represents\_simulating\_cert\_system}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative form] \textbf{(S)}
Extend a certification system with a witness $w : S \to \N$ and a threshold $k$ such that each step raises $w$ by at most its cost and every certified state has $w \ge k$ (record \texttt{QuantitativeCertificationSystem}). A trace from a state with $w = 0$ to a certified state costs at least $k$.
Coq: \texttt{universal\_nfi\_quantitative}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[A2 is a restriction] \textbf{(S)}
A cost-bearing system without A2 can switch certification on for free, and A2 excludes exactly that.
Coq: \texttt{honest\_cost\_tracking\_strict\_restriction}, \texttt{free\_forgery\_violates\_A2}.
\end{theorem}

\section{A2 among cost frameworks}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
A step system satisfies A2 if and only if every step has non-negative amortized cost under the potential that is $1$ on uncertified states and $0$ on certified ones; No Free Insight is then the potential method's telescoping bound.
Coq: \texttt{a2\_iff\_nonnegative\_amortized\_cost}, \texttt{nfi\_by\_potential}.
Under A2 the number of certification flips along a trace is at most its cost (\texttt{flips\_le\_cost}), and A2 together with the matching upper-bound condition is equivalent to exact pricing of a permanent certificate (\texttt{a2\_and\_aara\_iff\_exact}).
\end{theorem}

These are local correspondences, not embeddings of the cited cost-framework families (graded monads, amortized resource analysis, cost translations, linear logic).

\section{Several floors and one charge}

\begin{theorem}[Joint floors] \textbf{(S)}
For a finite family of events, the least cost schedule meeting every unit floor charges one on a step where any event occurs and zero otherwise.
Coq: \texttt{joint\_floor\_is\_least}. On pairs of Booleans, two independent coordinates that both rise in one step are met by a single charge of $1$ (\texttt{independent\_coordinates\_joint\_change\_costs\_one}).
\end{theorem}

Independent state coordinates do not by themselves force additive cost.

\begin{theorem}[Exact pricing] \textbf{(S)}
For trusted local-predicate pricing systems, a schedule that charges exactly one on the steps its predicate marks meets the certification floor and never overcharges exactly when its predicate marks precisely the certification flips.
Coq: \texttt{exact\_commitment\_pricing\_characterization}.
\end{theorem}

\chapter{Finite Machines and the Price of Merging}\label{ch:finite}

Fix a state type $S$ with a finite enumeration, instructions $I$, a step function and a reading $\cert$. The certificate is \emph{permanent} when no step switches it off. An instruction is \emph{injective} when its step function is.

\begin{theorem}[A permanent flip merges] \textbf{(S)}
On a finite state space with a permanent certificate, an instruction that switches the certificate on at some state is not injective.
Coq: \texttt{permanent\_flip\_is\_not\_injective}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If the instruction were injective it would map the certified states injectively into the certified states, so onto them, leaving no certified image for the uncertified state it switches on.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A2 from merge pricing] \textbf{(S)}
On a finite state space with a permanent certificate, if every non-injective instruction costs at least $1$, A2 holds.
Coq: \texttt{a2\_from\_merging\_price\_and\_permanence}; the trace form is \texttt{permanent\_certification\_trace\_floor}, and an honest erasure account gives the same bill (\texttt{honest\_erasure\_accounting\_implies\_a2}).
\end{theorem}

Each premise is needed. Unbounded history escapes (\texttt{unbounded\_history\_escapes}), a revocable certificate escapes (\texttt{revocable\_certificate\_escapes}), and an unpriced merge escapes (\texttt{free\_merge\_escapes}).

\begin{theorem}[The size of the bill] \textbf{(S)}
With $m$ certified states and $k$ uncertified states that one instruction switches on, the instruction collapses at least $k$ states (\texttt{permanent\_flips\_collapse\_at\_least}). Priced one unit per halving of the number of distinct states, it costs at least $\log_2((m+k)/m)$.
Coq: \texttt{permanent\_flips\_log\_bound}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Which flips are forced] \textbf{(S)}
Over all schedules that price merges, an instruction is forced to have positive price if and only if it merges two states (\texttt{forced\_priced\_iff\_merges}). An instruction that switches a reading on either merges or switches it off somewhere else (\texttt{flip\_merges\_or\_revokes}). So every permanent-record write is forced, and the only free flips are revocable ones.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Refutation] \textbf{(R)}
``The forced events are exactly the permanent-record writes'' is false: a three-state instruction flips, revokes, and merges at once.
Coq: \texttt{forced\_price\_without\_permanent\_record}.
\end{theorem}

\section{Entropy and heat}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
After a permanent step, any distribution on the $m$ certified states and the $k$ flipping ones has Shannon entropy at most $\log_2 m$ (\texttt{permanent\_step\_entropy\_ceiling}). From the uniform distribution the step removes at least $\log_2((m+k)/m)$ bits (\texttt{permanent\_flip\_uniform\_entropy\_drop}). An injective step removes none (\texttt{step\_entropy\_invariant\_if\_injective}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Heat] \textbf{(C)}
Given the named premise \texttt{landauer\_heat}, which states Landauer's principle for a step and a distribution, the flip dissipates at least $k_B T \ln((m+k)/m)$ from the uniform distribution (\texttt{permanent\_flip\_heat\_floor}), and positive heat from any distribution that leaves every certified state and the flipping one possible (\texttt{permanent\_flip\_full\_support\_heat\_positive}). For a state known in advance, the premise is equivalent to heat $\ge 0$, so it forces no heat (\texttt{known\_state\_flip\_forces\_no\_heat}).
\end{theorem}

\section{An eight-state instance}

\begin{example}
Four program slots and a certification flag make eight states. An advance step is injective (\texttt{fnext\_injective}); the certify step and every jump merge states (\texttt{fcertify\_merges}). The cost rule prices exactly the merges, so A2 holds there as a theorem (\texttt{fin\_a2\_from\_merging\_price}).
\end{example}

\section{No intrinsic joule value}

\begin{theorem}[Refutation] \textbf{(R)}
For every state type and natural-number ledger, two real-valued scales agree on the ledger and differ in energy; the ledger fixes no energy unit.
Coq: \texttt{mu\_has\_no\_intrinsic\_joule\_value}. Choosing the scale $k_B T \ln 2$ gives one ledger unit that energy by definition (\texttt{calibrated\_mu\_landauer\_energy}); the scale is a premise, not a measurement.
\end{theorem}

\chapter{Windows, Pricing and Narrowing}\label{ch:windows}

\begin{theorem}[Window adequacy] \textbf{(S)}
A query on states can be answered by a function of a window only if it is constant on the window's fibers, the sets of states the window shows the same way (\texttt{decoding\_requires\_fiber\_constancy}). Given a representative for every view, fiber constancy is equivalent to factoring through a decoder (\texttt{selected\_representatives\_give\_decoder}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Shadow pricing] \textbf{(S)}
If a certifying step and a non-certifying step show the same window before and after, no price computed from the window meets the certification floor without charging the non-certifying step.
Coq: \texttt{shadow\_cannot\_price\_exactly}. A window that shows the reading prices certification exactly (\texttt{window\_showing\_reading\_prices\_exactly}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Retained history] \textbf{(S)}
Keeping the previous state in a history makes every deterministic step injective without changing its observed transition.
Coq: \texttt{retained\_history\_step\_injective}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Narrowing] \textbf{(S)}
On a finite machine that prices merges, each halving of the machine's own set of possible states along a run is paid for (\texttt{run\_narrowing\_priced\_log}). What an observer knows can shrink for free: a measurement that copies a hidden bit into a blank display forgets nothing, and one admissible merge-priced cost assigns it zero (\texttt{observer\_narrowing\_can\_be\_free}); wiping the display is a merge and costs at least one (\texttt{wipe\_costs\_at\_least\_one}).
\end{theorem}

\chapter{The Small Machine}\label{ch:small}

The small machine is defined in \texttt{minimal/EarnedCore.v} on the Coq standard library alone.

\begin{definition}[State]
A core has two counters $a, b \in \N$, their versions $v_a, v_b \in \N$, a program counter $\mathit{pc} \ge 1$, a table of facts, a commitment channel holding at most one fact, and a trap latch. A fact is a triple (property, counter, version); the properties are ``is zero'', ``is even'' and ``is at least $n$'', each with a checker \texttt{eval} and a meaning \texttt{holds} tied by \texttt{eval\_iff}. A state is a core with a ledger $\mu \in \N$ and a flag $\cert \in \B$. The table holds at most $16$ facts. The start state for $(a, b)$ has no facts, an empty channel, the flag down and $\mu = 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Instructions and costs]
$\texttt{INC}\ c$ adds one to counter $c$. $\texttt{DEC}\ c\ j$ subtracts one and jumps to $j$ if $c > 0$, and otherwise falls through. \texttt{HALT} stops. $\texttt{CHECK}\ p\ c$ evaluates $p$ on counter $c$ and, if it holds and the table has room, writes the fact $(p, c, v_c)$. $\texttt{COMMIT}\ p\ c$ succeeds only if the fact $(p, c, v_c)$ is in the table, and then sets the channel to it. \texttt{CERTIFY} succeeds only if the channel names a fact. Every failure sets the trap latch, after which no instruction changes anything but the ledger. Every write to a counter adds one to its version. CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY cost $1$; the others cost $0$. The ledger adds the cost of every executed instruction, and the flag rises exactly on a passing CERTIFY.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Universality] \textbf{(S)}
The INC/DEC fragment is the two-counter machine step for step, and a program halts on the small machine exactly when it halts as a two-counter program.
Coq: \texttt{simulation\_run}, \texttt{halting\_correspondence}. Its halting problem is undecidable, by reduction from the vendored two-counter result (\texttt{earned\_core\_halting\_undecidable}). The Turing-universality of two-counter machines is Minsky's theorem, cited.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The toll] \textbf{(S)}
The ledger is the exact sum of executed costs (\texttt{mu\_conservation\_trace}); only CERTIFY raises the flag (\texttt{only\_certify\_certifies}); the flag never drops (\texttt{cert\_permanent}); A2 holds (\texttt{a2}); and a run from an uncertified state to a certified one pays at least $1$ (\texttt{nfi\_floor}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Earned provenance] \textbf{(S)}
From a clean start, every certified run contains a passing CHECK of some claim, then a passing COMMIT of the same claim with that counter untouched in between, then CERTIFY.
Coq: \texttt{earned\_certification\_provenance}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Checker soundness and no forging] \textbf{(S)}
On a run from a clean start, a fact whose counter has not been written since its check names a true claim about that counter (\texttt{checker\_soundness}), and every fact in the table was written by a passing CHECK of exactly that claim (\texttt{no\_forging}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Price] \textbf{(S)}
From a clean start a certified run costs at least $3$ (\texttt{certified\_run\_min\_cost}), and a three-instruction program pays exactly $3$ (\texttt{min\_cost\_tight}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[A check that can fail] \textbf{(S)}
The program CHECK ``$a$ is zero'', COMMIT, CERTIFY certifies from a start with $a = 0$, paying exactly $3$, and from any other start traps with the flag down, for every number of steps.
Coq: \texttt{earned\_run\_check\_can\_fail}, \texttt{earned\_run\_refused\_forever}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Links] \textbf{(S)}
The small machine is a certification system with the trace floor (\texttt{earned\_core\_floor}), and as a record-carrying machine its record moves as a latch on one base event (\texttt{earned\_core\_is\_latch}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
\texttt{minimal/EarnedGeneric.v} states the same machine over any property language with an exact checker; sorted lists are one instance (\texttt{sorted\_cs\_sound}). \texttt{minimal/EarnedMulti.v} has a counter for every natural number and is the host machine of Chapter~\ref{ch:universal}.
\end{remark}

\chapter{Thiele Completeness}\label{ch:complete}

\begin{definition}[Machine, weak completeness]
A machine is a state type, a move type, a step, a cost per move and a record $\cert : S \to \B$ (record \texttt{machine}). It is weakly Thiele-complete when it is a certification system whose base is universal.
\end{definition}

The weak notion is nearly empty: a two-counter machine that charges $1$ for every move meets it for any record (\texttt{clock\_weakly\_thiele\_complete}).

\begin{definition}[Thiele complete]
A machine is Thiele-complete when its moves can be read as base moves and three record moves, CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY of a claim, over a type of claims with a meaning on states, a checker, a notion of the thing a claim is about being unchanged, a set of clean starts and a ledger, such that:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item \emph{Universal base.} Every two-counter instruction has a base move that acts on a window of the state exactly as the instruction acts on a two-counter configuration, every two-counter start has a clean live state showing it, base moves never change the record, and the record once up stays up.
  \item \emph{Earned record.} A clean start has the record down. Every run from a clean start that ends with the record up contains a passing CHECK of some claim, then a COMMIT of the same claim with the thing it is about unchanged in between, then the CERTIFY that raises the record. A passing check means its claim holds, and a claim that holds keeps holding while the thing it is about is unchanged.
  \item \emph{Exact toll.} Base moves cost $0$, each record move costs exactly $1$, and the ledger grows by the cost of each move.
  \item \emph{Non-vacuity.} Some claim is true at some clean start and false at another, and the bare chain CHECK, COMMIT, CERTIFY on it raises the record from a clean start exactly when the claim holds there.
\end{enumerate}
Coq: \texttt{thiele\_complete} in \texttt{minimal/ThieleComplete.v}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Consequences] \textbf{(S)}
In every Thiele-complete machine: the ledger counts the record moves (\texttt{ledger\_counts\_record\_moves}); a certificate costs at least $3$ (\texttt{certificate\_costs\_three}); a committed claim holds when committed (\texttt{committed\_claim\_holds}); some check fails (\texttt{check\_can\_fail}); some run certifies (\texttt{some\_run\_certifies}); the move raising the record from a clean start is a CERTIFY (\texttt{only\_certify\_raises}); and some move costs $0$ (\texttt{complete\_has\_free\_move}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Machines that meet it] \textbf{(S)}
The small machine is Thiele-complete (\texttt{earned\_core\_thiele\_complete}), and so are the machine over any property language with an exact checker and a property true of one value and false of another (\texttt{earned\_generic\_thiele\_complete}) and the sorted-list machine (\texttt{sorted\_machine\_thiele\_complete}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Machines that fail it] \textbf{(R)}
Every clock-style machine, with any record, is weakly Thiele-complete and not Thiele-complete (\texttt{clock\_not\_thiele\_complete}); so is a clock whose record is a latch on its configuration (\texttt{latch\_clock\_not\_thiele\_complete}). A machine with a free base and one paid move that raises a latch meets clauses (a) and (c) and fails (b) (\texttt{one\_move\_record\_excluded}, \texttt{paid\_latch\_not\_thiele\_complete}). A machine whose record never rises meets (a), (b) and (c) and fails (d) (\texttt{silent\_not\_thiele\_complete}).
\end{theorem}

\section{The window theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Every Thiele-complete machine hides its record] \textbf{(S)}
For every Thiele-complete machine there are a clean start and two runs from it, the second made of base moves only, that end at the same base window; the first ends certified with its ledger at least $3$ above the start, and the second ends uncertified with its ledger unchanged (\texttt{complete\_two\_runs}). Hence no function of the base window recovers the record (\texttt{complete\_hides\_record}) or the ledger (\texttt{complete\_hides\_ledger}) on every reachable state (\texttt{thiele\_complete\_hides}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Clause (d) and clause (b) give a clean start and a certifying run. The base is universal, so base moves alone reach the window the certifying run ends at; base moves change neither the record nor the ledger.
\end{proof}

Every Thiele-complete machine is a certification system with the trace floor, and its window is blind to its reading (\texttt{thiele\_complete\_floor}, \texttt{complete\_cs\_window\_blind}).

\chapter{The Universal Machine}\label{ch:universal}

\section{The universal host}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
The host of \texttt{minimal/UniversalThiele.v} runs any small-machine program as a guest. Its mirror of the guest's flag always equals that flag (\texttt{record\_agreement}). The mirror rises only in a guest step that passed CERTIFY, charged in that same host step (\texttt{no\_free\_host\_certification\_step}, \texttt{toll\_enforced\_by\_host\_step}). The host is a certification system with the trace floor (\texttt{host\_nfi}).
\end{theorem}

A machine from elsewhere enters through the two-counter encoding; its computation and the toll are kept, its own price list is not.

\section{U}

U is one fixed program of the machine of \texttt{minimal/EarnedMulti.v}, with the single property of \texttt{minimal/UniversalCodes.v}, made of the small machine's own instruction kinds and with no guest step built into anything (\texttt{coq/kernel/foundation/UniversalLayout.v}). For a small-machine program $P$ and a start $(x, y)$, $g_m$ is the guest after $m$ steps and $h_n$ is U after $n$ steps from the loaded state.

\begin{theorem}[Simulation] \textbf{(S)}
For every $m$ there is $n \ge m$ with $h_n$ related to $g_m$ by the simulation relation, or both machines have stopped.
Coq: \texttt{U\_simulation}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Halting and output] \textbf{(S)}
The guest halts if and only if U halts (\texttt{universal\_halting}); at halting, U's first two registers hold the guest's counters, and the trap latches, ledgers and flags are equal (\texttt{universal\_output}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The flag is earned again] \textbf{(S)}
The guest's flag ever rises if and only if U's flag ever rises (\texttt{universal\_flag\_iff}). A raised flag of U stands on U's own passing CHECK, its own COMMIT of the same claim with the slot untouched, and the CERTIFY that raised it; when checked, the slot held the guest's claim and value, and the guest's own run contains its chain on the same claim (\texttt{universal\_earned}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The ledger] \textbf{(S)}
At matching points U's ledger equals the guest's, and every ledger of U lies between the guest's ledger at some $m$ and at $m+1$.
Coq: \texttt{universal\_ledger\_exact}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
The machine U runs on, read with INC and DEC as its base and CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY as its record moves, is Thiele-complete (\texttt{universal\_thiele\_complete}). Its halting problem is undecidable (\texttt{interp\_halting\_undecidable}).
\end{theorem}

\chapter{Recursion and Undecidability}\label{ch:undecidability}

\begin{definition}[Substrate]
A substrate supplies states, programs, a partial run function, a ledger that no step decreases, an encoding of programs into states with a decoder, behavioral equivalence (equal runs from every state), a representability predicate on program transformers, and Kleene's recursion theorem for representable transformers as a field (class \texttt{Substrate}). A shortcut predicate on programs respects behavioral equivalence and has one accepted and one rejected canonical program (class \texttt{WithShortcutPredicate}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Structural undecidability] \textbf{(S)}
In every substrate with a shortcut predicate, no decider whose flip transformer is representable returns true exactly when the predicate holds.
Coq: \texttt{structural\_shortcut\_undecidable}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $D$ be such a decider and $f(p)$ the rejected canonical program if $D(p)$ is true and the accepted one otherwise. The recursion theorem gives $q$ behaviorally equivalent to $f(q)$. If $D(q)$ is true, $q$ behaves like the rejected program, so the predicate fails at $q$; if false, it holds. Either way $D$ is wrong at $q$.
\end{proof}

The natural-number substrate meets the recursion premise by construction, since its fixed-point program copies the transformer's output (\texttt{nat\_structural\_shortcut\_undecidable}).

\begin{theorem}[L] \textbf{(S)}
For the weak call-by-value lambda calculus L, defined in \texttt{coq/kernel/foundation/LRecursion.v} from its reduction rules, Kleene's second recursion theorem holds (\texttt{second\_recursion}), every extensional nontrivial property of L terms is undecidable (\texttt{L\_rice}), and L halting is undecidable (\texttt{L\_halting\_undecidable}). The Turing-completeness of L is cited from Forster and Smolka, not re-proved.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
The complement of two-counter halting is undecidable, composed from the vendored library's reductions (\texttt{MM2\_HALTING\_compl\_undec}).
\end{theorem}

The diagonal uses recursion and behavioral equivalence; cost plays no part. Deciding a predicate over arbitrary programs is a different task from reading one field of a given state.

\chapter{The Record Axis}\label{ch:record}

\begin{definition}[Bases and honest extensions]
A base is a deterministic record-free machine: states, a next function, initial states and halting (record \texttt{BaseMachine}). A record-carrying machine has states, a next function, initial states, a record, a ledger and halting (record \texttt{RCM}). A cover maps it onto a base step for step, preserving initial states and halting. The extension is \emph{honest} when its record's next value depends only on the base state and itself, the ledger never decreases, A2 holds, the record is permanent, and some reachable step writes it (\texttt{HonestBaseExtension}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The record axis is a latch] \textbf{(S)}
Every honest extension of every base factors as a latch: there is a base event $h$ such that the base state and the record evolve as $(b, r) \mapsto (\mathrm{next}(b), r \vee h(b))$.
Coq: \texttt{record\_axis\_is\_latch\_holds}. Two permanent records factor as two latches, each reading the other (\texttt{record\_pair\_is\_two\_latches\_holds}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Both premises do work] \textbf{(R)}
A record that can switch back off is not a latch (\texttt{toggle\_not\_latch}). A record switched on by a hidden clock is permanent and not driven by the computation (\texttt{clock\_record\_not\_driven}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Which machines carry the axis] \textbf{(S)}
Every base carries it: the base plus a latch on any event it reaches, charging one unit when the latch sets, is an honest extension (\texttt{latch\_core\_honest}). A reversible base with unbounded memory carries it reversibly (\texttt{history\_latch\_honest}). With finite memory, a step that writes a permanent record is not injective (\texttt{finite\_reversible\_cannot\_write}).
\end{theorem}

\section{Growing and probabilistic records}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
A growing record in a partial order satisfies a complete family of threshold-latch equations and keeps its price schedule (\texttt{growing\_record\_decomposes\_holds}). A monotone chain of pairwise-distinct $k$-bit vectors has at most $k+1$ members (\texttt{chain\_needs\_bits\_holds}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(R)}
One Boolean latch does not carry every growing record: a three-value chain over a one-state base is a counterexample (\texttt{one\_latch\_refuted}). In a finite branching model a deterministic latch does not handle branching (\texttt{deterministic\_latch\_handles\_branching\_refuted}), and equal branch support and charges do not determine the branch weights (\texttt{schedule\_determines\_probabilities\_refuted}).
\end{theorem}

\section{Bases}

The relation \texttt{weak\_base\_equiv}, agreement on chosen observations and halting with stuttering steps, is an equivalence (\texttt{weak\_base\_equiv\_trans\_holds}). The record axis is a latch over the Cook and Reckhow RAM and over L (\texttt{record\_axis\_is\_latch\_on\_ram\_holds}, \texttt{record\_axis\_is\_latch\_on\_l\_holds}); these instances follow from the general theorem and do not distinguish the bases. Cost-respecting simulations between different bases are not constructed.

\chapter{CHSH and Tsirelson}\label{ch:chsh}

The results in this chapter are mathematics about correlators and trial counts. No machine is fixed.

\begin{theorem}[Classical bound] \textbf{(S)}
A factorizable correlator has $|S| \le 2$ (\texttt{factorizable\_CHSH\_classical\_bound}); trial counts consistent with one deterministic local strategy cannot exceed the classical bound (\texttt{chsh\_stat\_violation\_not\_local}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Algebraic Tsirelson bound] \textbf{(S)}
A rational correlator satisfying the absolute-value bounds and four selected $3\times 3$ polynomial minors has $|S| \le 2\sqrt{2}$ (\texttt{algebraically\_coherent\_tsirelson\_general}). The bound also follows from row constraints and Cauchy--Schwarz (\texttt{tsirelson\_from\_row\_bounds}) and is reached (\texttt{tsirelson\_tight}). No Hilbert space is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The biconditional] \textbf{(S)}
On the zero-marginal slice, the column-contractivity conditions on $E_{00}, E_{01}, E_{10}, E_{11}$ hold if and only if the zero-marginal $5\times 5$ NPA moment matrix is positive semidefinite.
Coq: \texttt{column\_contractive\_iff\_npa\_psd}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The integer check] \textbf{(S)}
If the integer column check accepts eight trial counts, the zero-marginal NPA matrix built from their correlators is positive semidefinite (\texttt{column\_contractive\_check\_witness\_npa\_psd}), and a PSD matrix of that form satisfies the Tsirelson bound (\texttt{npa\_psd\_implies\_tsirelson\_bound}). The check is sound in this direction only; a rejection is not proved to mean the conditions fail.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Elliptope] \textbf{(S)}
The CHSH correlator quantum set is an existential completion of the zero-marginal NPA matrix; points of it satisfy the Tsirelson bound (\texttt{elliptope\_tsirelson}), and the PR box is outside it (\texttt{pr\_box\_not\_elliptope}). An integer membership check is sound into realizability (\texttt{elliptope\_check\_full\_sound}).
\end{theorem}

No theorem here identifies a correlator with a physical experiment or proves that any device produces CHSH-violating statistics.

\chapter{The Physical Boundary}\label{ch:physics}

\begin{theorem}[Calorimeter] \textbf{(S)}
In a two-state calorimeter protocol, a canonical reset from half-excited to ground satisfies a discrete master equation exactly, and at fixed Hamiltonian the bath receives $\Delta/2$ (\texttt{canonical\_reset\_heat\_exact}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Refutation] \textbf{(R)}
Population dynamics do not fix heat: two gaps with identical dynamics release different heat (\texttt{master\_equation\_does\_not\_fix\_heat\_scale}).
\end{theorem}

Ruling a gap out needs a thermal premise such as local detailed balance, or device evidence. Converting the ledger to energy requires thermal and device premises; the ledger has no intrinsic energy unit (Chapter~\ref{ch:finite}).

\chapter{External Systems and the Pointer Conjecture}\label{ch:external}

Each model below is narrow. None is a full security, consensus, database or audit-log theorem.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RFC 9162.} The iterative inclusion and consistency checks of the specification reject the boundary cases and accept the specification's examples under symbolic digests (\texttt{rfc9162\_inclusion\_boundary\_safe}, \texttt{rfc9162\_consistency\_boundary\_safe}). Collision resistance and signed-tree-head authenticity are cryptographic premises, not proved.
  \item \textbf{Proof-carrying code.} A toy checker accepts exactly when the address-bound verification condition holds (\texttt{pcc\_checker\_accepts\_iff\_vc}). It mirrors the roles of Necula's system; it is not that system.
  \item \textbf{TPM quotes.} A bare sign/verify interface does not imply quote authenticity (\texttt{tpm\_interface\_authenticity\_refuted}).
  \item \textbf{Casper FFG.} Two finalized blocks on conflicting branches imply a slashable quorum (\texttt{accountable\_safety}). This is the known accountable-safety theorem.
\end{itemize}

The addressed-RAM and reversible-update files prove only their named fragments. The cost-semantics correspondences are not full cost-framework embeddings.

\section{The pointer criterion}

The pointer-observable criterion proposes that the event a step rule is forced to price is singled out by redundant record proliferation across observers. Twelve candidate events over six observer maps proliferate as stated (\texttt{twelve\_candidate\_measurements\_checked}). The MAC-labelled model does not proliferate (\texttt{mac\_model\_not\_proliferating}); applying that to a real system depends on the observer model. A two-observer toggle game with consensus, authenticity and coordinator-free updates revokes its event (\texttt{toggle\_game\_refutes\_strong\_pointer\_necessity}), so those properties do not force permanence. Durable views do imply permanence, by making persistence a premise (\texttt{durable\_consensus\_implies\_permanence}). The external consequence survey contains five narrow statements already known from their governing specifications and five stronger statements without enough protocol or hardware semantics; no surveyed consequence is simultaneously record-axis dependent, novel, and ready for use. The pointer criterion is a conjecture.
