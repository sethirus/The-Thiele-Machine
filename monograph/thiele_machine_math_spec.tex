\medskip

\noindent\textbf{The Thiele Machine is an abstract model of computation, and an argument made with it about what an account of computation should preserve.}

It is a machine in the sense a Turing machine is one: a mathematical object. No device is meant. The model is the abstract \texttt{CertificationSystem} record and the \texttt{Substrate} interface. The small machine of \texttt{minimal/EarnedCore.v}, a two-counter machine whose commitments are earned, is one instance; the universal machine U built on it is another. They are witnesses of the model, which is the record and the interface.

Certification is one example of a wider question about structural state, observation and cost. A window, a function from states to what can be seen of them (Chapter~\ref{ch:windows}), can lose distinctions that the full state keeps, such as the record and the ledger; the cost laws of Chapters~\ref{ch:nfi} and~\ref{ch:finite} constrain specified events under hypotheses each theorem names. A conventional encoding can preserve those distinctions. A window that discards them cannot recover them in general. The stronger proposal that these distinctions belong in a necessary foundation of computation is a conjecture. The status of each result is stated below.

\begin{abstract}
This is a mathematical reference for the Thiele Machine: a model of computation in which a step that raises a certification record must pay into a ledger that only grows. It presents the abstract interfaces, the main theorems, and the definitions of the small machine from the machine-checked Coq corpus. The Coq source is the complete definition; this document states each result with its premises and names the declaration that carries it.

Coq establishes that conclusions follow from premises. It does \emph{not} establish that premises model physical reality. Every result is tagged as one of three types: \textbf{(S)}~structural theorems about the model or a stated machine, \textbf{(C)}~conditional results that hold under a named premise no machine fact discharges, and \textbf{(R)}~refutations, where a theorem states the negation of a proposed claim and a named construction supplies the counterexample. One proposal, the pointer criterion of Chapter~\ref{ch:external}, has no proof; it carries a fourth tag, conjecture.

The core claims are structural (S): No Free Insight for every certification system; on finite machines, the toll derived from merge pricing for permanent certificates; the window and pricing results; the small machine's earned provenance, checker soundness and minimum price; Thiele completeness of the small machine; the window theorem for every Thiele-complete machine; the lifting of every universal base to a Thiele-complete machine and its converse; the composition of Thiele-complete machines; the universal machine U and the priced host's program U\textsubscript{P}; a verified compiler onto these machines; the record axis over any base and the toll over any order of records; the recursion theorem and Rice's theorem (the logician Henry Rice proved it in his 1951 doctoral thesis and published it in 1953: no program decides a property of what programs do, unless it holds of every program or of none) for L, and Rice's theorem for the two-counter machine itself; the classical CHSH bound; the algebraic Tsirelson bound; and, on the zero-marginal slice, the equivalence of column contractivity with positive semidefiniteness of one fixed $5\times 5$ NPA matrix. The failure of every clock-style machine, the limits of lifting and of composition, and the necessity of each clause of Thiele completeness are refutations (R). Results stated over Landauer heat are conditional (C) on the named premise \texttt{landauer\_heat}.
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Introduction}

\chapter{Notation and Conventions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\N$ is the natural numbers, $\B = \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$, $\Reals$ the reals.
  \item A \emph{trace} is a finite list of instructions. $\mathrm{run}(\tau, s)$ folds the step function over $\tau$ from $s$.
  \item $\cert$ is the certification reading, a function from states to $\B$. In Coq it is \texttt{cs\_cert} on a \texttt{CertificationSystem}.
  \item $\cost(i) \in \N$ is the cost of instruction $i$; $\cost(\tau)$ is the sum over a trace.
  \item $\mu$ is the ledger. Where a state carries it, conservation means $\mu(\mathrm{step}(s,i)) = \mu(s) + \cost(i)$.
  \item A \emph{window} is a function from states to observations.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Certification Systems and No Free Insight}\label{ch:nfi}

\begin{definition}[Certification system]
A certification system is a tuple $(S, I, \mathrm{step}, \cost, \cert)$ with $\mathrm{step} : S \times I \to S$, $\cost : I \to \N$, $\cert : S \to \B$, satisfying
\[
\textbf{A2:}\quad \forall s\, i,\ \cert(s) = \mathrm{false} \wedge \cert(\mathrm{step}(s,i)) = \mathrm{true} \implies \cost(i) \ge 1.
\]
Coq: the record \texttt{CertificationSystem}, with A2 as its field \texttt{cs\_cert\_costs}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[No Free Insight]\label{thm:nfi} \textbf{(S)}
In every certification system, if $\cert(s_0) = \mathrm{false}$ and $\cert(\mathrm{run}(\tau, s_0)) = \mathrm{true}$, then $\cost(\tau) \ge 1$.
Coq: \texttt{universal\_nfi\_any\_substrate}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induction on $\tau$. Since $\cert$ is false at the start and true at the end, some step in $\tau$ takes it from false to true. By A2 that step costs at least $1$; every other step costs at least $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Simulating systems] \textbf{(S)}
Let a base certification system be simulated by a host: an embedding of base states into host states, a decoding of each base instruction into a list of host instructions, a commuting step law, and certification reflected by the embedding (record \texttt{SimulatingCertificationSystem}). A base trace from uncertified to certified costs at least $1$ in the base, the host run of its decoded trace ends certified, and that host run costs at least $1$.
Coq: \texttt{host\_represents\_simulating\_cert\_system}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative form] \textbf{(S)}
Extend a certification system with a witness $w : S \to \N$ and a threshold $k$ such that each step raises $w$ by at most its cost and every certified state has $w \ge k$ (record \texttt{QuantitativeCertificationSystem}). A trace from a state with $w = 0$ to a certified state costs at least $k$.
Coq: \texttt{universal\_nfi\_quantitative}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[A2 is a restriction] \textbf{(S)}
A cost-bearing system without A2 can switch certification on for free, and A2 excludes exactly that.
Coq: \texttt{honest\_cost\_tracking\_strict\_restriction}, \texttt{free\_forgery\_violates\_A2}.
\end{theorem}

\section{A2 among cost frameworks}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
A step system satisfies A2 if and only if every step has non-negative amortized cost under the potential that is $1$ on uncertified states and $0$ on certified ones; No Free Insight is then the potential method's telescoping bound.
Coq: \texttt{a2\_iff\_nonnegative\_amortized\_cost}, \texttt{nfi\_by\_potential}.
Under A2 the number of certification flips along a trace is at most its cost (\texttt{flips\_le\_cost}), and A2 together with the matching upper-bound condition is equivalent to exact pricing of a permanent certificate (\texttt{a2\_and\_aara\_iff\_exact}).
\end{theorem}

These are local correspondences. The cost-framework families they touch (graded monads, amortized resource analysis, cost translations, linear logic) are not embedded here.

\section{Several floors and one charge}

\begin{theorem}[Joint floors] \textbf{(S)}
For a finite family of events, the least cost schedule meeting every unit floor charges one on a step where any event occurs and zero otherwise.
Coq: \texttt{joint\_floor\_is\_least}. On pairs of Booleans, two independent coordinates that both rise in one step are met by a single charge of $1$ (\texttt{independent\_coordinates\_joint\_change\_costs\_one}).
\end{theorem}

Independent state coordinates do not by themselves force additive cost.

\begin{theorem}[Exact pricing] \textbf{(S)}
For trusted local-predicate pricing systems, a schedule meets the certification floor and never overcharges exactly when it charges on precisely the certification flips, one unit each, and nothing elsewhere.
Coq: \texttt{exact\_commitment\_pricing\_characterization}.
\end{theorem}

\chapter{Finite Machines and the Price of Merging}\label{ch:finite}

Fix a state type $S$ with a finite enumeration, instructions $I$, a step function and a reading $\cert$. The certificate is \emph{permanent} when no step switches it off. An instruction is \emph{injective} when its step function is.

\begin{theorem}[A permanent flip merges] \textbf{(S)}
On a finite state space with a permanent certificate, an instruction that switches the certificate on at some state is not injective.
Coq: \texttt{permanent\_flip\_is\_not\_injective}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If the instruction were injective it would map the certified states injectively into the certified states, so onto them, leaving no certified image for the uncertified state it switches on.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A2 from merge pricing] \textbf{(S)}
On a finite state space with a permanent certificate, if every non-injective instruction costs at least $1$, A2 holds.
Coq: \texttt{a2\_from\_merging\_price\_and\_permanence}; the trace form is \texttt{permanent\_certification\_trace\_floor}, and an honest erasure account gives the same bill (\texttt{honest\_erasure\_accounting\_implies\_a2}).
\end{theorem}

Each premise is needed. Unbounded history escapes (\texttt{unbounded\_history\_escapes}), a revocable certificate escapes (\texttt{revocable\_certificate\_escapes}), and an unpriced merge escapes (\texttt{free\_merge\_escapes}).

\begin{theorem}[The size of the bill] \textbf{(S)}
On a finite state space with a permanent certificate, with $m$ certified states and $k$ uncertified states that one instruction switches on, the instruction collapses at least $k$ states (\texttt{permanent\_flips\_collapse\_at\_least}). Priced one unit per halving of the number of distinct states, its cost $c$ satisfies $m + k \le 2^c m$, so $c \ge \log_2((m+k)/m)$; in rounded-up logarithms, $\lceil\log_2(m+k)\rceil \le c + \lceil\log_2 m\rceil$.
Coq: \texttt{permanent\_flips\_compression\_bound}, \texttt{permanent\_flips\_log\_bound}.
\end{theorem}

These bounds are tight, and A2 is equivalent to the floor. For every system, A2 holds exactly when every run from an uncertified state to a certified one costs at least $1$ (\texttt{nec\_f\_floor\_iff\_a2}). For every $m$ and $c$, a grid machine with $m$ certified states and one move of cost $c$ flips $2^c m - m$ states, so the bound $m + k \le 2^c m$ of the theorem above is met with equality (\texttt{nec\_f\_squeeze\_tight}). Its rounded-logarithm form is strictly weaker: $m = 3$, $k = 1$ and cost $0$ meet the rounded form and fail $m + k \le 2^c m$ (\texttt{nec\_f\_rounded\_log\_form\_weaker}).

\begin{theorem}[Which flips are forced] \textbf{(S)}
When instructions have decidable equality, over all schedules that price merges, an instruction is forced to have positive price if and only if it merges two states (\texttt{forced\_priced\_iff\_merges}). An instruction that switches a reading on either merges or switches it off somewhere else (\texttt{flip\_merges\_or\_revokes}). So every permanent-record write is forced, and the only free flips are revocable ones.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Refutation] \textbf{(R)}
``The forced events are exactly the permanent-record writes'' is false: a three-state instruction flips, revokes, and merges at once.
Coq: \texttt{forced\_price\_without\_permanent\_record}.
\end{theorem}

\section{Entropy and heat}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
After a permanent step, any distribution on the $m$ certified states and the $k$ flipping ones has Shannon entropy at most $\log_2 m$ (\texttt{permanent\_step\_entropy\_ceiling}). From the uniform distribution the step removes at least $\log_2((m+k)/m)$ bits (\texttt{permanent\_flip\_uniform\_entropy\_drop}). An injective step removes none (\texttt{step\_entropy\_invariant\_if\_injective}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Heat] \textbf{(C)}
Given the named premise \texttt{landauer\_heat}, which states Landauer's principle for a step and a distribution (Rolf Landauer argued at IBM in 1961 that a step that cannot be run backwards requires a minimal heat generation; the premise reads this as heat at least $k_B T \ln 2$ per bit of entropy the step removes), the flip dissipates, when $k_B T \ge 0$, at least $k_B T \ln((m+k)/m)$ from the uniform distribution (\texttt{permanent\_flip\_heat\_floor}), and, when $k_B T > 0$, positive heat from any distribution that leaves every certified state and the flipping one possible (\texttt{permanent\_flip\_full\_support\_heat\_positive}). For a state known in advance, the premise is equivalent to heat $\ge 0$, so it forces no heat (\texttt{known\_state\_flip\_forces\_no\_heat}).
\end{theorem}

\section{An eight-state instance}

\begin{example}
Four program slots and a certification flag make eight states. An advance step is injective (\texttt{fnext\_injective}); the certify step and every jump merge states (\texttt{fcertify\_merges}, \texttt{fjump\_merges}). The cost rule prices exactly the merges, so A2 holds there as a theorem (\texttt{fin\_a2\_from\_merging\_price}).
\end{example}

\section{No intrinsic joule value}

\begin{theorem}[Refutation] \textbf{(R)}
For every state type and natural-number ledger, two real-valued scales agree on the ledger and differ in energy; the ledger fixes no energy unit.
Coq: \texttt{mu\_has\_no\_intrinsic\_joule\_value}. Choosing the scale $k_B T \ln 2$ gives one ledger unit that energy by definition (\texttt{calibrated\_mu\_landauer\_energy}); the scale is a premise, and nothing here measures it.
\end{theorem}

\chapter{Windows, Pricing and Narrowing}\label{ch:windows}

\begin{theorem}[Window adequacy] \textbf{(S)}
A query on states can be answered by a function of a window only if it is constant on the window's fibers, the sets of states the window shows the same way (\texttt{decoding\_requires\_fiber\_constancy}). Given a representative for every view, fiber constancy is equivalent to factoring through a decoder (\texttt{selected\_representatives\_give\_decoder}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Shadow pricing] \textbf{(S)}
If a certifying step and a non-certifying step show the same window before and after, no price computed from the window meets the certification floor without charging the non-certifying step.
Coq: \texttt{shadow\_cannot\_price\_exactly}. A window that shows the reading prices certification exactly (\texttt{window\_showing\_reading\_prices\_exactly}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Retained history] \textbf{(S)}
Keeping the previous state in a history makes every deterministic step injective, and the state part of the new pair is still the original step's result, so the observed transition is unchanged.
Coq: \texttt{retained\_history\_step\_injective}, \texttt{history\_simulates\_observed\_step}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Narrowing] \textbf{(S)}
On a finite machine priced one unit per halving of the number of distinct states, each halving of the machine's own set of possible states along a run is paid for (\texttt{run\_narrowing\_priced\_log}). What an observer knows can shrink for free: a measurement that copies a hidden bit into a blank display forgets nothing, and one admissible merge-priced cost assigns it zero (\texttt{observer\_narrowing\_can\_be\_free}); wiping the display is a merge and costs at least one (\texttt{wipe\_costs\_at\_least\_one}).
\end{theorem}

\chapter{The Small Machine}\label{ch:small}

The small machine is defined in \texttt{minimal/EarnedCore.v} on the Coq standard library alone.

\begin{definition}[State]
A core has two counters $a, b \in \N$, their versions $v_a, v_b \in \N$, a program counter $\mathit{pc} \in \N$ whose lines are numbered from $1$ (a value with no instruction, $0$ included, is a stop), a table of facts, a commitment channel holding at most one fact, and a trap latch. A fact is a triple (property, counter, version); the properties are ``is zero'', ``is even'' and ``is at least $n$'', each with a checker \texttt{eval} and a meaning \texttt{holds} tied by \texttt{eval\_iff}. A state is a core with a ledger $\mu \in \N$ and a flag $\cert \in \B$. The table holds at most $16$ facts. The start state for $(a, b)$ has no facts, an empty channel, the flag down and $\mu = 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Instructions and costs]
$\texttt{INC}\ c$ adds one to counter $c$. $\texttt{DEC}\ c\ j$ subtracts one and jumps to $j$ if $c > 0$, and otherwise falls through. \texttt{HALT} stops. $\texttt{CHECK}\ p\ c$ evaluates $p$ on counter $c$ and, if it holds and the table has room, writes the fact $(p, c, v_c)$. $\texttt{COMMIT}\ p\ c$ succeeds only if the fact $(p, c, v_c)$ is in the table, and then sets the channel to it. \texttt{CERTIFY} succeeds only if the channel names a fact. Every failure sets the trap latch, after which no instruction changes anything but the ledger. Every write to a counter adds one to its version. CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY cost $1$; the others cost $0$. The ledger adds the cost of every executed instruction, and the flag rises exactly on a passing CERTIFY.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Universality] \textbf{(S)}
The INC/DEC fragment is the two-counter machine step for step, and a program halts on the small machine exactly when it halts as a two-counter program.
Coq: \texttt{simulation\_run}, \texttt{halting\_correspondence}. Its halting problem is undecidable, by reduction from the vendored two-counter result (\texttt{earned\_core\_halting\_undecidable}). The Turing-universality of two-counter machines is a theorem of Marvin Minsky, who studied how bare a machine can be and still compute what a Turing machine computes (Annals of Mathematics 74, 1961; \emph{Computation: Finite and Infinite Machines}, Prentice-Hall, 1967). It is cited and not re-proved here; the undecidability of two-counter halting used above is checked in Coq in the vendored Coq Library of Undecidability Proofs of Yannick Forster, Dominique Larchey-Wendling and coauthors.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The toll] \textbf{(S)}
The ledger is the exact sum of executed costs (\texttt{mu\_conservation\_trace}); only CERTIFY raises the flag (\texttt{only\_certify\_certifies}); the flag never drops (\texttt{cert\_permanent}); A2 holds (\texttt{a2}); and a run from an uncertified state to a certified one pays at least $1$ (\texttt{nfi\_floor}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Earned provenance] \textbf{(S)}
From a clean start, every certified run contains a passing CHECK of some claim, then a passing COMMIT of the same claim with that counter untouched in between, then CERTIFY.
Coq: \texttt{earned\_certification\_provenance}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Checker soundness and no forging] \textbf{(S)}
On a run from a clean start, a fact whose counter has not been written since its check names a true claim about that counter (\texttt{checker\_soundness}), and every fact in the table was written by a passing CHECK of exactly that claim (\texttt{no\_forging}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Price] \textbf{(S)}
From a clean start a certified run costs at least $3$ (\texttt{certified\_run\_min\_cost}), and a three-instruction program pays exactly $3$ (\texttt{min\_cost\_tight}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[A check that can fail] \textbf{(S)}
The program CHECK ``$a$ is zero'', COMMIT, CERTIFY certifies from a start with $a = 0$, paying exactly $3$, and from any other start has trapped by its fourth step and never raises the flag, for any number of steps.
Coq: \texttt{earned\_run\_check\_can\_fail}, \texttt{earned\_run\_refused\_forever}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Links] \textbf{(S)}
The small machine is a certification system with the trace floor (\texttt{earned\_core\_floor}), and as a record-carrying machine its record moves as a latch on one base event (\texttt{earned\_core\_is\_latch}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
\texttt{minimal/EarnedGeneric.v} states the same machine over any property language with an exact equality test on properties and an exact checker; sorted lists are one instance (\texttt{sorted\_cs\_sound}). \texttt{minimal/EarnedMulti.v} has a counter for every natural number and is the host machine of Chapter~\ref{ch:universal}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Each part of the clean start is needed] \textbf{(S)}
From any state of the small machine, every run that ends certified costs at least $3$ exactly when the flag is down and either the trap latch is up or the channel is empty and every fact in the table is about an older version of its counter (\texttt{nec\_s\_floor3\_iff}). Without the empty table some certifying run from a state with an empty channel and the flag down costs less than $3$ (\texttt{nec\_s\_min\_cost\_needs\_empty\_table}); without the empty channel some certifying run costs less than $2$ (\texttt{nec\_s\_min\_cost\_needs\_empty\_channel}); without the lowered flag some run costs less than $1$ (\texttt{nec\_s\_min\_cost\_needs\_flag\_down}). From every clean start with the trap latch down, CHECK, COMMIT, CERTIFY certifies and adds exactly $3$ to the ledger (\texttt{nec\_s\_witness\_from\_every\_clean\_start}).
\end{theorem}

\chapter{Thiele Completeness}\label{ch:complete}

\begin{definition}[Machine, weak completeness]
A machine is a state type, a move type, a step, a cost per move and a record $\cert : S \to \B$ (record \texttt{machine}). It is weakly Thiele-complete when it is a certification system whose base is universal.
\end{definition}

The weak notion is nearly empty: a two-counter machine that charges $1$ for every move meets it for any record (\texttt{clock\_weakly\_thiele\_complete}).

\begin{definition}[Thiele complete]
A machine is Thiele-complete when its moves can be read as base moves and three record moves, CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY of a claim, over a type of claims with a meaning on states, a checker, a notion of the thing a claim is about being unchanged, a set of clean starts and a ledger, such that:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item \emph{Universal base.} Every two-counter instruction has a base move that acts on a window of the state exactly as the instruction acts on a two-counter configuration, every two-counter start has a clean live state showing it (its loaded start), base moves never change the record, and the record once up stays up.
  \item \emph{Earned record.} A clean start has the record down. Every run from a clean start that ends with the record up contains a passing CHECK of some claim, then a COMMIT of the same claim with the thing it is about unchanged in between, then the CERTIFY that raises the record. A passing check means its claim holds, and a claim that holds keeps holding while the thing it is about is unchanged.
  \item \emph{Exact toll.} Base moves cost $0$, each record move costs exactly $1$, and the ledger grows by the cost of each move.
  \item \emph{Non-vacuity.} Some claim is true at some loaded start and false at another, and the bare chain CHECK, COMMIT, CERTIFY on it raises the record from a loaded start exactly when the claim holds there.
\end{enumerate}
Coq: \texttt{thiele\_complete} in \texttt{minimal/ThieleComplete.v}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Consequences] \textbf{(S)}
In every Thiele-complete machine: the ledger counts the record moves (\texttt{ledger\_counts\_record\_moves}); a certificate reached from a clean start costs at least $3$ (\texttt{certificate\_costs\_three}); the claim such a certificate stands on held when it was checked and still held when it was committed (\texttt{Minimal.ThieleComplete.committed\_claim\_holds}); some check fails (\texttt{check\_can\_fail}); some run certifies (\texttt{some\_run\_certifies}); the move raising the record from a clean start is a CERTIFY (\texttt{only\_certify\_raises}); and some move costs $0$ (\texttt{complete\_has\_free\_move}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Machines that meet it] \textbf{(S)}
The small machine is Thiele-complete (\texttt{earned\_core\_thiele\_complete}), and so are the machine over any property language with an exact equality test on properties, an exact checker and a property true of one value and false of another (\texttt{earned\_generic\_thiele\_complete}) and the sorted-list machine (\texttt{sorted\_machine\_thiele\_complete}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Machines that fail it] \textbf{(R)}
Every clock-style machine, with any record, is weakly Thiele-complete and not Thiele-complete (\texttt{clock\_weakly\_thiele\_complete}, \texttt{clock\_not\_thiele\_complete}); so is a clock whose record is a latch on its configuration (\texttt{latch\_clock\_weakly\_thiele\_complete}, \texttt{latch\_clock\_not\_thiele\_complete}). A machine with a free base and one paid move that raises a latch meets clauses (a) and (c), and since that move raises the record from every state, the machine is Thiele-complete through no interface (\texttt{paid\_latch\_meets\_base\_and\_toll}, \texttt{one\_move\_record\_excluded}, \texttt{paid\_latch\_not\_thiele\_complete}). A machine whose record never rises meets (a), (b) and (c) and fails (d) (\texttt{silent\_meets\_base\_record\_toll}, \texttt{silent\_not\_thiele\_complete}).
\end{theorem}

\section{The window theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Every Thiele-complete machine hides its record] \textbf{(S)}
For every Thiele-complete machine there are a clean start and two runs from it, the second made of base moves only, that end at the same base window; the first ends certified with its ledger at least $3$ above the start, and the second ends uncertified with its ledger unchanged (\texttt{complete\_two\_runs}). Hence no function of the base window recovers the record (\texttt{complete\_hides\_record}) or the ledger (\texttt{complete\_hides\_ledger}) on every reachable state (\texttt{thiele\_complete\_hides}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Clause (d) and clause (b) give a clean start and a certifying run. The base is universal, so base moves alone reach the window the certifying run ends at; base moves change neither the record nor the ledger.
\end{proof}

Every Thiele-complete machine is a certification system with the trace floor, and its window is blind to its reading (\texttt{thiele\_complete\_floor}, \texttt{complete\_cs\_window\_blind}).

\section{Every model of computation}

\begin{theorem}[Lifting] \textbf{(S)}
For any machine with a universal base in the sense of clause (a), any claim language over its states that has an exact equality test and a checker proved equal to its meaning, and any fact-table capacity of at least $1$, if some claim is true at one loaded start and false at another, the machine that adds the earned layer (base moves that raise a version, and CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY costing $1$ each) is Thiele-complete (\texttt{lift\_thiele\_complete}). With the window claims (a register of the window is at least $n$, or the program line is $n$) the premise on claims always holds (\texttt{lift\_window\_thiele\_complete}). Conversely, every Thiele-complete machine has an interface making it Thiele-complete whose base moves form a universal base (\texttt{lift\_reduct\_universal}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Limits of lifting] \textbf{(R)}
A base whose moves have only finitely many different effects at each state (one with finitely many moves, for instance), or one whose step ignores the current state, lifts to no Thiele-complete machine under any claim language and capacity (\texttt{lift\_finite\_branching\_not\_complete}, \texttt{lift\_stateless\_not\_complete}). A one-counter program of $k$ instructions started at $n$ halts exactly when it has stopped after $k(n+k+1)$ steps, so one-counter halting is decidable (\texttt{lift\_oc\_halts\_bound}, \texttt{lift\_oc\_halts\_dec}); two-counter halting is undecidable (\texttt{lift\_cm\_halting\_undecidable}), and a halting-preserving translation of two-counter instances into one-counter instances would make it decidable (\texttt{lift\_no\_oc\_translation}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Models of computation] \textbf{(S)}
A relation on numbers computable in any one of the seven models of the vendored library (the lambda calculus L, the alternate Minsky machines MMA, Turing machines, binary stack machines, Minsky machines MM, FRACTRAN and mu-recursive functions) is computable in all of them (\texttt{lift\_num\_models\_agree}). The number-coded two-counter machine has a universal base, its step relation is computable in all seven models, and the window lifts, with a fact table of $16$, of that machine and of the random-access machine are Thiele-complete (\texttt{lift\_classic\_bases}). Over any record that is a preorder with binary joins (Chapter~\ref{ch:record}), every universal base, with a claim language that has an exact equality test and a proved checker and a fact table with room for one fact, lifts to a machine that is Thiele-complete on the axis, given a claim whose point is not at or below the floor and which is true at one load and false at another (\texttt{lift\_lax\_thiele\_complete}). When every record is below every other, no machine over that axis is Thiele-complete (\texttt{lift\_ax\_indiscrete\_no\_machine}).
\end{theorem}

\section{Composition}

\begin{theorem}[Products and hand-offs] \textbf{(S)}
Two machines Thiele-complete on the axis give a product, whose moves act on one part each and whose record is the pair of the two records, that is Thiele-complete under the product interface, with every certification keeping its own earned chain (\texttt{cmpz\_prod\_tc}, \texttt{cmpz\_prod\_thiele\_complete}). The cost of an interleaved run is the cost of its left moves plus the cost of its right moves (\texttt{cmpz\_cost\_prod}), two interleavings of the same moves reach the same state at the same cost (\texttt{cmpz\_interleaving\_invariant}), and with record-preserving simulations as morphisms the product is the categorical product (\texttt{cmpz\_prod\_universal}). Running one machine and handing its two output counters to a second through free loading moves gives a Thiele-complete machine when both are (\texttt{cmpz\_seq\_tc}), and it computes the second program's run on the first program's output (\texttt{cmpz\_seq\_composes}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Limits of composition] \textbf{(R)}
From a clean start of the product, every run that raises the AND of the two records makes at least six record moves (\texttt{cmpz\_and\_costs\_six}), and the point-level interface through ``both flags are up'' fails non-vacuity (\texttt{cmpz\_and\_not\_complete}). The product of two clocks is weakly Thiele-complete and not Thiele-complete (\texttt{cmpz\_or\_clocks\_not\_complete}). A second writer that changes counter $A$ of the small machine without raising its version breaks the earned-record clause (\texttt{cmpz\_shared\_not\_earned}). Two finite machines that each have an exact shadow price through a window can collide as a pair (\texttt{cmpz\_exactness\_not\_preserved}). If two load states of the second machine have records with the first not at or below the second, the second machine with free loads added fails the toll (\texttt{cmpz\_ld\_unclean}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Nesting] \textbf{(S)}
If $Y$ runs $X$ and $Z$ runs $Y$ by simulation, then $Z$ runs $X$ with the record bit equal at matched points, halting and the rising of the record corresponding, and the surcharge bounds adding (\texttt{cmpz\_link\_compose}); a tower of $k$ links adds between the sum of the lower bounds and the sum of the upper bounds (\texttt{cmpz\_tower}). A computably presented machine whose reading starts at no and whose moves cost at most $1$, run on U\textsubscript{P} (Chapter~\ref{ch:universal}), pays exactly $2$ more once its latch is up (\texttt{cmpz\_host\_link\_exact}). A record-preserving simulation that reflects the order of records, into a machine that pays the toll, tracks every run of the guest by a host run costing at least the number of the guest run's exits (\texttt{cmpz\_sim\_cost\_ge\_exits}).
\end{theorem}

\section{Each clause is needed}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(R)}
Take any interface that makes a machine Thiele-complete. Charging every move twice its cost keeps clauses (a), (b) and (d), fails (c), and gives a machine that is Thiele-complete through no interface (\texttt{nec\_t\_doubled\_cost\_meets\_all\_but\_toll}). A ledger that always reads $0$ keeps (a), (b) and (d) and fails (c) (\texttt{nec\_t\_ledgerless\_meets\_all\_but\_ledger}). An extra move ZAP that raises the record from every state at cost $1$, read as CERTIFY, keeps (a), (c) and (d) and fails (b), and the machine with ZAP is Thiele-complete through no interface (\texttt{nec\_t\_zap\_meets\_all\_but\_chain}, \texttt{nec\_t\_zap\_not\_thiele\_complete}). A checker that accepts every claim keeps (a), (c) and (d) and loses checker soundness (\texttt{nec\_t\_unsound\_check\_meets\_all\_but\_soundness}). A machine whose states pair one of six control states with a ledger, with one free move and the ordered record moves CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY, meets the loose notion, which keeps every clause except the universal base, and is Thiele-complete through no interface (\texttt{nec\_t\_nb\_loose\_complete}, \texttt{nec\_t\_nb\_not\_thiele\_complete}). For the generic machine, the exact equality test, the exact checker and a property true of one value and false of another each do work. An equality test that calls a different property equal to one the checker accepts on some value, a checker that accepts a property on a value where it does not hold, or a property language in which every property that holds of one value holds of all, each leaves the generic machine with its interface not Thiele-complete (\texttt{nec\_t\_generic\_needs\_exact\_eqb}, \texttt{nec\_t\_generic\_needs\_exact\_eval}, \texttt{nec\_t\_generic\_needs\_varying\_property}).
\end{theorem}

\chapter{The Universal Machine}\label{ch:universal}

\section{The universal host}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
The host of \texttt{minimal/UniversalThiele.v} runs any small-machine program as a guest. From a loaded host, or any state where the two agree, its mirror of the guest's flag always equals that flag (\texttt{record\_agreement}, \texttt{hload\_agrees}). The mirror rises only in a guest step that passed CERTIFY, charged in that same host step (\texttt{no\_free\_host\_certification\_step}, \texttt{toll\_enforced\_by\_host\_step}). The host is a certification system with the trace floor (\texttt{host\_nfi}).
\end{theorem}

On this host, a machine from elsewhere enters through the two-counter encoding, and its computation and the toll are kept. Its own price list is not kept. On U\textsubscript{P}, in the section on the priced host below, a computably presented machine keeps its own ledger plus a surcharge.

\section{U}

U is one fixed program of the machine of \texttt{minimal/EarnedMulti.v}, with the single property of \texttt{minimal/UniversalCodes.v}, made of the small machine's own instruction kinds and with no guest step built into anything (\texttt{coq/kernel/foundation/UniversalLayout.v}). For a small-machine program $P$ and a start $(x, y)$, $g_m$ is the guest after $m$ steps and $h_n$ is U after $n$ steps from the loaded state.

\begin{theorem}[Simulation] \textbf{(S)}
For every $m$ there is $n \ge m$ with $h_n$ related to $g_m$ by the simulation relation, or both machines have stopped.
Coq: \texttt{U\_simulation}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Halting and output] \textbf{(S)}
The guest halts if and only if U halts (\texttt{universal\_halting}); at halting, U's first two registers hold the guest's counters, and the trap latches, ledgers and flags are equal (\texttt{universal\_output}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The flag is earned again] \textbf{(S)}
The guest's flag ever rises if and only if U's flag ever rises (\texttt{universal\_flag\_iff}). A raised flag of U stands on U's own passing CHECK, its own COMMIT of the same claim with the slot untouched, and the CERTIFY that raised it; when checked, the slot held the guest's claim and value, and the guest's own run contains its chain on the same claim (\texttt{universal\_earned}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The ledger] \textbf{(S)}
At matching points U's ledger equals the guest's, and every ledger of U lies between the guest's ledger at some $m$ and at $m+1$.
Coq: \texttt{universal\_ledger\_exact}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
The machine U runs on, read with INC and DEC as its base and CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY as its record moves, is Thiele-complete (\texttt{universal\_thiele\_complete}). U's halting problem, whether U started on the code of $P$ and the inputs $x, y$ ever halts, is undecidable (\texttt{interp\_halting\_undecidable}).
\end{theorem}

\section{The priced host and U\textsubscript{P}}

The priced host of \texttt{minimal/EarnedMultiPriced.v} is the machine with a counter for every natural number and one more paid move, PAY. Its fixed program U\textsubscript{P} runs priced guest programs.

\begin{theorem}[U\textsubscript{P}] \textbf{(S)}
A priced guest halts if and only if U\textsubscript{P} run on it halts (\texttt{pu\_universal\_halting}), and the priced host, read with INC and DEC as its base, CHECK, COMMIT and CERTIFY as its record moves and PAY as a check of the empty claim, is Thiele-complete (\texttt{pu\_universal\_thiele\_complete}). A computably presented machine runs on U\textsubscript{P}: U\textsubscript{P} halts exactly when the machine does, and at every step before the halt some host step decodes the machine's state, carries a flag equal to the machine's latch and a ledger equal to the machine's ledger plus a surcharge (\texttt{presented\_universal}). When the machine's reading starts at no, the surcharge is at most $2$ (\texttt{presented\_universal\_surcharge\_le\_two}). If the four code-level relations of a presentation (next move, step, cost and reading) are computable in any one of the seven models of computation of the vendored library, the machine has a presentation that runs on U\textsubscript{P} this way (\texttt{lift\_computable\_runs\_on\_U}).
\end{theorem}

\section{A verified compiler and the extracted machines}

\begin{theorem}[The compiler] \textbf{(S)}
For a source program with variables, addition, truncated subtraction, conditionals, while loops and non-recursive procedures with several results, whose shape a Boolean check decides (\texttt{cmp\_wf\_b\_spec}), the program computes $y$ exactly when the fast runner halts with $y$, exactly when the compiled host program halts with $y$ at the output, exactly when the compiled two-counter guest does, and exactly when U\textsubscript{P} running that guest halts with $y$, the first counter $0$, ledger $0$ and the flag down (\texttt{cmp\_final}). If the source program has a run, there is a $k$ such that the runner halts with any fuel of at least $k$ and uses exactly $k$ steps (\texttt{cmp\_exec\_complete}); a runner that does not halt has used all its fuel (\texttt{cmp\_exec\_unhalted}).
\end{theorem}

The interpreter, shape check, compiler and runner, and the host programs U and U\textsubscript{P}, are extracted to OCaml (\texttt{ocaml/CmpExtract.v}, \texttt{ocaml/RealizeExtract.v}). The extracted definitions are proved equal to the originals: the extracted instruction list is U (\texttt{rlz\_host\_program\_is}), the extracted host state after $n$ steps is the original host's, for U and for U\textsubscript{P} (\texttt{rlz\_host\_at\_is}, \texttt{rlz\_phost\_at\_eq}), and replacing the register storage of a run by a table changes no field of any state (\texttt{rlz\_host\_sched\_sound\_original}). Extraction maps natural numbers to unbounded integers; the hand-written mapping lines and the two OCaml drivers are trusted and carry no proof. U on a guest that certifies is proved and not run.

\chapter{Recursion and Undecidability}\label{ch:undecidability}

\begin{definition}[Substrate]
A substrate supplies states, programs, a partial run function, a ledger that no step decreases, an encoding of programs into states with a decoder, behavioral equivalence (equal runs from every state), a representability predicate on program transformers, and the recursion theorem stated and proved by the logician Stephen Kleene in 1938, for representable transformers, as a field (class \texttt{Substrate}). A shortcut predicate on programs respects behavioral equivalence and has one accepted and one rejected canonical program (class \texttt{WithShortcutPredicate}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Structural undecidability] \textbf{(S)}
In every substrate with a shortcut predicate, no decider whose flip transformer is representable returns true exactly when the predicate holds.
Coq: \texttt{structural\_shortcut\_undecidable}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $D$ be such a decider and $f(p)$ the rejected canonical program if $D(p)$ is true and the accepted one otherwise. The recursion theorem gives $q$ behaviorally equivalent to $f(q)$. If $D(q)$ is true, $q$ behaves like the rejected program, so the predicate fails at $q$; if false, it holds. Either way $D$ is wrong at $q$.
\end{proof}

The natural-number substrate meets the recursion premise by construction, since its fixed-point program copies the transformer's output (\texttt{nat\_structural\_shortcut\_undecidable}).

\begin{theorem}[L] \textbf{(S)}
For the weak call-by-value lambda calculus L, defined in \texttt{coq/kernel/foundation/LRecursion.v} from its reduction rules, Kleene's second recursion theorem holds (\texttt{second\_recursion}), every extensional nontrivial property of L terms is undecidable (\texttt{L\_rice}), and L halting is undecidable (\texttt{L\_halting\_undecidable}). L is the calculus Yannick Forster and Gert Smolka presented as a model of computation in which computability theory can be carried out in Coq (Interactive Theorem Proving, ITP 2017, LNCS 10499). Yannick Forster, Fabian Kunze, Gert Smolka and Maximilian Wuttke later checked in Coq that L and Turing machines simulate each other (Interactive Theorem Proving, ITP 2021). The Turing-completeness of L is not re-proved in this repository's own files: it comes from the models-equivalence theorem of the vendored Coq Library of Undecidability Proofs (\texttt{vendor/coq-undecidability/theories/Synthetic/Models\_Equivalent.v}), which is checked with this build.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
The complement of two-counter halting is undecidable, composed from the vendored library's reductions (\texttt{MM2\_HALTING\_compl\_undec}).
\end{theorem}

The diagonal uses recursion and behavioral equivalence; cost plays no part. Deciding a predicate over arbitrary programs is a different task from reading one field of a given state.

\section{The two-counter machine itself}

\begin{theorem}[Rice and Kleene for two counters] \textbf{(S)}
For any set $X$ of inputs, a property of small-machine programs that cannot tell apart two programs behaving the same on every input in $X$ (same stopping, same counters, trap latch, ledger and flag, and the same shapes of the fact table and channel) is undecidable when it holds of some program and fails of another, and decidable when it holds of every program or of none (\texttt{tc\_rice}, \texttt{tc\_rice\_dichotomy}). On packed inputs, the powers of $2$, one program is universal (\texttt{tc\_universal}), and for every transformation $F$ of programs that some program carries out on program numbers there is a program $e$ with the same packed function as $F(e)$ (\texttt{tc\_kleene}). On the multi-register host, a property of programs that depends only on the partial function computed is undecidable when it holds of one program and fails of another (\texttt{sm\_host\_rice\_fun}), and Kleene's recursion theorem holds for computed partial functions (\texttt{sm\_kleene}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Where recursion fails] \textbf{(R)}
When sameness also compares the shapes of the fact table and the channel, some transformation carried out by a program on program numbers has no fixed point, even on packed inputs (\texttt{tc\_no\_fine\_kleene}). On plain inputs, the input in counter $A$ and $0$ in counter $B$, the recursion theorem fails: some transformation $F$ carried out by a program has no program $e$ equivalent to $F(e)$, computing the same plain function (\texttt{tc2\_plain\_recursion\_false}); the proof rests on the fact that no program $P$ multiplies every input by $c(\mathrm{length}\ P)$, the factorial of an explicit bound on the control size of programs of that length, plus $1$ (\texttt{tc2\_no\_mult}).
\end{theorem}

\chapter{The Record Axis}\label{ch:record}

\begin{definition}[Bases and base-driven records]
A base is a deterministic record-free machine: states, a next function, initial states and halting (record \texttt{BaseMachine}). A record-carrying machine has states, a next function, initial states, a record, a ledger and halting (record \texttt{RCM}). A cover maps it onto a base step for step, preserving initial states and halting. The extension is \emph{base-driven} when its record's next value depends only on the base state and itself, the ledger never decreases, A2 holds, the record is permanent, and some reachable step writes it (\texttt{HonestBaseExtension}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The record axis is a latch] \textbf{(S)}
Every base-driven record over every base factors as a latch: there is a base event $h$ such that the base state and the record evolve as $(b, r) \mapsto (\mathrm{next}(b), r \vee h(b))$.
Coq: \texttt{record\_axis\_is\_latch\_holds}. Two permanent records factor as two latches, each reading the other (\texttt{record\_pair\_is\_two\_latches\_holds}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Both premises do work] \textbf{(R)}
A record that can switch back off is not a latch (\texttt{toggle\_not\_latch}). A record switched on by a hidden clock is permanent and not driven by the computation (\texttt{clock\_record\_permanent}, \texttt{clock\_record\_not\_driven}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Which machines carry the axis] \textbf{(S)}
Every base carries it: the base plus a latch on any event it reaches, charging one unit when the latch sets, is a base-driven record (\texttt{latch\_core\_honest}). A reversible base with unbounded memory carries it reversibly (\texttt{history\_latch\_honest}, \texttt{history\_latch\_injective}). With finite memory, a step that writes a permanent record is not injective (\texttt{finite\_reversible\_cannot\_write}).
\end{theorem}

\section{Growing and probabilistic records}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(S)}
A record whose next value is a function of the base state and its current value, that only grows in a partial order, whose ledger charges every strict change, and that changes at some reachable step satisfies a complete family of threshold-latch equations and keeps its price schedule (\texttt{growing\_record\_decomposes\_holds}). A monotone chain of pairwise-distinct $k$-bit vectors has at most $k+1$ members (\texttt{chain\_needs\_bits\_holds}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem} \textbf{(R)}
One Boolean latch does not carry every growing record: a three-value chain over a one-state base is a counterexample (\texttt{one\_latch\_refuted}). In a finite branching model a deterministic latch does not handle branching (\texttt{deterministic\_latch\_handles\_branching\_refuted}), and equal branch support and charges do not determine the branch weights (\texttt{schedule\_determines\_probabilities\_refuted}).
\end{theorem}

\section{Bases}

The relation \texttt{weak\_base\_equiv}, agreement on chosen observations and halting with stuttering steps, is an equivalence (\texttt{weak\_base\_equiv\_refl\_holds}, \texttt{weak\_base\_equiv\_sym\_holds}, \texttt{weak\_base\_equiv\_trans\_holds}). The record axis is a latch over the Cook and Reckhow RAM, the unit-cost random-access machine Stephen Cook and Robert Reckhow defined to measure time bounds (Journal of Computer and System Sciences 7, 1973), and over L (\texttt{record\_axis\_is\_latch\_on\_ram\_holds}, \texttt{record\_axis\_is\_latch\_on\_l\_holds}); these instances follow from the general theorem and do not distinguish the bases. Cost-respecting simulations between different bases are not constructed.

\section{The record over any order}

Let the record be a point of a preorder given by a Boolean test. A step \emph{leaves the down-set} when its new record is not at or below the old one. An \emph{axis system} has states, instructions, a step, a cost per instruction and such a record; it pays the \emph{toll} when every step that leaves the down-set costs at least $1$, and meets the \emph{floor} when every run whose final record is not at or below its starting record costs at least $1$.

\begin{theorem}[The whole direction] \textbf{(S)}
For every axis system the floor holds exactly when the toll holds (\texttt{ax\_floor\_iff\_a2}), and under the toll every run costs at least the number of its steps that leave the down-set (\texttt{ax\_cost\_ge\_exits}). The toll holds exactly when, for every point $a$, the bit ``$a$ is at or below the record'' pays the one-bit toll (\texttt{ax\_a2\_iff\_views}). A record that never drops and whose next value is a function of the base state and the current record factors through thresholds: some event $h$ makes $a$ at or below the next record exactly when $a$ is at or below the current record or $h$ fires (\texttt{ax\_decompose}). A repeat-free chain of $k$-bit words, each word's set bits among the next word's, has at most $k + 1$ members, and some chain has exactly $k + 1$ (\texttt{chain\_bits\_exact}). With finitely many states and a growing record, every instruction that leaves the down-set from some state merges two states (\texttt{ax\_growth\_exit\_merges}), and pricing every merge gives the toll (\texttt{ax\_a2\_from\_merge\_price}). For finitely many states and moves and decidable observations, some price computed from a window is exact exactly when the window has no collision (\texttt{ax\_window\_exact\_iff\_no\_collision}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Completeness and entropy on the axis] \textbf{(S)}
An interface makes a machine of Chapter~\ref{ch:complete} Thiele-complete exactly when the same interface, read over the two-point order with false below true, meets the four clauses on the axis (\texttt{ax\_tc\_two\_point\_iff}). Every Thiele-complete axis machine pays the toll (\texttt{ax\_tc\_a2}), every run from a clean start that ends with the record not at or below the floor makes at least three record moves and raises the ledger by at least $3$ (\texttt{ax\_tc\_certificate\_three}), and no function of the counter-machine window gives the record (\texttt{ax\_tc\_independence}). On the multi-register host, for every reading of final states into a preorder that respects agreement on everything a program can read, a property of programs that respects behaving alike on the record is undecidable when it holds of some program and fails of another (\texttt{ax\_rice\_record}); some host program has runs whose claim sets climb in $m$ strict steps exactly when $m \le 16$, the size of the fact table (\texttt{ax\_single\_run\_boundary}). Under the halving price, on a state space of any size, for a non-negative weighting and any finite list of landing spots, each given with a repeat-free list of exactly the states the move sends to it, the move's conditional entropy loss over those lists is at most their total weight times the move's cost times $\ln 2$ (\texttt{ax\_inf\_loss\_bits}).
\end{theorem}

\chapter{CHSH and Tsirelson}\label{ch:chsh}

Until the last section, the results in this chapter are mathematics about correlators and trial counts, and no machine is fixed. The bound named for Boris Tsirelson, who showed in 1980 that quantum correlations give a CHSH value of at most $2\sqrt{2}$, appears here as algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Classical bound] \textbf{(S)}
A factorizable correlator has $|S| \le 2$ (\texttt{factorizable\_CHSH\_classical\_bound}); trial counts consistent with one deterministic local strategy cannot exceed the classical bound (\texttt{chsh\_stat\_violation\_not\_local}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Algebraic Tsirelson bound] \textbf{(S)}
A rational correlator satisfying the absolute-value bounds and four selected $3\times 3$ polynomial minors has $|S| \le 2\sqrt{2}$ (\texttt{algebraically\_coherent\_tsirelson\_general}). The bound also follows from row constraints and Cauchy--Schwarz (\texttt{tsirelson\_from\_row\_bounds}) and is reached (\texttt{tsirelson\_tight}). No Hilbert space is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The biconditional] \textbf{(S)}
On the zero-marginal slice, the column-contractivity conditions on $E_{00}, E_{01}, E_{10}, E_{11}$ hold if and only if the zero-marginal $5\times 5$ NPA moment matrix is positive semidefinite.
Coq: \texttt{column\_contractive\_iff\_npa\_psd}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The integer check] \textbf{(S)}
If the integer column check accepts eight trial counts, the zero-marginal NPA matrix built from their correlators is positive semidefinite (\texttt{column\_contractive\_check\_witness\_npa\_psd}), and a PSD matrix of that form satisfies the Tsirelson bound (\texttt{npa\_psd\_implies\_tsirelson\_bound}). The check is sound in this direction only; a rejection is not proved to mean the conditions fail.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Elliptope] \textbf{(S)}
Call a correlator $(E_{00}, E_{01}, E_{10}, E_{11})$ elliptope-realizable when some choice of the two within-party cross moments completes the zero-marginal NPA matrix to a positive semidefinite matrix (\texttt{elliptope\_realizable}). The comments of \texttt{coq/kernel/quantum/ElliptopeCompletion.v} read this existential completion as the CHSH correlator quantum set, following Tsirelson's characterization; no theorem here proves that identification. Elliptope-realizable points satisfy the Tsirelson bound (\texttt{elliptope\_tsirelson}), and the PR box is not elliptope-realizable (\texttt{pr\_box\_not\_elliptope}). An integer membership check is sound into realizability (\texttt{elliptope\_check\_full\_sound}).
\end{theorem}

No theorem here identifies a correlator with a physical experiment or proves that any device produces CHSH-violating statistics.

\section{The check as a property of the multi-register machine}

This section fixes a machine: the multi-register machine of \texttt{minimal/EarnedMulti.v}, with a counter for every number and one property that reads a counter as a tally of eight trial counts and that passes exactly when the integer check \texttt{small\_chsh\_check} passes.

\begin{theorem}[The check on the machine] \textbf{(S)}
The integer check passes exactly when every question pair was sampled and the pinned $5\times 5$ moment matrix is symmetric and positive semidefinite (\texttt{small\_chsh\_check\_iff}). From a clean start, a raised flag stands on a CHECK, a COMMIT and a CERTIFY of the CHSH claim on one counter, and that counter held a tally passing the check, with $S^2 \le 8$ (\texttt{small\_chsh\_flag\_implies\_tsirelson}). Every passing tally has $S^2 < 8$ (\texttt{nec\_e\_check\_strict}), so none reaches $2\sqrt{2}$ (\texttt{nec\_e\_check\_never\_tsirelson}), and for every $\varepsilon > 0$ some passing tally has $S^2 > 8 - \varepsilon$ (\texttt{nec\_e\_check\_sharp}). Each of the check's seven integer facts is needed (\texttt{nec\_e\_check\_facts\_irredundant}), and the check refuses the tally of one round per question pair of every deterministic plan (\texttt{nec\_e\_check\_refuses\_all\_plans}).
\end{theorem}

\chapter{The Physical Boundary}\label{ch:physics}

\begin{theorem}[Calorimeter] \textbf{(S)}
In a two-state calorimeter protocol, a canonical reset from half-excited to ground satisfies a discrete master equation exactly (\texttt{canonical\_reset\_satisfies\_master\_equation}), and at fixed Hamiltonian the bath receives $\Delta/2$ (\texttt{canonical\_reset\_heat\_exact}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Refutation] \textbf{(R)}
Population dynamics do not fix heat: two gaps with identical dynamics release different heat (\texttt{master\_equation\_does\_not\_fix\_heat\_scale}).
\end{theorem}

Ruling a gap out needs a thermal premise such as local detailed balance, or device evidence. Converting the ledger to energy requires thermal and device premises; the ledger has no intrinsic energy unit (Chapter~\ref{ch:finite}).

\chapter{External Systems and the Pointer Thesis}\label{ch:external}

Each model below is narrow. None is a full security, consensus, database or audit-log theorem.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RFC 9162 (Certificate Transparency).} The iterative inclusion and consistency checks of the specification reject the boundary cases (\texttt{rfc9162\_inclusion\_boundary\_safe}, \texttt{rfc9162\_consistency\_boundary\_safe}) and accept the specification's examples under symbolic digests (\texttt{rfc9162\_example\_inclusion\_d0}, \texttt{rfc9162\_example\_consistency\_4\_7}). Collision resistance and signed-tree-head authenticity are cryptographic premises; neither is proved.
  \item \textbf{Proof-carrying code.} A toy checker accepts exactly when the address-bound verification condition holds (\texttt{pcc\_checker\_accepts\_iff\_vc}). It mirrors the roles in George C. Necula's proof-carrying code (POPL 1997), where a program ships with a proof that it is safe; the full system is outside this model.
  \item \textbf{Trusted Platform Module (TPM) quotes.} A bare sign/verify interface does not imply quote authenticity (\texttt{tpm\_interface\_authenticity\_refuted}).
  \item \textbf{Casper FFG.} Casper FFG is the finality gadget Vitalik Buterin and Virgil Griffith proposed for Ethereum in 2017. Two finalized blocks on conflicting branches imply a slashable quorum (\texttt{accountable\_safety}). This is the accountable-safety theorem of their paper.
\end{itemize}

The addressed-RAM and reversible-update files prove only their named fragments. The cost-semantics correspondences are not full cost-framework embeddings.

\section{The pointer criterion}

The pointer-observable criterion proposes that the event a step rule is forced to price is singled out by redundant record proliferation across observers. Twelve candidate events over seven observer maps proliferate as stated (\texttt{twelve\_candidate\_measurements\_checked}). The MAC-labelled model does not proliferate (\texttt{mac\_model\_not\_proliferating}); applying that to a real system depends on the observer model. A two-observer toggle game with consensus, authenticity and coordinator-free updates revokes its event (\texttt{toggle\_game\_refutes\_strong\_pointer\_necessity}), so those properties do not force permanence. Durable views do imply permanence, by making persistence a premise (\texttt{durable\_consensus\_implies\_permanence}). The external consequence survey contains five narrow countermodels, each for a point a governing source already addresses, and five stronger statements without enough protocol or hardware semantics; no surveyed consequence is simultaneously record-axis dependent, novel, and ready for use. The pointer criterion is a thesis: ``faithfully model'' and ``deployed'' tie it to the world and cannot be defined, as in the Church--Turing thesis.
