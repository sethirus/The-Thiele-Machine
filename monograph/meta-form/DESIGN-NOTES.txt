The Thiele Machine — rejected design experiment

STATUS: Devon rejected this experiment as unreadable and as failing the
artistic brief. Its construction checks establish only that the PDF was
built as specified. They do not establish artistic or reading success.
The account below documents the attempted approach, not an accepted
direction. See ../design-direction/ARTISTIC-BRIEF.txt for the subsequent
research and whole-book reconsideration.

The material for this study is Devon Thiele's account of thinking in shapes,
from “How I got here”. The passage undergoes its own verbs. A description of
an activity becomes the activity's material. There is no separate image
standing in for it.

Fifteen settings contain the same 314 word objects. Relationships, position,
scale, orientation, and sequence change. One page removes the ink of “me,”
while keeping the space it occupied: the remaining words carry its contour.
Alphabetizing preserves the words and removes their sentence relationships.
The ordinary setting returns exactly, down to the rendered pixels.

This is a completed experiment, not a completed design of the monograph.
Its useful discovery is a way for writing and form to act on one another.
Its unresolved problem is scale: the full argument contains long proofs,
tables, references, and hundreds of pages of sustained reading. Simply
inserting this sequence into an otherwise conventional book would not
answer the original brief. A full design must make its reading structure,
its claims, and its apparatus participate without damaging their accuracy.

Other experiments made during this work are withheld as solutions:

* Two synchronized readings of actual revisions. This produced meaningful
  absences but looked and read too much like an editorial comparison.
* New text flowing around the ink of old claims. It made a real spatial
  constraint, but the oversized quotations dominated like magazine layouts.
* The earlier machine-state study. The user rejected its diagrammatic
  treatment; it is not a design direction to carry forward.

Precedents consulted to test claims of novelty and understand book form:

Ulises Carrión, Princeton exhibition on bookworks and sequence:
https://dpul.princeton.edu/ulises_carrion/feature/a-book-is-a-sequence-of-spaces

Keith Smith, Structure of the Visual Book:
https://keithsmithbooks.com/Structure

Tom Phillips, A Humument:
https://www.tomphillips.co.uk/works/humument

Olafur Eliasson, Your House:
https://olafureliasson.net/artwork/your-house-2006

These precedents are reasons not to describe self-reference, typographic
play, altered books, or sequence as unprecedented. No claim that this study
is unlike anything ever made has been established.

Only repository material and public sources were examined. The private
Claude conversations and hundreds of rejected ideas were not available.
