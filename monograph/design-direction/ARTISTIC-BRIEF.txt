THE PROBLEM

The book must be readable as a sustained argument and experienced as a
coherent artwork. Devon asks for an encounter involving the person making
the argument, the machine, the reader, and the reader's grounds for knowing.
The PDF and printed book must carry the same composition. An art insert,
illustrated explanation of self-reference, or spectacular but unreadable
page does not meet that brief.

The failed Shape experiment made a sentence's verbs into typographic
instructions. That reduced a person's description of thought to a sequence
of demonstrations. It sacrificed the actual encounter with the writing.
The machine-state study similarly put a visible lesson in place of an
artistic experience. Neither should supply the new book's visual language.

WHAT THE PRECEDENTS CONTRIBUTE

Las Meninas, Diego Velázquez. The Prado's account discusses the painter's
presence, the mirror, representation, and the spectator's role. The scene
is legible while the relationships between looking, making, and being
looked at remain difficult to exhaust. My design inference: compose the
reader's relationship to the work, as carefully as the objects within it.
This does not license the simplistic claim that every viewer occupies one
definitively established identity in the painting.

Pale Fire, Vladimir Nabokov. The poem, foreword, and commentary participate
in the fiction. The apparatus that appears to explain the work has its
own position inside it. My design inference: an apparatus can carry an
artistic relationship. Here it must remain truthful and useful; fictional
unreliability, invented citations, or sabotaged navigation would destroy
the purpose of this particular book.

James Turrell. In the Guggenheim overview transcript, Turrell describes
working with light itself and with awareness of perception. Curator Nat
Trotman describes structures that recede so that light, air, and space can
be experienced. My design inference: the designed conditions can do more
than an image representing the desired experience. A reader's acts of
following, comparing, accepting, returning, and refusing are available
material. This is not a reason to print empty or dimly legible pages.

Stalker, Andrei Tarkovsky. Mark Le Fanu's Criterion essay describes the
film's concentration into one journey, its silences, faces, tenderness,
and resistance to a single exhaustive allegory. My design inference:
duration and a vulnerable human presence matter. A succession of clever
moments cannot substitute for an experience accumulated over time.

These are structural lessons, not a mood board or evidence of unprecedented
originality. The sources consulted were curatorial material, an artist
transcript, publisher material, and criticism. This work does not claim a
new viewing of the film or a complete rereading of the novel.

THE BOOK'S EXISTING COMPOSITION

The manuscript contains a sustained tension: Devon wants the argument to
hold, and refuses to let wanting it count as evidence. The same person
imagines, directs, insists, checks, and admits what remains unestablished.
The formal machinery matters because somebody has something at stake.

An artistic reading of the existing sequence follows. This is an
interpretation of the book, not an additional mathematical result.

Opening — The point; the two computers; keeping the trace.
The reader is invited to judge what would establish a difference between
identical reported outcomes. The apparent solution, retaining a trace,
raises the question of what establishes the trace. Each move gives the
reader something real and exposes its conditions. There is no need to
add a puzzle, warning, or instruction to distrust the book.

Part I — The picture; How I got here; On the name.
The person responsible enters in detail. The account of seeing, directing,
and refusing must remain continuous, readable prose. The name belongs to
the author, the machine, and a family history. Treat these relationships
with the same seriousness as the formal argument. They do not need a
portrait, a giant ME, or a graphic explaining the title.

Part II — The axiom.
The rule becomes available for examination, including the premises under
which the toll follows. The composition must give the reader room to
follow the argument and encounter its qualifications in the same reading
movement. Do not manufacture suspense by separating a strong claim from
the conditions that make it true.

Part III — The logic.
The reader works inside increasingly exact constructions. Sustained
legibility is essential here. The difficulty should belong to the thought.
The visual rhythm must accommodate long arguments rather than continually
interrupt them with declarations of importance.

Part IV — The machine.
The proposal becomes something inspectable in instructions and executions.
Design attention moves to correspondence: the reader must be able to move
between a definition, its use, and its consequences without losing place.
Existing necessary diagrams and tables have a job; decorative machine
imagery adds none.

Part V — Checking it, and its edge.
The account turns to the means used to check the account. The distinction
between a checked statement and its intended meaning becomes explicit
again. The checker has an exact role; Devon remains answerable for what
he says it establishes. The composition should make that return felt
without suggesting that the proofs have become unreliable or meaningless.

Conclusion and appendices.
The warehouse and two computers return. The last sentence addresses the
reader. The appendices then provide means to investigate. The reader has
been addressed, but the book remains available to be checked and disputed.
Keep that sequence. Do not turn the last sentence into a promotional
poster or make the apparatus an inaccessible back room.

CONSEQUENCES FOR THE ACTUAL DESIGN

1. Compose across the whole book before making a showcase spread.
   The opening warehouse and its return are one compositional pair.
   The account of personal refusal and the account of Coq's refusal are
   another. Establish related paragraph measures, entry positions, and
   surrounding space at those actual passages. Recognition should develop
   through reading. Do not duplicate passages, add arrows between them,
   or label these relationships for the reader.

2. Let a consistent reading surface accumulate meaning.
   Personal prose, mathematical prose, and qualifications should feel
   written by the same person in the same book. Establish distinctions
   needed to read a theorem, proof, citation, or program; avoid giving
   ordinary authorial judgement the appearance of machine certification.
   A consistent surface alone is not the artwork. Its use across the
   relationships above is the compositional work still to be demonstrated.

3. Make pacing specific to the argument.
   Place a turn at a completed movement of thought where possible. Keep
   a short proposition and its hypotheses together. Let a long proof
   continue clearly across pages. Preserve ordinary places to stop,
   recover, and resume. Do not allocate identical theatrical openings
   to every section, or enlarge empty space to simulate profundity.

4. Give investigation a physical form.
   Legible references, reliable folios, a useful contents structure,
   and an opening that stays readable near the gutter are part of the
   relationship being offered. A reader should be able to hold one place
   and return to another. The PDF must retain precisely the same pages,
   sequence, and reference destinations in print. Links can supplement
   that structure but cannot supply a missing artistic effect.

5. Treat material choices as decisions requiring evidence.
   Page size must be tested against real equations, tables, reading
   distance, and the final bulk. Paper opacity, grain, surface, binding,
   and opening behavior require a physical dummy. A luminous screen
   cannot establish how stock feels in the hand. No stock, trim, or
   binding is selected by this brief, and none has been physically tested.
   No effect should depend on cut pages, translucency, animation, or an
   electronic interaction unavailable in the ordinary printed edition.

6. Keep the author present without turning him into a design device.
   The admissions, irritation, curiosity, and insistence are already in
   Devon's sentences. Preserve their continuity and force. Do not add
   an invented narrator or rewrite the argument into a drama about an
   unreliable author. All authorship remains Devon Thiele's.

HOW TO JUDGE THE NEXT REALIZATION

A convincing realization must work in a continuous reading sequence that
includes demanding mathematics, not just in a contact sheet. Its distant
returns must also work in the complete book. Compare the opening, personal
account, a sustained proof, machine construction, checking chapter, and
ending as parts of one composition.

Technical accuracy and clean typesetting are necessary, but they cannot
certify artistic success. The reader must be able to follow the argument
without a design explanation. The visual work must contribute something
that would disappear if the same passages were casually repaginated.
An attractive conventional setting with a philosophical artist statement
attached would still leave the central problem unsolved.

The next realization therefore needs both detailed typesetting and a
whole-book pagination study. A one-paragraph typographic performance
cannot test this direction. This brief establishes what to compose and
why; it does not pretend that the finished visual composition exists.
